# Immunopathology Hypersensitivity

## Hypersensitivity Reactions (Gell & Coombs Classification)
| Type | Mechanism | Time Course | Classic Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type I (Immediate/Anaphylactic)** | IgE-mediated mast cell/basophil degranulation → histamine, leukotrienes | Minutes | Anaphylaxis, allergic rhinitis, asthma, urticaria, food allergy |
| **Type II (Antibody-mediated/Cytotoxic)** | IgG/IgM against cell surface/matrix antigens → complement activation, ADCC, or receptor blockade/stimulation | Minutes-hours | Goodpasture syndrome, Hemolytic disease of newborn, Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, Graves disease, Myasthenia gravis, Pemphigus vulgaris, Pernicious anemia |
| **Type III (Immune complex-mediated)** | Antigen-antibody complexes deposit in tissues → complement activation → neutrophilic inflammation | Hours-days | SLE, Serum sickness, Post-streptococcal GN, Polyarteritis nodosa, Arthus reaction |
| **Type IV (Delayed-type/Cell-mediated)** | T-cell mediated (CD4+ Th1 delayed hypersensitivity or CD8+ direct cytotoxicity) — NO antibody involvement | 24-72 h (delayed) | TB skin test (Mantoux), contact dermatitis (poison ivy, nickel), granulomatous inflammation, transplant rejection (acute cellular), Type 1 DM |

*Mnemonic: "ACID" — Anaphylactic (I), Cytotoxic (II), Immune complex (III), Delayed (IV)*

## Type I Hypersensitivity — Mechanism Detail
1. **Sensitization phase**: allergen exposure → Th2 response → IL-4 → B cells class-switch to IgE → IgE binds FcεRI on mast cells/basophils
2. **Effector phase**: re-exposure → antigen cross-links IgE on mast cells → degranulation
   - **Immediate (minutes)**: preformed mediators — histamine, tryptase
   - **Late-phase (2-24h)**: newly synthesized mediators — leukotrienes (LTC4/D4/E4 = bronchospasm), prostaglandins, cytokines → recruitment of eosinophils/neutrophils

## Type II Hypersensitivity — Subtypes
| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| Complement-mediated cell lysis | Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, transfusion reactions |
| Opsonization/phagocytosis | Warm AIHA (Fc receptor-mediated splenic clearance) |
| Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) | NK cell-mediated killing |
| Antibody-mediated cellular dysfunction (no cell damage) | **Graves disease** (TSH-receptor stimulating antibody), **Myasthenia gravis** (blocking anti-ACh receptor antibody) |
| Antibody against basement membrane/matrix | Goodpasture syndrome (anti-GBM, anti-alpha3 chain of type IV collagen) |

## Type III Hypersensitivity — Key Concepts
- Immune complexes deposit in vessel walls/glomeruli/joints → complement activation (C3a, C5a) → neutrophil recruitment → **fibrinoid necrosis**
- **Arthus reaction**: localized type III reaction (e.g., at injection site)
- **Serum sickness**: systemic type III reaction — fever, urticaria, arthralgia, lymphadenopathy, ~1-2 weeks after antigen exposure (classically after antitoxin/antiserum administration)
- SLE = prototype systemic type III disease (anti-dsDNA/anti-Sm immune complexes)

## Type IV Hypersensitivity — Subtypes
| Subtype | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed-type (DTH) | CD4+ Th1 cells → IFN-γ → macrophage activation | TB skin test (Mantoux), granulomas, contact dermatitis |
| T-cell mediated cytotoxicity | CD8+ CTLs directly kill target cells | Viral hepatitis, Type 1 DM (beta cell destruction), graft rejection |

## Immunodeficiency Disorders — High Yield
| Disorder | Defect | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| X-linked (Bruton) agammaglobulinemia | BTK gene, no B-cell maturation | Recurrent bacterial infections after 6 months (maternal Ab wane); absent tonsils/lymph nodes |
| Common variable immunodeficiency | ↓ plasma cell differentiation | Recurrent sinopulmonary infections, ↑ risk autoimmune disease/lymphoma |
| Selective IgA deficiency | Most common primary immunodeficiency | Often asymptomatic; anaphylaxis risk with blood transfusion (anti-IgA antibodies) |
| DiGeorge syndrome | 22q11 deletion, thymic aplasia | T-cell deficiency, hypocalcemia, cardiac defects (CATCH-22) |
| SCID | Various (IL2RG most common - X-linked; ADA deficiency) | Absent T & B cell function, "bubble boy," fatal without transplant |
| Chronic granulomatous disease | NADPH oxidase defect | Recurrent catalase+ organism infections |
| Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome | WAS gene (X-linked) | Thrombocytopenia, eczema, recurrent infections (WIPE mnemonic) |
| Chediak-Higashi syndrome | LYST gene | Recurrent infections, giant granules, albinism, peripheral neuropathy |
| Hyper-IgM syndrome | CD40L defect (X-linked) | Recurrent pyogenic infections, ↑IgM, ↓IgG/IgA/IgE |
| Complement C5-C9 deficiency | Terminal complement/MAC | Recurrent Neisseria infections |
| C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency | ↓ C1-INH | Hereditary angioedema |

## Transplant Rejection Types
| Type | Timing | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperacute | Minutes-hours | Preformed antibodies against donor antigens (ABO/HLA) → thrombosis |
| Acute | Weeks-months | Cellular (CD8+ T-cell) and/or antibody-mediated |
| Chronic | Months-years | Immune + non-immune mediated fibrosis/vasculopathy |
| Graft-versus-host disease | Variable | Donor T cells attack recipient tissue (skin, gut, liver) — seen in bone marrow/stem cell transplant |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Type I = IgE, mast cells, immediate (anaphylaxis, atopy); Type IV = only T-cell mediated type, no antibody involved, delayed (TB skin test)
- Arthus reaction = localized Type III; Serum sickness = systemic Type III
- Graves disease and myasthenia gravis are both Type II but involve receptor dysfunction without cell destruction
- c-ANCA/PR3 = GPA (Wegener); p-ANCA/MPO = microscopic polyangiitis/EGPA (Churg-Strauss)
- Anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith = most specific for SLE; ANA = most sensitive screening test but non-specific
- Anti-centromere = limited scleroderma (CREST); Anti-Scl-70 = diffuse scleroderma
- Selective IgA deficiency = most common primary immunodeficiency; risk of anaphylactoid transfusion reaction
- DiGeorge = 22q11 deletion, thymic aplasia, hypocalcemia, conotruncal cardiac defects
- Terminal complement deficiency (C5-C9/MAC) → recurrent Neisseria (meningococcal/gonococcal) infections
- Bruton agammaglobulinemia presents after 6 months of age once maternal IgG wanes
