# Cell Injury Adaptation

## Cellular Adaptations
| Adaptation | Definition | Classic Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Atrophy | ↓ cell size (↓ organelles, autophagy) | Disuse muscle atrophy, senile atrophy, denervation atrophy |
| Hypertrophy | ↑ cell size (↑ protein synthesis, no new cells) | Physiologic: pregnant uterus, athlete's heart; Pathologic: hypertensive LVH |
| Hyperplasia | ↑ cell number (cells capable of division) | Physiologic: compensatory (post-hepatectomy liver), hormonal (breast/endometrium); Pathologic: BPH, endometrial hyperplasia |
| Metaplasia | Reversible change of one differentiated cell type → another | Barrett esophagus (squamous→columnar), smoker's bronchus (columnar→squamous), squamous metaplasia of endocervix |
| Dysplasia | Disordered growth with cytologic atypia — **pre-neoplastic**, not a true adaptation | Cervical dysplasia (CIN), Barrett dysplasia |

*High-yield: Metaplasia is always reversible on removal of stimulus; dysplasia is not truly reversible and may progress to carcinoma. Metaplasia itself does not directly become cancer — dysplasia arising within the metaplastic epithelium does.*

## Causes of Cell Injury
- Hypoxia/ischemia (most common cause)
- Physical agents (trauma, thermal, radiation, electric)
- Chemical agents/drugs, toxins
- Infectious agents
- Immunologic reactions (hypersensitivity, autoimmunity)
- Genetic derangements
- Nutritional imbalances

## Reversible vs Irreversible Injury
| Feature | Reversible | Irreversible (Necrosis) |
|---|---|---|
| Cell swelling | Present (ATP depletion → Na+/K+ pump failure) | Present, progresses |
| ER swelling, ribosome detachment | Present | Present |
| Mitochondria | Swelling, no large amorphous densities | **Large amorphous densities**, membrane damage |
| Plasma membrane | Blebbing but intact | Damaged/disrupted → enzyme leakage, Ca2+ influx |
| Nucleus | Normal | Pyknosis → karyorrhexis → karyolysis |
| Myelin figures | Present (reversible) | Present, more extensive |
| Lysosomal membranes | Intact | Ruptured → autolysis |

**Point of no return** for irreversible injury: massive Ca2+ influx with activation of phospholipases/proteases/endonucleases, and mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening → loss of ability to generate ATP.

## Mechanisms of Cell Injury
- **ATP depletion**: failure of Na+/K+ ATPase → cell swelling; failure of Ca2+ pump → Ca2+ influx; ↑ anaerobic glycolysis → ↓ pH, glycogen depletion
- **Mitochondrial damage**: ↓ ATP, formation of mitochondrial permeability transition pore, release of cytochrome c → apoptosis
- **Ca2+ influx**: activates phospholipases (membrane damage), proteases (cytoskeleton damage), endonucleases (chromatin damage), ATPases
- **ROS (oxidative stress)**: lipid peroxidation of membranes, protein cross-linking, DNA damage — generated by normal metabolism, ischemia-reperfusion, radiation, oxygen toxicity
- **Membrane damage**: plasma membrane, mitochondrial membrane, lysosomal membrane (leads to autodigestion)

## Free Radical Injury & Antioxidant Defense
| Free radical generation | Antioxidant defense |
|---|---|
| Ischemia-reperfusion, radiation, oxygen toxicity, drugs (e.g., paracetamol overdose), inflammation (respiratory burst) | Superoxide dismutase (O2•− → H2O2), catalase & glutathione peroxidase (H2O2 → H2O), vitamin E/A/C, ceruloplasmin, transferrin |

## Patterns of Necrosis (Morphology — high yield)
| Type | Morphology | Classic Site |
|---|---|---|
| Coagulative | Architecture preserved initially, cells lose nuclei, gray-firm/wedge-shaped | MI, kidney/spleen infarcts (all solid organs except brain) |
| Liquefactive | Tissue digested → viscous liquid | Brain infarcts, bacterial abscess (pus) |
| Caseous | Cheese-like, structureless, granular debris; surrounded by granulomatous inflammation | TB, fungal infections |
| Fat necrosis | Chalky-white areas from saponification (Ca2+ + fatty acids) | Acute pancreatitis (enzymatic), traumatic fat necrosis of breast |
| Fibrinoid | Deposits of fibrin-like proteinaceous material in vessel walls, bright pink/amorphous on H&E | Immune complex vasculitis, malignant hypertension, preeclampsia |
| Gangrenous | Not a distinct histologic type — coagulative necrosis + superimposed liquefactive (if infected = wet gangrene) | Lower limb ischemia (dry gangrene = coagulative; wet gangrene = + bacterial infection) |

## Apoptosis vs Necrosis
| Feature | Apoptosis | Necrosis |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Physiologic or pathologic, programmed | Always pathologic |
| Cell size | Shrinkage | Swelling |
| Nucleus | Fragmentation into nucleosome-size pieces | Pyknosis, karyolysis |
| Plasma membrane | Intact until late (blebs → apoptotic bodies) | Disrupted early |
| Inflammation | **Absent** (no leakage of contents) | **Present** (release of cellular contents) |
| Mechanism | Caspase cascade — intrinsic (mitochondrial, Bcl-2/Bax) or extrinsic (death receptor, Fas-FasL/TNF) | ATP depletion, membrane damage, Ca2+ influx |
| Morphology | Cell shrinkage, chromatin condensation (pyknosis), apoptotic bodies, phagocytosed without inflammation | Cell/organelle swelling, membrane rupture, inflammatory infiltrate |

### Apoptosis Pathways
- **Intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway**: regulated by Bcl-2 family — pro-apoptotic (Bax, Bak) vs anti-apoptotic (Bcl-2, Bcl-XL) → mitochondrial permeability → cytochrome c release → Apaf-1 → **caspase-9** → executioner caspases-3/6
- **Extrinsic (death receptor) pathway**: Fas-FasL, TNF-R1 → adaptor proteins (FADD) → **caspase-8** → executioner caspases
- Both converge on executioner caspases (caspase-3, -6, -7) → DNA fragmentation, cytoskeletal breakdown, apoptotic body formation
- Cytotoxic T cells also induce apoptosis via perforin-granzyme B (activates caspases directly)

## Intracellular Accumulations
| Substance | Stain | Classic Association |
|---|---|---|
| Fatty change (steatosis) | H&E — clear cytoplasmic vacuoles | Alcoholic/non-alcoholic liver disease, obesity, DM, toxins |
| Cholesterol esters | — | Atherosclerosis (foam cells), xanthomas |
| Proteins (Mallory-Denk bodies) | Eosinophilic hyaline inclusions | Alcoholic liver disease |
| Glycogen | PAS-positive, diastase-sensitive | Diabetes mellitus, glycogen storage diseases |
| Lipofuscin | Golden-brown, "wear and tear pigment" | Aging, cachexia (perinuclear) |
| Hemosiderin | Prussian blue (Perls) positive | Hemosiderosis, hemochromatosis |
| Melanin | Brown-black pigment | Melanocytes, melanoma |
| Calcium (dystrophic) | Von Kossa stain | Necrotic tissue, normal serum Ca2+ (TB, atherosclerotic plaques) |
| Calcium (metastatic) | — | Hypercalcemia, deposits in normal tissue (kidney, lung, gastric mucosa) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Reversible injury hallmark: cell swelling with intact membranes; irreversible injury hallmark: mitochondrial membrane damage + massive Ca2+ influx
- Most common cause of cell injury overall: hypoxia (via ischemia)
- First morphologic change in reversible ischemic injury: ATP depletion → cellular swelling
- Fibrinoid necrosis = classic finding in malignant hypertension and immune complex-mediated vasculitis
- Fat necrosis with saponification (chalky white deposits) = classic for acute pancreatitis
- Caseous necrosis = granulomatous inflammation (TB, fungal) — cheese-like, no visible cell outlines
- Apoptosis is ATP-dependent and does NOT elicit inflammation; necrosis does
- Caspase-9 = intrinsic pathway; caspase-8 = extrinsic pathway; caspase-3/6/7 = executioner (common)
- Bcl-2 overexpression (t(14;18) in follicular lymphoma) → inhibits apoptosis → tumor cell survival
- Dystrophic calcification occurs in dead/dying tissue with normal serum calcium; metastatic calcification occurs in normal tissue with elevated serum calcium (hypercalcemia)
- Lipofuscin = "wear and tear" pigment, indicates aging/free radical injury, not injurious itself
