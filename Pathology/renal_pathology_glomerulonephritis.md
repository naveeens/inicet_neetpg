# Renal Pathology Glomerulonephritis

## Nephrotic vs Nephritic Syndrome
| Feature | Nephrotic Syndrome | Nephritic Syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| Proteinuria | Massive (>3.5 g/day) | Mild-moderate (<3.5 g/day) |
| Hematuria | Absent/minimal | Present — **dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts** |
| Edema | Marked (periorbital, generalized, due to hypoalbuminemia) | Mild-moderate |
| Hypertension | Uncommon | Common |
| Other | Hypoalbuminemia, hyperlipidemia/lipiduria, ↑ infection risk (loss of Ig), hypercoagulability (loss of antithrombin III) | Oliguria, azotemia |

## Nephrotic Syndrome — Causes
| Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Minimal Change Disease** | **Most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in children**; normal light microscopy/normal glomeruli on LM; **effacement of podocyte foot processes on EM**; excellent response to steroids; no immune complex deposits on IF |
| **Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)** | **Most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults (esp. Black patients)**; associated with HIV, heroin use, obesity; segmental sclerosis + hyalinosis; poor steroid response |
| **Membranous Nephropathy** | Most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in Caucasian adults; **thickened GBM with "spike and dome" appearance** (silver stain); subepithelial immune deposits (granular IF); associated with **anti-PLA2R antibodies**, hepatitis B, solid tumors (paraneoplastic), SLE, drugs (NSAIDs, penicillamine) |
| **Membranoproliferative GN (MPGN)** | "Tram-track" appearance (GBM splitting) on silver/PAS stain; Type I (subendothelial deposits, immune complex) vs Type II/dense deposit disease (C3 nephritic factor, intramembranous dense deposits) |
| **Diabetic nephropathy** | Nodular glomerulosclerosis (**Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules**), thickened GBM, mesangial expansion; most common cause of ESRD overall |
| Amyloidosis | Congo red positive deposits, apple-green birefringence |

*High-yield: Minimal change disease = normal glomeruli on LM but foot process effacement on EM ("nothing to see" on light microscopy, hence the name) — steroids are dramatically effective.*

## Nephritic Syndrome — Causes
| Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Post-streptococcal (Post-infectious) GN** | Children, 1-3 weeks post Group A strep pharyngitis/skin infection; **subepithelial "hump-shaped" deposits** on EM; granular IF ("starry sky" pattern) for IgG/C3; hypercellular glomeruli on LM; usually self-limited in children |
| **IgA Nephropathy (Berger disease)** | **Most common GN worldwide**; mesangial IgA deposits; presents with gross hematuria 1-2 days after URI/GI infection ("synpharyngitic"); variable prognosis |
| **Rapidly Progressive (Crescentic) GN** | **Crescents** (fibrin + proliferating parietal epithelial cells + macrophages in Bowman space) on LM — common final pathway of severe glomerular injury; 3 types: Type I (anti-GBM, linear IF — Goodpasture), Type II (immune complex, granular IF — SLE, PSGN, IgA), Type III (pauci-immune, ANCA-associated — GPA, MPA) |
| **Goodpasture Syndrome** | Anti-GBM antibodies (anti-alpha3 chain type IV collagen) — attacks kidney + lung basement membranes → **linear IF**, pulmonary hemorrhage + rapidly progressive GN |
| **Alport Syndrome** | X-linked, type IV collagen mutation (COL4A5); hereditary nephritis + sensorineural deafness + ocular abnormalities; "**basket-weave**" appearance of GBM on EM |
| **Lupus Nephritis** | Various classes (I-VI); Class IV (diffuse proliferative) = most common and most severe; "wire-loop" lesions; **full-house IF** (IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, C1q all positive) |
| **Membranoproliferative GN** | Can present as nephritic-nephrotic overlap |

## Renal Biopsy — Three-Modality Approach (High Yield)
1. **Light microscopy (LM)**: cellularity, sclerosis, crescents
2. **Immunofluorescence (IF)**: pattern of deposits — linear (anti-GBM diseases: Goodpasture) vs granular ("lumpy-bumpy" — immune complex diseases: PSGN, lupus, membranous) vs mesangial (IgA nephropathy) vs negative/pauci-immune (ANCA vasculitis, minimal change disease)
3. **Electron microscopy (EM)**: exact location of deposits (subepithelial = PSGN, membranous; subendothelial = MPGN, lupus; mesangial = IgA nephropathy; intramembranous = dense deposit disease); foot process effacement (minimal change, FSGS)

## Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)
| Type | Cause | Morphology |
|---|---|---|
| Ischemic ATN | Shock, hypotension, hemorrhage | Patchy necrosis, tubulorrhexis, affects straight segments (PCT, thick ascending limb — most metabolically active) |
| Nephrotoxic ATN | Aminoglycosides, contrast dye, heavy metals, myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis), hemoglobin (hemolysis) | Diffuse necrosis limited to proximal convoluted tubule |
- Clinical course: initiation → oliguric phase (days-weeks) → recovery phase (polyuric, risk of hypokalemia)
- Most common cause of intrinsic (renal) acute kidney injury

## Interstitial Nephritis
- Acute (allergic) interstitial nephritis: drug-induced (NSAIDs, penicillins, PPIs, sulfonamides) — eosinophilic interstitial infiltrate, fever/rash/eosinophilia, WBC casts
- Analgesic nephropathy: chronic NSAID/phenacetin use → renal papillary necrosis

## Renal Cystic & Congenital Disease
| Disease | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD)** | PKD1/PKD2 gene; bilateral, presents in adulthood; associated with berry aneurysms (SAH risk), hepatic cysts, mitral valve prolapse; progresses to ESRD |
| Autosomal Recessive PKD | Presents in infancy/childhood, associated with hepatic fibrosis, worse prognosis |
| Medullary cystic disease | Cysts at corticomedullary junction, shrunken kidneys, progressive renal failure |

## Renal Tumors
| Tumor | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| **Renal Cell Carcinoma (Clear cell type — most common)** | VHL gene (3p) mutation (sporadic or von Hippel-Lindau syndrome); clear cytoplasm (glycogen/lipid rich) on histology; hematogenous spread (renal vein invasion classic); paraneoplastic: EPO (polycythemia), PTHrP (hypercalcemia), renin (HTN); flank pain + hematuria + palpable mass (classic triad, uncommon to see all three) |
| **Wilms Tumor (Nephroblastoma)** | Most common renal tumor of childhood (2-5 years); **WT1 gene** mutation; triphasic histology (blastemal, stromal, epithelial); associated with WAGR syndrome, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome; presents as abdominal mass |
| Urothelial (Transitional cell) carcinoma | Most common tumor of renal pelvis/ureter/bladder; risk factors: smoking, aromatic amines, analgesic abuse, cyclophosphamide |
| Angiomyolipoma | Associated with tuberous sclerosis; blood vessels + smooth muscle + fat |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Minimal change disease = most common nephrotic syndrome cause in children, normal LM, foot process effacement on EM, steroid-responsive
- FSGS = most common nephrotic syndrome cause in adults (especially African American), associated with HIV/heroin
- Membranous nephropathy = "spike and dome," anti-PLA2R antibody, most common nephrotic cause in Caucasian adults
- IgA nephropathy (Berger disease) = most common glomerulonephritis worldwide, gross hematuria following URI
- Post-streptococcal GN = subepithelial humps, granular IF, occurs 1-3 weeks after strep throat/skin infection
- Linear IF pattern = anti-GBM disease (Goodpasture); granular ("lumpy-bumpy") IF = immune complex disease
- Crescents on light microscopy = rapidly progressive GN, regardless of underlying cause
- Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules = diabetic nephropathy, nodular glomerulosclerosis
- ADPKD associated with berry aneurysms and hepatic cysts; presents in adulthood (vs autosomal recessive PKD in infancy)
- Wilms tumor = most common renal malignancy in children, WT1 gene, triphasic histology
- Clear cell RCC = most common renal malignancy in adults, VHL gene, classic paraneoplastic triad (polycythemia, hypercalcemia, hypertension)
- ATN is the most common cause of intrinsic acute kidney injury; ischemic ATN spares distal tubule segments relatively (patchy) vs nephrotoxic ATN affects PCT diffusely
