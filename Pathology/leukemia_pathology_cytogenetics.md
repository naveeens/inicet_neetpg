# Leukemia Pathology Cytogenetics

## Overview — Age & Type Correlation
| Leukemia | Peak Age | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| ALL | Children (most common childhood malignancy overall) | Lymphoblasts, TdT+ |
| AML | Adults (median ~65 y), also can follow MDS | Myeloblasts, MPO+, Auer rods |
| CLL | Elderly (>60 y) | Most common leukemia in adults (Western), smudge cells |
| CML | Middle-aged adults (40-60y) | Philadelphia chromosome, massive splenomegaly |

## Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL)
- Most common cancer of childhood; blasts = **TdT+, CD10+ (common ALL), CD19+/CD79a+** (B-ALL) or CD2/CD3/CD7+ (T-ALL)
- Morphology: high N:C ratio blasts, scant cytoplasm, fine chromatin, inconspicuous nucleoli
- PAS positive (block pattern in lymphoblasts, vs diffuse in AML)
- Key cytogenetics:
  - **t(12;21) ETV6-RUNX1** — most common in children, **good prognosis**
  - **t(9;22) BCR-ABL1 (Philadelphia)** — poor prognosis, more common in adults; treat with TKI + chemo
  - t(4;11) MLL-AF4 — infants, poor prognosis
  - Hyperdiploidy — good prognosis; hypodiploidy — poor prognosis
- CNS and testes = classic sanctuary sites for relapse

## Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)
- Myeloblasts: **MPO+, CD13+, CD33+, CD117+**; **Auer rods** (needle-like azurophilic cytoplasmic inclusions — fused primary granules) = pathognomonic when present
- ≥20% blasts in bone marrow required for diagnosis (WHO)
- **Acute promyelocytic leukemia (APML/AML-M3)**: **t(15;17) PML-RARA** → blocks differentiation at promyelocyte stage; abnormal promyelocytes with bundles of Auer rods ("**faggot cells**"); high risk of **DIC** at presentation; treated with **ATRA (all-trans retinoic acid)** — induces differentiation, dramatic response
- AML-M4/M5 (monocytic): gingival infiltration, skin/soft tissue involvement
- AML with t(8;21) RUNX1-RUNX1T1 — good prognosis
- AML with inv(16)/t(16;16) CBFB-MYH11 — good prognosis, associated with abnormal eosinophils
- AML with 11q23 (MLL) rearrangement — poor prognosis, monocytic differentiation
- Prior chemotherapy/radiation (secondary AML) — often 11q23 or complex karyotype, poor prognosis

## Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) / SLL
- Most common leukemia in adults (Western countries)
- Neoplastic mature small B lymphocytes: **CD5+, CD19+, CD20+ (dim), CD23+**
- Peripheral smear: **smudge cells** (fragile lymphocytes disrupted during smear preparation) — classic buzzword
- CLL and SLL (small lymphocytic lymphoma) = same disease, different compartment (blood/marrow vs lymph node)
- Complications: hypogammaglobulinemia (recurrent infections), autoimmune hemolytic anemia (warm AIHA), transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (**Richter transformation**)
- Cytogenetics: del(13q14) — good prognosis; **del(17p) (TP53 loss)** — poor prognosis; del(11q) — poor prognosis; trisomy 12

## Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)
- **Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22)(q34;q11) → BCR-ABL1 fusion gene** — constitutively active tyrosine kinase
- Marked leukocytosis with full spectrum of granulocytic maturation (myelocytes, metamyelocytes, bands, segmented neutrophils, basophilia)
- Massive splenomegaly (classic)
- **Low/absent leukocyte alkaline phosphatase (LAP score)** — key distinguishing feature from a leukemoid reaction (reactive neutrophilia has HIGH LAP score)
- Natural history: chronic phase → accelerated phase → **blast crisis** (transformation to AML in ~2/3, ALL in ~1/3) — median untreated survival to blast crisis ~3-4 years
- Treatment: **Imatinib** and other BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase inhibitors — revolutionized prognosis

## Hairy Cell Leukemia
- Rare B-cell leukemia, elderly males
- **TRAP (tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase) positive**; "hairy" cytoplasmic projections on smear
- Massive splenomegaly with **"dry tap"** on bone marrow aspiration (marrow fibrosis)
- BRAF V600E mutation

## Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS)
- Clonal stem cell disorder → ineffective hematopoiesis, dysplasia in ≥1 lineage, cytopenias despite hypercellular marrow
- ↑ Risk of transformation to AML
- Ring sideroblasts (Prussian blue), dysplastic megakaryocytes, blasts <20% (by definition — differentiates from AML)
- Associated with prior chemo/radiation (alkylating agents, topoisomerase inhibitors), 5q deletion (better prognosis subtype)

## Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (Overview)
| Disease | Key Mutation | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| CML | BCR-ABL1 t(9;22) | Massive splenomegaly, low LAP |
| Polycythemia vera | **JAK2 V617F** (>95%) | ↑ RBC, ↑Hct, low EPO, pruritus after hot shower, erythromelalgia |
| Essential thrombocythemia | JAK2, CALR, MPL | Marked thrombocytosis, thrombosis/bleeding risk |
| Primary myelofibrosis | JAK2, CALR, MPL | Marrow fibrosis, **teardrop cells (dacrocytes)**, leukoerythroblastic peripheral smear, massive splenomegaly (extramedullary hematopoiesis) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Auer rods = pathognomonic for AML (never seen in ALL/CLL); "faggot cells" (bundles of Auer rods) = APML
- t(15;17) PML-RARA (APML) → treat with ATRA; high risk of DIC at presentation
- Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) BCR-ABL1 = CML hallmark; also seen in ~25-30% of adult ALL (poor prognosis subset)
- LAP score: LOW in CML, HIGH in leukemoid reaction — key distinguishing lab test
- Smudge cells on peripheral smear = CLL
- TRAP positive + dry tap on bone marrow = hairy cell leukemia
- JAK2 V617F = mutation of choice tested in polycythemia vera (>95% of cases)
- Teardrop cells (dacrocytes) = primary myelofibrosis
- TdT positivity = marker of lymphoblasts (ALL/lymphoblastic lymphoma) — not expressed in AML or mature lymphoid neoplasms
- Richter transformation = CLL transforming into diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
- MDS defined by <20% blasts with dysplasia; ≥20% blasts reclassifies as AML
