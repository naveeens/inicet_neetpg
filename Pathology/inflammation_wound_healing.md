# Inflammation Wound Healing

## Acute Inflammation
**Cardinal signs**: rubor (redness), calor (heat), tumor (swelling), dolor (pain), function laesa (loss of function).

### Vascular Changes
1. Transient vasoconstriction (seconds)
2. Vasodilation → ↑ blood flow (erythema, heat) — mediated by histamine, NO, prostaglandins
3. ↑ Vascular permeability → exudate formation (protein-rich fluid) → stasis

### Mechanisms of Increased Vascular Permeability
| Mechanism | Mediator/Cause | Vessels Affected |
|---|---|---|
| Endothelial contraction (immediate transient response) | Histamine, bradykinin, leukotrienes | Venules |
| Endothelial retraction (delayed prolonged) | Cytokines (IL-1, TNF, IFN-γ) | Venules |
| Direct endothelial injury (immediate sustained) | Burns, toxins | Arterioles, venules, capillaries |
| Leukocyte-mediated injury | Neutrophil ROS/enzymes | Venules |
| Increased transcytosis | VEGF | Venules |

### Leukocyte Recruitment (Sequence — high yield)
1. **Margination & Rolling** — selectins (E-selectin/P-selectin on endothelium bind sialyl-Lewis X on leukocytes)
2. **Adhesion/Firm attachment** — integrins (LFA-1, Mac-1 on leukocytes) bind ICAM-1/VCAM-1 on endothelium (upregulated by chemokines)
3. **Transmigration (diapedesis)** — through venule walls, mediated by PECAM-1 (CD31)
4. **Chemotaxis** — movement along chemical gradient: C5a, LTB4, IL-8, bacterial products (formyl-methionyl peptides)

*High-yield: Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type I = defect in β2 integrin (CD18) → recurrent bacterial infections, delayed umbilical cord separation, absent pus.*

### Phagocytosis
- **Recognition/attachment**: opsonins — IgG (Fc receptor), C3b (complement receptor), mannose-binding lectin
- **Engulfment**: pseudopod extension → phagosome → fuses with lysosome → phagolysosome
- **Killing**: O2-dependent (NADPH oxidase → superoxide → H2O2 → myeloperoxidase-halide system = most efficient) and O2-independent (lysozyme, defensins, lactoferrin)

*High-yield: Chronic granulomatous disease = NADPH oxidase deficiency → recurrent catalase-positive organism infections (Staph aureus, Aspergillus, Serratia, Burkholderia) — NBT/DHR flow cytometry test.*
*Myeloperoxidase deficiency and Chediak-Higashi syndrome (LYST gene, giant granules, defective phagolysosome fusion) also cause recurrent infections.*

### Chemical Mediators of Inflammation
| Mediator | Source | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Histamine | Mast cells, basophils, platelets | Vasodilation, ↑ permeability (immediate) |
| Prostaglandins | Arachidonic acid (COX pathway) | Vasodilation, pain, fever (PGE2) |
| Leukotrienes | Arachidonic acid (LOX pathway) | LTB4 = chemotaxis; LTC4/D4/E4 = vasoconstriction, bronchospasm, ↑ permeability |
| Platelet-activating factor | Various cells | Platelet aggregation, vasodilation/constriction dose-dependent |
| Complement (C3a, C5a) | Plasma | C5a = chemotaxis + anaphylatoxin; C3b = opsonin; C5b-9 = MAC |
| Bradykinin (kinin system) | Plasma (kininogen) | Pain, vasodilation, ↑ permeability |
| Cytokines (TNF, IL-1, IL-6) | Macrophages | Fever, acute phase response, endothelial activation |
| Chemokines (IL-8) | Macrophages, endothelium | Chemotaxis (neutrophils) |
| Nitric oxide | Endothelium, macrophages | Vasodilation, microbial killing |

## Types of Exudate
| Type | Protein/Cell content | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Serous | Low protein, few cells | Early inflammation, burns, viral |
| Fibrinous | High fibrinogen | Severe injury (pericarditis, lobar pneumonia) |
| Purulent/suppurative | Neutrophils + necrotic debris + organisms | Bacterial (pyogenic) infection, abscess |
| Hemorrhagic | RBCs | Severe vascular injury |

## Patterns of Acute Inflammation
- Serous, fibrinous, purulent (suppurative), ulcerative

## Outcomes of Acute Inflammation
1. Complete resolution
2. Healing by fibrosis/scarring
3. Abscess formation
4. Progression to chronic inflammation

## Chronic Inflammation
- Mononuclear infiltrate: macrophages, lymphocytes, plasma cells
- Tissue destruction + attempts at repair (angiogenesis, fibrosis) occurring simultaneously
- Causes: persistent infections (TB, viral), prolonged toxic agent exposure (silicosis), autoimmune diseases

### Granulomatous Inflammation
- Focal collection of activated macrophages (epithelioid cells) ± giant cells, surrounded by lymphocytes
- **Caseating granuloma**: central necrosis — TB (classic), fungal
- **Non-caseating granuloma**: no central necrosis — sarcoidosis, Crohn disease, foreign body, berylliosis
- Formation: Th1 response — macrophage presents antigen → CD4+ Th1 cells release IFN-γ → macrophage activation → epithelioid transformation
- Giant cell types: **Langhans giant cell** (horseshoe/peripheral arrangement of nuclei — TB); foreign body giant cell (haphazard nuclei); Touton giant cell (xanthoma, fat necrosis — central nuclei ring with peripheral foamy cytoplasm)

## Systemic Effects of Inflammation
- **Fever**: pyrogens (LPS) → macrophages release IL-1, TNF, IL-6 → ↑ PGE2 in hypothalamus → resets thermoregulatory set point
- **Acute phase proteins** (liver, IL-6 driven): CRP, fibrinogen, serum amyloid A (↑); albumin, transferrin (↓, negative acute phase reactants)
- **Leukocytosis**: neutrophilia (bacterial), lymphocytosis (viral), eosinophilia (parasitic/allergic), leukopenia (typhoid, viral, overwhelming sepsis)
- **Sepsis**: TNF, IL-1, IL-12 → septic shock, DIC

## Wound Healing

### Phases of Wound Healing
| Phase | Timing | Key Events |
|---|---|---|
| Hemostasis/Inflammation | 0–3 days | Clot formation, neutrophils (day 1–2) then macrophages (day 3+) |
| Proliferation | Day 3–2 weeks | **Granulation tissue** formation — fibroblasts + angiogenesis; re-epithelialization |
| Remodeling/Maturation | Weeks to months | Type III collagen replaced by **type I collagen**; ↑ tensile strength; wound contraction (myofibroblasts) |

### Healing by Primary vs Secondary Intention
| Feature | Primary Intention | Secondary Intention |
|---|---|---|
| Wound edges | Apposed (clean surgical incision) | Not apposed (large defect) |
| Granulation tissue | Minimal | Extensive |
| Wound contraction | Minimal | Significant (myofibroblasts) |
| Scar | Small, linear | Larger |

### Growth Factors in Repair
- **VEGF** — angiogenesis
- **PDGF** — fibroblast/smooth muscle chemotaxis & proliferation
- **FGF** — angiogenesis, epithelialization
- **TGF-β** — fibrosis, collagen synthesis (also anti-inflammatory)
- **EGF** — epithelial proliferation

### Collagen in Wound Healing
- Granulation tissue: **type III collagen** (thin, laid early)
- Mature scar: **type I collagen** (thick, replaces type III during remodeling, via collagenases/MMPs — zinc-dependent, need vitamin C and zinc for proper function)

### Factors Impairing Wound Healing
- **Local**: infection (most common cause of delayed healing), poor blood supply/ischemia, foreign body, denervation, mechanical stress, size/location of wound
- **Systemic**: malnutrition (protein, **vitamin C — needed for prolyl/lysyl hydroxylase, collagen cross-linking**), diabetes mellitus, steroids (inhibit collagen synthesis & anti-inflammatory), old age, zinc deficiency

### Aberrations of Wound Healing
| Condition | Description |
|---|---|
| Hypertrophic scar | Excess collagen, confined to wound margins |
| Keloid | Excess collagen extending **beyond** wound margins; more common in Black/dark-skinned individuals, familial; type III → I collagen imbalance |
| Exuberant granulation ("proud flesh") | Excess granulation tissue protruding above skin, needs cautery/excision |
| Wound dehiscence | Mechanical wound breakdown |
| Contracture | Exaggerated wound contraction (e.g., burns → limits joint movement) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy) → defective collagen cross-linking → impaired wound healing, perifollicular hemorrhages
- Type III collagen (granulation tissue) is replaced by type I collagen during remodeling — net gain in tensile strength but never reaches 100% of original
- Keloid = collagen beyond wound margins; hypertrophic scar = confined to margins
- Leukocyte adhesion deficiency: CD18 (integrin) defect → delayed cord separation, no pus, recurrent infection
- Chronic granulomatous disease: NADPH oxidase deficiency → recurrent catalase-positive organism infection; diagnosed by NBT/DHR test
- Langhans giant cell (peripheral horseshoe nuclei) = TB; Touton giant cell = fat necrosis/xanthoma
- C5a = most important chemotactic & anaphylatoxin complement fragment
- Myeloperoxidase-H2O2-halide system = most effective bactericidal mechanism of neutrophils
- PGE2 (via COX) mediates fever and pain; that's why NSAIDs (COX inhibitors) are antipyretic/analgesic
- Steroids delay wound healing primarily by inhibiting collagen synthesis and suppressing inflammation
