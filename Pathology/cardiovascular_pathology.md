# Cardiovascular Pathology

## Atherosclerosis
### Pathogenesis (Response to Injury Hypothesis)
1. Chronic endothelial injury (hyperlipidemia, HTN, smoking, hemodynamic stress, toxins)
2. ↑ Vascular permeability → LDL enters intima → oxidized LDL
3. Macrophages engulf oxidized LDL → **foam cells** → fatty streak (earliest lesion, can appear in childhood)
4. Smooth muscle cell migration/proliferation from media to intima, produce ECM/collagen → fibrous cap
5. **Atheromatous plaque**: lipid core (cholesterol clefts, necrotic debris) + fibrous cap + inflammatory cells
6. Complications: calcification, ulceration, hemorrhage into plaque, rupture → thrombosis

### Risk Factors
- Major modifiable: hyperlipidemia (↑LDL, ↓HDL), hypertension, smoking, diabetes mellitus
- Non-modifiable: age, male sex/postmenopausal female, family history/genetics
- Sites of predilection: abdominal aorta > coronary > popliteal > carotid arteries; branch points favored (turbulent flow)

### Vulnerable Plaque
- Thin fibrous cap, large lipid core, high inflammatory cell content → prone to rupture → acute thrombosis (ACS)
- Stable plaque: thick fibrous cap, more calcified/fibrotic, less likely to rupture (but causes stable angina from stenosis)

## Hypertensive Vascular Disease
| Vessel Change | Type of Hypertension |
|---|---|
| **Hyaline arteriolosclerosis** | Benign hypertension, diabetes — homogeneous pink hyaline thickening of arteriolar wall, luminal narrowing |
| **Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis ("onion-skinning")** | Malignant hypertension — concentric laminated smooth muscle thickening ± fibrinoid necrosis |
| Fibrinoid necrosis | Malignant hypertension — necrotizing arteriolitis, classic in kidney |

## Aneurysms
| Type | Cause | Classic Location |
|---|---|---|
| Atherosclerotic (fusiform) | Atherosclerosis | Abdominal aorta (infrarenal) |
| Syphilitic (luetic) | Tertiary syphilis — obliterative endarteritis of vasa vasorum → medial ischemia/scarring | Ascending aorta/aortic root → aortic regurgitation, "tree bark" appearance |
| Berry (saccular) aneurysm | Congenital wall weakness | Circle of Willis (anterior communicating artery most common) — associated with ADPKD, Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan; risk of subarachnoid hemorrhage |
| Dissecting aneurysm/Aortic dissection | Hypertension (most common), cystic medial degeneration (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos) | Intimal tear → blood dissects media; presents as tearing chest pain radiating to back |

*High-yield: Marfan syndrome (fibrillin-1 defect) and Ehlers-Danlos (type IV, COL3A1) predispose to cystic medial degeneration and aortic dissection.*

## Vasculitis — Classification by Vessel Size
| Vessel Size | Disease | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Large vessel | **Giant cell (temporal) arteritis** | Elderly, unilateral headache, jaw claudication, ↑ESR, risk of blindness (ophthalmic artery), granulomatous inflammation with giant cells; responds to steroids |
| Large vessel | **Takayasu arteritis** | Young Asian women, "pulseless disease," aortic arch and branches, granulomatous |
| Medium vessel | **Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN)** | Systemic, spares lungs, associated with Hepatitis B; fibrinoid necrosis, "string of pearls" on angiography; p-ANCA sometimes |
| Medium vessel | **Kawasaki disease** | Children, mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome, coronary artery aneurysms (major complication), fever + conjunctivitis + rash + cervical lymphadenopathy + strawberry tongue |
| Medium vessel | Buerger disease (thromboangiitis obliterans) | Heavy smokers, distal extremities, severe pain, may lead to gangrene |
| Small vessel | **Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Wegener)** | c-ANCA/PR3, necrotizing granulomas — upper respiratory, lungs, kidneys (rapidly progressive GN) |
| Small vessel | **Microscopic polyangiitis** | p-ANCA/MPO, no granulomas, lungs + kidneys |
| Small vessel | **Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Churg-Strauss)** | p-ANCA, asthma + eosinophilia + granulomas |
| Small vessel | Henoch-Schönlein purpura (IgA vasculitis) | Children, palpable purpura + arthralgia + abdominal pain + IgA nephropathy, follows URI |

## Cardiomyopathies
| Type | Cause | Echo/Morphology | Clinical |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dilated (DCM)** | Idiopathic (most common), viral myocarditis (Coxsackie B), alcohol, peripartum, doxorubicin | Four-chamber dilation, thin walls, ↓EF (systolic dysfunction) | Progressive CHF, S3 gallop, eccentric hypertrophy |
| **Hypertrophic (HCM)** | Autosomal dominant, sarcomere protein mutations (beta-myosin heavy chain most common) | Asymmetric septal hypertrophy, "banana-shaped" LV, myofiber disarray on histology | Sudden cardiac death in young athletes, dynamic LVOT obstruction, systolic anterior motion of mitral valve |
| **Restrictive** | Amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, hemochromatosis, endomyocardial fibrosis, Loeffler endocarditis | Normal-sized ventricles, ↑ stiffness, diastolic dysfunction | Preserved EF, signs of diastolic heart failure |

## Infective Endocarditis
| Feature | Acute (Staph aureus) | Subacute (Strep viridans) |
|---|---|---|
| Valve | Previously normal valve | Previously damaged/abnormal valve (rheumatic, prosthetic) |
| Course | Rapid, destructive | Indolent |
| Vegetations | Large, friable | Smaller |

- Culture-negative endocarditis: think **HACEK organisms** or prior antibiotic use
- IV drug users → tricuspid valve involvement (Staph aureus most common)
- Complications: septic emboli, valve destruction/regurgitation, glomerulonephritis (immune complex), Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, splinter hemorrhages, Roth spots

## Rheumatic Fever & Rheumatic Heart Disease
- Post-**Group A Streptococcus (S. pyogenes)** pharyngitis (NOT skin infection) — molecular mimicry (antibodies against M protein cross-react with cardiac myosin/valve tissue)
- **Jones criteria**: major (carditis, migratory polyarthritis, Sydenham chorea, erythema marginatum, subcutaneous nodules) + minor (fever, ↑ESR/CRP, arthralgia, prolonged PR)
- Acute: **pancarditis**, mitral valve most commonly affected (mitral > aortic > tricuspid); **Aschoff bodies** (foci of fibrinoid necrosis with plump activated macrophages = **Anitschkow cells**, "caterpillar nuclei")
- Chronic RHD: fibrous thickening, commissural fusion → **mitral stenosis** ("fish-mouth" valve) — classic long-term sequela

## Valvular Heart Disease — Key Associations
| Valve Lesion | Classic Cause |
|---|---|
| Mitral stenosis | Rheumatic heart disease (almost always) |
| Mitral regurgitation | Myxomatous degeneration (mitral valve prolapse), rheumatic, papillary muscle rupture (post-MI), endocarditis |
| Aortic stenosis (elderly) | Senile calcific (degenerative) — most common valve lesion overall in elderly |
| Aortic stenosis (young) | Bicuspid aortic valve (congenital) |
| Aortic regurgitation | Syphilitic aortitis, Marfan/aortic root dilation, endocarditis, rheumatic |

## Congenital Heart Disease — Shunt Classification
| Type | Examples | Physiology |
|---|---|---|
| Left-to-right (acyanotic initially) | **VSD (most common CHD)**, ASD, PDA | Can lead to Eisenmenger syndrome (reversal to right-to-left) if uncorrected |
| Right-to-left (cyanotic) | **Tetralogy of Fallot (most common cyanotic CHD)**, Transposition of great arteries, Truncus arteriosus, Tricuspid atresia, TAPVR | Early cyanosis |
| Obstructive | Coarctation of aorta (adult type = post-ductal, associated with notching of ribs, HTN in upper extremities), Aortic/pulmonic stenosis | — |

*High-yield: Tetralogy of Fallot = VSD + pulmonic stenosis + overriding aorta + RVH ("boot-shaped heart" on X-ray); "tet spells" relieved by squatting (↑SVR reduces right-to-left shunt).*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Fatty streak = earliest atherosclerotic lesion, foam cells (lipid-laden macrophages), can be seen even in children
- Hyaline arteriolosclerosis = benign HTN/diabetes; hyperplastic ("onion-skin") arteriolosclerosis = malignant HTN
- Berry aneurysm = anterior communicating artery most common site, associated with ADPKD
- Tertiary syphilis → ascending aortic aneurysm ("tree bark" aorta) via obliterative endarteritis of vasa vasorum
- Giant cell arteritis = most common vasculitis in elderly, risk of blindness, dramatic steroid response
- Kawasaki disease's major complication is coronary artery aneurysm
- Aschoff body with Anitschkow ("caterpillar") cells = pathognomonic for rheumatic fever/rheumatic heart disease
- Mitral valve is most commonly affected valve in rheumatic heart disease; mitral stenosis = classic chronic sequela
- HCM = most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes
- Senile calcific aortic stenosis = most common valvular lesion in the elderly; bicuspid valve = cause in younger patients
- VSD = most common congenital heart defect overall; Tetralogy of Fallot = most common cyanotic CHD
- Coxsackievirus B = most common cause of viral myocarditis, can progress to dilated cardiomyopathy
