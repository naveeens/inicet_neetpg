# Neoplasia General Oncogenes

## Nomenclature
| Tissue | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Glandular epithelium | Adenoma | Adenocarcinoma |
| Squamous epithelium | Papilloma | Squamous cell carcinoma |
| Connective tissue (mesenchymal) | -oma (e.g., lipoma, chondroma, leiomyoma) | Sarcoma (e.g., liposarcoma, chondrosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma) |
| Blood-forming cells | — | Leukemia, lymphoma |
| Melanocytes | Nevus | Melanoma |
| Germ cells (totipotent) | Mature teratoma | Immature teratoma, other germ cell tumors |

*High-yield exam traps (misnomers — "-oma" but malignant): hepatoma (= hepatocellular carcinoma), melanoma, seminoma, lymphoma, mesothelioma — all malignant despite the suffix.*

## Benign vs Malignant — Differentiating Features
| Feature | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiation | Well differentiated, resembles parent tissue | Variable, often poorly differentiated (anaplasia) |
| Growth rate | Slow | Rapid, mitoses common/atypical |
| Nuclear features | Normal N:C ratio | ↑ N:C ratio, hyperchromasia, pleomorphism, prominent nucleoli |
| Border/margin | Well circumscribed, often encapsulated | Irregular, infiltrative |
| Local invasion | No | Yes |
| Metastasis | No | Yes |
| Necrosis/hemorrhage | Rare | Common (outgrows blood supply) |

## Anaplasia — Morphologic Features (high yield for histology questions)
- Pleomorphism (variation in size/shape)
- ↑ Nuclear:cytoplasmic ratio
- Hyperchromatic nuclei with coarse chromatin
- Prominent/multiple nucleoli
- Numerous, often abnormal mitotic figures
- Loss of polarity
- Tumor giant cells

## Local Invasion vs Metastasis
- **Invasion**: direct extension into surrounding tissue — malignant tumors invade, benign tumors compress/push (pseudocapsule)
- **Metastasis** = definitive marker of malignancy (with rare exceptions — basal cell carcinoma & gliomas rarely metastasize despite being malignant/locally aggressive)

### Routes of Metastasis
| Route | Classic Examples |
|---|---|
| Hematogenous | Sarcomas (favor this route); carcinomas — RCC, HCC, choriocarcinoma, follicular thyroid carcinoma invade veins; liver & lung = most common hematogenous sites (via portal & systemic circulation respectively) |
| Lymphatic | Carcinomas (favor this route) — follows natural lymphatic drainage; e.g., breast carcinoma → axillary nodes |
| Transcoelomic (seeding of body cavities) | Ovarian carcinoma → peritoneal seeding, Krukenberg tumor (bilateral ovarian mets, classically from gastric signet ring adenocarcinoma) |

*High-yield: Sentinel lymph node = first node in lymphatic drainage basin — biopsied in breast cancer/melanoma staging.*

## Carcinogenesis — Molecular Basis
Cancer = multistep process of accumulated mutations in:
1. **Oncogenes** (mutated/overexpressed proto-oncogenes) — gain of function, act in dominant fashion (one allele sufficient)
2. **Tumor suppressor genes** — loss of function, generally need both alleles inactivated ("two-hit hypothesis" — Knudson)
3. **DNA repair genes** — mutations lead to genomic instability (mismatch repair, nucleotide excision repair, etc.)
4. **Genes regulating apoptosis**

## Key Tumor Suppressor Genes
| Gene | Function | Associated Tumor / Syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| **RB** | Cell cycle checkpoint (G1-S) | Retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma |
| **TP53** ("guardian of genome") | Cell cycle arrest, apoptosis after DNA damage | **Most commonly mutated gene in human cancer overall**; Li-Fraumeni syndrome (germline) |
| APC | Wnt pathway regulation | Familial Adenomatous Polyposis → colon cancer |
| BRCA1/BRCA2 | DNA repair (homologous recombination) | Hereditary breast & ovarian cancer |
| NF1/NF2 | RAS-GAP / cytoskeleton | Neurofibromatosis type 1/2 |
| VHL | Regulates HIF degradation | von Hippel-Lindau — RCC, hemangioblastoma, pheochromocytoma |
| WT1 | Transcription factor | Wilms tumor |
| PTEN | PI3K/AKT pathway | Cowden syndrome, endometrial carcinoma |
| DPC4/SMAD4 | TGF-β signaling | Pancreatic carcinoma |
| MEN1 | — | Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 1 |

*High-yield: TP53 = most commonly mutated gene across all human cancers; RB = first tumor suppressor discovered ("two-hit" model demonstrated in retinoblastoma).*

## Hallmarks of Cancer (Hanahan & Weinberg)
- Self-sufficiency in growth signals
- Insensitivity to growth-inhibitory (anti-growth) signals
- Evasion of apoptosis
- Limitless replicative potential (telomerase reactivation)
- Sustained angiogenesis
- Invasion and metastasis
- Evasion of immune destruction
- Reprogrammed energy metabolism (Warburg effect — aerobic glycolysis)

## Grading vs Staging
| | Grading | Staging |
|---|---|---|
| Based on | Degree of differentiation/histologic aggressiveness | Anatomic extent of spread |
| System | Grade I–IV (well to poorly differentiated) | TNM (Tumor size, Nodes, Metastasis) |
| Clinical relevance | Prognosis (weaker) | **Prognosis (stronger predictor overall)** |

## Tumor Suppressor "Two-Hit" vs Oncogene "One-Hit"
- Oncogenes: single mutated allele sufficient (gain of function, dominant) — e.g., RAS
- Tumor suppressors: require inactivation of both alleles (loss of function, recessive at cellular level) — first hit may be inherited (familial cancers), second hit is somatic (Knudson's two-hit hypothesis, classically demonstrated in retinoblastoma)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- TP53 = single most commonly mutated gene in human cancer overall
- RAS = most commonly mutated oncogene (proto-oncogene) in human tumors
- Metastasis is the single most reliable indicator of malignancy (exceptions: BCC, gliomas rarely metastasize)
- Sarcomas spread hematogenously; carcinomas spread via lymphatics (general rule, with exceptions like RCC/HCC/choriocarcinoma which favor hematogenous spread)
- Krukenberg tumor = bilateral ovarian metastases, classically from gastric signet-ring cell adenocarcinoma
- Liver and lungs are the most common sites of hematogenous metastasis (rich blood supply, capillary beds)
- Two-hit hypothesis (Knudson) explains recessive tumor suppressor gene inactivation, first demonstrated in retinoblastoma (RB gene)
- HPV E6 degrades p53; E7 degrades RB — dual inactivation of tumor suppressors drives cervical carcinogenesis
- Aflatoxin B1 causes a specific p53 mutation (codon 249) associated with HCC
- Warburg effect = cancer cells preferentially use aerobic glycolysis even in presence of oxygen (basis of FDG-PET imaging)
- Staging (TNM) is generally a stronger prognostic predictor than grading
