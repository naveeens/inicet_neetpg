# Lymphoma Plasma Cell Dyscrasias

## Hodgkin vs Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma
| Feature | Hodgkin Lymphoma | Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma |
|---|---|---|
| Spread | Contiguous, orderly (nodal → adjacent nodal) | Non-contiguous, unpredictable |
| Extranodal involvement | Rare | Common |
| Cell of origin | Reed-Sternberg cells (B-cell origin, clonal) in a background of reactive cells | Malignant lymphocytes (B or T) |
| Constitutional (B) symptoms | Common (fever, weight loss, night sweats) | Variable |
| Curability | Generally good prognosis, often curable | Variable by subtype |

## Hodgkin Lymphoma
- **Reed-Sternberg cell**: large binucleate/multinucleate cell with prominent eosinophilic nucleoli ("**owl-eye**" appearance) — classic diagnostic cell
- Immunophenotype: **CD15+, CD30+**, usually CD20−/PAX5 weak+ (differs from most B-cell lymphomas)
- Associated with **EBV** (esp. mixed cellularity, classic type)

### Subtypes of Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma
| Subtype | Frequency | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Nodular sclerosis** | Most common (~70%) | Young women, mediastinal mass, collagen bands dividing nodules, **lacunar cells** (RS variant) |
| Mixed cellularity | 2nd most common | Older patients, EBV association strong, better mix of inflammatory cells |
| Lymphocyte-rich | Uncommon | Best prognosis |
| Lymphocyte-depleted | Rare | Elderly, HIV+, worst prognosis, numerous RS cells |
| Nodular lymphocyte-predominant (non-classical) | ~5% | **"Popcorn cells" (LP cells)** — CD20+, CD15−/CD30− (unlike classical HL); indolent |

*High-yield: Nodular sclerosis = most common overall & most common in young women with mediastinal mass; Lymphocyte-depleted = worst prognosis; Lymphocyte-rich = best prognosis among classical subtypes.*

## Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma — Key Subtypes
| Lymphoma | Cell of Origin | Cytogenetics | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Follicular lymphoma** | Mature B-cell (germinal center) | **t(14;18) BCL2-IGH** | Most common indolent NHL in adults; BCL2 overexpression → resistance to apoptosis; can transform to DLBCL |
| **Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)** | Mature B-cell | MYC, BCL2, BCL6 rearrangements (variable) | Most common NHL overall (adults); aggressive but potentially curable |
| **Burkitt lymphoma** | Mature B-cell | **t(8;14) MYC-IGH** (or t(2;8), t(8;22)) | "**Starry sky**" pattern (macrophages amid high-turnover tumor cells); endemic (African, jaw mass, EBV+) vs sporadic (abdominal) vs immunodeficiency-associated; very high proliferation (Ki-67 ~100%) |
| **Mantle cell lymphoma** | Naive B-cell | **t(11;14) CCND1(Cyclin D1)-IGH** | CD5+, CD23−; moderate aggressiveness, often advanced stage at diagnosis |
| **Marginal zone/MALT lymphoma** | Mature B-cell | t(11;18) | Associated with chronic inflammation — **H. pylori (gastric MALT)**, Sjögren (salivary), Hashimoto (thyroid); may regress with H. pylori eradication |
| **Small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL)** | Mature B-cell | del(13q), del(17p) | Tissue equivalent of CLL |
| Lymphoblastic lymphoma | Precursor T (usually) | — | TdT+; essentially tissue phase of ALL, mediastinal mass in adolescent males |
| Anaplastic large cell lymphoma | T-cell | t(2;5) ALK-NPM1 | CD30+, ALK+ (better prognosis subset) |
| Adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma | T-cell | — | HTLV-1 associated, hypercalcemia, skin lesions |
| Mycosis fungoides/Sézary syndrome | Mature T-cell (skin homing) | — | Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma; **Pautrier microabscesses** (epidermal collections of atypical lymphocytes); Sézary cells (cerebriform nuclei) in blood in leukemic phase |

*High-yield mechanism: t(14;18) juxtaposes BCL2 (anti-apoptotic) next to IGH enhancer → BCL2 overexpression → follicular lymphoma cells evade apoptosis (not increased proliferation).*

## Plasma Cell Dyscrasias

### Multiple Myeloma
- Neoplastic proliferation of plasma cells in bone marrow producing monoclonal immunoglobulin (M protein/paraprotein)
- **CRAB criteria**: hyperCalcemia, Renal insufficiency, Anemia, Bone lesions (lytic — "**punched-out**" lesions on skull X-ray)
- Pathogenesis of bone lesions: plasma cells activate osteoclasts via **RANKL** and inhibit osteoblasts (via DKK1) → pure osteolysis (no reactive new bone → bone scan often negative, unlike metastatic carcinoma)
- Labs: **M-spike on serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP)**, **Bence Jones proteins (free light chains) in urine**, **rouleaux formation** on peripheral smear (RBCs stacked like coins, due to hyperglobulinemia), ↑ ESR
- Bone marrow: ≥10% clonal plasma cells (sheets of plasma cells with "**fried-egg**"/clock-face chromatin, eccentric nucleus, perinuclear halo)
- Complications: recurrent infections (functional hypogammaglobulinemia), **AL amyloidosis** (light chain deposition), renal failure ("myeloma kidney" — light chain/Bence Jones cast nephropathy), pathologic fractures, hyperviscosity syndrome (less common than Waldenström)
- Most common Ig type: **IgG > IgA**; light chain restriction usually kappa

### MGUS (Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance)
- M protein present but **<3 g/dL**, **<10% plasma cells** in marrow, **no CRAB features** (asymptomatic)
- Most common plasma cell dyscrasia overall; ~1%/year risk of progression to multiple myeloma

### Waldenström Macroglobulinemia
- Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma with **monoclonal IgM** production
- Hyperviscosity syndrome (IgM is a large pentamer) — visual disturbances, neurologic symptoms, bleeding
- No lytic bone lesions (key differentiator from myeloma)
- MYD88 L265P mutation common

### Comparison Table
| Feature | MGUS | Multiple Myeloma | Waldenström |
|---|---|---|---|
| M protein | <3 g/dL | Variable, often high | IgM monoclonal |
| Marrow plasma cells | <10% | ≥10% (with CRAB) | Lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate |
| Bone lesions | Absent | Present (lytic) | Absent |
| Hyperviscosity | No | Uncommon | Classic |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Reed-Sternberg cell = "owl-eye" binucleate cell, CD15+/CD30+, hallmark of classical Hodgkin lymphoma
- Nodular sclerosis = most common HL subtype, young women, mediastinal mass
- Lymphocyte-depleted HL = worst prognosis; lymphocyte-rich = best prognosis
- Follicular lymphoma: t(14;18), BCL2 overexpression → apoptosis evasion, not hyperproliferation
- Burkitt lymphoma: t(8;14) MYC-IGH, "starry sky" histology, EBV-associated (endemic form)
- Mantle cell lymphoma: t(11;14), Cyclin D1 overexpression, CD5+/CD23−
- MALT lymphoma of stomach can regress with H. pylori eradication therapy alone
- Multiple myeloma CRAB: hyperCalcemia, Renal failure, Anemia, Bone (punched-out lytic) lesions
- Bence Jones proteins = monoclonal free light chains in urine, cause "myeloma kidney" (cast nephropathy)
- Rouleaux formation on peripheral smear = classic for multiple myeloma (also seen in any hyperglobulinemic state)
- MGUS = M protein present but asymptomatic, <10% plasma cells, no CRAB — most common plasma cell dyscrasia
- Waldenström = monoclonal IgM + hyperviscosity syndrome, NO lytic bone lesions (unlike myeloma)
- AL amyloidosis arises from myeloma light chains — always screen myeloma patients for amyloid if unexplained nephrotic syndrome/cardiomyopathy
