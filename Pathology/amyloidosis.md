# Amyloidosis

## Definition
Extracellular deposition of misfolded proteins that aggregate into insoluble **beta-pleated sheet fibrils**, resistant to degradation, progressively compromising organ function.

## Staining & Diagnosis
- **Congo red stain** → apple-green birefringence under polarized light (gold standard, high-yield exam image)
- H&E: amorphous, eosinophilic, extracellular hyaline material
- Electron microscopy: non-branching fibrils, ~7.5-10 nm diameter
- Immunohistochemistry/mass spectrometry to type the specific amyloid protein (important for treatment)

## Classification by Protein Type
| Type | Precursor Protein | Clinical Setting |
|---|---|---|
| **AL (primary)** | Monoclonal immunoglobulin **light chains** (kappa/lambda) | Plasma cell dyscrasias — multiple myeloma, MGUS, Waldenström |
| **AA (secondary/reactive)** | **Serum Amyloid A** (acute phase protein) | Chronic inflammatory/infectious conditions — RA, IBD, chronic infections (TB, osteomyelitis), FMF |
| **Aβ2M (dialysis-associated)** | Beta-2 microglobulin | Long-term hemodialysis (not filtered by dialysis membranes) |
| **ATTR (familial/senile)** | Transthyretin (mutant = familial; wild-type = senile systemic) | Familial amyloid polyneuropathy/cardiomyopathy; senile cardiac amyloidosis (elderly) |
| **Aβ** | Amyloid precursor protein (APP) cleavage product | Alzheimer disease (cerebral plaques), Down syndrome |
| **IAPP (amylin)** | Islet amyloid polypeptide | Type 2 diabetes mellitus (islet deposits) |
| Calcitonin-derived | Procalcitonin | Medullary thyroid carcinoma |
| Beta-microglobulin variants | — | Localized/organ-specific amyloid |

*High-yield correction on nomenclature: AL = light chain-derived (myeloma-associated); AA = serum Amyloid A-derived (chronic inflammation-associated) — do not confuse the letters with location.*

## Systemic vs Localized
- **Systemic**: AL, AA, ATTR — deposits across multiple organs
- **Localized**: isolated to one organ/tissue (e.g., Aβ in Alzheimer brain, IAPP in pancreatic islets, isolated atrial amyloidosis)

## Clinical Features by Organ (Systemic Amyloidosis)
| Organ | Manifestation |
|---|---|
| Kidney | **Most commonly and severely affected** — nephrotic-range proteinuria, progressive renal failure |
| Heart | Restrictive cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, "**speckled/granular sparkling appearance**" on echocardiography |
| Liver/spleen | Hepatosplenomegaly |
| Nerves | Peripheral/autonomic neuropathy (carpal tunnel syndrome — classic early sign, especially in ATTR/dialysis-associated) |
| GI tract | Macroglossia (classic for AL amyloidosis), malabsorption, GI bleeding |
| Skin | Periorbital purpura ("raccoon eyes" — pinch/pathognomonic sign of AL amyloidosis) |
| Vasculature | Vessel wall infiltration → easy bruising |

*High-yield: Macroglossia + periorbital ecchymoses ("raccoon eyes") on minor trauma = classic exam vignette for AL amyloidosis.*
*High-yield: Carpal tunnel syndrome is a classic early presentation of both dialysis-associated (Aβ2M) and senile systemic (ATTR) amyloidosis.*

## Kidney — Gross & Microscopic
- Gross: enlarged, pale, waxy kidneys (early); shrunken in late stage
- Microscopic: amyloid deposition in glomerular mesangium and capillary walls, tubular basement membranes, and interstitial vessels — Congo red positive with apple-green birefringence

## Diagnosis Workup
- Biopsy of affected organ, or often abdominal fat pad aspirate/rectal biopsy (less invasive screening)
- Congo red stain with polarized light microscopy = confirmatory
- Serum/urine protein electrophoresis + immunofixation to detect underlying plasma cell dyscrasia (if AL suspected)
- Genetic testing for familial ATTR

## Familial Mediterranean Fever (FMF) — high-yield association
- Autosomal recessive, mutation in **MEFV gene** (pyrin protein) → recurrent fever + serositis attacks
- Long-term complication: **AA amyloidosis** (secondary to chronic/recurrent inflammation) — most feared complication, managed with colchicine prophylaxis

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Congo red stain + apple-green birefringence under polarized light = confirmatory test for all amyloid types
- AL amyloidosis = light chain-derived, associated with plasma cell dyscrasias (multiple myeloma)
- AA amyloidosis = serum amyloid A-derived, associated with chronic inflammatory conditions (RA most common cause)
- Kidney is the most commonly and severely involved organ in systemic amyloidosis
- Macroglossia + periorbital "raccoon eyes" ecchymoses = pathognomonic for AL amyloidosis
- Beta-2 microglobulin amyloidosis = classic complication of long-term hemodialysis (carpal tunnel syndrome)
- Senile systemic amyloidosis = wild-type transthyretin (ATTR), affects the heart in the elderly
- Alzheimer disease = localized cerebral Aβ amyloid (from APP); Down syndrome patients develop early Alzheimer changes (APP gene on chromosome 21)
- Medullary thyroid carcinoma stroma classically shows amyloid deposits derived from calcitonin
- Familial Mediterranean fever's most feared long-term complication is AA (secondary) amyloidosis
