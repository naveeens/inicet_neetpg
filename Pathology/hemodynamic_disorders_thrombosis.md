# Hemodynamic Disorders Thrombosis

## Edema
- **Transudate**: low protein (<3 g/dL), low specific gravity (<1.012), few cells — due to ↑ hydrostatic pressure or ↓ oncotic pressure (non-inflammatory)
- **Exudate**: high protein, high specific gravity (>1.020), many cells — due to ↑ vascular permeability (inflammatory)

### Causes of Edema
| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| ↑ Hydrostatic pressure | CHF, venous obstruction (DVT), cirrhosis (portal HTN) |
| ↓ Plasma oncotic pressure (hypoalbuminemia) | Nephrotic syndrome, liver failure, protein malnutrition (kwashiorkor) |
| Lymphatic obstruction (lymphedema) | Filariasis, radical mastectomy/lymph node dissection, tumor infiltration, Milroy disease |
| Na+/water retention | Renal failure, ↑ RAAS activity |
| Inflammation | ↑ vascular permeability (exudate) |

*High-yield: Nephrotic syndrome edema starts periorbitally (loose connective tissue) due to hypoalbuminemia; cardiac edema is dependent/gravitational (ankles → sacrum).*

## Hyperemia vs Congestion
- **Active hyperemia**: ↑ arterial inflow (exercise, inflammation) — bright red
- **Passive congestion**: impaired venous outflow — blue-red (cyanotic)
- **Chronic passive congestion (liver)**: "**nutmeg liver**" — centrilobular congestion/necrosis (zone 3, farthest from blood supply) alternating with paler periportal areas; seen in right heart failure; can progress to **cardiac cirrhosis**
- **Chronic passive congestion (lung)**: seen in left heart failure/mitral stenosis — hemosiderin-laden macrophages = "**heart failure cells**"

## Hemorrhage
- Petechiae (1–2 mm, thrombocytopenia/platelet defect) < Purpura (>3 mm) < Ecchymoses (>1–2 cm, bruise)
- Hematoma (confined hemorrhage within tissue), hemothorax/hemopericardium/hemoperitoneum (body cavity), hemarthrosis (joint — classic in hemophilia)

## Virchow's Triad (Thrombosis)
| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| Endothelial injury | Atherosclerosis, vasculitis, trauma, hypertension, turbulent flow, toxins/smoking |
| Abnormal blood flow (stasis/turbulence) | AF, immobilization, aneurysms, MI (stasis in akinetic segment), hyperviscosity |
| Hypercoagulability | Factor V Leiden (most common inherited), protein C/S deficiency, antithrombin III deficiency, antiphospholipid syndrome, malignancy, pregnancy, OCPs, nephrotic syndrome (loss of antithrombin III) |

*High-yield: Factor V Leiden = most common inherited hypercoagulable state (resistance to inactivation by activated protein C). Prothrombin G20210A mutation = second most common.*

## Arterial vs Venous Thrombus
| Feature | Arterial (White) Thrombus | Venous (Red) Thrombus |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | Platelet-rich, lines of Zahn prominent | RBC + fibrin rich |
| Site | Coronary, cerebral, femoral arteries — superimposed on atherosclerotic plaque | Deep veins of leg (most common — DVT) |
| Propagation | Retrograde | Toward the heart |

## Lines of Zahn
Alternating layers of platelets/fibrin (pale) and RBCs (red) — indicates **antemortem thrombus formation in flowing blood** (helps distinguish from postmortem clot, which lacks this layering and is gelatinous/dark red-currant jelly-like).

## Fate of a Thrombus
1. Propagation
2. Embolization
3. Dissolution (fibrinolysis — most effective if recent)
4. Organization and recanalization (ingrowth of fibroblasts/endothelium)

## Embolism
| Type | Source | Classic Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Pulmonary thromboembolism | DVT (femoral/iliac veins — most common source) | Sudden death (saddle embolus), pulmonary infarct, cor pulmonale |
| Systemic thromboembolism | Left heart mural thrombus (post-MI), AF, valvular vegetations, aortic aneurysm | Stroke, limb ischemia, splenic/renal infarct |
| Fat embolism | Long bone fracture, orthopedic surgery, liposuction | Petechial rash, dyspnea, neuro symptoms 1-3 days after trauma; frothy marrow globules in lung capillaries |
| Air embolism | Decompression sickness (Caisson disease/"the bends"), chest wall injury, laparoscopy, childbirth/abortion | >100 mL needed for effect, "gas bubbles" |
| Amniotic fluid embolism | Labor/delivery, C-section | Sudden dyspnea/cyanosis/shock/DIC; squamous cells, mucin, lanugo hair in maternal pulmonary vessels — high mortality |

*High-yield: >60-75% of pulmonary emboli are clinically silent; saddle embolus at bifurcation of pulmonary artery → sudden death.*

## Infarction
- Ischemic necrosis (usually coagulative, except brain = liquefactive)
- **Red (hemorrhagic) infarcts**: venous occlusion, loose/dual blood supply tissues (lung, intestine), reperfusion injury
- **White (anemic) infarcts**: arterial occlusion in solid organs with single blood supply (heart, spleen, kidney)
- Factors affecting outcome: nature of vascular supply (dual > single), rate of occlusion (slow = collateral development), tissue vulnerability to hypoxia (neurons > cardiac myocytes > fibroblasts), oxygen content of blood

## Shock
| Type | Mechanism | Classic Cause | Hemodynamics (CO / SVR / PCWP) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | ↓ blood/fluid volume | Hemorrhage, burns, dehydration | ↓CO / ↑SVR / ↓PCWP |
| Cardiogenic | Pump failure | MI, arrhythmia, tamponade | ↓CO / ↑SVR / ↑PCWP |
| Distributive - Septic | Vasodilation (endotoxin-mediated cytokine storm — TNF, IL-1) | Gram-negative sepsis | ↑CO (early)/↓SVR / ↓PCWP |
| Distributive - Anaphylactic | Type I hypersensitivity, histamine-mediated vasodilation | Drug/insect/food allergy | ↓SVR |
| Distributive - Neurogenic | Loss of sympathetic tone | Spinal cord injury | ↓SVR |

### Stages of Shock
1. **Compensated (non-progressive)**: reflex tachycardia, vasoconstriction, fluid conservation — reversible
2. **Progressive/Decompensated**: tissue hypoperfusion, metabolic acidosis, worsening — needs aggressive intervention
3. **Irreversible**: widespread cell injury, lysosomal enzyme leakage, multi-organ failure — death even with correction of hemodynamics

*High-yield: Kidney and lung are especially vulnerable in shock → acute tubular necrosis and diffuse alveolar damage ("shock kidney" and "shock lung"/ARDS).*

## DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation)
- Widespread activation of coagulation → consumption of platelets & clotting factors → simultaneous thrombosis + hemorrhage
- Causes: sepsis (most common), obstetric complications (abruption, amniotic fluid embolism, retained products), malignancy (mucin-secreting adenocarcinoma, APML — acute promyelocytic leukemia), massive trauma
- Labs: ↓ platelets, ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ PT/PTT, **↑ D-dimer/FDP** (most specific), schistocytes on peripheral smear (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Virchow's triad: endothelial injury, stasis/turbulence, hypercoagulability
- Factor V Leiden = most common inherited thrombophilia
- Lines of Zahn = antemortem thrombus (alternating platelet-fibrin and RBC layers)
- Nutmeg liver = chronic passive congestion from right heart failure; centrilobular (zone 3) involvement
- Heart failure cells = hemosiderin-laden macrophages in alveoli, from left heart failure/pulmonary congestion
- Most common source of pulmonary embolism = deep veins of the leg (femoral/iliac/popliteal)
- Red infarcts occur in organs with dual blood supply/venous occlusion (lung, intestine); white infarcts in single-supply solid organs (spleen, kidney, heart)
- Brain infarction = liquefactive necrosis (exception to coagulative pattern elsewhere)
- Fat embolism syndrome classically follows long bone fracture, presents 1–3 days later with dyspnea, petechiae, confusion
- Amniotic fluid embolism: squames + mucin + lanugo hair in maternal pulmonary vasculature, associated with DIC
- D-dimer = most specific lab marker for DIC and thrombosis in general
- Septic shock is the most common cause of distributive shock and often has warm extremities early (vasodilation) vs cold/clammy in hypovolemic/cardiogenic shock
