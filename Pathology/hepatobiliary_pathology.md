# Hepatobiliary Pathology

## Patterns of Liver Injury
- Hepatocellular (transaminase predominant — ALT/AST elevated) vs Cholestatic (ALP/GGT/bilirubin predominant)
- **AST:ALT ratio >2** classically suggests **alcoholic liver disease**; ALT > AST in most other hepatocellular injury (viral hepatitis, NAFLD)

## Viral Hepatitis
| Virus | Transmission | Chronicity | Key Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| HAV | Fecal-oral | Never chronic | Self-limited, no carrier state |
| HBV | Blood/sexual/vertical | Can be chronic (~5-10% adults, up to 90% neonates) | DNA virus; risk of HCC even without cirrhosis; vaccine available |
| HCV | Blood (IV drug use) | **Most common cause of chronic viral hepatitis → cirrhosis/HCC** | RNA virus, no vaccine, highest chronicity rate (~80%) |
| HDV | Blood, requires HBV co-infection (defective virus needs HBsAg) | Co-infection vs superinfection (superinfection = worse prognosis) | |
| HEV | Fecal-oral (contaminated water) | Never chronic (except immunosuppressed) | High mortality in pregnant women (fulminant hepatitis) |

### Hepatitis B Serology (classic exam table)
| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HBsAg | Active infection (acute or chronic) |
| Anti-HBs | Immunity (resolved infection or vaccination) |
| Anti-HBc IgM | Acute infection |
| Anti-HBc IgG | Past exposure (resolved or chronic) |
| HBeAg | Active viral replication, high infectivity |
| Anti-HBe | Lower infectivity, seroconversion |
| **"Window period"** | HBsAg negative, Anti-HBs negative, Anti-HBc IgM positive — only marker positive |

## Morphologic Patterns of Hepatitis
- Acute: hepatocyte swelling, ballooning degeneration, spotty (Councilman) apoptotic bodies, lobular disarray, mononuclear portal infiltrate
- Chronic: portal/periportal lymphocytic infiltrate, **piecemeal necrosis** (interface hepatitis), progressive fibrosis → bridging fibrosis → cirrhosis
- Fulminant: massive hepatocyte necrosis, "bridging necrosis"

## Alcoholic Liver Disease — Spectrum
1. **Hepatic steatosis (fatty liver)** — reversible with abstinence, macrovesicular fat
2. **Alcoholic hepatitis** — hepatocyte swelling/necrosis, **Mallory-Denk bodies** (eosinophilic intracytoplasmic hyaline inclusions of damaged intermediate filaments), neutrophilic infiltrate, perivenular/pericellular fibrosis
3. **Cirrhosis** — micronodular pattern classically, irreversible end-stage

## Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD)/NASH
- Associated with obesity, metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, T2DM
- Histologically identical to alcoholic hepatitis (steatosis, ballooning, Mallory-Denk bodies) but NO significant alcohol history
- NASH (steatohepatitis) can progress to cirrhosis — now a leading indication for liver transplant

## Cirrhosis
- Diffuse transformation of liver into fibrous septa-surrounded regenerative nodules — end-stage of chronic liver injury, irreversible
- Causes: alcohol, viral hepatitis (B/C), NASH, autoimmune, hemochromatosis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, biliary (PBC/PSC)
- Complications: portal hypertension (varices, splenomegaly, ascites, caput medusae, hemorrhoids), hepatic encephalopathy, hepatorenal syndrome, ↑ risk HCC, coagulopathy (↓ clotting factors)

## Autoimmune & Metabolic Liver Diseases
| Disease | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| **Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC)** | Middle-aged women; **anti-mitochondrial antibody (AMA)**; destruction of intrahepatic bile ducts, granulomatous; pruritus, ↑ALP |
| **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)** | Associated with **ulcerative colitis**; "onion-skin" periductal fibrosis, "beaded" appearance on ERCP/MRCP; ↑ risk of cholangiocarcinoma |
| Autoimmune hepatitis | Anti-smooth muscle antibody (type 1), anti-LKM1 (type 2); interface hepatitis with plasma cells |
| **Hereditary Hemochromatosis** | HFE gene mutation (C282Y), ↑ intestinal iron absorption; bronze diabetes (skin pigmentation + DM), cirrhosis, cardiomyopathy, arthropathy; Prussian blue positive iron in hepatocytes; ↑ ferritin, ↑ transferrin saturation |
| **Wilson Disease** | ATP7B gene defect, ↓ copper excretion into bile; ↓ ceruloplasmin, ↑ urinary copper; hepatolenticular degeneration, **Kayser-Fleischer rings**, basal ganglia damage, psychiatric symptoms; Rhodanine stain for copper |
| **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency** | PiZZ genotype; PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules in hepatocytes (periportal); panacinar emphysema + liver disease/cirrhosis |

## Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)
- Risk factors: cirrhosis (any cause), chronic HBV/HCV (HBV can cause HCC even without cirrhosis, via direct integration), aflatoxin (Aspergillus, p53 mutation), hemochromatosis
- Tumor marker: **AFP** (also useful for surveillance in cirrhotic patients)
- Morphology: can be trabecular or pseudoglandular pattern; may invade hepatic/portal veins → hematogenous spread
- Fibrolamellar variant: young patients, no cirrhosis/HBV association, better prognosis

## Cholangiocarcinoma
- Risk factors: PSC, liver flukes (Clonorchis sinensis, Opisthorchis), choledochal cysts, Caroli disease
- Klatskin tumor = hilar cholangiocarcinoma

## Gallbladder Pathology
| Condition | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| Cholelithiasis | Risk factors: "Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile" (also rapid weight loss, hemolysis for pigment stones); Cholesterol stones (most common in West) vs pigment stones (hemolysis, biliary infection) |
| Acute cholecystitis | Usually gallstone obstruction of cystic duct → inflammation; Murphy sign |
| Chronic cholecystitis | Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses, thickened wall, "porcelain gallbladder" (dystrophic calcification) — ↑ risk of gallbladder carcinoma |
| Gallbladder carcinoma | Associated with porcelain gallbladder, chronic cholelithiasis; adenocarcinoma most common histology |

## Pancreatic Pathology (High Yield Overlap)
- Acute pancreatitis: gallstones and alcohol = most common causes; autodigestion by activated pancreatic enzymes; fat necrosis with saponification, hemorrhage; Ranson criteria for severity
- Chronic pancreatitis: alcohol = most common cause; parenchymal fibrosis/calcification, exocrine/endocrine insufficiency
- Pancreatic adenocarcinoma: ductal origin, **KRAS mutation** most common; CA 19-9; Trousseau sign, Courvoisier sign (painless jaundice + palpable gallbladder), poor prognosis

## High-Yield One-Liners
- AST:ALT ratio >2 = classic for alcoholic liver disease
- HCV = most common cause of chronic viral hepatitis leading to cirrhosis/HCC worldwide relevance
- "Window period" in HBV serology = only IgM anti-HBc positive
- Mallory-Denk bodies = classic for alcoholic hepatitis (also seen in NASH, Wilson disease)
- AMA (anti-mitochondrial antibody) = PBC; PSC = associated with ulcerative colitis, "onion-skin" fibrosis
- Hemochromatosis = HFE gene, bronze diabetes, iron overload — Prussian blue stain
- Wilson disease = ATP7B gene, Kayser-Fleischer rings, ↓ ceruloplasmin
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency = PAS-positive diastase-resistant hepatocyte globules + panacinar emphysema
- AFP = tumor marker for HCC; cirrhosis is the single most important risk factor for HCC
- Porcelain gallbladder = strong association with gallbladder carcinoma, usually warrants prophylactic cholecystectomy
- KRAS mutation = most common mutation in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma
- Gallstone risk factors mnemonic: Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile (estrogen increases cholesterol saturation of bile)
