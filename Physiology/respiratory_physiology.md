# Respiratory Physiology

## Lung Volumes & Capacities
| Volume/Capacity | Definition | Approx. value (70 kg adult male) |
|---|---|---|
| Tidal Volume (TV) | Normal breath volume | ~500 mL |
| Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) | Max additional inspiration above TV | ~3000 mL |
| Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) | Max additional expiration below TV | ~1100 mL |
| Residual Volume (RV) | Air remaining after max expiration (cannot be measured by spirometry — needs helium dilution/body plethysmography) | ~1200 mL |
| Inspiratory Capacity (IC) = TV + IRV | | ~3500 mL |
| Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) = ERV + RV | Volume at end of normal expiration | ~2300 mL |
| Vital Capacity (VC) = TV + IRV + ERV | Max volume that can be exhaled after max inspiration | ~4600 mL |
| Total Lung Capacity (TLC) = VC + RV | | ~5800 mL |

*High-yield: Any measurement including RV (FRC, TLC) cannot be measured by simple spirometry — requires helium dilution, nitrogen washout, or body plethysmography.*

- **Dead space (anatomic) ≈ 150 mL** — conducting airways, no gas exchange
- **Physiologic dead space** = anatomic + alveolar dead space (normally physiologic ≈ anatomic; alveolar dead space increases in V/Q mismatch, e.g., PE)
- **Bohr equation**: physiologic dead space = TV × (PaCO2 − PECO2)/PaCO2
- **Alveolar ventilation (VA) = (TV − dead space) × respiratory rate**; this, not total minute ventilation, determines alveolar gas exchange

## Compliance & Elastic Properties
- **Lung compliance = ΔVolume/ΔPressure** — normal ≈ 200 mL/cm H2O
- ↓ Compliance (stiffer lung): pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary edema, ARDS — increased work of breathing
- ↑ Compliance: emphysema (loss of elastic recoil) — easier inspiration, but airway collapse on expiration
- **Surfactant** (dipalmitoyl phosphatidylcholine, produced by **type II pneumocytes**): reduces alveolar surface tension → ↑ compliance, prevents alveolar collapse (Laplace's law: P = 2T/r — without surfactant, smaller alveoli would collapse into larger ones); matures late in gestation → **Neonatal/Infant Respiratory Distress Syndrome** in premature infants due to surfactant deficiency
- **Chest wall + lung combined compliance** is lower than either alone

## Airway Resistance & Flow
- Resistance mainly in medium-sized bronchi (not smallest airways — huge cross-sectional area in parallel)
- Bronchial smooth muscle: **sympathetic (β2) → bronchodilation; parasympathetic (vagal, M3) → bronchoconstriction**
- **FEV1/FVC ratio**: obstructive disease (asthma, COPD) → ↓ ratio (<70%, disproportionate ↓ FEV1); restrictive disease (fibrosis) → normal/↑ ratio with ↓ both FEV1 and FVC proportionally

## Ventilation-Perfusion (V/Q) Relationships
- Normal V/Q ratio ≈ 0.8 (overall)
- **Apex of lung** (upright): higher V/Q (~3) — relatively more ventilation than perfusion (perfusion falls faster than ventilation due to gravity)
- **Base of lung**: lower V/Q (~0.6) — relatively more perfusion; both ventilation and perfusion are greater at base than apex, but perfusion increases proportionally more
- V/Q = 0 → shunt (perfused, not ventilated — e.g., atelectasis) — does not improve with 100% O2
- V/Q = ∞ → dead space (ventilated, not perfused — e.g., PE) 
- **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction**: alveolar hypoxia → local pulmonary vasoconstriction → diverts blood to better-ventilated areas — unique adaptive mechanism (opposite of systemic circulation response to hypoxia)

## Oxygen Transport
- **Oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve** — sigmoid shape (cooperative binding)
- **Right shift (↓ affinity, O2 unloads more easily to tissues)** — mnemonic "CADET, face Right": **C**O2 ↑, **A**cid (↓pH), **2,3-DPG ↑, **E**xercise, **T**emperature ↑
- **Left shift (↑ affinity)**: ↓ CO2, ↑ pH, ↓ 2,3-DPG, ↓ temperature, CO poisoning, fetal Hb (HbF has lower affinity for 2,3-DPG → higher O2 affinity than adult Hb — facilitates placental O2 transfer)
- **P50** (PO2 at 50% saturation) normal ≈ 26-27 mmHg; ↑ P50 = right shift
- **Bohr effect**: ↑ CO2/↓ pH in tissues → ↓ Hb-O2 affinity → facilitates O2 unloading at tissues; reverse occurs in lungs
- **Haldane effect**: ↑ O2 binding to Hb → ↓ Hb affinity for CO2 → facilitates CO2 release in lungs
- O2 carried: ~98% bound to hemoglobin (each g Hb carries 1.34 mL O2 at full saturation), ~2% dissolved in plasma

## CO2 Transport
- ~70% as **bicarbonate** (via carbonic anhydrase in RBCs: CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 → H+ + HCO3-; HCO3- exits RBC via Cl-/HCO3- exchanger — "chloride shift")
- ~20-23% bound to hemoglobin (carbaminohemoglobin, binds globin not heme — doesn't compete with O2 binding site directly but affects affinity)
- ~7-10% dissolved in plasma

## Control of Respiration
- **Central chemoreceptors** (medulla, near ventral surface): respond to **CSF [H+]**, indirectly to arterial CO2 (CO2 crosses BBB, forms H+ via carbonic anhydrase) — the dominant driver of resting ventilation
- **Peripheral chemoreceptors** (carotid & aortic bodies): respond to ↓ **PaO2** (mainly significant when PaO2 <60 mmHg), ↑ PaCO2, ↓ pH — carotid bodies also respond to pH changes directly
- In chronic CO2 retainers (e.g., COPD with chronic hypercapnia), central chemoreceptor sensitivity to CO2 blunts over time → **hypoxic drive becomes the dominant stimulus** — caution with high-flow O2 (can suppress hypoxic drive, though the mechanism is more nuanced — includes loss of hypoxic vasoconstriction/V-Q mismatch, Haldane effect)
- **Respiratory centers**: medullary (dorsal respiratory group = inspiration rhythm; ventral respiratory group = forced expiration/inspiration), pontine (pneumotaxic center — limits inspiration, controls rate; apneustic center — prolongs inspiration)
- **Hering-Breuer reflex**: lung stretch receptors (vagal afferents) → inhibit further inspiration — prevents overinflation

## High-Yield One-Liners
- RV, FRC, TLC cannot be measured by spirometry (contain unmeasurable residual volume) — need helium dilution or body plethysmography
- Surfactant (type II pneumocytes) reduces surface tension and prevents small-alveolar collapse (Laplace's law); its deficiency causes neonatal RDS
- Apex of lung has the highest V/Q ratio; base has the lowest — relevant to TB reactivation (apex) vs. aspiration pneumonia (base, especially right lower lobe)
- Right shift of O2-Hb curve = easier O2 unloading to tissues (↑CO2, ↑H+, ↑2,3-DPG, ↑temp — "CADET face Right")
- Bohr effect = CO2/H+ effect on O2 affinity; Haldane effect = O2 effect on CO2 affinity/transport
- Central chemoreceptors respond to CSF H+ (i.e., CO2 diffusing across BBB) and are the primary drivers of resting ventilation; peripheral chemoreceptors are the main sensors of hypoxia
- Fetal hemoglobin has higher O2 affinity than adult Hb (weaker 2,3-DPG binding) — facilitates placental O2 transfer
- Obstructive disease: ↓FEV1/FVC ratio; restrictive disease: normal/increased ratio with proportional ↓ in both FEV1 and FVC
- Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction diverts blood away from poorly ventilated alveoli — the opposite of the systemic vascular response to hypoxia
