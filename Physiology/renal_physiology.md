# Renal Physiology

## Nephron Overview
- Functional unit = nephron: glomerulus (Bowman's capsule + capillary tuft) → PCT → loop of Henle (thin descending, thin + thick ascending) → DCT → collecting duct
- **Cortical nephrons** (~85%): short loops of Henle; **Juxtamedullary nephrons** (~15%): long loops, essential for medullary concentration gradient
- Blood supply: afferent arteriole → glomerular capillaries → efferent arteriole → peritubular capillaries / vasa recta

## Glomerular Filtration
- **GFR normal ≈ 120-125 mL/min** (~180 L/day); **Renal plasma flow (RPF) ≈ 600-650 mL/min**; **Renal blood flow (RBF) ≈ 1000-1200 mL/min** (~20-25% of cardiac output)
- **Filtration fraction = GFR/RPF ≈ 20%**
- Filtration barrier: fenestrated endothelium → basement membrane (charge barrier, negatively charged — repels albumin) → podocyte foot processes with slit diaphragm (size barrier)
- Starling forces across glomerular capillary: glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure (favors filtration) vs. capsular hydrostatic pressure + capillary oncotic pressure (oppose filtration)
- **Autoregulation of GFR/RBF**: myogenic mechanism + **tubuloglomerular feedback** (macula densa senses ↑ NaCl delivery to distal tubule → releases adenosine → afferent arteriolar constriction → ↓ GFR) — maintained over MAP range ~80-180 mmHg

## Measurement of Renal Function
| Marker | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Inulin clearance | Gold standard for GFR | Freely filtered, not reabsorbed/secreted |
| Creatinine clearance | Clinical estimate of GFR | Slightly overestimates true GFR (some tubular secretion) |
| PAH (para-aminohippurate) clearance | Estimates renal plasma flow | Filtered + actively secreted, ~90% removed in one pass at low plasma levels |
| Clearance formula | C = (U × V)/P | U = urine conc., V = urine flow rate, P = plasma conc. |

- If clearance of a substance = GFR → freely filtered, neither reabsorbed nor secreted (e.g., inulin, creatinine ~approx.)
- Clearance > GFR → net secretion (e.g., PAH, urea to a lesser extent, K+ in some states)
- Clearance < GFR → net reabsorption (e.g., glucose, Na+, most electrolytes)
- **Filtered load = GFR × plasma concentration**

## Tubular Function by Segment
| Segment | Key transport | % Na+/water reabsorbed | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCT | Na+-glucose (SGLT2/1), Na+-AA cotransport, Na+/H+ exchange (NHE3), HCO3- reabsorption (via carbonic anhydrase) | ~65% Na+, ~65% water (isosmotic) | Site of glucose, amino acid, most bicarbonate reabsorption; glucose reabsorption saturable (Tm) |
| Thin descending loop | Water permeable (via medullary hypertonicity), impermeable to solute | Water only | Concentrates tubular fluid |
| Thick ascending loop | Na+-K+-2Cl- cotransporter (NKCC2) — target of **loop diuretics** | ~25% Na+ | Impermeable to water — "diluting segment"; generates medullary hypertonicity (countercurrent multiplier) |
| DCT | Na+-Cl- cotransporter (NCC) — target of **thiazide diuretics** | ~5% Na+ | Impermeable to water; site of PTH-mediated Ca2+ reabsorption |
| Collecting duct | Principal cells: ENaC (Na+ reabsorption, aldosterone-regulated) + ROMK (K+ secretion); Aquaporin-2 (ADH-regulated water reabsorption); Intercalated cells: H+/HCO3- transport (acid-base) | ~3-5% Na+ | Site of final urine concentration/dilution, fine-tuning of K+ and acid-base balance |

*High-yield: Loop diuretics (furosemide) act on NKCC2 in thick ascending limb — abolish the countercurrent multiplier, cause the greatest natriuresis; thiazides act on NCC in DCT.*

## Countercurrent Mechanism (Urine Concentration)
- **Countercurrent multiplier**: loop of Henle — descending limb passively permeable to water (not solute), ascending limb actively pumps out NaCl (impermeable to water) → progressively increasing medullary interstitial osmolarity, maximal at papilla (~1200-1400 mOsm/L in humans with ADH)
- **Countercurrent exchanger**: vasa recta — passive exchange that preserves medullary hypertonicity by preventing washout
- **ADH (vasopressin)**: acts on V2 receptors on principal cells → inserts aquaporin-2 channels → ↑ water permeability of collecting duct → concentrated urine
- Without ADH: collecting duct impermeable to water → dilute urine (as low as 50 mOsm/L); Diabetes insipidus = failure of this mechanism (central: ↓ ADH; nephrogenic: renal resistance to ADH)

## Sodium, Potassium & Acid-Base Regulation
- **Aldosterone**: acts on principal cells of collecting duct → ↑ ENaC and Na+/K+ ATPase → ↑ Na+ reabsorption, ↑ K+ secretion, ↑ H+ secretion (via intercalated cells indirectly)
- **RAAS cascade**: ↓ renal perfusion/↓ Na+ delivery to macula densa/↑ sympathetic → renin from JG cells → angiotensinogen → **Angiotensin I** → (ACE, mainly in lung) → **Angiotensin II** → vasoconstriction + aldosterone release + ADH release + thirst
- **ANP (atrial natriuretic peptide)**: released from atrial stretch → ↑ GFR, ↓ renin, ↓ aldosterone → natriuresis, diuresis — physiological antagonist of RAAS
- **Renal handling of K+**: freely filtered, reabsorbed in PCT and thick ascending limb; **fine-tuned by secretion in collecting duct principal cells** — regulated by aldosterone, plasma K+, acid-base status, flow rate
- **Renal acid-base**: PCT reabsorbs ~80-90% of filtered HCO3- (via carbonic anhydrase, both luminal and cytoplasmic); intercalated cells (type A: secrete H+/reabsorb HCO3- in acidosis; type B: secrete HCO3-/reabsorb Cl- in alkalosis) fine-tune net acid excretion
- Urinary buffers for H+ excretion: **titratable acid (phosphate)** and **ammonium (NH4+, most adaptable/increases in chronic acidosis)**

## Renal Regulation of Calcium & Phosphate
- **PTH**: ↑ Ca2+ reabsorption in DCT, ↓ phosphate reabsorption in PCT (phosphaturic), stimulates renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase (activates vitamin D)
- **Vitamin D (calcitriol)**: ↑ intestinal Ca2+/phosphate absorption (major site of action), also ↑ renal reabsorption

## Renal Clearance-Based Diagnostics
- **Free water clearance (CH2O) = V − Cosm**: positive = excreting free water (dilute urine); negative = retaining free water (concentrating urine)
- **Anion gap = Na+ − (Cl- + HCO3-)**, normal ~8-12 mEq/L — used to classify metabolic acidosis

## High-Yield One-Liners
- GFR normal ≈ 120 mL/min; filtration fraction ≈ 20% of renal plasma flow
- Tubuloglomerular feedback via macula densa (senses distal NaCl) is the key autoregulatory mechanism keeping GFR stable despite BP swings between 80-180 mmHg
- PAH clearance approximates renal plasma flow (near-complete extraction in one pass); inulin clearance = gold standard for GFR
- Thick ascending limb is impermeable to water and generates the medullary concentration gradient — loop diuretics act here (NKCC2) and are the most potent diuretics
- ADH acts via V2 receptors → aquaporin-2 insertion in collecting duct principal cells — determines urine concentration
- Aldosterone increases Na+ reabsorption and K+/H+ secretion at the principal/intercalated cells of the collecting duct
- PCT reabsorbs ~65% of filtered Na+/water and nearly all of the filtered glucose and amino acids (isosmotic reabsorption)
- Ammonium is the most adaptable urinary buffer and increases substantially in chronic metabolic acidosis
- Angiotensin II is the most potent vasoconstrictor of the RAAS and independently stimulates aldosterone, ADH, and thirst
- ANP opposes RAAS — causes natriuresis and diuresis via increased GFR and suppressed renin/aldosterone
