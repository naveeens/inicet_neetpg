# Cardiovascular Physiology

## Cardiac Action Potential (Ventricular Myocyte) — 5 Phases
| Phase | Event | Main ion movement |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (Rapid depolarization) | Fast Na+ channels open | Na+ influx |
| 1 (Early repolarization) | Na+ channels inactivate, transient K+ efflux | K+ efflux |
| 2 (Plateau) | Slow L-type Ca2+ channels open, balanced by K+ efflux — unique to cardiac muscle, allows sustained contraction | Ca2+ influx = K+ efflux |
| 3 (Repolarization) | Ca2+ channels close, K+ channels remain open | K+ efflux |
| 4 (Resting) | RMP ≈ -90 mV | K+ leak (IK1) |

- **SA node** (pacemaker): automatic slow depolarization due to **funny current (If)** — Na+ influx through HCN channels; no true stable resting potential; reaches threshold and fires spontaneously; intrinsic rate ~100-110/min but vagal tone brings resting HR to ~70-80/min
- Conduction velocity: **Purkinje fibers (fastest) > atrial muscle > ventricular muscle > AV node (slowest)** — AV nodal delay allows atrial contraction to complete before ventricular contraction begins
- Normal PR interval: 0.12-0.20 s; QRS: 0.08-0.10 s; QT: ~0.35-0.44 s (rate-corrected)

## Cardiac Cycle
- **Systole**: isovolumetric contraction → ejection (rapid then reduced)
- **Diastole**: isovolumetric relaxation → rapid filling → slow filling (diastasis) → atrial contraction ("atrial kick," ~20-30% of filling)
- **Wiggers diagram** (know the sequence): mitral valve closes (S1) → isovolumetric contraction → aortic valve opens → ejection → aortic valve closes (S2, dicrotic notch) → isovolumetric relaxation → mitral valve opens → rapid filling → diastasis → atrial systole → repeat

## Heart Sounds
| Sound | Cause | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Mitral + tricuspid valve closure | Onset of systole |
| S2 | Aortic + pulmonary valve closure | End of systole |
| S3 | Rapid ventricular filling (normal in children/pregnancy; pathologic in volume overload/CHF) | Early diastole |
| S4 | Atrial contraction against stiff ventricle (pathologic — HTN, HOCM, diastolic dysfunction) | Late diastole (pre-S1) |

- **Physiological splitting of S2**: widens on inspiration (↑ venous return delays pulmonic closure)
- **Wide fixed split S2**: ASD (classic exam finding)
- **Paradoxical splitting**: seen in LBBB, aortic stenosis (reversed pattern, narrows on inspiration)

## Cardiac Output — Determinants
CO = HR × Stroke Volume (SV)

- **Preload** = end-diastolic volume/pressure (relates to venous return) — governed by **Frank-Starling law**: within physiological limits, ↑ preload (venous return) → ↑ stretch of myocardium → ↑ force of contraction
- **Afterload** = resistance ventricle must pump against (≈ arterial pressure/SVR); ↑ afterload → ↓ SV (for a given contractility)
- **Contractility** = intrinsic force independent of preload/afterload; ↑ by sympathetic stimulation (catecholamines → ↑ intracellular Ca2+), digoxin; ↓ by heart failure, negative inotropes (beta-blockers, Ca2+ channel blockers)
- **Heart rate**: autonomic control — sympathetic (β1, ↑ HR & contractility via ↑ cAMP), parasympathetic vagal (↓ HR via M2 receptors, ↑ K+ conductance in SA node)

## Fick Principle & Cardiac Output Measurement
- CO = VO2 consumption / (arterial O2 content − venous O2 content)
- Used in cardiac catheterization; also basis for detecting intracardiac shunts (step-up in O2 saturation)

## Blood Pressure Regulation
| Mechanism | Type | Response time |
|---|---|---|
| Baroreceptor reflex (carotid sinus/aortic arch, via glossopharyngeal/vagus to NTS) | Rapid, short-term | Seconds |
| Chemoreceptor reflex (carotid/aortic bodies) | Rapid (mainly for hypoxia, also BP if severe) | Seconds |
| Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) | Intermediate | Minutes-hours |
| Capillary fluid shift | Intermediate | Minutes-hours |
| Renal pressure-natriuresis (most powerful long-term regulator) | Long-term | Hours-days |
| Aldosterone/vasopressin (ADH) | Long-term | Hours-days |

- **Baroreceptors adapt/reset within 1-2 days** — not useful for long-term BP control (classic exam point)
- ↑ BP → ↑ baroreceptor firing → ↑ vagal (parasympathetic) tone + ↓ sympathetic → ↓ HR, ↓ contractility, vasodilation → BP falls (negative feedback)

## Regional Circulations — Autoregulation Highlights
| Circulation | Key regulator | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Coronary | Local metabolic (adenosine — most important), O2 demand | Perfusion mainly during **diastole**; ↓ diastolic time (tachycardia) can compromise flow |
| Cerebral | CO2 (most potent — vasodilator), autoregulated 60-160 mmHg MAP | Myogenic + metabolic autoregulation |
| Renal | Myogenic + tubuloglomerular feedback | Autoregulated over wide pressure range |
| Skeletal muscle | Local metabolites at rest → sympathetic; exercise → local metabolic override (active hyperemia) | Exercise ↑ flow up to 20x |
| Skin | Sympathetic (thermoregulation dominates over metabolic need) | Only tissue where sympathetic control overrides local metabolic control |
| Pulmonary | Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (unique — opposite of systemic response to hypoxia) | Diverts blood from poorly ventilated areas |

## Capillary Dynamics — Starling Forces
Net filtration = [(Pc − Pi) − σ(πc − πi)] × Kf

- **Capillary hydrostatic pressure (Pc)** favors filtration (out); **plasma oncotic pressure (πc)** favors reabsorption (in)
- Arteriolar end: filtration predominates; venous end: reabsorption predominates (classical teaching); excess interstitial fluid drained by **lymphatics**
- Edema causes: ↑ capillary hydrostatic pressure (CHF), ↓ plasma oncotic pressure (nephrotic syndrome, liver failure, malnutrition), ↑ capillary permeability (inflammation), lymphatic obstruction (filariasis)

## Venous Return & JVP
- Determinants: right atrial pressure, mean systemic filling pressure, venous resistance
- **JVP waveform**: a wave (atrial contraction) → c wave (tricuspid bulging during ventricular contraction/carotid pulsation artifact) → x descent (atrial relaxation) → v wave (atrial filling against closed tricuspid) → y descent (tricuspid opens, rapid filling)
- **Absent a wave**: atrial fibrillation
- **Large a wave**: tricuspid stenosis, pulmonary hypertension
- **Large v wave (cv wave)**: tricuspid regurgitation
- **Kussmaul sign** (JVP rises with inspiration, opposite of normal): constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy, severe right heart failure

## Cardiovascular Reflexes — Exam Favorites
| Reflex | Stimulus | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Baroreceptor | ↑ BP (stretch) | ↓ HR, ↓ BP (via ↑ vagal tone) |
| Bainbridge reflex | ↑ right atrial stretch (↑ venous return) | ↑ HR (opposes baroreceptor effect at high venous return) |
| Cushing reflex | ↑ ICP (cerebral ischemia) | ↑ BP, ↓ HR (bradycardia), irregular respiration — protects cerebral perfusion |
| Bezold-Jarisch reflex | Stimulation of ventricular chemoreceptors/mechanoreceptors (e.g., inferior MI) | Bradycardia + hypotension |
| Valsalva response | ↑ intrathoracic pressure | Phase-dependent BP/HR changes — tested in autonomic function assessment |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Plateau phase (phase 2) of cardiac AP is due to L-type Ca2+ channels and is responsible for the long refractory period preventing tetany in cardiac muscle
- AV node has the slowest conduction velocity — allows optimal ventricular filling before ventricular contraction
- Frank-Starling law: the heart pumps whatever blood returns to it (up to a physiological limit) — SV increases with EDV
- Coronary blood flow occurs predominantly during diastole; tachycardia reduces diastolic filling time and can precipitate ischemia
- CO2 is the most potent regulator of cerebral blood flow; hypoxia causes vasodilation everywhere except the pulmonary circulation, where it causes vasoconstriction
- Baroreceptors reset within 1-2 days and are not the long-term regulator of BP; the kidney (pressure-natriuresis) is
- Widened, fixed splitting of S2 = ASD; paradoxical splitting = LBBB/severe AS
- S3 = rapid filling (volume overload); S4 = atrial kick against a stiff/hypertrophied ventricle
- Bainbridge reflex increases HR with increased venous return; opposite direction to the baroreceptor reflex
- Cushing reflex (↑ICP → HTN + bradycardia) is a protective response to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure
