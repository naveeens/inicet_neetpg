# Nerve Muscle Physiology

## Resting Membrane Potential (RMP)
- Nerve fiber RMP ≈ **-70 mV**; skeletal muscle ≈ **-90 mV**; cardiac ventricular myocyte ≈ **-90 mV**; SA nodal cell ≈ **-55 to -60 mV**
- Determined mainly by **K+ leak (high resting K+ conductance)** — RMP is close to K+ equilibrium potential (Nernst), not Na+
- Na+-K+ ATPase (3 Na+ out : 2 K+ in) maintains gradients — electrogenic, contributes a small direct component but its main role is gradient maintenance
- Nernst equation: E = ±61/z × log(Co/Ci) at 37°C — gives equilibrium potential for a single ion
- Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz (GHK) equation: gives actual membrane potential when membrane is permeable to multiple ions (Na+, K+, Cl-)

## Action Potential — Phases (Nerve/Skeletal Muscle)
| Phase | Event |
|---|---|
| Resting | -70/-90 mV, Na+ channels closed (activation gate closed, inactivation gate open) |
| Depolarization | Stimulus → threshold (~-55 mV) → voltage-gated Na+ channels open (activation gate) → rapid Na+ influx → overshoot to ~+35 mV |
| Repolarization | Na+ channel inactivation gate closes (fast) + voltage-gated K+ channels open (delayed) → K+ efflux |
| After-hyperpolarization | K+ channels slow to close → transient hyperpolarization below RMP |

- **Absolute refractory period**: Na+ channels inactivated — no stimulus can generate a new AP
- **Relative refractory period**: some Na+ channels recovered — stronger-than-normal stimulus can fire
- All-or-none law: AP is either full amplitude or does not occur (once threshold is reached)

## Nerve Fiber Types (Erlanger-Gasser Classification)
| Type | Example | Diameter | Conduction velocity | Myelin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aα | Motor to skeletal muscle, proprioception (Ia, Ib) | Largest | Fastest (70-120 m/s) | Yes |
| Aβ | Touch, pressure | Large | Fast | Yes |
| Aγ | Motor to muscle spindle (intrafusal) | Medium | Medium | Yes |
| Aδ | Fast/sharp pain, cold, touch | Small | 12-30 m/s | Thin myelin |
| B | Preganglionic autonomic | Small | 3-15 m/s | Thin myelin |
| C | Slow/dull pain, temperature, postganglionic autonomic | Smallest | 0.5-2 m/s (slowest) | Unmyelinated |

*High-yield: Conduction velocity is proportional to fiber diameter and directly proportional to myelination — largest myelinated fibers conduct fastest.*

- **Saltatory conduction**: in myelinated fibers, AP "jumps" node to node (Nodes of Ranvier) — faster and more energy-efficient than continuous conduction in unmyelinated fibers
- Order of blockade by local anesthetics: small/unmyelinated fibers (pain, autonomic) blocked first; large myelinated (motor) blocked last
- Order of blockade by pressure/ischemia (tourniquet): large myelinated motor fibers blocked first, small C fibers most resistant

## Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ) & Excitation-Contraction Coupling
1. AP reaches presynaptic terminal → voltage-gated **Ca2+** channels open → Ca2+ influx
2. Ca2+ triggers ACh vesicle fusion/exocytosis (quantal release)
3. ACh diffuses across cleft → binds nicotinic (N-M) receptors on motor end plate → ligand-gated Na+/K+ channels open → **end-plate potential (EPP)**
4. EPP (if above threshold) triggers muscle fiber AP → spreads along sarcolemma and down **T-tubules**
5. AP at T-tubule → conformational change in **dihydropyridine (DHP) receptor** → mechanically/electrically coupled to **ryanodine receptor (RyR1)** on sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) → Ca2+ release from SR
6. Ca2+ binds **troponin C** → tropomyosin shifts → exposes myosin-binding sites on actin → cross-bridge cycling (sliding filament mechanism)
7. ACh terminated by **acetylcholinesterase** in the synaptic cleft

*High-yield: Cardiac muscle E-C coupling additionally requires extracellular Ca2+ entry via L-type channels triggering further SR Ca2+ release — "calcium-induced calcium release" — unlike skeletal muscle, which does not require extracellular Ca2+.*

## Sliding Filament Theory — Cross-Bridge Cycle
- Thick filament = **myosin**; thin filament = **actin** + troponin + tropomyosin
- Myosin head (ATPase) binds actin → power stroke (pulls actin, releases ADP+Pi) → ATP binds myosin → detachment → ATP hydrolysis re-cocks head → cycle repeats while Ca2+ present
- **Rigor mortis**: no ATP available → myosin heads cannot detach from actin → sustained rigid contraction
- Sarcomere: Z line to Z line; A band (thick filament, constant length); I band (thin filament only, shortens on contraction); H zone (shortens/disappears on contraction); M line

## Length-Tension & Force-Velocity Relationships
- Maximum active tension generated at **resting length (optimal overlap of actin-myosin)** — too short = filament overlap interferes; too long = insufficient overlap
- Force-velocity: inverse relationship — max velocity at zero load; max force at zero velocity (isometric)

## Types of Muscle Contraction
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Isotonic | Tension constant, length changes (walking, lifting) |
| Isometric | Length constant, tension changes (postural muscles) |
| Isokinetic | Constant velocity (used in rehab testing) |

## Muscle Spindle & Golgi Tendon Organ (Stretch Reflex)
- **Muscle spindle**: intrafusal fibers (nuclear bag/chain), innervated by **Ia (annulospiral, dynamic + static)** and **II (flower-spray, static)** afferents; detects **muscle length and rate of change** — monitors via gamma motor neurons (adjust spindle sensitivity)
- **Golgi tendon organ (GTO)**: in series with muscle at musculotendinous junction, innervated by **Ib afferents**; detects **muscle tension** — protective, causes **autogenic inhibition** (inverse myotatic reflex) to prevent muscle/tendon damage
- **Stretch reflex (myotatic)**: muscle stretch → Ia afferent → monosynaptic excitation of alpha motor neuron to same muscle → contraction (e.g., knee jerk, deep tendon reflexes)
- **Reciprocal inhibition**: Ia afferents also inhibit antagonist muscle via inhibitory interneuron
- **Gamma loop**: gamma motor neurons keep spindle taut during muscle shortening so it remains sensitive to stretch throughout range of motion

## Neuromuscular Disorders — Key Correlations
| Disease | Mechanism | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Myasthenia Gravis | Autoantibodies vs post-synaptic nicotinic ACh receptor | Fatigable weakness, improves with rest/anticholinesterase (edrophonium/neostigmine test), decremental response on repetitive nerve stimulation |
| Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome | Autoantibodies vs pre-synaptic voltage-gated Ca2+ channels | Weakness improves with repeated use (incremental response on repetitive stimulation), associated with small cell lung cancer |
| Botulinum toxin | Blocks SNARE-mediated ACh vesicle release (presynaptic) | Flaccid paralysis, autonomic dysfunction |
| Organophosphate poisoning | Irreversible acetylcholinesterase inhibition | Cholinergic crisis (SLUDGE), muscle fasciculation then paralysis |
| Curare/non-depolarizing blockers | Competitive antagonist at nicotinic receptor | Flaccid paralysis, reversed by anticholinesterases |
| Succinylcholine | Depolarizing blocker — persistent depolarization | Initial fasciculations then flaccid paralysis; not reversed by anticholinesterases |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- RMP is set mainly by K+ permeability (leak channels); Na+/K+ ATPase maintains the ionic gradients that make this possible
- Nerve conduction velocity: fastest in large myelinated Aα fibers; slowest in unmyelinated C fibers
- Local anesthetics block small unmyelinated fibers first (pain/autonomic); pressure/ischemia blocks large myelinated motor fibers first
- Ca2+ is the universal trigger linking electrical excitation to mechanical contraction (excitation-contraction coupling) in all muscle types
- Troponin C is the Ca2+-binding regulatory protein in skeletal/cardiac muscle; smooth muscle instead uses calmodulin-MLCK
- Skeletal muscle E-C coupling does not require extracellular Ca2+ (DHP-RyR1 direct mechanical coupling); cardiac muscle does (Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release)
- Rigor mortis occurs due to absence of ATP, preventing myosin-actin detachment
- Muscle spindle (Ia/II afferents) senses length and velocity of stretch; Golgi tendon organ (Ib afferent) senses tension and mediates autogenic inhibition
- Myasthenia gravis = postsynaptic, decremental response, improves with anticholinesterases; Lambert-Eaton = presynaptic, incremental response, associated with malignancy
