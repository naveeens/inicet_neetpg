# GI Endocrine Reproductive Physiology

## GI Hormones — Source, Stimulus, Action
| Hormone | Source | Stimulus for release | Main actions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gastrin | G cells, antrum | Distension, peptides, vagal stimulation (GRP) | ↑ HCl secretion (parietal cells), ↑ gastric motility, trophic to gastric mucosa |
| CCK (cholecystokinin) | I cells, duodenum/jejunum | Fatty acids, amino acids in duodenum | Gallbladder contraction, pancreatic enzyme secretion, ↓ gastric emptying, sphincter of Oddi relaxation |
| Secretin | S cells, duodenum | Acid (H+) in duodenum | ↑ pancreatic HCO3- secretion, ↓ gastric acid secretion, ↑ bile HCO3- |
| GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide) | K cells, duodenum/jejunum | Glucose, fat in duodenum | ↑ insulin release (incretin effect), ↓ gastric acid secretion |
| Motilin | M cells, small intestine | Fasting state, cyclical | Migrating motor complex — interdigestive motility |
| Somatostatin | D cells (stomach, pancreas) | Acid in stomach | Inhibits gastrin, acid secretion, most GI hormones (universal inhibitor) |
| VIP | Enteric neurons | Neural | Relaxation of GI smooth muscle/sphincters, ↑ intestinal secretion; excess (VIPoma) → WDHA/Verner-Morrison syndrome (watery diarrhea, hypokalemia, achlorhydria) |

## Phases of Gastric Acid Secretion
| Phase | Trigger | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Cephalic | Sight/smell/taste of food, vagal stimulation | ~30% |
| Gastric | Food distension of stomach, peptides, gastrin | ~60% |
| Intestinal | Chyme in duodenum (initially stimulatory via distension/AA, then inhibitory) | ~10% |

- **Parietal cells**: secrete HCl (via H+/K+ ATPase — proton pump, target of PPIs) and **intrinsic factor** (essential for B12 absorption, absorbed in terminal ileum)
- **Chief cells**: pepsinogen → pepsin (activated by acid)
- Acid secretion stimulated by: gastrin, histamine (via H2 receptors — synergistic with gastrin/ACh), ACh (vagal, M3); inhibited by somatostatin, secretin, prostaglandins (mucosal protection — hence NSAIDs → ulcers via COX/prostaglandin inhibition)

## Digestion & Absorption
- **Carbohydrates**: salivary/pancreatic amylase → oligosaccharides → brush border enzymes (maltase, sucrase, lactase) → monosaccharides absorbed via SGLT1 (glucose/galactose, Na+-dependent) and GLUT5 (fructose, facilitated diffusion)
- **Proteins**: pepsin (stomach) + pancreatic proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase — trypsin activates the others, itself activated by enterokinase/enteropeptidase from duodenal brush border) → di/tripeptides and amino acids absorbed via specific transporters
- **Fats**: emulsified by bile salts → pancreatic lipase hydrolyzes triglycerides → monoglycerides + free fatty acids → incorporated into **micelles** → absorbed into enterocytes → re-esterified → packaged into **chylomicrons** → lymphatics (lacteals) → thoracic duct (bypasses portal circulation, unlike carbs/proteins)
- **Vitamin B12**: binds intrinsic factor (parietal cells) → absorbed in terminal ileum (receptor-mediated)
- **Bile salts**: reabsorbed in terminal ileum (**enterohepatic circulation**) — ileal resection/disease causes bile salt malabsorption and steatorrhea
- **Iron**: absorbed as Fe2+ in duodenum (ferrous form, facilitated by acid/vitamin C); regulated by hepcidin

## Pancreatic Exocrine Secretion
- Acinar cells: enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases — secreted as zymogens except amylase/lipase)
- Ductal cells: HCO3--rich fluid (stimulated by secretin) to neutralize duodenal acid, optimal pH for enzyme activity

## Endocrine Pancreas
| Cell | Hormone | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Beta cells (majority, central) | Insulin | ↓ blood glucose: ↑ glucose uptake (GLUT4 in muscle/fat), ↑ glycogenesis, ↑ lipogenesis, ↓ gluconeogenesis/glycogenolysis/lipolysis; anabolic |
| Alpha cells (peripheral) | Glucagon | ↑ blood glucose: ↑ glycogenolysis, ↑ gluconeogenesis, ↑ ketogenesis; catabolic |
| Delta cells | Somatostatin | Inhibits insulin, glucagon, GH |
| PP cells | Pancreatic polypeptide | Inhibits pancreatic secretion |

- Insulin release: glucose enters beta cell via GLUT2 → glucokinase → ↑ ATP → closes K-ATP channel → depolarization → Ca2+ influx → insulin release (site of action of sulfonylureas — close K-ATP channel directly)
- **Incretin effect**: oral glucose causes greater insulin release than IV glucose of same level (via GLP-1, GIP) — basis of GLP-1 agonists/DPP-4 inhibitors

## Hypothalamic-Pituitary Axis
| Hypothalamic hormone | Pituitary hormone regulated | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| TRH | TSH | + |
| CRH | ACTH | + |
| GnRH (pulsatile) | LH, FSH | + |
| GHRH | GH | + |
| Somatostatin | GH, TSH | − |
| Dopamine | Prolactin | − (tonic inhibition — unique, prolactin is the only anterior pituitary hormone under predominant inhibitory control) |

- **Posterior pituitary**: stores/releases **ADH (vasopressin, from supraoptic nucleus)** and **Oxytocin (from paraventricular nucleus)** — synthesized in hypothalamus, transported via axons
- **Oxytocin**: milk ejection reflex (suckling → sensory afferents → hypothalamus → oxytocin release), uterine contraction (positive feedback in labor)
- **Prolactin**: milk production; inhibits GnRH (lactational amenorrhea)

## Thyroid Hormones
- T4 (thyroxine, majority secreted) converted peripherally to active **T3** (more potent, deiodinase enzymes); reverse T3 (rT3) is inactive (increased in sick euthyroid syndrome)
- Synthesis: iodide trapped (Na+/I- symporter) → oxidized (thyroid peroxidase) → organified onto tyrosine on thyroglobulin → coupling (MIT+DIT=T3; DIT+DIT=T4) → stored in colloid → released on TSH stimulation
- Actions: ↑ basal metabolic rate, ↑ O2 consumption, essential for normal growth and CNS development (deficiency in fetal/neonatal life → irreversible mental retardation — cretinism), permissive for catecholamine action, ↑ heart rate/contractility

## Adrenal Hormones
| Zone | Hormone | Regulation |
|---|---|---|
| Zona glomerulosa | Aldosterone | RAAS (angiotensin II), plasma K+ — NOT ACTH-dependent long-term |
| Zona fasciculata | Cortisol | ACTH (diurnal — peak early morning, trough midnight) |
| Zona reticularis | Androgens (DHEA) | ACTH |
| Medulla | Epinephrine (80%), Norepinephrine (20%) | Sympathetic preganglionic fibers (splanchnic nerve) |

- Cortisol: gluconeogenesis, protein catabolism, lipolysis, anti-inflammatory/immunosuppressive, permissive for catecholamines, ↑ vascular tone
- **Mnemonic**: "GFR — Salt, Sugar, Sex" (Glomerulosa-Fasciculata-Reticularis → mineralocorticoid-glucocorticoid-androgen)

## Male Reproductive Physiology
- **HPG axis**: GnRH (pulsatile) → LH acts on **Leydig cells** → testosterone; FSH acts on **Sertoli cells** → supports spermatogenesis, produces **inhibin** (negative feedback on FSH) and ABP (androgen-binding protein)
- Testosterone: negative feedback on LH (and GnRH); responsible for spermatogenesis (with FSH), secondary sexual characteristics, anabolic effects
- Spermatogenesis duration: ~64-74 days

## Female Reproductive Physiology — Menstrual Cycle
| Phase | Ovarian event | Hormone pattern | Endometrial correlate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Follicular (Day 1-14, variable) | Follicle growth (FSH-driven), dominant follicle selection | Rising estrogen | Proliferative |
| Ovulation (~Day 14) | LH surge (triggered by peak estrogen — positive feedback) → oocyte release | LH surge | — |
| Luteal (Day 14-28, fixed ~14 days) | Corpus luteum forms, secretes progesterone | Progesterone dominant (+ some estrogen); both fall if no fertilization → menses | Secretory |

- **Estrogen**: low/moderate levels = negative feedback on FSH/LH; high sustained levels (late follicular) = positive feedback → LH surge → ovulation
- **Progesterone**: negative feedback on GnRH/LH-FSH; maintains endometrium, thickens cervical mucus, ↑ basal body temperature (used to detect ovulation)
- **hCG**: maintains corpus luteum in early pregnancy until placenta takes over steroidogenesis (~7-10 weeks); doubling time ~48 hours in early normal pregnancy — basis of early pregnancy monitoring
- **Inhibin B** (from granulosa cells): negative feedback on FSH

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Secretin stimulates pancreatic bicarbonate secretion; CCK stimulates enzyme secretion and gallbladder contraction — classic complementary pair
- Somatostatin is the universal GI inhibitory hormone — suppresses gastrin, acid secretion, and most other GI hormones
- Parietal cells make HCl and intrinsic factor; chief cells make pepsinogen
- Dietary fat absorption uniquely bypasses the portal vein — chylomicrons enter lymphatics (lacteals) rather than portal blood
- Vitamin B12 and bile salts are both absorbed specifically in the terminal ileum — resection of this segment causes B12 deficiency and bile-salt-related steatorrhea
- Prolactin is the only anterior pituitary hormone under predominant tonic inhibitory (dopamine) control — pituitary stalk section raises prolactin but lowers other anterior pituitary hormones
- Estrogen exerts positive feedback at high sustained levels (triggering the LH surge) but negative feedback at low/moderate levels — a classic exception to the usual negative feedback rule in endocrinology
- Aldosterone secretion is primarily regulated by the renin-angiotensin system and plasma K+, not by ACTH (unlike cortisol and adrenal androgens)
- hCG maintains the corpus luteum until the placenta autonomously produces enough progesterone (luteal-placental shift, ~7-10 weeks)
- FSH stimulates Sertoli cells (spermatogenesis, inhibin); LH stimulates Leydig cells (testosterone) — testosterone negative-feedbacks LH, inhibin negative-feedbacks FSH
