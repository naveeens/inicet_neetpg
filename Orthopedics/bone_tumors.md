# Bone Tumors

## Overall Facts
- **Most common bone tumor overall**: metastasis (secondary) — primary sites: breast, lung, prostate, thyroid, kidney (mnemonic: **BLT with Kosher Pickle** / "Bone loves to play kickball")
- **Most common primary malignant bone tumor**: multiple myeloma
- **Most common primary malignant bone tumor of childhood**: osteosarcoma
- **Most common benign bone tumor**: osteochondroma
- **Most common benign bone tumor of hand**: enchondroma

## Classification by Tissue Origin
| Origin | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Bone-forming | Osteoma, osteoid osteoma, osteoblastoma | Osteosarcoma |
| Cartilage-forming | Osteochondroma, chondroma (enchondroma), chondroblastoma | Chondrosarcoma |
| Fibrous | Fibrous cortical defect, non-ossifying fibroma, fibrous dysplasia | Fibrosarcoma, malignant fibrous histiocytoma |
| Round cell / marrow | — | Ewing's sarcoma, multiple myeloma, lymphoma |
| Notochordal | — | Chordoma |
| Vascular | Hemangioma | Angiosarcoma |
| Unknown/other | Giant cell tumor (GCT), simple bone cyst, aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC) | GCT (locally aggressive, can be malignant) |

| Tumor | Peak age | Typical site |

| Osteoblastoma | 10–20 yrs | Posterior elements of vertebrae |
| Chondroblastoma | <20 yrs | Epiphysis of long bones (femur, humerus) |
| Giant cell tumor | 20–40 yrs, epiphyseal plate fused | Epiphysis/metaphysis of long bones (distal femur, proximal tibia, distal radius) |
| Enchondroma | 10–40 yrs | Small bones of hand/foot |
| Simple (unicameral) bone cyst | <20 yrs | Proximal humerus, proximal femur (metaphysis) |
| Aneurysmal bone cyst | <20 yrs | Metaphysis of long bones, posterior vertebral elements |
| Fibrous dysplasia | Children/young adults | Any bone; polyostotic in McCune-Albright |

## Radiological Patterns (High-Yield)
| Sign | Seen in |
|---|---|
| **Codman triangle** (periosteal reaction) | Osteosarcoma (also Ewing's, infection) |
| **Sunburst / sunray** appearance | Osteosarcoma |
| **Onion-skin** periosteal reaction | Ewing's sarcoma (also seen in osteomyelitis) |
| **Soap-bubble** / "bag of marbles" lytic lesion | Giant cell tumor, ABC |
| **Moth-eaten** permeative lysis | Ewing's sarcoma, myeloma, lymphoma, infection |
| **Ground-glass** appearance | Fibrous dysplasia |
| **Popcorn calcification** | Chondroma/enchondroma, chondrosarcoma |
| **Punched-out lytic lesions** (skull) | Multiple myeloma |
| **Rind sign / cortical thickening with radiolucent nidus** | Osteoid osteoma (nidus <1.5–2 cm, dense reactive sclerosis around) |
| **Falling leaf sign** | Simple bone cyst pathological fracture (fallen fragment) |
| **Sclerotic "ivory vertebra"** | Metastasis (prostate/breast — osteoblastic), lymphoma, Paget's |

## Benign Tumors — Key Points
- **Osteochondroma**: cartilage-capped bony projection from metaphysis, grows away from joint; risk of malignant transformation to chondrosarcoma if painful/growing post-puberty (especially in Multiple Hereditary Exostoses)
- **Osteoid osteoma**: severe night pain, **dramatically relieved by NSAIDs/aspirin** (due to prostaglandin-mediated pain) — classic exam clue; nidus on CT
- **Giant cell tumor (osteoclastoma)**: epiphyseal, "soap bubble" lytic lesion, locally aggressive, ~5–10% can metastasize to lung (benign pulmonary implants); treated by curettage + adjuvant (phenol/cement) or en-bloc resection; can recur
- **Fibrous dysplasia**: McCune-Albright syndrome = polyostotic fibrous dysplasia + café-au-lait spots (coast of Maine border) + precocious puberty
- **Simple bone cyst**: often presents as pathological fracture; "fallen fragment sign"
- **Aneurysmal bone cyst**: expansile, blood-filled cystic spaces; can be primary or secondary (to GCT/other lesions)

## Malignant Tumors — Key Points
### Osteosarcoma
- Arises from primitive mesenchymal bone-forming cells
- Most common site: metaphysis around the knee (distal femur > proximal tibia)
- Associated with: Paget's disease (elderly, 2° osteosarcoma), retinoblastoma (RB1 gene), Li-Fraumeni syndrome (p53), prior radiation
- X-ray: Codman triangle, sunburst appearance, permeative destruction
- Treatment: neoadjuvant chemotherapy → limb salvage surgery (wide resection) or amputation → adjuvant chemotherapy
- Lungs = most common site of metastasis

### Ewing's Sarcoma
- Small round blue cell tumor of neuroectodermal origin; t(11;22) translocation (EWS-FLI1)
- Diaphysis of long bones/pelvis in children; onion-skin periosteal reaction
- Often presents with fever, raised ESR — mimics osteomyelitis
- Highly chemosensitive: chemotherapy + surgery/radiotherapy

### Chondrosarcoma
- Cartilage-forming malignant tumor, older age group (40–60), axial skeleton/pelvis
- Can be primary or secondary (from osteochondroma/enchondroma)
- Relatively radio/chemo-resistant → wide surgical excision is mainstay

### Multiple Myeloma
- Plasma cell malignancy; most common primary malignant bone tumor overall (in adults)
- Punched-out lytic lesions on skull X-ray; M-band on serum electrophoresis; Bence-Jones proteins in urine
- CRAB criteria: hyperCalcemia, Renal failure, Anemia, Bone lesions
- Bone scan often falsely negative (purely lytic, no osteoblastic reaction) — skeletal survey/whole-body MRI preferred over bone scan

## Biopsy Principles (High-Yield Surgical Oncology)
- Biopsy tract must be planned along the line of definitive surgical incision (excised en-bloc with tumor later) — wrong biopsy placement can compromise limb salvage
- Longitudinal incision preferred (not transverse) in extremities
- Avoid violating uninvolved compartments/neurovascular structures
- Core needle/trephine biopsy generally preferred over open biopsy where feasible

## Staging
- **Enneking staging** (musculoskeletal tumor society) for malignant bone/soft-tissue tumors — based on grade (G), local extent (T: intra- vs extra-compartmental), and metastasis (M)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Osteoid osteoma pain is classically relieved dramatically by NSAIDs (prostaglandin-mediated)
- Codman triangle + sunburst appearance = osteosarcoma; onion-skin = Ewing's sarcoma
- GCT occurs in epiphysis after physeal closure (20–40 yrs) — an important exception since most bone tumors favor metaphysis
- Most common benign bone tumor: osteochondroma; most common malignant primary bone tumor overall: multiple myeloma; most common in children: osteosarcoma
- Ewing's sarcoma can mimic osteomyelitis clinically (fever, raised ESR/CRP, lytic destruction)
- Osteosarcoma associated with Paget's disease in elderly and retinoblastoma gene mutation in children
- Chondrosarcoma is relatively resistant to chemo/radiotherapy — wide excision is treatment of choice
- Biopsy tract for a suspected malignant bone tumor must lie within the planned resection specimen
