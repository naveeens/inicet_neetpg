# Joint Diseases Arthritis

## Osteoarthritis (OA)
### Pathophysiology
- Degenerative "wear and tear" disease of articular cartilage — imbalance between cartilage breakdown and chondrocyte repair; secondary changes in subchondral bone and synovium
- Primary (idiopathic, age-related) vs secondary (post-trauma, congenital dysplasia, avascular necrosis, inflammatory arthritis sequela)

### Clinical Features
- Weight-bearing joints (knee, hip, spine) most commonly affected; also DIP/PIP (Heberden's/Bouchard's nodes) and 1st CMC joint
- Pain worse with activity, relieved by rest (contrast with inflammatory arthritis)
- Morning stiffness <30 min (contrast with RA where it's >1 hour)
- Crepitus, reduced range of motion, bony enlargement, joint deformity in late disease

### Radiological Findings
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Joint space narrowing | Asymmetric, in weight-bearing area |
| Osteophytes | Marginal bony outgrowths |
| Subchondral sclerosis | Increased bone density beneath cartilage |
| Subchondral cysts | Geode formation |

- **Kellgren-Lawrence grading** (0–4): Grade 0 = normal; 1 = doubtful narrowing ± osteophyte; 2 = definite osteophyte, possible narrowing; 3 = moderate multiple osteophytes/narrowing/sclerosis; 4 = large osteophytes, marked narrowing, severe sclerosis

### Management
- Conservative: weight loss, quadriceps strengthening exercises, activity modification, walking aids
- Pharmacological: paracetamol first-line, NSAIDs, intra-articular steroid/hyaluronic acid injections
- Surgical: arthroscopic debridement (limited role), osteotomy (younger patients), total joint replacement (end-stage disease)

## Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)
### Pathophysiology
- Chronic autoimmune symmetric polyarthritis; synovial hypertrophy (pannus) → cartilage/bone erosion
- Associated with HLA-DR4; Rheumatoid factor (anti-IgG) and anti-CCP antibodies (anti-CCP more specific)

### Clinical Features
- Symmetric small joint polyarthritis — MCP, PIP, wrist (spares DIP — key differentiator from OA)
- Morning stiffness >1 hour, improves with activity
- Hand deformities: **swan-neck deformity, boutonniere deformity, ulnar deviation of fingers, Z-thumb deformity**
- Extra-articular: rheumatoid nodules, interstitial lung disease, pericarditis, Felty syndrome (RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia), Sjögren's syndrome, episcleritis/scleritis, atlantoaxial subluxation (C1-C2 instability — important pre-anesthesia/intubation check)

### Radiological Findings
- Soft tissue swelling early → periarticular osteopenia → symmetric joint space narrowing → marginal erosions → joint destruction/subluxation

### Management
- **DMARDs** (disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs) — start early to prevent joint destruction
  - Conventional: Methotrexate (first-line), Hydroxychloroquine, Sulfasalazine, Leflunomide
  - Biologic: TNF-α inhibitors (etanercept, infliximab), rituximab, tocilizumab — for refractory disease
- NSAIDs/steroids for symptom control (bridge therapy) — do not alter disease course alone
- Surgery for deformity correction/joint replacement in advanced disease

## Septic Arthritis
- Emergency — joint destruction can occur within 24–48 hours if untreated
- Most common organism overall: **Staphylococcus aureus**; in sexually active young adults consider **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**; in sickle cell disease/children think **Salmonella** and **Kingella kingae** (esp. <4 yrs); neonates — Group B Streptococcus
- Route: hematogenous spread most common (also direct inoculation, contiguous spread from osteomyelitis)
- **Kocher criteria** (differentiating septic arthritis from transient synovitis of hip in children): fever >38.5°C, non-weight-bearing, ESR >40 mm/hr, WBC >12,000/mm³ (higher number of criteria = higher probability of septic arthritis)
- Investigation: joint aspiration is gold standard — synovial fluid WBC typically >50,000/mm³ with neutrophil predominance; send for Gram stain/culture
- Treatment: emergency surgical drainage/arthrotomy or arthroscopic washout + IV antibiotics — do not wait for culture results in high clinical suspicion

## Tuberculous Arthritis
- Usually monoarticular; hip and knee most commonly affected large joints (spine most common site overall — Pott's disease, covered separately)
- Phemister's triad (X-ray): peri-articular osteoporosis, peripherally located osseous erosions, gradual narrowing of joint space (unlike pyogenic arthritis where narrowing is early/rapid)
- Stages of TB hip (Shanmugasundaram): synovitis → early arthritis → arthritis with articular destruction → advanced arthritis with dislocation
- Treatment: ATT (anti-tubercular therapy) for 6–12 months + rest/splintage; surgery (synovectomy/debridement/arthrodesis) if needed

## Crystal Arthropathies
| Feature | Gout | Pseudogout (CPPD) |
|---|---|---|
| Crystal | Monosodium urate | Calcium pyrophosphate |
| Shape/birefringence | Needle-shaped, negatively birefringent | Rhomboid, positively birefringent |
| Classic joint | 1st MTP joint (podagra) | Knee (most common) |
| Radiology | Punched-out erosions with overhanging edges (late), tophi | Chondrocalcinosis (linear calcification in cartilage) |
| Precipitants | Purine-rich diet, alcohol, diuretics, tumor lysis | Hyperparathyroidism, hemochromatosis, hypothyroidism |
| Treatment (acute) | NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids | NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids |
| Treatment (chronic) | Allopurinol/febuxostat (urate-lowering) | Treat underlying cause |

## Seronegative Spondyloarthropathies
- Group: Ankylosing spondylitis, Reactive arthritis, Psoriatic arthritis, Enteropathic arthritis (IBD-associated)
- Common features: RF-negative, HLA-B27 association, sacroiliac joint involvement, enthesitis, extra-articular (uveitis, IBD, psoriasis)

### Ankylosing Spondylitis
- Young males, chronic low back pain/stiffness — inflammatory pattern (worse in morning, improves with activity, unlike mechanical back pain)
- Progressive spinal fusion — **"bamboo spine"** on X-ray (late finding); sacroiliitis is earliest radiological sign
- Schober's test: reduced lumbar flexion
- Extra-articular: anterior uveitis, aortic regurgitation, apical pulmonary fibrosis, Achilles enthesitis
- Treatment: NSAIDs first-line, physiotherapy (maintain posture/mobility), TNF-α inhibitors for refractory disease

### Reactive Arthritis (Reiter's Syndrome)
- Classic triad: **arthritis + conjunctivitis/uveitis + urethritis** ("can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree")
- Post-infectious (Chlamydia, Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter, Yersinia)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- OA morning stiffness <30 min; RA morning stiffness >1 hour — classic differentiator
- RA spares the DIP joints (typically); OA classically involves DIP (Heberden's nodes) and PIP (Bouchard's nodes)
- Septic arthritis is an orthopedic emergency — joint destruction within 24–48 hours; synovial fluid WBC >50,000/mm³ with neutrophil predominance is diagnostic
- Most common organism in septic arthritis: S. aureus; think Salmonella/Kingella in special populations
- Gout crystals: needle-shaped, negatively birefringent; Pseudogout: rhomboid, positively birefringent
- Ankylosing spondylitis: earliest X-ray finding is sacroiliitis, not bamboo spine
- Atlantoaxial subluxation in RA is critical to screen for before any procedure requiring neck extension/intubation
- Methotrexate is the first-line DMARD for RA; start DMARDs early to prevent erosive joint damage
