# Fractures Classification Management

## Definitions
- **Fracture**: break in structural continuity of bone
- **Closed vs Open (Compound)**: skin integrity over/near fracture site
- **Displacement described by**: translation, angulation, rotation, shortening — of distal fragment relative to proximal

## Gustilo-Anderson Classification (Open Fractures)
| Type | Wound size | Soft tissue damage | Contamination | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | <1 cm | Minimal | Clean | Low energy |
| II | 1–10 cm | Moderate | Moderate | — |
| IIIA | >10 cm | Extensive, but adequate soft tissue coverage of bone | Often high | Segmental/high-energy |
| IIIB | >10 cm | Extensive, periosteal stripping, needs flap coverage | Significant | — |
| IIIC | Any size | Associated arterial injury requiring repair | — | Worst prognosis |

*High-yield: All open fractures need urgent IV antibiotics + tetanus prophylaxis + thorough debridement; Gustilo grading finalized intra-operatively, not on ER exam alone.*

## Salter-Harris Classification (Physeal/Growth Plate Injuries)
| Type | Description | Mnemonic | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Fracture through physis only (epiphysis separates from metaphysis) | **S**traight across | Good |
| II | Through physis + metaphysis (metaphyseal fragment) | **A**bove (fragment above physis, i.e., metaphyseal) | Good (most common type) |
| III | Through physis + epiphysis (intra-articular) | **L**ow (epiphyseal) | Fair — may need surgery |
| IV | Through metaphysis + physis + epiphysis | **T**hrough everything | Poor if not perfectly reduced |
| V | Crush injury to physis | **ER**asure of growth plate | Worst — growth arrest common |

Mnemonic: **SALTR** — Straight, Above, Lower, Through, Rammed(crush).

## Common Fracture-Specific Classifications
| Fracture | Classification | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Femoral neck | **Garden** (I–IV, based on displacement/trabecular alignment) | Garden III/IV (displaced) → high AVN risk → arthroplasty in elderly |
| Proximal humerus | **Neer** classification (based on number of parts displaced) | ≥45° angulation or 1 cm displacement = separate "part" |
| Tibial plateau | **Schatzker** (I–VI) | Type V/VI = high energy, bicondylar |
| Supracondylar humerus (pediatric) | **Gartland** (I–III, extension type) | Type III = complete displacement, highest neurovascular risk |
| Ankle | **Weber/Danis-Weber** (A/B/C, based on fibular fracture level relative to syndesmosis) | C = above syndesmosis, most unstable |
| Distal radius | Colles (dorsal angulation/displacement), Smith (volar), Barton (intra-articular) | "Dinner fork deformity" = Colles |
| Calcaneus | Essex-Lopresti / Sanders (CT-based) | Fall from height; associated with spinal compression fractures |
| Acetabulum | Letournel-Judet | Complex, CT-based |

## Named Fracture-Dislocations
| Injury | Description |
|---|---|
| Monteggia | Fracture proximal ulna + dislocation of radial head |
| Galeazzi | Fracture distal radius shaft + dislocation of distal radio-ulnar joint (DRUJ) |
| Barton | Intra-articular distal radius fracture with carpal subluxation |
| Bennett | Intra-articular fracture-dislocation of base of 1st metacarpal |
| Boxer's fracture | Neck of 5th metacarpal |

*Mnemonic: MUGR — Monteggia = Ulna (proximal) + Radial head dislocation; Galeazzi = Radius (shaft) + DRUJ.*

## Fracture Healing
| Stage | Timeline (approx.) | Events |
|---|---|---|
| Hematoma formation | Hours | Bleeding, inflammatory cells |
| Soft callus (fibrocartilaginous) | ~2 weeks | Fibroblasts, chondroblasts bridge fracture |
| Hard callus | 4–12 weeks | Woven bone replaces cartilage (endochondral ossification) |
| Remodeling | Months–years | Woven bone → lamellar bone along stress lines (Wolff's law) |

- Healing is **faster in children** (thicker periosteum, more osteogenic potential) and in **cancellous bone** (vs cortical)
- Primary (direct) healing occurs only with rigid fixation and anatomical apposition (compression plating) — no callus formation
- Secondary healing (callus formation) occurs with relative stability (casts, IM nails, external fixators)

## Principles of Management
- **Closed reduction + immobilization** (cast/splint/traction) — most stable, minimally displaced, pediatric fractures
- **Traction** (skin/skeletal) — temporizing measure, or definitive in select fractures
- **ORIF** (open reduction internal fixation) — displaced, intra-articular, unstable fractures; plates/screws/IM nails
- **External fixation** — open fractures with soft-tissue compromise, damage control, infected nonunion
- **Rule of thumb**: intra-articular fractures need anatomical reduction; diaphyseal fractures tolerate more angulation, especially in children

## Complications
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Immediate (local) | Vascular injury, nerve injury, compartment syndrome |
| Immediate (systemic) | Fat embolism syndrome, hemorrhagic shock (esp. pelvis/femur) |
| Early | Infection, DVT/PE, ARDS |
| Late | Delayed union, nonunion, malunion, AVN, growth disturbance (children), myositis ossificans, Volkmann's ischemic contracture, complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS/Sudeck's atrophy), osteoarthritis (post-traumatic) |

### Compartment Syndrome
- Most common site: leg (anterior compartment) after tibial fracture; also forearm (volar compartment) after supracondylar fracture
- Clinical: **6 Ps** — Pain (out of proportion, worse on passive stretch — earliest sign), Pallor, Paresthesia, Pulselessness (late), Paralysis (late), Poikilothermia
- Diagnosis: clinical + intracompartmental pressure >30 mmHg (or within 30 mmHg of diastolic BP)
- Treatment: emergency **fasciotomy** — do not wait for pulses to disappear

### Fat Embolism Syndrome
- Classic triad: **hypoxemia, neurological abnormalities (confusion), petechial rash** (chest/axilla/conjunctiva)
- Occurs 24–72 h after long bone/pelvic fractures
- Treatment: supportive, oxygen; early fracture fixation reduces risk

### Volkmann's Ischemic Contracture
- Sequela of missed/inadequately treated compartment syndrome (classically forearm, after supracondylar humerus fracture)
- Results in flexion contracture of wrist/fingers, claw hand

### Nonunion vs Delayed Union vs Malunion
- **Delayed union**: healing slower than expected but progressing
- **Nonunion**: healing has stopped (no progression on serial X-rays, typically >6–9 months); hypertrophic (elephant foot — mobility, good biology, poor fixation) vs atrophic (poor biology) vs infected
- **Malunion**: healed in unacceptable position (angulation/rotation/shortening)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Salter-Harris mnemonic: SALTR (Straight, Above, Lower, Through, Rammed/crush) — Type V has worst prognosis (growth arrest)
- Garden III/IV femoral neck fracture in elderly → hemiarthroplasty/THR (not internal fixation) due to AVN risk
- Compartment syndrome: earliest and most reliable sign = pain on passive stretch, disproportionate to injury
- Fat embolism triad: hypoxemia + confusion + petechiae, typically 24–72 h post long-bone fracture
- Monteggia = proximal ulna fracture + radial head dislocation; Galeazzi = distal radius fracture + DRUJ dislocation
- Primary bone healing (no callus) requires rigid compression fixation with anatomical reduction
- Gustilo IIIC = open fracture with vascular injury needing repair — worst prognosis, highest amputation rate
- Children's fractures remodel and heal faster; greater angulation is acceptable, especially away from joints
