# Antimicrobial Pharmacology Antibiotics

## Classification by Mechanism
| Mechanism | Class |
|---|---|
| Cell wall synthesis inhibition | Beta-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems, monobactams), Vancomycin, Bacitracin |
| Protein synthesis inhibition — 30S | Aminoglycosides (bactericidal), Tetracyclines (bacteriostatic) |
| Protein synthesis inhibition — 50S | Macrolides, Chloramphenicol, Clindamycin, Linezolid (bacteriostatic mostly) |
| Nucleic acid synthesis inhibition | Fluoroquinolones (DNA gyrase/topoisomerase), Rifampicin (RNA polymerase), Metronidazole |
| Folate synthesis inhibition | Sulfonamides (PABA analog), Trimethoprim (dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor) |
| Cell membrane disruption | Polymyxins (Colistin), Daptomycin |

## Beta-Lactams
| Drug | Spectrum/Use | Key notes |
|---|---|---|
| Penicillin G/V | Streptococcus, Syphilis (Treponema — drug of choice at all stages), Neisseria | Hypersensitivity (immediate anaphylaxis to delayed rash); Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction when treating syphilis |
| Amoxicillin/Ampicillin | Broader gram-negative cover, enterococcus, Listeria | Ampicillin rash in EBV/mononucleosis (classic exam trap — do not mistake for true allergy) |
| Piperacillin-Tazobactam | Pseudomonas + broad anaerobic/gram-negative cover | Beta-lactamase inhibitor combination |
| Methicillin/Nafcillin/Oxacillin | Penicillinase-resistant, anti-staphylococcal | Interstitial nephritis (methicillin classic) |
| Cephalosporins (Gen 1→5) | Increasing gram-negative, decreasing gram-positive cover from Gen 1→3; Gen 4 (Cefepime) broad + Pseudomonas; Gen 5 (Ceftaroline) covers MRSA | Ceftriaxone: biliary sludging, avoid with calcium-containing IV fluids in neonates (precipitation risk); Cross-reactivity with penicillin allergy is low (~1-2%) but caution with severe/anaphylactic PCN allergy |
| Carbapenems (Imipenem, Meropenem) | Broadest spectrum, reserved for severe/resistant infections | Imipenem + cilastatin (cilastatin inhibits renal dehydropeptidase to prevent imipenem inactivation); seizure risk (esp. imipenem) |
| Aztreonam (monobactam) | Gram-negative only (including Pseudomonas) | Safe in penicillin allergy (no cross-reactivity — different side chain) |

*High-yield: Beta-lactams are time-dependent bactericidal (efficacy depends on duration above MIC), unlike aminoglycosides/fluoroquinolones which are concentration-dependent.*

## Aminoglycosides
- Gentamicin, Amikacin, Streptomycin, Tobramycin
- Bactericidal, concentration-dependent, requires O2 (ineffective against anaerobes)
- Used for severe gram-negative infections, synergy with beta-lactams for enterococcal endocarditis
- **Toxicity**: nephrotoxicity (reversible, ATN), **ototoxicity** (irreversible — vestibular with gentamicin/streptomycin, auditory with amikacin/kanamycin), neuromuscular blockade (avoid with NMJ blockers/myasthenia)
- Once-daily dosing preferred (post-antibiotic effect, minimizes toxicity vs. multiple daily doses)

## Tetracyclines
- Tetracycline, Doxycycline, Minocycline
- Broad spectrum: Rickettsia, Chlamydia, Mycoplasma, Vibrio cholerae, Borrelia (Lyme), acne
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy and children <8 years** — tooth discoloration/enamel hypoplasia, inhibits bone growth
- Chelation with divalent cations (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺, Al³⁺) — avoid antacids/dairy/iron concurrently
- Photosensitivity; doxycycline safer in renal impairment (fecal elimination)
- Tigecycline (glycylcycline) — broad spectrum including MRSA, reserved agent

## Macrolides
- Erythromycin, Azithromycin, Clarithromycin
- Atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Legionella, Chlamydia), pertussis, in penicillin-allergic patients (streptococcal infections)
- **QT prolongation/torsades**, GI upset (erythromycin — motilin agonist, also used as prokinetic), cholestatic hepatitis, CYP3A4 inhibition (erythromycin/clarithromycin — drug interactions with statins, warfarin)
- Azithromycin — longest half-life, best tissue penetration, fewer drug interactions (weak CYP inhibitor)

## Fluoroquinolones
- Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin
- Gram-negative predominant (cipro), respiratory cover added in later generations (levo/moxi)
- **Tendinopathy/tendon rupture** (Achilles), **contraindicated in children/pregnancy** (cartilage damage in animal studies), QT prolongation, CNS effects (seizures, esp. with NSAIDs), avoid with divalent cation antacids (chelation, like tetracyclines)
- Ciprofloxacin — good for Pseudomonas, typhoid, UTI

## Glycopeptides
- **Vancomycin**: MRSA, C. difficile (oral, non-absorbed), gram-positive resistant infections
- Toxicity: nephrotoxicity, **"Red man syndrome"** (histamine release from rapid infusion — not true allergy, managed by slowing infusion/antihistamine premedication), ototoxicity (less common now with purer formulations)

## Others
| Drug | Use | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|
| Chloramphenicol | Broad spectrum, historically for typhoid/meningitis | **Aplastic anemia** (idiosyncratic, dose-independent), reversible dose-dependent bone marrow suppression, **Gray baby syndrome** (neonates — immature glucuronidation) |
| Clindamycin | Anaerobes, toxic shock syndrome (toxin suppression), dental infections | **C. difficile colitis** (classic high association), no CNS penetration |
| Metronidazole | Anaerobes, Giardia, Entamoeba, Trichomonas, C. difficile (alternative) | **Disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol**, metallic taste, peripheral neuropathy (long-term) |
| Linezolid | MRSA, VRE | Reversible myelosuppression, serotonin syndrome risk (weak MAO inhibitor — avoid with SSRIs) |
| Colistin/Polymyxins | Multidrug-resistant gram-negative (last resort, e.g., carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae) | Nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity |
| Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (Cotrimoxazole) | UTI, Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) prophylaxis/treatment, Nocardia | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, hyperkalemia, megaloblastic anemia, kernicterus risk in neonates (avoid near term/in newborns), G6PD hemolysis with sulfonamide component |
| Rifampicin | TB, meningococcal/H. influenzae prophylaxis, leprosy | **Orange-red body secretions** (harmless), potent enzyme inducer (drug interactions — OCPs, warfarin, antiretrovirals), hepatotoxicity, flu-like syndrome |
| Nitrofurantoin | Uncomplicated UTI (esp. in pregnancy) | Pulmonary fibrosis (chronic use), hemolysis in G6PD deficiency, avoid near term pregnancy/neonates |

## Antitubercular Drugs (ATT) — high-yield toxicity table
| Drug | Key toxicity |
|---|---|
| Isoniazid (H) | Peripheral neuropathy (pyridoxine deficiency — co-administer B6), hepatotoxicity, drug-induced lupus, sideroblastic anemia |
| Rifampicin (R) | Hepatotoxicity, orange secretions, enzyme induction, flu-like syndrome, thrombocytopenia |
| Pyrazinamide (Z) | Hepatotoxicity, hyperuricemia (avoid in gout), arthralgia |
| Ethambutol (E) | **Optic neuritis** (red-green color blindness, ↓ visual acuity — dose-related) |
| Streptomycin (S) | Ototoxicity (vestibular), nephrotoxicity |

*High-yield: All first-line ATT drugs except ethambutol are hepatotoxic — monitor LFTs. Isoniazid-induced neuropathy is prevented/treated with pyridoxine (Vitamin B6).*

## Beta-Lactamase Inhibitors
- Clavulanic acid, Tazobactam, Sulbactam, Avibactam — combined with beta-lactams to overcome bacterial beta-lactamase resistance; do not have significant antibacterial activity alone

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Penicillin = drug of choice for syphilis at all stages
- Ampicillin rash in a patient with infectious mononucleosis is not a true drug allergy
- Aminoglycosides: nephrotoxicity is reversible, ototoxicity is largely irreversible
- Tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones both chelate divalent cations and are largely avoided in pregnancy/young children
- Chloramphenicol → aplastic anemia (idiosyncratic) and gray baby syndrome in neonates
- Clindamycin has the strongest classic association with C. difficile colitis
- Metronidazole + alcohol → disulfiram-like reaction
- Ethambutol → optic neuritis (red-green color vision loss); isoniazid → peripheral neuropathy (prevented by pyridoxine)
- Vancomycin red man syndrome is due to histamine release from rapid infusion, not true allergy
- Cotrimoxazole is first-line for PCP prophylaxis/treatment in immunocompromised patients
