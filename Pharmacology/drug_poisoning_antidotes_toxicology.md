# Drug Poisoning Antidotes Toxicology

## Overview
One of the highest-yield rapid-fire topics — poison/toxin-antidote pairs, toxidromes, and decontamination principles are tested almost every exam cycle.

## General Principles of Management
1. **ABC** (airway, breathing, circulation) — stabilize first
2. **Decontamination**:
   - Activated charcoal — most effective within 1 hour of ingestion; binds most drugs/toxins (does NOT bind: **alcohols, iron, lithium, most metals, cyanide, strong acids/alkalis** — mnemonic variants exist, e.g., "PHAILS": Pesticides/hydrocarbons, Heavy metals, Alcohols, Iron, Lithium, Solvents)
   - Gastric lavage — largely abandoned for most poisonings (limited evidence, aspiration risk); still occasionally considered very early/large ingestion of specific agents
   - Whole bowel irrigation — for iron, lithium, sustained-release preparations, body packers
3. **Enhance elimination**: urinary alkalinization (salicylates, phenobarbital — weak acids), hemodialysis (for dialyzable toxins — see below)

## Classic Toxidromes
| Toxidrome | Features | Causative agents |
|---|---|---|
| Cholinergic | SLUDGE (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI upset, emesis) + miosis, bradycardia, bronchorrhea/bronchospasm | Organophosphates, carbamates |
| Anticholinergic | "Hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter" — hyperthermia, mydriasis, dry skin, flushing, delirium, urinary retention | Atropine, TCAs, antihistamines, datura |
| Opioid | Miosis (pinpoint pupils), respiratory depression, CNS depression, ↓ bowel sounds | Morphine, heroin, fentanyl |
| Sympathomimetic | Mydriasis, tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, agitation/seizures | Cocaine, amphetamines |
| Sedative-hypnotic | CNS depression, respiratory depression, normal pupils (variable) | Benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohol |
| Serotonin syndrome | Hyperthermia, clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation, tremor | SSRIs + MAOI/tramadol/other serotonergic combos |
| Salicylate | Hyperventilation (respiratory alkalosis early → mixed metabolic acidosis later), tinnitus, hyperthermia, fever | Aspirin overdose |

## Specific High-Yield Poisonings

### Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
- Toxic mechanism: saturation of normal glucuronidation/sulfation pathways → shunted to CYP450 (CYP2E1) → toxic metabolite **NAPQI** → depletes glutathione → hepatocellular necrosis (centrilobular)
- N-acetylcysteine most effective within 8–10 hours of ingestion (repletes glutathione); still given later if presentation delayed
- Rumack-Matthew nomogram used to guide treatment based on level and time post-ingestion (only valid for acute single ingestion, plot from 4 hours post-ingestion onward)
- Clinical stages: Stage 1 (asymptomatic/nonspecific, first 24h) → Stage 2 (RUQ pain, ↑ transaminases, 24–72h) → Stage 3 (peak hepatotoxicity/liver failure, 72–96h) → Stage 4 (recovery or death)

### Organophosphate Poisoning
- Irreversible acetylcholinesterase inhibition → cholinergic toxidrome
- Atropine titrated to dry secretions (not to a specific heart rate); pralidoxime reactivates enzyme (must be given before "aging"/permanent enzyme inactivation occurs — time-critical)
- **Intermediate syndrome**: proximal muscle weakness/respiratory failure occurring 24–96 hours after apparent recovery from acute cholinergic crisis — classic delayed complication requiring ventilatory support

### Salicylate (Aspirin) Poisoning
- Uncouples oxidative phosphorylation, directly stimulates respiratory center
- Early: respiratory alkalosis (hyperventilation); later: mixed respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis (high anion gap)
- Management: urinary alkalinization (ion trapping enhances excretion of this weak acid), hemodialysis for severe toxicity, activated charcoal if early

### Iron Poisoning
- Direct corrosive GI effect + mitochondrial toxicity/free radical damage; deferoxamine chelates free iron (urine turns "vin rosé" color — classic sign of effective chelation)
- Activated charcoal ineffective (does not bind iron) — whole bowel irrigation preferred for decontamination

### Lithium Toxicity
- Narrow therapeutic index; precipitated by dehydration, NSAIDs, ACEI/ARB, thiazides (↓ renal clearance)
- Tremor, ataxia, confusion, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (chronic use), seizures/coma in severe toxicity
- Not bound by activated charcoal; hemodialysis for severe toxicity

## Drugs Removed by Hemodialysis (mnemonic: "I STUMBLED")
Isoniazid, Salicylates, Theophylline, Uremia (underlying), Methanol, Barbiturates (phenobarbital), Lithium, Ethylene glycol, Depakote (valproate) — high-yield list for "which poisoning benefits from dialysis" questions.

## High-Yield One-Liners
- N-acetylcysteine is the antidote for paracetamol overdose — most effective within 8–10 hours
- Pralidoxime must be given early in organophosphate poisoning before enzyme "aging" occurs
- Intermediate syndrome in organophosphate poisoning presents 24–96 hours after recovery from the acute cholinergic crisis, with proximal weakness/respiratory failure
- Activated charcoal does NOT bind alcohols, iron, lithium, or heavy metals
- Deferoxamine causes characteristic "vin rosé" urine in effective iron chelation
- Fomepizole is the preferred antidote for methanol/ethylene glycol poisoning over ethanol
- Flumazenil can precipitate seizures in chronic benzodiazepine users — use cautiously
- Physostigmine is the antidote for anticholinergic toxicity because it crosses the BBB (unlike neostigmine)
- Glucagon and high-dose insulin-euglycemia therapy are used for beta-blocker and calcium channel blocker overdose
- Methylene blue treats methemoglobinemia but is avoided in G6PD deficiency (use ascorbic acid instead)
