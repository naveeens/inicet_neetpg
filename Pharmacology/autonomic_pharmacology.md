# Autonomic Pharmacology

## Overview
The autonomic nervous system (ANS) has two limbs — sympathetic (adrenergic) and parasympathetic (cholinergic). Understanding receptor subtypes and their effects is the key to predicting drug action across nearly every organ system.

## Receptor Overview
| Receptor | Location/Action | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | CNS, gastric parietal cells (acid secretion) | Gq |
| M2 | Heart — ↓ HR, ↓ AV conduction | Gi |
| M3 | Smooth muscle contraction (gut, bladder), glands, eye (miosis, accommodation), vascular endothelium (NO → vasodilation) | Gq |
| α1 | Vasoconstriction, mydriasis, prostate/bladder sphincter contraction | Gq |
| α2 | Presynaptic autoreceptor (↓ NE release), CNS (↓ sympathetic outflow) | Gi |
| β1 | Heart — ↑ HR, ↑ contractility, ↑ conduction; kidney — renin release | Gs |
| β2 | Bronchodilation, vasodilation (skeletal muscle), uterine relaxation (tocolysis), ↑ insulin/glycogenolysis | Gs |
| β3 | Bladder detrusor relaxation, lipolysis | Gs |

## Cholinergic (Parasympathomimetic) Drugs
### Direct Agonists
| Drug | Feature |
|---|---|
| Acetylcholine | Not used clinically (rapid hydrolysis) |
| Carbachol, Pilocarpine | Used topically in **glaucoma** (↑ outflow via ciliary muscle contraction → opens trabecular meshwork); pilocarpine also for xerostomia (Sjögren's) |
| Bethanechol | Selective M3 agonist — used in **urinary retention (atonic bladder), postoperative ileus** |

### Anticholinesterases (indirect agonists)
| Drug | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Edrophonium | Very short (used in **diagnosis** of myasthenia gravis — classic, though largely historic now) | Diagnostic |
| Neostigmine, Pyridostigmine | Intermediate | Myasthenia gravis treatment; **reversal of neuromuscular blockade** post-surgery (with atropine/glycopyrrolate to prevent muscarinic side effects); paralytic ileus, atonic bladder |
| Physostigmine | Crosses BBB | Antidote for **anticholinergic (atropine) poisoning**; glaucoma (historic) |
| Organophosphates (irreversible) | Long-lasting | Insecticides/nerve agents — toxicology relevance |

*High-yield: Neostigmine does NOT cross BBB (quaternary amine) — no CNS effects/toxicity; physostigmine (tertiary amine) crosses BBB — used for CNS anticholinergic toxidrome.*

### Anticholinergics (Antimuscarinics)
| Drug | Use | Notable feature |
|---|---|---|
| Atropine | Bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning (antidote), preanesthetic (↓ secretions), mydriasis for fundus exam | Crosses BBB; classic toxidrome: "hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter" |
| Glycopyrrolate | Preanesthetic, ↓ secretions | Does NOT cross BBB — preferred when CNS effects undesired |
| Hyoscine (Scopolamine) | Motion sickness, IBS (antispasmodic) | Crosses BBB — sedation |
| Ipratropium/Tiotropium | Bronchodilation (COPD/asthma) | Inhaled — minimal systemic absorption |
| Tolterodine/Oxybutynin/Solifenacin | Overactive bladder | M3 selective |

## Adrenergic (Sympathomimetic) Drugs
| Drug | Receptor selectivity | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Phenylephrine | α1 | Nasal decongestant, mydriasis (no cycloplegia), hypotension |
| Clonidine, Methyldopa | α2 (central) | Hypertension (methyldopa = drug of choice in pregnancy); clonidine also for opioid/alcohol withdrawal, ADHD |
| Dobutamine | β1 predominant | Cardiogenic shock/inotropic support |
| Salbutamol, Terbutaline | β2 | Asthma/COPD (bronchodilation); terbutaline also tocolytic |
| Isoproterenol | β1 = β2 (non-selective β) | Heart block (temporary), historic asthma use |
| Epinephrine | α1, α2, β1, β2 | Anaphylaxis (drug of choice), cardiac arrest, local hemostasis (with LA) |
| Norepinephrine | α1 > β1 | Septic shock (vasopressor of choice) |
| Dopamine | Dose-dependent: low = D1 (renal vasodilation, largely historic use), mid = β1, high = α1 | Historic use in shock — largely replaced by norepinephrine |

*High-yield: Epinephrine reverses anaphylactic hypotension via both α1 (vasoconstriction) and β1 (inotropy/chronotropy); also β2-mediated bronchodilation — hence drug of choice in anaphylaxis over antihistamines/steroids (which are adjuncts, slower onset).*

*High-yield trap: Epinephrine + non-selective beta-blocker (e.g., propranolol) → unopposed alpha action → severe hypertension ("epinephrine reversal" of vasodilation, described classically with epinephrine).*

## Ganglionic Blockers
- Hexamethonium, Trimethaphan — historic antihypertensives; block both sympathetic and parasympathetic ganglia (mixed, unpredictable effects) — mostly of historical/exam interest only

## Neuromuscular Blockers (Skeletal muscle NMJ — nicotinic N-M receptor)
| Type | Drug | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Depolarizing | **Succinylcholine (Suxamethonium)** | Fast onset/short acting; used for rapid sequence intubation; causes initial fasciculations, then flaccid paralysis; **hyperkalemia risk** (contraindicated in burns, spinal cord injury, neuromuscular disease); triggers **malignant hyperthermia** in susceptible individuals; prolonged apnea in atypical pseudocholinesterase deficiency |
| Non-depolarizing (competitive) | Vecuronium, Rocuronium, Atracurium, Pancuronium, Tubocurarine | Reversed by anticholinesterases (neostigmine + atropine/glycopyrrolate) or sugammadex (rocuronium/vecuronium specific); Atracurium undergoes Hofmann elimination — safe in renal/hepatic failure; histamine release (tubocurarine, atracurium) → hypotension, bronchospasm |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Atropine crosses BBB and causes CNS effects; glycopyrrolate does not
- Physostigmine (crosses BBB) is the antidote for atropine/anticholinergic poisoning
- Epinephrine is drug of choice in anaphylaxis — acts via α1, β1, and β2
- Norepinephrine = vasopressor of choice in septic shock
- Succinylcholine → hyperkalemia and malignant hyperthermia risk; contraindicated in burns/denervation injury
- Methyldopa = antihypertensive of choice in pregnancy (central α2 agonist)
- Pilocarpine/carbachol used in glaucoma to increase aqueous outflow via ciliary muscle contraction
- Non-selective beta-blocker + epinephrine → unopposed alpha vasoconstriction → hypertensive crisis
