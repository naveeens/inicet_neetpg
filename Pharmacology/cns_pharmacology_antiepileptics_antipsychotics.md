# CNS Pharmacology Antiepileptics Antipsychotics

## Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs)

### Mechanism-Based Classification
| Mechanism | Drugs |
|---|---|
| Na channel blockade | Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Lamotrigine, Valproate (also other mechanisms), Oxcarbazepine |
| GABA potentiation | Benzodiazepines, Barbiturates, Vigabatrin (↓ GABA breakdown), Tiagabine |
| T-type Ca channel blockade | **Ethosuximide** (drug of choice for absence seizures) |
| Multiple mechanisms | Valproate (Na channel + ↑ GABA + T-type Ca block) |
| SV2A binding | Levetiracetam |
| α2δ Ca channel subunit | Gabapentin, Pregabalin |

### Seizure-Type-Specific Drug of Choice
| Seizure type | Drug of choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Generalized tonic-clonic | Valproate (or Phenytoin/Carbamazepine) | Valproate broad-spectrum |
| Absence (petit mal) | **Ethosuximide** or Valproate | Carbamazepine/Phenytoin can worsen absence seizures — avoid |
| Myoclonic | Valproate | — |
| Focal/partial | Carbamazepine or Lamotrigine/Levetiracetam | — |
| Infantile spasms (West syndrome) | ACTH/Vigabatrin | — |
| Status epilepticus | IV Lorazepam/Diazepam (first line) → Phenytoin/Fosphenytoin (second line) → Phenobarbital/anesthesia (refractory) | — |
| Eclampsia (seizure) | **Magnesium sulfate** (not a classic AED but drug of choice) | — |

### Key Individual Drug Notes
| Drug | Key toxicity | High-yield note |
|---|---|---|
| Phenytoin | Gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, coarse facies, megaloblastic anemia (↓ folate), osteomalacia, cerebellar signs (nystagmus, ataxia at toxic levels), fetal hydantoin syndrome, Stevens-Johnson syndrome | Zero-order kinetics near therapeutic range; strong enzyme inducer |
| Valproate | Hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, weight gain, tremor, alopecia, **neural tube defects (highly teratogenic)**, thrombocytopenia | Avoid in pregnancy/women of childbearing age if possible; also mood stabilizer (bipolar), migraine prophylaxis |
| Carbamazepine | Aplastic anemia/agranulocytosis, SIADH (hyponatremia), Stevens-Johnson syndrome (esp. HLA-B*1502 in certain populations), enzyme induction (autoinduction) | Also used in trigeminal neuralgia, bipolar disorder |
| Lamotrigine | Stevens-Johnson syndrome/rash (risk ↑ with rapid titration or valproate co-administration) | Slow titration required |
| Ethosuximide | GI upset, drowsiness | Drug of choice for absence seizures, no hepatotoxicity concern |
| Gabapentin/Pregabalin | Sedation, weight gain, peripheral edema | Also used in neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia; renally cleared |
| Levetiracetam | Behavioral changes/irritability | Minimal drug interactions — favored with polypharmacy |
| Phenobarbital | Sedation, enzyme induction, respiratory depression | Historic first-line, still used in neonatal seizures |

*High-yield: All major AEDs (phenytoin, valproate, carbamazepine) are teratogenic to varying degrees — valproate carries the highest neural tube defect risk. Folic acid supplementation recommended pre-conception in women on AEDs.*

## Antipsychotics

### Typical (First-Generation) — D2 receptor antagonism
| Drug | Potency | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorpromazine | Low potency | Sedation, anticholinergic effects, orthostatic hypotension, **corneal/lens deposits**, photosensitivity |
| Haloperidol | High potency | More extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS), less sedation/anticholinergic |
| Fluphenazine | High potency | Available as depot injection |

### Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS) Timeline (classic exam sequence)
| Onset | Symptom | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Hours | Acute dystonia | IV/IM anticholinergic (benztropine) or antihistamine (diphenhydramine) |
| Days | Akathisia | Beta-blocker (propranolol) or benzodiazepine |
| Weeks | Parkinsonism (rigidity, bradykinesia, tremor) | Anticholinergics, dose reduction |
| Months–years | **Tardive dyskinesia** | Often irreversible; stop offending drug, consider valbenazine; anticholinergics can worsen it |

*Mnemonic for EPS timeline: "4 hours, 4 days, 4 weeks, 4 months" — dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia.*

### Atypical (Second-Generation) — D2 + 5HT2A antagonism (fewer EPS, more metabolic effects)
| Drug | Key feature |
|---|---|
| **Clozapine** | Most effective for treatment-resistant schizophrenia; **agranulocytosis** (mandatory regular blood count monitoring), seizures, myocarditis, severe metabolic syndrome, sialorrhea; least EPS of all antipsychotics |
| Olanzapine | High risk of weight gain/metabolic syndrome |
| Risperidone | Highest EPS/prolactin elevation among atypicals (behaves more like a typical at higher doses) |
| Quetiapine | Sedation, orthostatic hypotension; lower EPS |
| Aripiprazole | Partial D2 agonist — lower metabolic/EPS burden |

### Antipsychotic Adverse Effects — Cross-Cutting
- **Hyperprolactinemia** (D2 blockade in tuberoinfundibular pathway) → galactorrhea, amenorrhea, gynecomastia — most with risperidone/typicals; least with aripiprazole (partial agonist, can even lower prolactin)
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: hyperthermia, rigidity ("lead-pipe"), autonomic instability, altered mental status, ↑ CPK — medical emergency; treat with **dantrolene, bromocriptine**, stop offending drug, supportive cooling
- **Metabolic syndrome**: worst with clozapine/olanzapine; monitor weight, glucose, lipids

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Ethosuximide = drug of choice for absence seizures; carbamazepine/phenytoin can worsen absence seizures
- Valproate = most teratogenic AED (neural tube defects) — avoid in pregnancy if alternatives exist
- Phenytoin toxicity mnemonic: gum hypertrophy, hirsutism, coarse facies, cerebellar ataxia/nystagmus
- Status epilepticus: benzodiazepine first, then phenytoin/fosphenytoin, then phenobarbital/anesthesia
- Clozapine = most effective for treatment-resistant schizophrenia but requires mandatory ANC monitoring for agranulocytosis
- EPS timeline: dystonia (hours) → akathisia (days) → parkinsonism (weeks) → tardive dyskinesia (months, often irreversible)
- NMS treated with dantrolene and bromocriptine; distinguish from serotonin syndrome (clonus/hyperreflexia more prominent, faster onset)
- Aripiprazole (partial D2 agonist) has the lowest prolactin-elevating and EPS potential among antipsychotics
