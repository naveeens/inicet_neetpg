# Respiratory Pharmacology

## Overview
Bronchial asthma and COPD pharmacology form the core of this topic — bronchodilators, anti-inflammatory agents, and their step-wise use are frequently tested.

## Bronchodilators

### Beta-2 Agonists
| Drug | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (Albuterol), Levosalbutamol | Short-acting (SABA), onset minutes | Rescue/reliever therapy for acute bronchospasm — used in all asthma/COPD regardless of severity step |
| Salmeterol, Formoterol | Long-acting (LABA), 12 h | Maintenance therapy — **never used as monotherapy in asthma** (must be combined with inhaled corticosteroid — ↑ risk of severe exacerbation/death if used alone, classic exam fact) |
| Vilanterol, Indacaterol | Ultra-long-acting (24 h) | Once-daily maintenance, especially in COPD |

- Mechanism: β2 receptor → ↑ cAMP → bronchial smooth muscle relaxation
- Adverse effects: tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia (β2-mediated intracellular K shift), hyperglycemia

### Anticholinergics (Antimuscarinics)
| Drug | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ipratropium | Short-acting (SAMA) | Acute asthma/COPD exacerbation (add-on to SABA) |
| Tiotropium, Glycopyrronium, Umeclidinium | Long-acting (LAMA) | COPD maintenance — core therapy; also add-on in severe asthma |

- Mechanism: M3 receptor blockade → ↓ bronchoconstriction/secretions
- Minimal systemic absorption (quaternary ammonium, inhaled) — dry mouth main side effect

### Methylxanthines
- Theophylline, Aminophylline
- Mechanism: phosphodiesterase inhibition (↑ cAMP) + adenosine receptor antagonism
- Narrow therapeutic index; toxicity: nausea, arrhythmia, seizures; metabolized by CYP1A2 — levels affected by smoking (induction, ↓ levels), macrolides/fluoroquinolones/cimetidine (inhibition, ↑ levels → toxicity)
- Largely reserved as add-on therapy now, not first-line, due to narrow therapeutic window and better alternatives

## Anti-Inflammatory / Controller Drugs

### Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)
- Budesonide, Fluticasone, Beclomethasone
- Mainstay of persistent asthma control (mechanism: ↓ airway inflammation, ↓ mucosal edema, ↑ β2 receptor expression)
- Local adverse effects: oral candidiasis, dysphonia — minimize by rinsing mouth/spacer use
- Systemic absorption low but possible with high doses (growth suppression in children, adrenal suppression — monitor with long-term high-dose use)

### Leukotriene Modifiers
| Drug | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Montelukast, Zafirlukast | Leukotriene receptor (CysLT1) antagonist | Add-on for asthma, allergic rhinitis, exercise-induced bronchospasm; neuropsychiatric effects (mood changes) — noted caution |
| Zileuton | 5-lipoxygenase inhibitor | Less commonly used; hepatotoxicity |

*High-yield: Leukotriene antagonists are particularly useful in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (Samter's triad: asthma + aspirin sensitivity + nasal polyps) since leukotrienes mediate this NSAID-induced bronchospasm.*

### Mast Cell Stabilizers
- Cromolyn sodium, Nedocromil — prevent mast cell degranulation; prophylactic only (no role in acute attack), used more in pediatric/exercise-induced asthma historically; largely replaced by ICS

### Biologics (severe asthma, add-on)
| Drug | Target | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Omalizumab | Anti-IgE | Severe allergic asthma |
| Mepolizumab, Reslizumab | Anti-IL5 | Severe eosinophilic asthma |
| Dupilumab | Anti-IL4/IL13 receptor | Severe asthma, atopic dermatitis |

## Asthma Step-Wise Management (concept, not exact numeric step — verify against latest GINA guideline)
- Mild intermittent: as-needed SABA (or per newer GINA guidance, as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol is increasingly favored over SABA-only even in mild asthma)
- Persistent: regular low-dose ICS, stepping up to ICS-LABA combination, then higher dose ICS-LABA ± LAMA, then biologics/oral corticosteroids for severe refractory disease
- Acute severe exacerbation: nebulized SABA + ipratropium, systemic corticosteroids, oxygen; IV magnesium sulfate in severe cases; consider IV aminophylline/theophylline as further add-on in refractory cases

*High-yield: GINA guidelines have shifted away from SABA-only "reliever" therapy in recent years toward ICS-containing reliever strategies even in mild asthma — this is a notable shift from older exam convention; know both the classic stepwise approach and this updated direction, and verify against the current GINA report for exact wording expected in current exams.*

## COPD-Specific Notes
- LAMA and LABA are core maintenance therapy (often preferred over ICS as first step, unlike asthma) — ICS added in patients with frequent exacerbations/eosinophilia
- Roflumilast — PDE4 inhibitor, used in severe COPD with chronic bronchitis phenotype and frequent exacerbations; weight loss, GI side effects
- Long-term oxygen therapy — mortality benefit in COPD with resting hypoxemia (classic evidence-based intervention)

## Other Respiratory Drugs
| Drug | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| N-acetylcysteine | Mucolytic (breaks disulfide bonds in mucus); also antidote for paracetamol overdose | Dual high-yield use |
| Dornase alfa | Mucolytic in cystic fibrosis | Breaks down extracellular DNA from neutrophils |
| Codeine, Dextromethorphan | Antitussive (cough suppressant) | Codeine — opioid, caution in children (variable CYP2D6 metabolism to morphine); dextromethorphan — NMDA antagonist, non-opioid |
| Pulmonary surfactant (Beractant) | Neonatal RDS | Exogenous surfactant replacement in premature infants |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- LABAs must never be used as monotherapy in asthma — always combined with ICS (black box warning historically for isolated exacerbation/death risk)
- Ipratropium/tiotropium have minimal systemic absorption due to quaternary ammonium structure
- Theophylline has a narrow therapeutic index; levels raised by macrolides/fluoroquinolones/cimetidine, lowered by smoking
- Leukotriene antagonists (montelukast) are especially useful in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease
- Cromolyn is prophylactic only — no role in acute bronchospasm
- N-acetylcysteine is both a mucolytic and the antidote for paracetamol overdose
- GINA guidance has shifted toward ICS-containing reliever therapy even in mild asthma — verify latest GINA report for exact current recommendation
- Omalizumab targets IgE; mepolizumab/reslizumab target IL-5 — both used in severe/refractory asthma phenotypes
