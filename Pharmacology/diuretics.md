# Diuretics

## Overview
Diuretics act at distinct nephron sites, giving each class a characteristic potency and electrolyte disturbance profile — a favorite for site-of-action and side-effect matching questions.

## Site of Action (Nephron Map)
| Site | Class | % Na reabsorption affected |
|---|---|---|
| Proximal tubule | Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, Osmotic diuretics | ~5% (PCT normally reabsorbs ~65% but CAIs act on a small fraction of that via bicarb) |
| Thick ascending loop of Henle | **Loop diuretics** | ~25% — most potent |
| Distal convoluted tubule | **Thiazides** | ~5–8% |
| Collecting duct | **Potassium-sparing diuretics** | ~2–3% |

## Loop Diuretics
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Furosemide, Torsemide, Bumetanide | Inhibit Na-K-2Cl cotransporter (NKCC2) in thick ascending loop | Acute pulmonary edema, CHF, refractory edema, hypercalcemia (promotes Ca excretion) | **Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypercalciuria (opposite of thiazide), ototoxicity (especially with aminoglycosides), hyperuricemia, metabolic alkalosis** |
| Ethacrynic acid | Same mechanism, non-sulfonamide | Used in sulfa allergy | Most ototoxic |

*High-yield: Loop diuretics → hypercalciuria (risk of stones); Thiazides → hypocalciuria (used therapeutically in recurrent Ca renal stones and osteoporosis). Classic paired contrast.*

## Thiazide Diuretics
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone, Indapamide | Inhibit Na-Cl cotransporter (NCC) in DCT | First-line for essential HTN; nephrogenic DI (paradoxical antidiuretic effect); recurrent Ca-containing renal stones; osteoporosis (↓ Ca excretion) | **Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hypercalcemia, hyperuricemia (precipitates gout), hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia** — mnemonic "hyperGLUC" |

*High-yield: Thiazides in nephrogenic diabetes insipidus work via paradoxical mechanism — volume depletion → ↑ proximal tubular Na/water reabsorption → ↓ delivery to collecting duct → net antidiuretic effect.*

## Potassium-Sparing Diuretics
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spironolactone, Eplerenone | Aldosterone receptor antagonist (collecting duct) | Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's), heart failure (mortality benefit), ascites (cirrhosis) — drug of choice, hirsutism/PCOS (antiandrogen) | Hyperkalemia, gynecomastia/menstrual irregularity (spironolactone — antiandrogenic; eplerenone more selective, less gynecomastia) |
| Amiloride, Triamterene | Block ENaC directly (aldosterone-independent) | Combined with thiazides/loop diuretics to prevent hypokalemia | Hyperkalemia |

## Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors
| Drug | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|
| Acetazolamide | Glaucoma (↓ aqueous humor production), altitude sickness prophylaxis, metabolic alkalosis correction, idiopathic intracranial hypertension | Metabolic acidosis (hyperchloremic, non-anion gap), renal stones (alkaline urine → Ca phosphate stones), paresthesias, sulfa allergy cross-reactivity |

## Osmotic Diuretics
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mannitol | Osmotically active, filtered but not reabsorbed | ↓ ICP (cerebral edema), ↓ IOP (acute angle-closure glaucoma emergency) | Contraindicated in anuria/CHF (initial volume expansion can precipitate pulmonary edema); can cause dehydration/hypernatremia later |

## ADH-Related / Vasopressin Receptor Antagonists
- **Vaptans (Tolvaptan, Conivaptan)**: V2 receptor antagonists → aquaresis (water excretion without electrolyte loss) — used in SIADH/hyponatremia; risk of overly rapid correction → osmotic demyelination syndrome

## Electrolyte Disturbance Quick Reference
| Diuretic | K+ | Ca²⁺ | Uric acid | Glucose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loop | ↓ | ↓ (↑ excretion) | ↑ | ↑ (mild) |
| Thiazide | ↓ | ↑ (↓ excretion) | ↑ | ↑ |
| K-sparing | ↑ | — | — | — |
| CAI | ↓ (mild) | — | — | — |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Loop diuretics = most potent; act on thick ascending limb; cause hypercalciuria
- Thiazides cause hypercalcemia (↓ urinary Ca excretion) — useful in recurrent Ca stones
- Spironolactone = drug of choice for ascites in cirrhosis and for hyperaldosteronism
- Acetazolamide causes non-anion-gap metabolic acidosis and can cause renal stones (alkaline urine)
- Mannitol is contraindicated in anuria and can worsen pulmonary edema initially (volume expansion before diuresis)
- Ethacrynic acid is the loop diuretic of choice in sulfa allergy
- Aminoglycoside + loop diuretic = synergistic ototoxicity (avoid combination when possible)
