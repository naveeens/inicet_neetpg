# GI Pharmacology

## Acid-Peptic Disease Drugs

### Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)
- Omeprazole, Pantoprazole, Esomeprazole, Rabeprazole
- Mechanism: irreversibly inhibit H+/K+ ATPase on gastric parietal cells — most potent acid suppression
- Use: GERD, peptic ulcer disease, H. pylori eradication regimens, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, stress ulcer prophylaxis
- Adverse effects: long-term use associated with ↓ Ca/Mg/B12 absorption (fracture risk, hypomagnesemia), ↑ risk of C. difficile infection and community-acquired pneumonia (↓ gastric acid barrier), rebound acid hypersecretion on withdrawal
- Best taken 30–60 min before meals (need active proton pumps to bind)

### H2 Receptor Blockers
- Ranitidine (largely withdrawn/restricted in many markets due to NDMA contamination concerns — verify current regulatory status), Famotidine, Cimetidine
- Mechanism: block H2 receptor on parietal cells → ↓ acid secretion (less potent than PPI)
- **Cimetidine**: strong CYP450 inhibitor (drug interactions), antiandrogenic (gynecomastia, ↓ libido) — largely avoided now in favor of famotidine

### Antacids
- Aluminum hydroxide (constipating), Magnesium hydroxide (diarrhea — often combined to offset each other), Calcium carbonate (can cause acid rebound, milk-alkali syndrome with excess use)
- Neutralize existing acid; short-acting; can chelate/reduce absorption of tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, iron

### Mucosal Protective Agents
| Drug | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Sucralfate | Forms protective barrier over ulcer base (needs acidic pH to activate) | Peptic ulcer, stress ulcer prophylaxis |
| Misoprostol | PGE1 analog — ↑ mucus/bicarbonate secretion, ↓ acid secretion | Prevention of NSAID-induced ulcers; also used in obstetrics (labor induction/abortion) — **contraindicated in pregnancy for GI indication** (abortifacient/uterotonic) |
| Bismuth subsalicylate | Coats ulcer, antibacterial against H. pylori | Part of quadruple H. pylori therapy, traveler's diarrhea |

### H. pylori Eradication
- **Triple therapy**: PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin (7–14 days)
- **Quadruple therapy** (used if clarithromycin resistance suspected or first-line failure): PPI/Bismuth + Metronidazole + Tetracycline + Bismuth
- Metronidazole substituted for amoxicillin in penicillin allergy

## Antiemetics
| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5HT3 antagonists | Ondansetron, Granisetron | Block 5HT3 in CTZ/gut | Chemotherapy-induced, postoperative nausea/vomiting; **QT prolongation, constipation** |
| D2 antagonists | Metoclopramide, Domperidone | Block D2 in CTZ; also prokinetic (↑ gastric emptying) | GERD, gastroparesis, diabetic gastroparesis; **metoclopramide crosses BBB → EPS/tardive dyskinesia risk** (domperidone doesn't cross BBB — fewer CNS effects but QT prolongation risk) |
| Antihistamines | Meclizine, Promethazine | H1 blockade | Motion sickness, vertigo |
| Anticholinergics | Hyoscine (scopolamine) | M1 blockade | Motion sickness (patch) |
| NK1 antagonists | Aprepitant | Substance P/NK1 blockade | Delayed chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting (combined with 5HT3 antagonist + dexamethasone) |
| Cannabinoids | Dronabinol | CB1 agonist | Refractory chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting, appetite stimulation |
| Corticosteroids | Dexamethasone | Unclear mechanism | Adjunct in chemotherapy-induced nausea/vomiting prophylaxis |

*High-yield: Standard highly-emetogenic chemotherapy prophylaxis = 5HT3 antagonist + Dexamethasone + NK1 antagonist (triple combination).*

## Laxatives
| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk-forming | Psyllium, Methylcellulose | ↑ stool bulk/water retention | Safest, first-line |
| Osmotic | Lactulose, Polyethylene glycol, Magnesium salts | Draw water into gut lumen | Lactulose also used in **hepatic encephalopathy** (↓ colonic pH → traps NH4+, ↓ NH3 absorption) |
| Stimulant | Bisacodyl, Senna | ↑ intestinal motility (direct colonic stimulation) | Fast-acting, avoid chronic use (dependence, melanosis coli with senna) |
| Stool softener | Docusate | Detergent action | Mild, used post-op/avoid straining |
| Lubricant | Mineral oil | Lubricates stool | Risk of lipoid pneumonia if aspirated |
| Chloride channel activator | Lubiprostone | ↑ intestinal fluid secretion | Chronic constipation, IBS-C |

## Antidiarrheals
| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Loperamide | Opioid receptor agonist (peripheral, minimal CNS penetration) | First-line symptomatic; avoid in bloody diarrhea/dysentery (risk of worsening invasive infection/toxic megacolon) |
| Diphenoxylate + Atropine (Lomotil) | Opioid agonist + subtherapeutic atropine (deters abuse) | Similar cautions as loperamide |
| Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) | Na-glucose cotransport | Mainstay of acute diarrhea management, especially in children (WHO ORS) |
| Racecadotril | Enkephalinase inhibitor — antisecretory | Adjunct in acute diarrhea |

*High-yield: Antimotility agents (loperamide) are contraindicated in invasive/inflammatory diarrhea (dysentery, C. difficile, EHEC) — risk of toxic megacolon and prolonged toxin exposure.*

## IBD Drugs
| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| 5-ASA (Sulfasalazine, Mesalamine) | Mild-moderate ulcerative colitis; sulfasalazine also used in rheumatoid arthritis |
| Corticosteroids | Acute flares (induction, not maintenance) |
| Azathioprine, 6-Mercaptopurine | Maintenance/steroid-sparing |
| Anti-TNF (Infliximab, Adalimumab) | Moderate-severe Crohn's/UC, especially fistulizing Crohn's |

*High-yield: Sulfasalazine is metabolized by gut flora into sulfapyridine (responsible for most adverse effects — hemolysis in G6PD deficiency, hypersensitivity) + 5-ASA (active moiety, anti-inflammatory).*

## Prokinetics
- Metoclopramide, Domperidone (D2 antagonists — see antiemetics)
- Erythromycin — motilin receptor agonist, used off-label for diabetic gastroparesis (tachyphylaxis with prolonged use)

## Drugs for Pancreatic/Hepatic Conditions
- Pancreatic enzyme replacement (lipase/amylase/protease) — chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis
- Ursodeoxycholic acid — primary biliary cholangitis, gallstone dissolution (cholesterol stones)
- Lactulose, Rifaximin — hepatic encephalopathy (↓ ammonia-producing gut flora and absorption)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- PPIs are the most potent acid suppressants; take 30–60 minutes before meals for maximal effect
- Long-term PPI use is associated with hypomagnesemia, B12 deficiency, and increased C. difficile/pneumonia risk
- Misoprostol prevents NSAID-induced ulcers but is contraindicated in pregnancy (abortifacient/uterotonic)
- Metoclopramide crosses the BBB and carries EPS/tardive dyskinesia risk; domperidone does not cross BBB
- Lactulose is used both as a laxative and in hepatic encephalopathy (ammonia trapping)
- Loperamide and other antimotility agents are contraindicated in bloody/invasive diarrhea
- Standard triple therapy for H. pylori = PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin
- Highly emetogenic chemotherapy prophylaxis = 5HT3 antagonist + dexamethasone + NK1 antagonist
- Sulfasalazine's adverse effects are mainly due to its sulfapyridine component, not the 5-ASA moiety
