# Cardiovascular Pharmacology

## Overview
Covers antihypertensives, antianginals, antiarrhythmics, heart failure drugs, and lipid-lowering agents — a core high-yield block that overlaps heavily with Medicine.

## Antihypertensives
| Class | Drugs | Mechanism | Key notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACE inhibitors | Enalapril, Ramipril, Lisinopril | ↓ Angiotensin II, ↑ bradykinin | Cough (bradykinin-mediated), hyperkalemia, angioedema, teratogenic (contraindicated in pregnancy), renoprotective in diabetic nephropathy |
| ARBs | Losartan, Telmisartan, Valsartan | Block AT1 receptor | No cough (no bradykinin accumulation); same renal/teratogenic cautions as ACEI |
| CCBs (dihydropyridine) | Amlodipine, Nifedipine | Block L-type Ca²⁺ channels — vascular smooth muscle | Ankle edema, flushing, gingival hyperplasia (esp. with concurrent cyclosporine) |
| CCBs (non-DHP) | Verapamil, Diltiazem | ↓ SA/AV conduction + vasodilation | Avoid with beta-blockers (additive AV block/bradycardia); constipation (verapamil) |
| Thiazides | Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone | Inhibit Na-Cl cotransporter (DCT) | First-line for uncomplicated HTN; hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperuricemia, hyperglycemia, hypercalcemia |
| Beta-blockers | Metoprolol, Atenolol, Bisoprolol | ↓ HR, ↓ contractility, ↓ renin | Not first-line for uncomplicated HTN per current guidelines; preferred with comorbid CAD/HF/arrhythmia |
| Central α2 agonists | Methyldopa, Clonidine | ↓ sympathetic outflow | Methyldopa = drug of choice in pregnancy |
| Direct vasodilators | Hydralazine, Minoxidil | Direct arteriolar dilation | Reflex tachycardia, fluid retention; hydralazine → drug-induced lupus; minoxidil → hypertrichosis |
| Alpha-1 blockers | Prazosin, Doxazosin | Block α1 | First-dose hypotension; useful in BPH + HTN |

*High-yield: In pregnancy-induced hypertension, avoid ACEI/ARB (teratogenic — fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios) and avoid most diuretics; use Labetalol, Methyldopa, Nifedipine (extended release), or Hydralazine (acute severe HTN/eclampsia).*

## Antianginal Drugs
| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrates | GTN (sublingual/IV), Isosorbide dinitrate/mononitrate | ↑ NO → venodilation predominant → ↓ preload → ↓ myocardial O2 demand | Headache, tolerance with continuous use (need nitrate-free interval), hypotension; contraindicated with **PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil)** — profound hypotension |
| Beta-blockers | Metoprolol, Atenolol | ↓ HR, ↓ contractility → ↓ O2 demand | First-line in stable angina |
| CCBs | Amlodipine, Diltiazem | ↓ afterload / coronary vasodilation | Used when beta-blockers contraindicated; diltiazem/verapamil also good for vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina |
| Ranolazine | Late Na⁺ current inhibitor | Add-on for refractory angina | Does not affect HR/BP |
| Nicorandil | K-ATP channel opener + nitrate-like | Alternative antianginal | — |

*High-yield: Prinzmetal (vasospastic) angina → treat with CCB/nitrates; beta-blockers can worsen coronary spasm (unopposed alpha vasoconstriction) — avoid.*

## Heart Failure Drugs
| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACEI/ARB/ARNI | Enalapril; Sacubitril-Valsartan | RAAS blockade; ARNI additionally ↑ natriuretic peptides via neprilysin inhibition | Mortality benefit; ARNI superior to ACEI in reducing HF hospitalization/mortality per current guideline-directed therapy |
| Beta-blockers | Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol succinate | Mortality benefit in HFrEF | Start low, titrate slowly; avoid in decompensated/acute HF |
| Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists | Spironolactone, Eplerenone | ↓ aldosterone effect | Mortality benefit in HFrEF; hyperkalemia, gynecomastia (spironolactone) |
| SGLT2 inhibitors | Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin | ↓ glucose reabsorption; cardiorenal protective mechanism | Now part of first-line quadruple therapy for HFrEF per current guidelines (relatively recent addition — verify latest heart failure guideline for exact positioning) |
| Loop diuretics | Furosemide | Symptomatic relief of congestion | No mortality benefit, but essential for symptom control |
| Digoxin | Na-K ATPase inhibition → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ → positive inotropy; also ↑ vagal tone | Symptom/hospitalization benefit, no mortality benefit; narrow therapeutic index | See toxicology notes below |
| Hydralazine + Isosorbide dinitrate | Vasodilation | Alternative in ACEI/ARB intolerant, particularly benefit shown in African-American population per classic trial data |

## Digoxin — High-Yield Toxicology
- Narrow therapeutic index; toxicity precipitated by **hypokalemia** (competes with K+ at Na-K ATPase), hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia, renal failure, hypothyroidism
- **ECG**: characteristic "reverse tick" ST depression (therapeutic effect, not toxicity); toxicity → any arrhythmia, but **bidirectional ventricular tachycardia** and **atrial tachycardia with AV block** are classically toxic
- GI symptoms (nausea, vomiting, anorexia), **visual disturbance (yellow-green vision, halos)** — classic exam clue
- Antidote: **Digoxin-specific antibody (Digibind/Digoxin immune Fab)**
- Drugs that increase digoxin levels: Quinidine, Verapamil, Amiodarone (displace from tissue binding/↓ clearance) — need dose reduction

## Antiarrhythmic Drugs (Vaughan Williams Classification)
| Class | Mechanism | Drugs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| IA | Na channel block (moderate) + K channel block | Quinidine, Procainamide, Disopyramide | Prolong QT; procainamide → drug-induced lupus |
| IB | Na channel block (weak, fast dissociation) | Lidocaine, Mexiletine | Post-MI ventricular arrhythmia (lidocaine, IV) |
| IC | Na channel block (strong) | Flecainide, Propafenone | Avoid in structural heart disease/post-MI (proarrhythmic) |
| II | Beta-blockers | Metoprolol, Esmolol | Rate control (AF), SVT |
| III | K channel block, prolong repolarization | **Amiodarone**, Sotalol, Dronedarone | Amiodarone: broad spectrum, also has class I/II/IV properties; multiple toxicities (below) |
| IV | Ca channel block (non-DHP) | Verapamil, Diltiazem | Rate control in AF/SVT; avoid in WPW with AF (can accelerate conduction via accessory pathway) |
| Other | Adenosine | ↑ K efflux, ↓ AV conduction | Drug of choice for **acute termination of SVT**; very short half-life (~10 sec), transient flushing/chest discomfort/asystole sensation |
| Other | Digoxin, Magnesium | — | Digoxin for rate control in AF; Mg for torsades de pointes |

### Amiodarone Toxicity (classic exam list)
- Pulmonary fibrosis (most serious)
- Hepatotoxicity
- Thyroid dysfunction — both hypo- and hyperthyroidism (high iodine content)
- Corneal microdeposits, blue-gray skin discoloration (photosensitivity)
- Bradycardia, QT prolongation (but paradoxically low risk of torsades compared to other class III agents)

## Lipid-Lowering Drugs
| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Key effect/toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statins | Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin | HMG-CoA reductase inhibition | ↓↓ LDL; myopathy/rhabdomyolysis, hepatotoxicity; first-line for ASCVD risk reduction |
| Fibrates | Fenofibrate, Gemfibrozil | PPAR-α activation | ↓↓ Triglycerides; used in severe hypertriglyceridemia; myopathy risk ↑ with statin combination |
| Bile acid sequestrants | Cholestyramine | ↓ bile acid reabsorption → ↑ LDL receptor expression | ↓ LDL; GI upset, ↓ absorption of fat-soluble vitamins |
| Niacin | — | ↓ VLDL synthesis | ↑ HDL (best agent for this); flushing (prostaglandin-mediated, prevented by aspirin pretreatment) |
| Ezetimibe | — | ↓ intestinal cholesterol absorption (NPC1L1) | Add-on to statin |
| PCSK9 inhibitors | Evolocumab, Alirocumab | ↑ LDL receptor recycling | Potent LDL lowering; injectable, used in familial hypercholesterolemia/statin-intolerant |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Methyldopa = antihypertensive of choice in pregnancy; ACEI/ARB contraindicated (teratogenic)
- Nitrates + sildenafil (PDE5 inhibitors) = contraindicated (severe hypotension)
- Digoxin toxicity worsened by hypokalemia; treat with digoxin-specific antibody
- Adenosine = drug of choice for acute SVT termination; ultra-short half-life
- Amiodarone toxicity: pulmonary fibrosis, thyroid dysfunction, hepatotoxicity, corneal deposits
- Beta-blockers avoided in Prinzmetal angina and in decompensated acute heart failure
- Verapamil/Diltiazem contraindicated in AF with WPW syndrome
- Statins = first-line lipid-lowering agents for ASCVD risk reduction; niacin best raises HDL, fibrates best lower triglycerides
- SGLT2 inhibitors now part of standard quadruple therapy in HFrEF (verify latest heart failure guideline positioning)
