# Antidepressants Anxiolytics

## Antidepressants

### SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors) — first-line for most depression/anxiety disorders
| Drug | Notable feature |
|---|---|
| Fluoxetine | Longest half-life (weeks-long active metabolite) — low discontinuation syndrome risk; activating (can cause insomnia/agitation) |
| Sertraline | Preferred in cardiac disease/post-MI depression; safest in pregnancy among SSRIs (relatively) |
| Paroxetine | Most anticholinergic/sedating of SSRIs; highest discontinuation syndrome risk (short half-life); avoid in pregnancy (category D-like teratogenic concern — cardiac defects) |
| Citalopram/Escitalopram | Dose-dependent **QT prolongation** — caution at high doses |
| Fluvoxamine | Used in OCD; strong CYP1A2/3A4 inhibitor |

**Class-wide adverse effects**: GI upset, sexual dysfunction (most common reason for discontinuation), initial anxiety/agitation, **hyponatremia (SIADH)** especially in elderly, ↑ bleeding risk (impaired platelet serotonin uptake), **serotonin syndrome** if combined with other serotonergic drugs, discontinuation syndrome if stopped abruptly.

### SNRIs
| Drug | Feature |
|---|---|
| Venlafaxine, Desvenlafaxine | Dose-dependent hypertension (at higher doses); also used in neuropathic pain, generalized anxiety disorder |
| Duloxetine | Used in diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia, stress urinary incontinence, depression/anxiety |

### TCAs (Tricyclic Antidepressants) — largely second-line now due to toxicity
| Drug | Feature |
|---|---|
| Amitriptyline, Imipramine, Nortriptyline | Block NE + serotonin reuptake; also anticholinergic, antihistaminic, alpha-blocking — hence broad side effect profile: sedation, weight gain, orthostatic hypotension, anticholinergic effects, **cardiotoxicity (QT prolongation, arrhythmia) — dangerous in overdose (quinidine-like effect, widened QRS)** |
| Clomipramine | Most serotonergic TCA — used in OCD |

*High-yield: TCA overdose is a classic toxicology emergency — triad of coma, seizures, cardiac arrhythmia (wide QRS); treat with IV sodium bicarbonate (alkalinization overcomes Na-channel blockade).*

### MAOIs — rarely first-line, historic importance
| Drug | Feature |
|---|---|
| Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine (non-selective, irreversible) | **Tyramine ("cheese") reaction** — hypertensive crisis with tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, cured meats, wine) due to unregulated NE release; serotonin syndrome risk with SSRIs/other serotonergic drugs — require 2-week washout switching to/from SSRIs (5 weeks after fluoxetine specifically, due to long half-life) |
| Selegiline (selective MAO-B) | Used in Parkinson's disease; lower tyramine reaction risk at standard doses |

### Atypical Antidepressants
| Drug | Feature |
|---|---|
| Mirtazapine | α2 antagonist + 5HT2/5HT3 blockade → sedation, weight gain (useful in depression with insomnia/anorexia, e.g., elderly/cancer patients); minimal sexual dysfunction |
| Bupropion | NE-dopamine reuptake inhibitor; used for depression and smoking cessation; **lowers seizure threshold** (contraindicated in seizure disorder/eating disorders); no sexual dysfunction — favored when this is a concern |
| Trazodone | Sedating, used mainly for insomnia (low-dose); **priapism** — rare but classic toxicity |

## Serotonin Syndrome vs Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (classic differentiator)
| Feature | Serotonin Syndrome | NMS |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Rapid (hours) | Slow (days) |
| Cause | Serotonergic drug combination (SSRI+MAOI, SSRI+tramadol, etc.) | Antipsychotics (D2 blockade) |
| Exam findings | **Hyperreflexia, clonus, tremor, myoclonus** | **Lead-pipe rigidity**, hyporeflexia |
| Treatment | Stop drug, supportive, **cyproheptadine** (5HT antagonist) | Stop drug, dantrolene, bromocriptine |

## Anxiolytics/Sedative-Hypnotics

### Benzodiazepines
| Drug | Half-life | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Diazepam, Chlordiazepoxide | Long-acting | Alcohol withdrawal (taper), status epilepticus (diazepam) |
| Lorazepam, Alprazolam | Short-intermediate | Preferred in hepatic impairment (lorazepam undergoes only glucuronidation, no oxidative metabolism — LOT drugs: Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam safe in liver disease) |
| Midazolam | Very short | Procedural sedation, induction |

- Mechanism: ↑ frequency of GABA-A chloride channel opening (potentiates GABA effect; does not act without GABA present — safer in overdose than barbiturates)
- **Flumazenil** = benzodiazepine antagonist/antidote; risk of precipitating seizures in chronic benzo users or mixed OD
- Adverse effects: sedation, dependence, tolerance, respiratory depression (esp. IV/combined with opioids), anterograde amnesia

### Barbiturates
- Mechanism: ↑ duration of GABA-A channel opening; at high doses can open channel directly (GABA-independent) — explains greater overdose lethality (respiratory depression) vs benzodiazepines
- Phenobarbital — used in neonatal seizures, refractory status epilepticus
- Strong enzyme inducer; low therapeutic index; no specific antidote (supportive care, urinary alkalinization for phenobarbital specifically, as it's a weak acid)

### Non-benzodiazepine hypnotics ("Z-drugs")
- Zolpidem, Zaleplon, Zopiclone — act on BZ1 (omega-1) subunit of GABA-A receptor; shorter-acting, lower dependence risk than benzodiazepines, but still risk of complex sleep behaviors (sleep-walking/driving) and dependence with prolonged use

### Buspirone
- 5HT1A partial agonist; anxiolytic without sedation/dependence/abuse potential; delayed onset (weeks) — not useful for acute anxiety, only chronic GAD

## High-Yield One-Liners
- SSRIs = first-line for most depression/anxiety disorders; sexual dysfunction is the most common reason for discontinuation
- Fluoxetine has the longest half-life among SSRIs; paroxetine has the highest discontinuation syndrome risk
- TCA overdose → wide QRS, arrhythmia, seizures; treat with IV sodium bicarbonate
- MAOIs + tyramine-rich food → hypertensive crisis; MAOIs + SSRIs → serotonin syndrome
- Serotonin syndrome = hyperreflexia/clonus (fast onset); NMS = rigidity/hyporeflexia (slow onset)
- Cyproheptadine is used to treat serotonin syndrome
- Lorazepam, Oxazepam, Temazepam ("LOT") are the safest benzodiazepines in hepatic impairment
- Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepine overdose but can precipitate seizures in dependent patients
- Bupropion lowers seizure threshold; avoided in seizure disorders and eating disorders
- Buspirone has delayed onset — not useful for acute/situational anxiety
