# Endocrine Pharmacology Diabetes Thyroid

## Diabetes Mellitus Drugs

### Insulin
| Type | Onset | Peak | Duration | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rapid-acting (Lispro, Aspart, Glulisine) | 10–15 min | 1–2 h | 3–5 h | Prandial control, insulin pumps |
| Short-acting (Regular) | 30 min | 2–4 h | 6–8 h | IV use in DKA (only insulin type given IV) |
| Intermediate (NPH) | 1–2 h | 4–10 h | 12–18 h | Basal coverage |
| Long-acting (Glargine, Detemir) | 1–2 h | Minimal peak (flat profile) | 20–24 h | Basal coverage, lower nocturnal hypoglycemia risk than NPH |
| Ultra-long (Degludec) | — | Flat | >40 h | Basal, flexible dosing |

- **Adverse effects**: Hypoglycemia (most important), weight gain, lipodystrophy at injection sites (rotate sites), rarely allergic reactions
- **Somogyi effect**: nocturnal hypoglycemia → rebound morning hyperglycemia (counter-regulatory hormones) — managed by decreasing evening dose
- **Dawn phenomenon**: early morning hyperglycemia due to nocturnal growth hormone/cortisol surge — managed by increasing/adjusting evening dose or timing

### Oral Hypoglycemics
| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Key notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biguanides | **Metformin** | ↓ hepatic gluconeogenesis, ↑ peripheral insulin sensitivity | First-line for T2DM (no hypoglycemia risk, weight neutral/loss, cardiovascular benefit); **lactic acidosis** risk (avoid in renal impairment, contrast studies — hold before/after iodinated contrast); B12 deficiency (long-term) |
| Sulfonylureas | Glimepiride, Glipizide, Glyburide | ↑ insulin secretion (block K-ATP channel on beta cells) | Hypoglycemia risk (esp. glyburide, long-acting), weight gain; glimepiride/glipizide safer in mild renal impairment |
| Meglitinides | Repaglinide, Nateglinide | Similar to sulfonylureas, shorter acting | Postprandial control, lower hypoglycemia risk than sulfonylureas |
| Thiazolidinediones | Pioglitazone | PPAR-γ agonist → ↑ insulin sensitivity | Fluid retention/edema (avoid in heart failure), weight gain, bladder cancer risk (pioglitazone — some controversy), bone fracture risk |
| DPP-4 inhibitors | Sitagliptin, Vildagliptin | ↑ incretin (GLP-1/GIP) levels | Weight neutral, low hypoglycemia risk; generally well tolerated; pancreatitis risk (rare) |
| GLP-1 agonists | Liraglutide, Semaglutide, Dulaglutide | Incretin mimetic — ↑ insulin, ↓ glucagon, ↓ gastric emptying | Weight loss (significant), cardiovascular benefit shown; GI side effects (nausea), pancreatitis risk, contraindicated in personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma/MEN2 |
| SGLT2 inhibitors | Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin, Canagliflozin | ↓ renal glucose reabsorption (proximal tubule) | Weight loss, cardiovascular/renal protective benefit (heart failure, CKD); **genital mycotic infections, UTI**, euglycemic DKA risk, Fournier's gangrene (rare) |
| Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors | Acarbose | ↓ intestinal carbohydrate absorption | Flatulence, GI upset; used for postprandial control |

*High-yield: Metformin is first-line for T2DM per current guidelines; SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists are increasingly positioned early/as add-ons in patients with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or CKD — verify latest ADA/national guideline for exact algorithm positioning as this has evolved.*

### DKA Management (high-yield acute scenario)
- IV fluids (isotonic saline) first, then IV regular insulin infusion (only IV insulin form), correct potassium (insulin drives K into cells — replace K before/with insulin if K is low-normal), monitor for cerebral edema (esp. in children with rapid correction)

## Thyroid Drugs

### Hypothyroidism
- **Levothyroxine (T4)** — drug of choice; long half-life (~7 days) allows once-daily dosing; take empty stomach, avoid calcium/iron/PPI co-administration (↓ absorption)
- Liothyronine (T3) — faster onset, used in myxedema coma (with or after T4)

### Hyperthyroidism
| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Methimazole (Carbimazole)** | Inhibits thyroid peroxidase (↓ hormone synthesis) | First-line in most non-pregnant patients; **teratogenic (aplasia cutis) — avoid in first trimester** |
| **Propylthiouracil (PTU)** | Same + inhibits peripheral T4→T3 conversion | Preferred in **first trimester of pregnancy** and **thyroid storm** (blocks peripheral conversion); **hepatotoxicity risk (black box) — reserve for short-term/specific indications** |
| Radioactive Iodine (I-131) | Destroys thyroid tissue | Definitive treatment for Graves'; contraindicated in pregnancy/breastfeeding; can worsen ophthalmopathy |
| Beta-blockers (Propranolol) | Symptom control (tachycardia, tremor) + ↓ peripheral T4→T3 conversion | First-line symptomatic control, especially useful in thyroid storm |
| Iodine (Lugol's solution/SSKI) | Wolff-Chaikoff effect — transiently ↓ hormone release | Used pre-operatively (thyroidectomy) and in thyroid storm |

*High-yield: Both methimazole and PTU can cause agranulocytosis (rare but classic — presents with sore throat/fever, requires immediate drug discontinuation).*

### Thyroid Storm Management (classic exam sequence)
1. **Beta-blocker** (propranolol) — symptom control + ↓ conversion
2. **PTU** (preferred over methimazole — blocks peripheral conversion too)
3. **Iodine solution** (given after PTU, at least 1 hour later, to prevent using iodine as substrate for new hormone synthesis)
4. Supportive: cooling, fluids, treat precipitating cause
5. Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone) — also ↓ conversion, treats possible relative adrenal insufficiency

## Adrenal/Corticosteroid Pharmacology
| Drug | Relative glucocorticoid potency | Relative mineralocorticoid potency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrocortisone | 1 | 1 | Physiologic replacement (Addison's) |
| Prednisolone | 4 | 0.8 | Common oral steroid |
| Methylprednisolone | 5 | 0.5 | IV pulse therapy |
| Dexamethasone | 25–30 | ~0 | No mineralocorticoid effect — used when fluid retention undesirable (cerebral edema, fetal lung maturity) |
| Fludrocortisone | 10 | 125 | Pure mineralocorticoid use (Addison's replacement, orthostatic hypotension) |

- **Adverse effects of long-term steroid use**: HPA axis suppression (taper, don't stop abruptly), osteoporosis, hyperglycemia, cushingoid features, immunosuppression/infection risk, peptic ulcer, cataracts/glaucoma, avascular necrosis (esp. femoral head), growth retardation in children
- **Antenatal corticosteroids** (betamethasone/dexamethasone) — accelerate fetal lung maturity in preterm labor (24–34 weeks per standard obstetric practice — verify exact gestational window against current guideline)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Metformin = first-line for T2DM; contraindicated in renal impairment (lactic acidosis risk) and held around iodinated contrast studies
- Sulfonylureas carry the highest hypoglycemia risk among oral agents
- SGLT2 inhibitors cause genital mycotic infections and can rarely precipitate euglycemic DKA
- GLP-1 agonists are contraindicated with personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma/MEN2
- PTU is preferred over methimazole in the first trimester of pregnancy and in thyroid storm
- Methimazole is teratogenic (aplasia cutis) — avoid in first trimester
- Thyroid storm management order: beta-blocker → PTU → iodine (at least 1 hour after PTU) → steroids
- Dexamethasone has essentially no mineralocorticoid activity, unlike hydrocortisone
- Levothyroxine should be taken on an empty stomach, separate from calcium/iron supplements
