# Antiparkinsonian Drugs

## Overview
Parkinson's disease results from **degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta**, causing a relative dopamine deficiency and unopposed cholinergic activity in the striatum. Treatment aims to restore dopaminergic tone or reduce cholinergic excess.

## Levodopa-Carbidopa (Gold Standard)
- **Levodopa**: dopamine precursor, crosses BBB (dopamine itself cannot); converted to dopamine by dopa-decarboxylase both peripherally and centrally
- **Carbidopa**: peripheral dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor (does NOT cross BBB) — combined with levodopa to:
  - ↓ peripheral conversion → ↓ peripheral side effects (nausea, vomiting, hypotension)
  - ↑ fraction of levodopa reaching CNS → allows lower dose
- Most effective drug for bradykinesia/rigidity; least effective for tremor (compared to anticholinergics)

### Levodopa Adverse Effects
- **On-off phenomenon / wearing-off** with long-term use (motor fluctuations)
- **Dyskinesias** (choreoathetoid movements) — dose-related, long-term complication
- Peripheral: nausea, postural hypotension, arrhythmia
- Psychiatric: hallucinations, psychosis, impulse control disorders
- Contraindicated with non-selective MAOIs (hypertensive crisis risk)
- Avoid abrupt withdrawal — risk of **neuroleptic malignant syndrome-like state**

*High-yield: Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) increases peripheral breakdown of levodopa (cofactor for dopa-decarboxylase) — reduces efficacy if carbidopa not co-administered. Always given as combination product now, so less practically relevant but classic exam fact.*

## Dopamine Agonists
| Drug | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bromocriptine, Pergolide | Ergot-derived | Cardiac valvulopathy, pulmonary/retroperitoneal fibrosis (ergot-related) — largely replaced by non-ergot agents |
| Pramipexole, Ropinirole | Non-ergot | Preferred in younger patients / early disease to delay levodopa-related dyskinesias; risk of impulse control disorders (pathological gambling, hypersexuality), sudden sleep attacks |
| Apomorphine | Rescue therapy for "off" episodes | Injectable |

## MAO-B Inhibitors
- **Selegiline, Rasagiline** — selectively inhibit MAO-B (dopamine metabolism in CNS), sparing peripheral MAO-A at standard doses → low tyramine reaction risk
- Used as monotherapy in early disease or adjunct to levodopa (reduces "off" time)
- Selegiline metabolized to amphetamine-like compounds — insomnia

## COMT Inhibitors
- **Entacapone, Tolcapone** — inhibit catechol-O-methyltransferase, blocking peripheral levodopa breakdown → prolongs levodopa action, reduces "off" time
- Tolcapone: hepatotoxicity risk (requires liver monitoring); entacapone safer, more commonly used
- Urine discoloration (orange-brown) — benign

## Anticholinergics
- **Trihexyphenidyl, Benzhexol** — block excess cholinergic activity (relative to dopamine deficiency)
- Most useful for **tremor** (less effect on bradykinesia/rigidity)
- Preferred in younger patients with tremor-dominant disease and in **drug-induced parkinsonism** (e.g., antipsychotic-induced)
- Avoid in elderly (confusion, urinary retention, worsens cognition) and in narrow-angle glaucoma, BPH

## Amantadine
- Mechanism: ↑ dopamine release, blocks NMDA receptors (antiviral drug repurposed)
- Used for mild PD symptoms and specifically for **levodopa-induced dyskinesias**
- Adverse effect: livedo reticularis, ankle edema, confusion (especially elderly)

## Drug Selection Summary
| Scenario | Preferred drug |
|---|---|
| Young patient, early/mild disease | MAO-B inhibitor or dopamine agonist (delay levodopa) |
| Older patient / significant disability | Levodopa-carbidopa (most effective) |
| Tremor-predominant | Anticholinergics |
| Drug-induced parkinsonism | Anticholinergics; stop offending antipsychotic if possible |
| Levodopa-induced dyskinesia | Amantadine; adjust levodopa dosing/schedule |
| Motor fluctuations ("wearing off") on levodopa | Add COMT inhibitor or MAO-B inhibitor, or dopamine agonist |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Levodopa is always combined with carbidopa to reduce peripheral side effects and increase CNS bioavailability
- Levodopa is most effective for bradykinesia/rigidity; anticholinergics are best for tremor
- Long-term levodopa use → motor fluctuations (on-off) and dyskinesias
- Amantadine specifically helps levodopa-induced dyskinesia
- Dopamine agonists carry higher risk of impulse control disorders (gambling, hypersexuality) than levodopa
- Ergot dopamine agonists (bromocriptine) risk cardiac valvulopathy/fibrosis — non-ergot agents preferred
- Anticholinergics are drug of choice in drug-induced (antipsychotic) parkinsonism, not levodopa
- Selegiline/Rasagiline (MAO-B selective) have low tyramine reaction risk at standard doses, unlike non-selective MAOIs
