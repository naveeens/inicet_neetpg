# Antifungal Antiviral Drugs

## Antifungal Drugs

### Polyenes
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amphotericin B** | Binds ergosterol → forms pores in fungal membrane | Broad-spectrum systemic mycoses (cryptococcal meningitis, invasive aspergillosis/candidiasis, mucormycosis) — gold standard for severe systemic fungal infection | **Nephrotoxicity** (classic — renal tubular acidosis, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia), infusion reaction (fever, chills — "shake and bake"), anemia (↓ EPO); liposomal formulation reduces nephrotoxicity/infusion reactions |
| Nystatin | Same mechanism, not absorbed orally | Topical/oral candidiasis (oral thrush, "swish and swallow") — too toxic systemically | Minimal (topical use only) |

### Azoles
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluconazole | Inhibits fungal CYP450 (14-α-demethylase) → ↓ ergosterol synthesis | Candidiasis (esp. good CNS/urine penetration — cryptococcal meningitis maintenance), no activity against Aspergillus | Hepatotoxicity, CYP inhibitor (drug interactions) |
| Itraconazole | Same mechanism | Histoplasmosis, blastomycosis, dermatophytes | Negative inotrope (avoid in heart failure), hepatotoxicity |
| Voriconazole | Same mechanism | Invasive aspergillosis — drug of choice | Visual disturbances (transient), hepatotoxicity, photosensitivity |
| Ketoconazole | Same mechanism, older | Largely replaced by newer azoles due to toxicity | **Strongest CYP450 inhibitor**, inhibits steroidogenesis (used off-label for Cushing's/hirsutism for this reason), gynecomastia (antiandrogenic), hepatotoxicity |

*High-yield: All azoles inhibit fungal (and to variable extent human) CYP450 — major drug interaction potential; ketoconazole is the most potent inhibitor of human steroidogenic enzymes among them.*

### Echinocandins
- **Caspofungin, Micafungin, Anidulafungin** — inhibit β-1,3-glucan synthase (fungal cell wall synthesis)
- Used for invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis (alternative); minimal toxicity (well-tolerated), no significant CYP interaction — favored in critically ill/multi-drug patients

### Others
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Griseofulvin | Disrupts microtubule function | Dermatophyte infections (tinea) of skin/hair/nails — NOT active against Candida | Enzyme inducer, hepatotoxicity, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol, teratogenic |
| Terbinafine | Inhibits squalene epoxidase | Dermatophytoses, onychomycosis (nail infections) — oral, first-line for nail | Hepatotoxicity, taste disturbance |
| Flucytosine | Inhibits fungal DNA/RNA synthesis (converted to 5-FU intracellularly) | Combined with amphotericin B for cryptococcal meningitis (synergy) | Bone marrow suppression |

## Antiviral Drugs

### Anti-Herpes (HSV/VZV/CMV)
| Drug | Mechanism | Use | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acyclovir, Valacyclovir, Famciclovir | Guanosine analog; activated by viral thymidine kinase → inhibits viral DNA polymerase | HSV, VZV (herpes zoster, genital herpes, encephalitis) | Nephrotoxicity (crystalline nephropathy — ensure hydration), neurotoxicity at high doses |
| Ganciclovir, Valganciclovir | Similar mechanism, activated differently | CMV retinitis/disease (esp. in immunocompromised/transplant patients) | Bone marrow suppression (neutropenia) — dose-limiting |
| Foscarnet | Direct viral DNA polymerase inhibition (no activation needed) | CMV/HSV resistant to acyclovir/ganciclovir | Nephrotoxicity, electrolyte disturbances (hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia) |
| Cidofovir | Similar class | CMV retinitis, alternative agent | Nephrotoxicity (co-administer probenecid + hydration) |

### Anti-Influenza
- **Oseltamivir, Zanamivir**: neuraminidase inhibitors — prevent viral release from infected cells; most effective if started within 48 hours of symptom onset
- Amantadine/Rimantadine: M2 ion channel blockers — largely obsolete due to widespread resistance (historic exam relevance for mechanism)

### Antiretrovirals (HIV) — Classes
| Class | Drugs | Mechanism | Key toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| NRTIs | Zidovudine (AZT), Tenofovir, Lamivudine, Abacavir | Chain terminators (need phosphorylation) | Zidovudine — bone marrow suppression (anemia); Tenofovir — nephrotoxicity/↓ bone density; Abacavir — hypersensitivity reaction (screen HLA-B*5701); all — lactic acidosis/mitochondrial toxicity (class effect, esp. older NRTIs) |
| NNRTIs | Efavirenz, Nevirapine | Non-competitive reverse transcriptase inhibition | Efavirenz — CNS effects (vivid dreams, psychiatric symptoms), teratogenic (historic caution — verify current guidance); Nevirapine — hepatotoxicity, Stevens-Johnson syndrome |
| Protease Inhibitors | Ritonavir, Lopinavir, Atazanavir | Inhibit viral protease (blocks maturation) | Metabolic syndrome (lipodystrophy, insulin resistance, dyslipidemia), strong CYP3A4 inhibition (ritonavir used as "booster" for this reason) |
| Integrase Inhibitors | Dolutegravir, Raltegravir, Bictegravir | Block HIV integrase | Generally well-tolerated; now part of preferred first-line regimens per current guidelines |
| Entry/Fusion Inhibitors | Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist), Enfuvirtide | Block viral entry | Rarely used, reserved for resistant cases |

*High-yield: Standard first-line ART is typically an integrase inhibitor-based regimen (e.g., dolutegravir + 2 NRTIs) per current WHO/national guidelines — verify latest guideline for exact preferred combination, as this has evolved over recent years.*

### Hepatitis Therapy
| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| Tenofovir, Entecavir | Chronic Hepatitis B |
| Sofosbuvir + Velpatasvir/Ledipasvir (direct-acting antivirals) | Chronic Hepatitis C — near-universal cure rates; largely replaced interferon-based regimens |
| Pegylated Interferon-alpha + Ribavirin | Older Hepatitis C regimen (mostly historic now), still used in select Hepatitis B/D scenarios |

*High-yield: Ribavirin is teratogenic and causes hemolytic anemia — contraindicated in pregnancy; requires effective contraception in both partners during and after treatment.*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Amphotericin B toxicity: nephrotoxicity with hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia, and infusion-related "shake and bake" reaction
- Voriconazole = drug of choice for invasive aspergillosis; fluconazole has no activity against Aspergillus
- Flucytosine + amphotericin B = synergistic combination for cryptococcal meningitis
- Griseofulvin only works on dermatophytes, not Candida; causes disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol
- Acyclovir toxicity is crystalline nephropathy — prevent with adequate hydration
- Ganciclovir's dose-limiting toxicity is bone marrow suppression (neutropenia)
- Abacavir requires HLA-B*5701 screening before use due to hypersensitivity reaction risk
- Zidovudine causes bone marrow suppression/anemia; Tenofovir causes nephrotoxicity
- Ritonavir is used at low ("booster") doses to inhibit CYP3A4 and increase levels of other protease inhibitors
- Ribavirin is teratogenic and hemolytic — strict contraception required during therapy
