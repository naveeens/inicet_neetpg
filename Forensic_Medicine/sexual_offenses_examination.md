# Sexual Offenses Examination

## Definitions and Legal Framework
- **Rape**: defined under **IPC Section 375** (as amended by the Criminal Law Amendment Act, 2013, and 2018) — the classic textbook/exam-bank definition; punishment under **IPC Section 376** and its sub-sections (376A–376E for aggravated forms — death causing, repeat offenders, gang rape, custodial rape, rape of a minor, etc.).
- **Current law**: IPC has been replaced by the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023**, effective 1 July 2024 — rape and its punishment are commonly cited around **BNS Sections 63 and 64**, but **verify the exact current BNS section number** before quoting it as settled, since this is a recent renumbering.
- **POCSO Act, 2012** (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences): special law for all sexual offences against persons under 18 years, defines penetrative/non-penetrative sexual assault, aggravated forms, and mandates child-friendly examination/procedure — takes precedence over general rape law for minors.
- **Consent** for the purposes of rape law must be free, voluntary, and given by a person capable of understanding the nature/consequences of the act; a statutory age floor of 18 years applies — consent below this age is not a valid defense regardless of apparent willingness.

## Role of the Examining Doctor
- The doctor's role is to **document findings objectively** (history, general and local examination findings, injuries, samples collected) — the legal conclusion of "whether rape occurred" is a matter for the court, not a medical opinion the doctor should render.
- **Consent of the survivor is mandatory** before examination — a doctor cannot examine without consent even at police request; for a minor, consent of guardian plus, where feasible, assent of the child.
- Examination should be conducted **without delay** (ideally within 24 hours for best yield of trace evidence), but must **not be refused** solely because of delay — findings and evidence collection (fibers, hair, DNA) may still be valuable **up to about 96 hours (variable by protocol/local guidelines)**, and examination/treatment (STI prophylaxis, emergency contraception, injury documentation) should be offered even beyond that window.

## Steps of Examination
1. **History**: detailed account of the incident (time, place, nature of act, use of force/weapon/restraint, loss of consciousness, alcohol/drug involvement, activities since the assault — bathing, changing clothes, urination/defecation, which affect evidence yield).
2. **General examination**: mental state, signs of restraint/struggle (bruises, abrasions, ligature marks), state of clothing, evidence of intoxication.
3. **Local (genital) examination**: 
   - Inspection of external genitalia, perineum, and anus for injury (tears, bruising, abrasions), swelling, bleeding.
   - **Hymen status is NOT diagnostic of virginity or of whether penetration occurred** — the hymen can remain intact despite penetration (elastic, various natural shapes), and can be torn by non-sexual causes; describe findings objectively (shape, tears with time-since-injury if possible) rather than in terms of "virgin/non-virgin," which are outdated and legally disfavored terms.
   - The **"two-finger test"** (assessing vaginal laxity to infer prior sexual activity) has been **explicitly declared unconstitutional/impermissible by the Supreme Court of India** (*Lillu @ Rajesh & Anr. v. State of Haryana*, 2013) — it is medically unscientific and must not be performed or referenced in a medico-legal report.
4. **Sample collection** (chain of custody critical): vaginal/cervical/anal/oral swabs for spermatozoa and seminal fluid, swabs/scrapings for DNA, victim's and suspect's clothing, hair/fiber evidence, blood/urine for alcohol/drug screening (especially in suspected drug-facilitated assault).
5. **Documentation**: body diagrams marking injuries, photography where feasible (with consent), completion of a medico-legal certificate (MLC) recording only objective findings.

## Detection of Semen/Spermatozoa
- Microscopic identification of spermatozoa on swabs/smears is the most direct evidence of recent sexual contact — but **absence of spermatozoa does not rule out sexual assault** (possible causes: assailant was azoospermic/vasectomized, used a condom, ejaculation did not occur, or delay in examination allowed degradation).
- Acid phosphatase and prostate-specific antigen (PSA/p30) testing on swabs supports semen identification when sperm are not found (e.g., in azoospermic individuals).

## Examination of the Accused
- Conducted under legal provision (classic **IPC/CrPC Section 53A, CrPC** for medical examination of a person accused of rape; now under the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, BNSS** — verify current section number).
- Findings recorded: any injury on the genitals suggestive of recent intercourse, presence of the victim's blood/hair/fibers on the accused's body or clothing, and swabs for DNA/semen — again, doctor documents findings, doesn't conclude guilt.
- **Potency vs capacity to consummate**: rare defense examination point — "impotence" (inability to have intercourse) is distinct from "sterility" (inability to procreate); sterility does not preclude the ability to commit rape/have intercourse, whereas true impotence may be medico-legally relevant.

## Age Estimation (relevant in POCSO/statutory rape cases where age is disputed)
- Ossification centers on X-ray (wrist, elbow, knee, iliac crest fusion) used to estimate skeletal age when documentary proof (birth certificate, school records) is unavailable or disputed.
- Dental age estimation (eruption pattern, radiographic root development) as an adjunct, especially in adolescents.
- Courts generally prefer documentary evidence of age over medical estimation when both are available; medical age estimation carries an inherent margin of error and should be reported as a range, not a fixed number.

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Doctors document objective findings only; the legal determination of "rape" is for the court, not the examining doctor.
- Examination requires the survivor's (or guardian's, for a minor) consent — it is never compulsory.
- Hymen status alone cannot confirm or exclude penetration or "virginity" — this terminology should be avoided in reports.
- The two-finger test is unconstitutional and medically invalid per the Supreme Court (*Lillu v. State of Haryana*, 2013) — must never be performed or cited.
- Absence of spermatozoa on examination does NOT rule out sexual assault.
- POCSO Act governs sexual offences against anyone under 18 years and overrides general rape law provisions for minors.
- Age of consent for sexual intercourse in India is 18 years, irrespective of the minor's apparent consent.
- IPC Sections 375/376 remain the classic textbook reference; current law is BNS (~Sections 63/64) — verify the exact BNS section before quoting it as current.
