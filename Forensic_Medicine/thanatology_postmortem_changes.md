# Thanatology Postmortem Changes

## Definitions
- **Somatic (clinical) death**: irreversible cessation of circulation, respiration, and brain function — the whole organism dies.
- **Molecular (cellular) death**: death of individual tissues/cells; occurs at different rates after somatic death (cornea/skin cells survive longest, hence used for grafting/donation shortly after death).
- **Brain death**: irreversible cessation of all brain stem function — legal definition of death for organ transplantation purposes (Transplantation of Human Organs Act).
- **Presumptive signs of death**: cessation of pulse/respiration, loss of reflexes — reversible, not conclusive.
- **Conclusive (certain) signs of death**: postmortem lividity, rigor mortis, putrefaction — irreversible.

## Classification of Postmortem Changes
| Category | Changes |
|---|---|
| Early/Immediate | Primary flaccidity, algor mortis (cooling), livor mortis (hypostasis), rigor mortis |
| Late | Putrefaction, adipocere formation, mummification, skeletonization |

## Algor Mortis (Postmortem Cooling)
- Body cools to match ambient temperature following (roughly) a sigmoid curve, not linear.
- Classic teaching: average fall of **~1.5°F (0.8°C) per hour** for first 12 hours, then slower — used only as a rough guide, not a precise PMI (postmortem interval) tool.
- Factors accelerating cooling: thin build, child/elderly, wet/exposed body, cold environment, moving air.
- Factors delaying cooling: obesity, thick clothing/bedding, fever/septicemia before death, hemorrhage into brain (hypothalamic damage), enclosed warm space.

## Livor Mortis (Postmortem Lividity / Hypostasis)
- Cause: gravitational pooling of blood in dependent capillaries/venules after circulation stops.
- **Onset**: 20 min – 3 h; **becomes well-marked**: 3–6 h; **"fixed" (does not shift on repositioning)**: by 6–12 h (up to 24 h — variable).
- Before fixation, lividity shifts with change in body position — useful to detect if a body was moved after death.
- Color: bluish-purple normally; **cherry-red/pink** in CO poisoning and cyanide; **brown** in nitrite/chlorate poisoning (methemoglobinemia); **greenish** with putrefaction.
- Does not develop over areas of contact/pressure (e.g., ground contact) — "pressure pallor."

## Rigor Mortis
- Cause: postmortem depletion of ATP → actin-myosin cross-bridges lock (not a chemical change like true muscle contraction).
- **Onset**: 1–2 h (small muscles of face/jaw first); becomes generalized/fully established by **~12 h**; **persists 24–48 h**; **passes off in the same order it appeared**, over the next 24–48 h — total cycle roughly 36–96 h (highly temperature/build dependent).
- **Nysten's Law**: rigor appears and disappears in a craniocaudal sequence — **face/jaw → neck → upper limbs → trunk → lower limbs** (some texts: face → neck → trunk → limbs).
- Small muscles (face, hands) stiffen first and pass off first; large muscles stiffen later and take longer to resolve.
- Accelerated by: high ambient temperature, strenuous exertion before death, high fever, electrocution, strychnine poisoning.
- Delayed/absent in: extreme cold, deep sepsis, very young/old, some poisonings (phosphorus).

### Cadaveric Spasm vs Rigor Mortis
| Feature | Cadaveric Spasm | Rigor Mortis |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Instantaneous, at the moment of death | Gradual, 1–2 h onward |
| Muscle group | One group only (usually hand clutching an object/weapon) | All muscles, generalized |
| Mechanism | Sudden depletion of ATP at moment of intense emotion/exertion | Gradual ATP depletion after death |
| Medico-legal value | Very high — proves the object was held voluntarily at time of death (rules out planting of weapon), suggests violent death/suicide | Only indicates time since death |

### Heat & Cold Stiffening (differentiate from true rigor)
- **Heat stiffening**: coagulation of muscle proteins on exposure to high heat (e.g., fire) — "pugilistic attitude" (flexed limbs, clenched fists), appears rapidly, muscles firm and cannot be overcome.
- **Cold stiffening**: freezing of subcutaneous fat/tissue fluid in extreme cold — joints stiff but "crackle"/cracking sensation on forcing, unlike true rigor.

## Putrefaction
- Bacterial (mainly *Clostridium welchii/perfringens*, coliforms) and autolytic decomposition of tissues.
- **First external sign**: greenish discoloration over the **right iliac fossa** (overlies cecum, highest bacterial load) — typically appears at **24–48 h** in temperate climate (faster in tropics, may be seen within 18–24 h in India).
- Sequence: greenish discoloration → **marbling** (dark branching pattern along superficial veins, from hemolysis + H2S–hemoglobin reaction, days 2–3) → bloating of face/abdomen/scrotum from putrefactive gases (days 3–5) → skin blistering and slippage, loosening of hair/nails → liquefaction.
- Body cavities distend with gas → bloating, protrusion of eyes/tongue, purging of blood-stained fluid from mouth/nostrils ("postmortem purge" — must not be mistaken for antemortem hemorrhage/injury).

## Adipocere and Mummification
| Change | Conditions favoring | Timeframe | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adipocere | Moist, warm, anaerobic (buried in wet soil, water) | Weeks to months | Preserves external body form/wounds — good for identification, delayed exams |
| Mummification | Hot, dry, well-ventilated environment | Weeks to months | Skin becomes dry, leathery, brown-black; preserves external features |
| Skeletonization | Any environment, prolonged interval, exposure/scavenging | Months to years (variable) | Only skeletal/dental identification possible |

## Estimation of Time Since Death (PMI) — Practical Approach
Combine multiple parameters (no single sign is precise): body temperature, degree/distribution of lividity and its fixation, extent of rigor, stage of putrefaction, stomach contents/degree of digestion, forensic entomology (fly egg-laying begins within hours; larval stage helps estimate PMI in decomposed bodies), vitreous humor potassium levels.

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Rigor mortis follows **Nysten's Law**: appears and disappears face → neck → upper limb → trunk → lower limb.
- Cadaveric spasm is instantaneous and involves only one muscle group — high medico-legal value (rules out weapon planting).
- First sign of putrefaction: greenish discoloration over the **right iliac fossa** (cecum).
- Cherry-red lividity → think **CO poisoning** or **cyanide**; brownish lividity → nitrite/chlorate (methemoglobinemia).
- Postmortem lividity fixes (stops shifting with position change) by about **6–12 hours**.
- Adipocere preserves body form (useful in bodies recovered from water/wet burial); mummification occurs in hot, dry conditions.
- "Postmortem purge" (fluid from mouth/nose during putrefaction) is a decomposition artifact, not evidence of antemortem injury.
