# Asphyxial Deaths

## Definition
Asphyxia = a state of impaired gas exchange leading to hypoxia and hypercapnia, ultimately causing death. Forensically classified by **mechanism** into hanging, strangulation (ligature/manual/mugging), suffocation (smothering, choking, entrapment/confined space), traumatic asphyxia (crush), and drowning.

## General Signs of Asphyxial Death
- **Cyanosis** of face, lips, nail beds
- **Petechial (Tardieu) hemorrhages** — subconjunctival, on face, and under serous membranes (pleura, pericardium, epicardium) — due to venous congestion + capillary rupture from raised venous pressure
- **Congestion** and **edema** of face (especially in strangulation/throttling with venous obstruction)
- **Fluidity of blood** in the right side of the heart/great vessels (classic but non-specific finding)
- **Froth** at mouth/nostrils (fine, white, persistent froth — more marked in drowning)

## Hanging vs Strangulation (Ligature) vs Throttling — Ligature Mark Comparison
| Feature | Hanging | Ligature strangulation | Throttling (manual) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level of mark | Above thyroid cartilage (upper neck) | At or below thyroid cartilage | Variable, often with nail marks/bruises rather than a linear mark |
| Direction | Oblique, rising toward the knot | Horizontal, encircling | Irregular finger-tip bruises/abrasions, crescentic nail marks |
| Continuity | Non-continuous — interrupted at the point of suspension (knot) | Continuous, complete circle around neck | Discontinuous, patchy |
| Depth | Deepest opposite the knot, fades toward it | Uniform depth all around | Variable, patterned by fingers |
| Hyoid/laryngeal fracture | Uncommon (soft tissues, less force through knot side) | More common if forceful | Common, especially hyoid bone/thyroid cartilage fracture |
| Petechiae | Often minimal (rapid vascular occlusion) | More marked | Very marked (prolonged struggle typical) |

## Hanging
- **Mechanism of death**: depends on completeness/position — (1) vascular occlusion of carotid/vertebral arteries and jugular veins (most common — only ~2 kg pressure needed to occlude jugular veins, ~5 kg for carotids), (2) vagal inhibition (reflex cardiac arrest from pressure on carotid sinus/vagus — sudden death with minimal signs), (3) asphyxia from airway occlusion (less common, requires more force/complete suspension).
- **Types**: 
  - *Complete hanging* — feet completely off the ground; *Incomplete hanging* — feet/some part of body touching ground/support (death can still occur — full body weight is not required).
  - *Typical* — knot at nape of neck, symmetrical; *Atypical* — knot elsewhere, asymmetrical mark.
- **Autopsy findings**: ligature mark (as above), subcutaneous ecchymosis beneath the mark, congestion above the ligature, dribbling of saliva from angle of mouth (antemortem sign, indicates the person was alive and upright while suspended), protruding tongue, sometimes fracture/dislocation of upper cervical vertebrae (**hangman's fracture** — fracture through pedicles of C2 — classically described in judicial/drop hanging, not typical in suicidal hanging without a significant drop).
- Most hangings encountered medico-legally are **suicidal**; homicidal hanging is rare (requires overpowering the victim first — look for other injuries/signs of restraint).

## Strangulation and Throttling
- **Ligature strangulation**: constricting force applied by a ligature tightened by a force other than the body's own weight (differentiates from hanging) — usually homicidal.
- **Throttling (manual strangulation)**: compression of the neck by hand(s) — always homicidal (cannot be self-inflicted beyond a certain point since unconsciousness relaxes the grip); look for fingertip bruises, crescentic nail abrasions, and fractured hyoid bone/thyroid cornu.
- **Mugging**: pressure on the neck from behind by the forearm (choke-hold) — often no external marks; internal neck structures may still show injury.
- **Bansdola**: a stick/pole placed across the throat, one person pressing each end (occasionally seen in India).

## Suffocation
- **Smothering**: mechanical obstruction of external airways (nose/mouth) — e.g., pillow, hand, plastic bag. May be accidental (overlay of infant — "overlaying"), homicidal, or rarely suicidal.
- **Choking (gagging)**: obstruction within the internal air passage — foreign body, bolus of food impacted in larynx/trachea (café coronary), blood/vomitus aspiration.
- **Traumatic asphyxia (crush asphyxia)**: fixation/crushing of the chest preventing respiratory movements (e.g., crowd crush, vehicle accident) — classic finding: **"masque ecchymotique"** (Perthes syndrome) — cyanosis, edema, and petechiae confined to the head/neck/upper chest above the level of compression.
- **Confined space/entrapment**: exhaustion of oxygen or accumulation of CO2 in an enclosed space.

## Drowning
- **Mechanism**: aspiration of fluid causing asphyxia; also laryngospasm, vagal-mediated cardiac arrest ("dry drowning"), and electrolyte/hemodilution effects (mainly experimental/less relevant to humans).
- **Findings suggestive of antemortem (true) drowning**:
  - Fine, white, persistent **froth cone** at mouth/nostrils
  - **Cutis anserina** ("goose flesh" — cold water + cadaveric spasm of arrector pili, not exclusive to drowning)
  - **Washerwoman's skin** — wrinkling/bleaching of skin of palms/soles from prolonged water immersion (a time-in-water sign, not proof of drowning per se)
  - Water/froth/debris (weeds, sand, diatoms) in air passages, stomach, and lungs
  - **Diatom test** — demonstration of diatoms (from the water source) in bone marrow, kidney, or other organs beyond the lung filter — supports antemortem drowning (diatoms reach systemic circulation only if the heart was still beating), though its reliability/specificity is debated
  - **Paltauf's hemorrhages** — large, pale, subpleural hemorrhages from overdistended, ruptured alveoli
  - Overinflated, waterlogged lungs ("emphysema aquosum")
- **Postmortem submersion** (body thrown into water after death by another cause): absence of the above signs, presence of ligature mark/injuries unrelated to drowning, no froth/diatoms in deep organs.
- **Time in water estimation**: cutis anserina, maceration/skin slippage of hands and feet ("washerwoman's hands"), adipocere formation with prolonged immersion — used as a rough guide, not exact.

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Petechial hemorrhages (Tardieu spots) are a general sign of all asphyxial deaths, most marked in throttling/strangulation.
- Hanging ligature mark: above thyroid cartilage, oblique, non-continuous (interrupted at the knot).
- Ligature strangulation mark: at/below thyroid cartilage, horizontal, continuous all around.
- Throttling is always homicidal; suicidal hanging is the most common form of hanging encountered medico-legally.
- Hyoid bone fracture is more typical of throttling/strangulation than of hanging.
- Masque ecchymotique (facial/upper-chest cyanosis + petechiae) = traumatic (crush) asphyxia.
- Diatom test and froth cone support antemortem drowning; their absence with other injuries suggests postmortem submersion.
- Café coronary = sudden death from a food bolus impacting the larynx, mimicking cardiac death.
