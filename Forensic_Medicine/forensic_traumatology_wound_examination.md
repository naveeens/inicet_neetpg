# Forensic Traumatology Wound Examination

## Definitions
- **Injury/Wound**: any harm illegally caused to a person's body, mind, reputation, or property (broad legal definition, IPC Section 44 → BNS equivalent, verify current numbering).
- Medico-legally, wounds are described in terms of **type, site, size/shape/direction, margins, depth, surrounding area, and age** — this description (not a diagnosis label) is what should go into the medico-legal certificate/report.

## Classification of Mechanical Injuries
| Type | Cause | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Abrasion** | Friction/blunt tangential force against a rough surface | Superficial, involves only epidermis; may show direction of force (heaping of epidermis at far end); types — scratch, graze, pressure, imprint (patterned) abrasion |
| **Contusion (bruise)** | Blunt force rupturing subcutaneous vessels without breaking skin | Extravasation of blood into tissue; may appear at a site distant from impact (tracking along fascial planes) — "contusion may not be under the site of impact" |
| **Laceration** | Blunt force exceeding tissue elasticity — tearing | Irregular, ragged edges, tissue bridging (strands of tissue/vessels crossing the wound gap) — hallmark distinguishing from incised wound |
| **Incised wound** | Sharp-edged weapon drawn across skin | Clean-cut, regular edges, no bridging, length > breadth > depth, edges gape |
| **Stab/punctured wound** | Sharp-pointed weapon, force along long axis | Depth > length > breadth; wound shape may indicate blade cross-section (single vs double-edged) |
| **Chop wound** | Heavy sharp-edged weapon (axe, sword) with force | Combination of incised + contusion/laceration features; may cause bone damage |
| **Firearm wound** | Bullet/projectile | See entry/exit wound table below |

*Tissue bridging = single most reliable feature differentiating a laceration (blunt) from an incised wound (sharp).*

## Entry vs Exit Gunshot Wound
| Feature | Entry wound | Exit wound |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Usually smaller (or equal) | Usually larger, irregular |
| Edges | Inverted (turned inward) | Everted (turned outward) |
| Abrasion collar/ring | Present | Absent |
| Blackening/fouling, tattooing, singeing (close-range signs) | May be present (depends on range) | Absent |
| Shape | Round/oval, fairly regular | Slit-like/stellate, irregular |

- **Contact range**: muzzle imprint, searing, blackening at the wound margin, cherry-red discoloration of tissue (CO in propellant gases).
- **Close range (<killer distance, roughly <2 ft)**: tattooing (embedded unburnt powder grains) + blackening + singeing of hair.
- **Distant range**: only the wound itself, no soot/tattooing.

## Age of a Bruise (Color Changes) — classic exam table
| Time after injury | Color |
|---|---|
| 0–2 days | Red/bluish-red (deoxygenated blood) |
| 2–4 days | Bluish-purple to black |
| 4–6 days | Green (biliverdin) |
| 7–10 days | Yellow (bilirubin) |
| 10–14 days | Disappears, normal skin color restored |

*Caution: color-based aging is only approximate — depth, size, site, and individual (age, skin color, vascularity) all affect the rate; never testify to an exact day.*

## Antemortem vs Postmortem Wound
| Feature | Antemortem | Postmortem |
|---|---|---|
| Bleeding | Free bleeding, clot formation | Little/no bleeding, no true clotting |
| Wound margins | Gaping, everted, retracted | Margins do not retract, appear pale/bloodless |
| Inflammatory reaction | Present if survived some time | Absent |
| Tissue infiltration with blood | Present | Absent (blood merely stains surface) |

## Suicidal vs Homicidal Incised Wounds (e.g., neck/wrist cutting)
| Feature | Suicidal | Homicidal |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Accessible area (front of neck, wrist, forearm — "election sites") | Anywhere, including inaccessible sites |
| Number | Multiple, parallel, superficial "hesitation/tentative cuts" near the fatal wound | Usually single deep wound; hesitation cuts absent |
| Clothing | Usually undisturbed/not cut (clothing moved aside before act) | Often cut through along with the body |
| Defense wounds | Absent | Often present (on hands/forearms) |
| Depth | Deepest at start, tailing off at the end (in neck cuts, deep near origin) | Variable, may be uniformly deep |

- **Defense wounds**: cuts/abrasions on palms, forearms, back of hands — indicate the victim tried to ward off an attack; strongly suggest homicide/assault rather than suicide.
- **Hesitation cuts**: superficial, parallel, tentative incisions near a fatal incised wound — typical of suicide.

## Classification of Hurt under Law
- **IPC Section 319** — "Hurt": bodily pain, disease, or infirmity.
- **IPC Section 320** — "Grievous hurt": an enumerated list including emasculation, permanent loss/impairment of sight or hearing, loss of any limb/joint, permanent disfiguration of the head/face, fracture/dislocation of a bone or tooth, and any hurt endangering life or causing severe bodily pain/inability to follow ordinary pursuits for 20 days.
- **IPC Sections 321–338** cover various forms of voluntarily causing hurt/grievous hurt, with/without weapons, and by negligence.
- These IPC sections are the ones used in most standard textbooks and older exam banks. **India's criminal code has been replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), effective 1 July 2024** — hurt and grievous hurt provisions have been renumbered under BNS (grievous hurt is commonly cited around BNS Section 116–118 range). **Verify the current BNS section number** before quoting it in an answer, since the exact renumbering is a live detail to confirm against the bare act.

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Tissue bridging in the wound bed = laceration (blunt), not incised wound (sharp).
- Length > breadth > depth → incised wound; depth > length > breadth → stab wound.
- Entry gunshot wound: inverted edges + abrasion collar; exit wound: everted edges, no abrasion collar.
- Bruise color sequence: red → blue/black → green → yellow → resolves (roughly 2 weeks total) — never over-precise on exact day.
- Hesitation cuts + accessible site + no defense wounds → suicidal; defense wounds + inaccessible site → homicidal.
- Grievous hurt is defined under IPC Section 320 (classic teaching); current law is BNS — confirm the equivalent BNS section number when citing in a legal context.
