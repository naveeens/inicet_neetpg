# Forensic Toxicology

## Definitions
- **Toxicology**: study of poisons — their source, properties, detection, mechanism of action, and treatment.
- **Poison**: a substance which, when administered/absorbed/introduced into the body in relatively small amounts, acts through a chemical mechanism to injure health or cause death.
- Dose–response matters: "the dose makes the poison" — almost any substance can be toxic in excess (Paracelsus principle).

## Classification of Poisons
| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Corrosive** | Strong acids (sulfuric, nitric, hydrochloric), strong alkalis (caustic soda/potash, ammonia) |
| **Irritant — inorganic** | Arsenic, mercury, lead, phosphorus, copper |
| **Irritant — organic (plant/animal)** | Abrus precatorius (Gunja/Rosary pea), castor (ricin), snake venom |
| **Irritant — mechanical** | Powdered glass, diamond dust |
| **Neurotic — cerebral** | Opioids, barbiturates, alcohol, cannabis |
| **Neurotic — spinal** | Nux vomica/strychnine |
| **Neurotic — peripheral** | Curare, organophosphorus/carbamate (via cholinesterase inhibition) |
| **Cardiac** | Oleander, aconite, digitalis, cardiac glycosides |
| **Asphyxiants (gases)** | Carbon monoxide, cyanide, hydrogen sulfide |

## Common Poisons in Indian Medico-Legal Practice
| Poison | Key clinical clue | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Organophosphorus (OP) compounds** | Cholinergic toxidrome: DUMBELS (Diarrhea, Urination, Miosis, Bradycardia, Bronchorrhea/Bronchospasm, Emesis, Lacrimation, Salivation), fasciculations | Historically the **most common agent for suicidal poisoning in India**; treatment: atropine (titrated to dry secretions) + pralidoxime (PAM) |
| **Aluminium phosphide ("rice/celphos tablet")** | Garlic-like odor, refractory hypotension, metabolic acidosis, multi-organ failure | Releases phosphine gas on contact with moisture/gastric acid; **no specific antidote** — management is supportive (gastric lavage with KMnO4/sodium bicarbonate, hemodynamic support); increasingly common agricultural-fumigant suicidal poison in India |
| **Corrosives (acids/alkalis)** | Acid: coagulative necrosis, pain, often affects stomach/pylorus more; Alkali: liquefactive necrosis, affects esophagus more severely | Avoid emesis/lavage in corrosive ingestion (risk of further mucosal damage/perforation) |
| **Cyanide** | Bitter almond odor (not all can detect it — genetic anosmia), cherry-red skin/lividity, rapid collapse | Inhibits cytochrome c oxidase → histotoxic hypoxia; treatment: amyl nitrite/sodium nitrite + sodium thiosulfate, or hydroxocobalamin |
| **Carbon monoxide** | Cherry-red lividity/skin, headache, confusion, normal-appearing pulse oximetry (falsely reassuring) | Forms carboxyhemoglobin; treatment: 100% oxygen, hyperbaric oxygen in severe cases |
| **Arsenic (chronic)** | Mees' lines (transverse white lines on nails), hyperpigmentation, peripheral neuropathy, garlic odor on breath (acute) | Classic "irritant poison"; chronic exposure historically important in homicide cases |
| **Opioids** | Miosis (pinpoint pupils), respiratory depression, CNS depression | Antidote: naloxone |
| **Barbiturates/sedative-hypnotics** | CNS depression, respiratory depression | Supportive care; multiple-dose activated charcoal in some cases |
| **Ethanol** | Legal driving limit in India per Motor Vehicles Act: **30 mg/100 mL blood** | Widmark formula used to estimate blood alcohol concentration from amount consumed |
| **Datura (Dhatura)** | Anticholinergic toxidrome — "hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter" | Historically used for robbery/poisoning (criminal poisoning agent) |
| **Snake venom** | Neurotoxic (elapid — cobra, krait: ptosis, respiratory paralysis) vs hemotoxic (viper: local swelling, coagulopathy, bleeding) | Antisnake venom (ASV, polyvalent) is the mainstay |

## Postmortem / Medico-Legal Toxicology Practice
- **Viscera preservation for chemical analysis**: stomach with contents, a loop of small intestine with contents, liver, kidney, and blood/urine (if available) are preserved — preferably in **saturated saline solution**, NOT formalin (formalin interferes with many chemical/toxicological tests). A separate sample may be sent in rectified spirit if volatile poisons like alcohol/cyanide are suspected, per local FSL protocol.
- **Formalin** is used for histopathological (microscopic) preservation, never for chemical/toxicological analysis of the same sample.
- **Chain of custody**: sealed, labeled containers, proper documentation from collection → transport → forensic science laboratory (FSL) — essential for evidentiary value in court.
- **Negative chemical report does not rule out poisoning** — consider decomposition, delayed exposure, poisons not tested for, or poisons rapidly eliminated/metabolized.

## Approach to Suspected Poisoning Death at Autopsy
- Look for external clues: corrosive burns around mouth/lips, needle marks, characteristic odor, staining of clothes.
- Internal: mucosal changes in the GI tract, organ congestion, characteristic color changes (cherry red in CO/cyanide).
- Correlate with history — circumstances of death, available containers/bottles at scene, occupation (pesticide exposure), suicide note if any.

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Historically, **organophosphorus compounds** are the most common agent of suicidal poisoning in India; treatment is atropine + pralidoxime.
- **Aluminium phosphide** ("rice tablet") poisoning has no specific antidote — garlic odor + refractory hypotension is the classic clue.
- Cherry-red lividity/skin → think **CO poisoning** or **cyanide poisoning**.
- Mees' lines on nails = classic sign of **chronic arsenic poisoning**.
- Viscera for chemical analysis are preserved in **saturated saline**, not formalin; formalin is reserved for histopathology samples.
- Legal blood alcohol limit for driving in India (Motor Vehicles Act) is **30 mg/100 mL**.
- A negative toxicology report never absolutely excludes poisoning as cause of death.
