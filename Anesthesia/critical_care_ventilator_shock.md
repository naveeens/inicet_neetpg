# Critical Care: Ventilator & Shock

## Overview
Core ICU/anesthesia topic spanning mechanical ventilation principles/modes, ARDS management, and classification/management of shock — heavily tested via scenario-based questions.

## Mechanical Ventilation — Key Parameters
- **Tidal volume**: 6–8 mL/kg (ideal body weight) in normal lungs; **lung-protective ventilation 4–6 mL/kg** in ARDS to prevent volutrauma
- **PEEP (Positive End-Expiratory Pressure)**: keeps alveoli open, improves oxygenation, prevents atelectasis; excess PEEP → barotrauma, ↓ venous return/cardiac output
- **FiO2**: titrate to lowest value maintaining SpO2 ~92–96% (avoid prolonged FiO2 >60% — oxygen toxicity)
- **Plateau pressure**: keep <30 cmH2O to minimize barotrauma (key ARDS target)

## Ventilator Modes
| Mode | Description | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Volume Control (VC) | Fixed tidal volume delivered, pressure varies | Guarantees minute ventilation; risk of high pressures if compliance falls |
| Pressure Control (PC) | Fixed pressure applied, volume varies | Limits peak pressure (barotrauma protection); tidal volume varies with compliance |
| SIMV | Synchronized mandatory breaths + patient-triggered spontaneous breaths | Used for weaning |
| Pressure Support Ventilation (PSV) | Patient triggers every breath, ventilator supports with set pressure | Used in weaning; patient must have adequate respiratory drive |
| CPAP | Continuous positive pressure, no mandatory breaths | Spontaneous breathing mode; used for weaning trials, sleep apnea, non-invasive support |

## ARDS (Berlin Definition)
- Onset within 1 week of a known insult (sepsis, pneumonia, aspiration, trauma, pancreatitis)
- Bilateral opacities on chest imaging not fully explained by effusion/collapse/nodules
- Not fully explained by cardiac failure/fluid overload
- Oxygenation criteria (PaO2/FiO2 ratio, on PEEP ≥5 cmH2O):
| Severity | PaO2/FiO2 |
|---|---|
| Mild | 200–300 |
| Moderate | 100–200 |
| Severe | <100 |

### ARDS Management
- **Lung-protective ventilation**: low tidal volume (4–6 mL/kg IBW), plateau pressure <30 cmH2O — landmark ARMA trial evidence, mortality benefit
- Adequate PEEP (titrate per ARDSnet tables) to keep alveoli recruited
- **Prone positioning** — improves oxygenation and mortality in moderate-severe ARDS (PROSEVA trial)
- Conservative fluid strategy
- Neuromuscular blockade in early severe ARDS (selected cases) to improve ventilator synchrony
- ECMO as rescue therapy in refractory hypoxemia

## Weaning from Mechanical Ventilation
- Criteria: resolved underlying cause, adequate oxygenation (PaO2/FiO2 >200, PEEP ≤5-8), hemodynamic stability, adequate mental status, ability to protect airway
- **Rapid Shallow Breathing Index (RSBI)** = respiratory rate/tidal volume(L); RSBI <105 predicts successful extubation/weaning
- Spontaneous breathing trial (SBT) is the standard test before extubation

## Shock — Classification
| Type | Mechanism | CO | SVR | Key example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | ↓ intravascular volume | ↓ | ↑ | Hemorrhage, dehydration |
| Cardiogenic | Pump failure | ↓ | ↑ | MI, arrhythmia, valvular failure |
| Obstructive | Mechanical obstruction to flow | ↓ | ↑ | Cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, massive PE |
| Distributive (septic) | Pathological vasodilation | ↑ (early)/↓ (late) | ↓ | Sepsis |
| Distributive (anaphylactic) | Vasodilation + capillary leak (histamine-mediated) | Variable | ↓ | Anaphylaxis |
| Distributive (neurogenic) | Loss of sympathetic tone | ↓ | ↓ | Spinal cord injury |

*High-yield: Neurogenic shock classically presents with hypotension + bradycardia (unopposed vagal tone) — distinguishes it from hypovolemic shock (hypotension + tachycardia).*

## Hemorrhagic Shock Classes (ATLS)
| Class | Blood loss | HR | BP | Signs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | <15% | Normal | Normal | Minimal |
| II | 15–30% | ↑ (>100) | Normal (narrowed pulse pressure) | Anxious |
| III | 30–40% | ↑ (>120) | ↓ | Confused, decreased urine output |
| IV | >40% | ↑ (>140) | Markedly ↓ | Lethargic, near cardiac arrest |

## Septic Shock
- Sepsis-3 definition: sepsis + persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg + serum lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation
- Management (Surviving Sepsis Campaign "hour-1 bundle"): blood cultures before antibiotics, broad-spectrum antibiotics, IV crystalloid 30 mL/kg for hypotension/lactate ≥4, vasopressors if MAP not maintained
- **Norepinephrine = first-line vasopressor** in septic shock
- Vasopressin/epinephrine as adjuncts in refractory shock

## Vasopressors & Inotropes
| Drug | Mechanism | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Norepinephrine | Alpha-1 (strong) + beta-1 | First-line septic shock vasopressor |
| Dopamine | Dose-dependent (D1 low, beta-1 moderate, alpha-1 high dose) | Historically used; more arrhythmias than norepinephrine |
| Dobutamine | Beta-1 (predominant) | Inotropic support in cardiogenic shock/low CO states |
| Epinephrine | Alpha-1 + beta-1/2 | Anaphylaxis (IM), cardiac arrest, refractory shock |
| Vasopressin | V1 receptor (vascular smooth muscle) | Adjunct in septic shock (catecholamine-sparing) |
| Phenylephrine | Pure alpha-1 | Spinal-anesthesia-induced hypotension |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Lung-protective ventilation (low tidal volume, plateau pressure <30) is the single biggest mortality-reducing intervention in ARDS (ARMA trial)
- Prone positioning improves survival in moderate-severe ARDS
- RSBI <105 predicts successful weaning/extubation
- Neurogenic shock = hypotension + bradycardia (unopposed vagal tone) — distinguishes from hemorrhagic shock (hypotension + tachycardia)
- Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor in septic shock per Surviving Sepsis Campaign
- Berlin criteria classify ARDS severity purely by PaO2/FiO2 ratio at PEEP ≥5
- Tension pneumothorax and cardiac tamponade are obstructive shock — both need mechanical decompression, not just fluids/pressors
- Excess PEEP can reduce venous return and cardiac output — watch for hemodynamic compromise when increasing PEEP
