# Pain Management

## Overview
Pain management spans acute post-operative pain, chronic pain, and cancer pain. WHO analgesic ladder remains the core framework tested in exams, alongside opioid pharmacology and regional/multimodal techniques.

## WHO Analgesic Ladder (Cancer Pain, extendable to chronic pain)
| Step | Pain severity | Drugs |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | Mild | Non-opioid: Paracetamol, NSAIDs ± adjuvant |
| Step 2 | Mild-moderate | Weak opioid (Codeine, Tramadol) + non-opioid ± adjuvant |
| Step 3 | Moderate-severe | Strong opioid (Morphine, Fentanyl, Oxycodone) + non-opioid ± adjuvant |

- Principle: "by the mouth, by the clock, by the ladder, for the individual" — regular scheduled dosing, not PRN, for chronic/cancer pain
- Adjuvants: antidepressants (amitriptyline, duloxetine) and anticonvulsants (gabapentin, pregabalin) — especially useful for **neuropathic pain**

## Opioid Analgesics
| Agent | Mechanism | Key use | Key side effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morphine | Mu-opioid receptor agonist | Gold standard strong opioid; MI pain, severe post-op/cancer pain | Respiratory depression, constipation, histamine release (hypotension, bronchospasm), miosis |
| Fentanyl | Mu-opioid agonist (highly potent, lipophilic) | Intraoperative analgesia, rapid onset; patches for chronic cancer pain | Respiratory depression; less histamine release than morphine — preferred in hemodynamically unstable/asthma |
| Pethidine (meperidine) | Mu-opioid agonist | Post-op shivering (specific indication), obstetric analgesia (less neonatal respiratory depression than morphine, historically) | Normeperidine metabolite → seizures in renal failure/high doses; avoid with MAOIs (serotonin syndrome) |
| Tramadol | Weak mu-opioid agonist + serotonin/NE reuptake inhibition | Moderate pain, neuropathic component | Serotonin syndrome risk (esp. with SSRIs/MAOIs), lowers seizure threshold |
| Codeine | Weak mu agonist (prodrug, needs CYP2D6 to morphine) | Mild-moderate pain, antitussive | Variable efficacy due to CYP2D6 polymorphism; constipation |
| Buprenorphine | Partial mu agonist, kappa antagonist | Chronic pain, opioid dependence | **Ceiling effect** on respiratory depression; naloxone less effective for reversal (high receptor affinity) |
| Naloxone | Competitive mu-opioid antagonist | Opioid overdose reversal | Short half-life — may need repeat dosing/infusion as opioid outlasts naloxone |

*High-yield: Pethidine is the specific drug of choice for post-anesthesia shivering (via kappa-receptor action), not for its analgesic property in that context.*

## NSAIDs and Paracetamol
- Mechanism: COX-1/COX-2 inhibition → ↓ prostaglandin synthesis → analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory
- Multimodal analgesia principle: combine paracetamol + NSAID + opioid-sparing adjuncts to reduce total opioid dose and side effects ("opioid-sparing" strategy)
- Paracetamol: analgesic/antipyretic but minimal anti-inflammatory effect; safe in most patients but hepatotoxic in overdose (N-acetylcysteine is antidote)
- NSAID caution: renal impairment, peptic ulcer disease, bleeding risk (avoid/caution with anticoagulation), aspirin-induced asthma

## Neuropathic Pain
- First-line: Gabapentin, Pregabalin (alpha-2-delta calcium channel subunit modulators — reduce neurotransmitter release)
- Also first-line: TCAs (amitriptyline), SNRIs (duloxetine)
- Second-line: Topical lidocaine/capsaicin patches, tramadol
- Classic conditions: diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, trigeminal neuralgia (carbamazepine is first-line specifically here), phantom limb pain

## Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA)
- Patient self-administers pre-set IV opioid bolus doses with a lockout interval — prevents overdose while allowing individualized titration
- Improves patient satisfaction vs nurse-administered PRN dosing

## Regional Techniques for Post-Op Analgesia
- Epidural analgesia — especially for major abdominal/thoracic surgery (reduces pulmonary complications by allowing better cough/deep breathing)
- Peripheral nerve blocks / catheters (e.g., continuous femoral block for knee surgery, TAP block for abdominal surgery)
- Multimodal approach reduces opioid consumption and associated side effects (ileus, respiratory depression, PONV)

## Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV) — related to pain management/anesthesia
- Apfel risk factors: female sex, non-smoker, history of motion sickness/PONV, use of post-op opioids
- Prophylaxis/treatment: Ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist), Dexamethasone, Droperidol, Propofol-based (TIVA reduces PONV vs volatile agents)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- WHO ladder step-up principle: start appropriate to severity, not always step 1 first, in acute severe pain
- Pethidine is specifically used for post-anesthesia/post-op shivering
- Buprenorphine's ceiling effect makes overdose respiratory depression less likely but also makes naloxone reversal less effective
- Tramadol lowers seizure threshold and risks serotonin syndrome with SSRIs/MAOIs
- Normeperidine (pethidine metabolite) accumulation in renal failure causes seizures — avoid repeated/prolonged pethidine use in renal impairment
- Carbamazepine is first-line specifically for trigeminal neuralgia among neuropathic pain conditions
- Multimodal analgesia (paracetamol + NSAID + regional block + minimal opioid) is preferred over opioid-only regimens to reduce side effects
- Naloxone has a shorter half-life than most opioids — watch for re-sedation after reversal
