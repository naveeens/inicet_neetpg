# Airway Management

## Overview
Airway assessment and securing a patent airway is the single most critical skill in anesthesia — "A" of ABC. Most anesthesia-related deaths/litigation trace back to failed airway management, not drug errors.

## Preoperative Airway Assessment
| Test | What it predicts | Positive/Difficult finding |
|---|---|---|
| Mallampati classification | Difficult laryngoscopy | Class III/IV (only base of uvula or hard palate visible) |
| Thyromental distance | Difficult intubation | <6 cm (3 finger-breadths) |
| Sternomental distance | Difficult intubation | <12.5 cm |
| Inter-incisor gap (mouth opening) | Difficult laryngoscopy | <3 cm (<2 finger-breadths) |
| Neck movement (extension) | Difficult laryngoscopy | <80° or fixed neck |
| Upper lip bite test | Difficult intubation | Class III (lower incisors cannot bite upper lip) |
| Wilson risk score | Composite risk | Score >2 predicts difficulty |

*High-yield: Mallampati is done sitting, mouth open, tongue out, phonating "aah" — NOT visualized with laryngoscope (that's Cormack-Lehane, done intraoperatively).*

## Cormack-Lehane Grading (Direct Laryngoscopy View)
| Grade | View |
|---|---|
| I | Full glottis visible |
| II | Partial glottis / posterior commissure only |
| III | Only epiglottis seen, no glottis |
| IV | Neither glottis nor epiglottis seen |

- Grade III/IV = difficult intubation; correlates with need for adjuncts (bougie, video laryngoscope)

## Airway Equipment
| Device | Key point |
|---|---|
| Oropharyngeal (Guedel) airway | Relieves tongue-based obstruction in unconscious patient; **causes gagging/laryngospasm if patient has intact reflexes** — do not use in semiconscious patients |
| Nasopharyngeal airway | Better tolerated in semiconscious patients; avoid in basal skull fracture/coagulopathy |
| Laryngeal Mask Airway (LMA) | Supraglottic device; does NOT protect against aspiration; contraindicated in full stomach, high airway pressures needed, poor pulmonary compliance |
| Endotracheal tube (ETT) | Gold standard for airway protection against aspiration; cuffed tube |
| Bougie/stylet | Aid for difficult intubation, Grade III view |
| Video laryngoscope (e.g., McGrath, Glidescope) | First-line rescue in anticipated/unanticipated difficult airway per most difficult airway algorithms now |

### ETT Sizing (Pediatric high-yield formula)
- Uncuffed ETT internal diameter (mm) = **(Age/4) + 4**
- ETT length (oral, cm) = (Age/2) + 12
- Cole's formula historically for uncuffed tubes; cuffed tubes now preferred even in infants in modern practice (0.5 mm smaller than uncuffed formula)

## Rapid Sequence Induction (RSI)
Used in patients at risk of aspiration (full stomach, pregnancy, bowel obstruction, trauma, GERD).
- Preoxygenation with 100% O2 for 3 min (or 4 vital capacity breaths)
- No mask ventilation (classically) to avoid gastric insufflation
- Cricoid pressure (Sellick maneuver) — ~10 N awake, increased to ~30 N after loss of consciousness — applied until cuffed ETT position confirmed
- Rapid-acting induction agent + rapid-acting muscle relaxant (Succinylcholine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV or Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg IV for RSI)
- *High-yield: Cricoid pressure is controversial and evidence for efficacy is weak, but still classically tested for RSI/full stomach scenarios.*

## Difficult Airway Algorithm (ASA) — Core Concept
1. Anticipate → have plan A/B/C/D ready (awake fiberoptic intubation if predicted very difficult)
2. Unanticipated difficult intubation → limit attempts (≤3), call for help, use video laryngoscope/bougie
3. If intubation fails but mask ventilation possible → wake up patient or continue with LMA if surgery permits
4. **"Cannot Intubate, Cannot Ventilate" (CICV)** → emergency invasive airway: **cricothyroidotomy** (needle or surgical) — do not delay for repeated attempts

## Laryngospasm
- Reflex glottic closure, common in children during light anesthesia / airway stimulation (secretions, extubation)
- Management: 100% O2, jaw thrust, **Larson's maneuver** (firm pressure at laryngospasm notch bilaterally, behind ear lobule), deepen anesthesia (propofol bolus); if severe/hypoxic → small dose succinylcholine to break spasm

## Extubation Criteria
- Full return of consciousness, obeying commands
- Adequate reversal of neuromuscular blockade (train-of-four ratio >0.9, sustained head lift >5 sec)
- Adequate spontaneous ventilation, stable hemodynamics
- Protective airway reflexes (cough/gag) present

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Mallampati Class IV and Cormack-Lehane Grade IV both indicate very difficult/impossible glottic visualization but are assessed at different times (pre-op vs intra-op)
- CICV situation → definitive answer is **cricothyroidotomy**, not repeated laryngoscopy attempts
- LMA does not protect the airway from aspiration — never use in full stomach/high aspiration risk patients
- Pediatric ETT ID formula: (Age/4) + 4
- Laryngospasm first-line non-drug maneuver: Larson's point pressure + jaw thrust with 100% O2
- Succinylcholine remains the fastest-onset muscle relaxant for RSI despite side-effect profile (hyperkalemia, malignant hyperthermia trigger, bradycardia)
