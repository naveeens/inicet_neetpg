# Regional Anesthesia

## Overview
Regional anesthesia blocks nerve conduction to a specific region using local anesthetics, avoiding general anesthesia risks. Broadly divided into central neuraxial blocks (spinal, epidural, CSE) and peripheral nerve blocks.

## Local Anesthetics — Mechanism & Comparison
Mechanism: block voltage-gated Na+ channels on nerve membrane (from the inside, in un-ionized form) → prevent depolarization → block conduction. Smaller, myelinated, and more rapidly firing fibers blocked first (order: autonomic B fibers > small unmyelinated C fibers [pain/temp] > larger A fibers [touch/motor]).

| Agent | Class | Onset | Duration | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lidocaine | Amide | Fast | Intermediate | Also used as antiarrhythmic (Class Ib); IV for attenuating intubation response |
| Bupivacaine | Amide | Slow | Long | Most cardiotoxic LA (highly protein-bound, avid Na channel binding) — cardiac arrest resistant to resuscitation |
| Ropivacaine | Amide | Slow | Long | Less cardiotoxic than bupivacaine; more motor-sensory differential block; preferred in labor epidural |
| Levobupivacaine | Amide | Slow | Long | S-enantiomer of bupivacaine — less cardiotoxic than racemic bupivacaine |
| Prilocaine | Amide | Fast | Intermediate | Causes **methemoglobinemia** (metabolite o-toluidine); used in EMLA cream |
| Procaine | Ester | Fast | Short | Metabolized by pseudocholinesterase; higher allergy risk (PABA metabolite) |
| Chloroprocaine | Ester | Fast | Short | Rapid plasma metabolism — safest in overdose; used in obstetric epidural for rapid onset |
| Cocaine | Ester | — | — | Only LA with intrinsic vasoconstrictor property (blocks NE reuptake) — used topically in ENT/nasal surgery |

*High-yield: Amides have two "i"s in name (lidocaine, bupivacaine except mepivacaine — remember by liver metabolism); Esters (one "i") are metabolized by plasma pseudocholinesterase — think "esters, esterases."*

### Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)
- Early CNS signs: perioral numbness, tinnitus, metallic taste → progresses to seizures → cardiovascular collapse
- Bupivacaine: cardiotoxicity can precede or occur without CNS warning signs — classic exam point
- **Treatment: IV Lipid emulsion (Intralipid 20%)** — "lipid sink" theory — plus standard ACLS (avoid vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers; use smaller epinephrine doses)

## Spinal (Subarachnoid Block) vs Epidural
| Feature | Spinal | Epidural |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Subarachnoid space (CSF) | Epidural space (outside dura) |
| Landmark | L3-L4 / L4-L5 (below spinal cord termination — L1 in adults, L3 in infants) | Any level (cervical to lumbar) |
| Onset | Fast (minutes) | Slower (15–20 min) |
| Dose of LA | Small volume, high concentration | Large volume, lower concentration |
| Catheter | Usually single-shot | Catheter can be left for continuous infusion/labor analgesia |
| Motor block | Dense | Can achieve differential sensory>motor block (lower concentration) |
| Headache risk | PDPH (post-dural puncture headache) | Rare unless accidental dural puncture ("wet tap") |

- Needle level for spinal must be below **L1 (adults)** to avoid spinal cord injury (conus medullaris)
- Tuffier's line (intercristal line) = line joining iliac crests, corresponds to L4 spinous process/L4-L5 interspace — landmark for spinal needle insertion

### Post-Dural Puncture Headache (PDPH)
- Due to CSF leak → traction on meninges; classically **postural** (worse sitting/standing, relieved lying flat)
- Risk factors: young age, female, pregnancy, large-bore/cutting-tip (Quincke) needles
- Prevention: smaller gauge, pencil-point (Whitacre/Sprotte) needles
- Treatment: conservative (fluids, caffeine, analgesics) first; definitive = **epidural blood patch** if refractory

## Peripheral Nerve Blocks (High-Yield Examples)
| Block | Region covered | Key complication |
|---|---|---|
| Brachial plexus (interscalene) | Shoulder, upper arm | Phrenic nerve palsy (100% incidence — avoid in severe COPD), Horner syndrome |
| Brachial plexus (supraclavicular) | Arm/forearm | Pneumothorax (highest risk among brachial plexus approaches) |
| Brachial plexus (axillary) | Forearm/hand | Safest — no pneumothorax/phrenic risk |
| Bier block (IV regional) | Forearm/hand, short procedures | Tourniquet must stay ≥20 min minimum; risk of LA toxicity on early deflation |
| Ankle block | Foot surgery | 5 nerves to block (posterior tibial, sural, superficial/deep peroneal, saphenous) |
| Transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block | Abdominal wall analgesia | Adjunct for post-op analgesia, not surgical anesthesia alone |

## Contraindications to Neuraxial Blocks
- Absolute: patient refusal, uncorrected coagulopathy, local infection at site, raised ICP, severe hypovolemia/shock
- Relative: fixed cardiac output states (severe aortic stenosis), pre-existing neurological deficit, sepsis

## Complications of Neuraxial Anesthesia
- Hypotension (sympathetic blockade — most common complication of spinal anesthesia) — treat with fluids, vasopressors (phenylephrine/ephedrine)
- High spinal/total spinal → respiratory arrest, needs airway support
- Epidural hematoma — coagulopathy/anticoagulation is major risk factor
- Cauda equina syndrome — rare, associated with high concentration lidocaine (5%) microcatheter techniques historically
- Backache, urinary retention

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Bupivacaine cardiotoxicity is disproportionately severe and resuscitation-resistant compared to lidocaine — treat with lipid emulsion
- Prilocaine → methemoglobinemia; treat with IV methylene blue
- Esters metabolized by plasma pseudocholinesterase; amides metabolized in the liver
- Spinal needle must be inserted below L1 to avoid conus medullaris injury; Tuffier's line marks L4
- PDPH is classically postural; definitive treatment is epidural blood patch
- Interscalene block → near-universal phrenic nerve palsy; avoid in patients who cannot tolerate hemidiaphragmatic paralysis
- Most common complication of spinal anesthesia: hypotension from sympathetic blockade
- Cocaine is the only local anesthetic with intrinsic vasoconstrictive activity
