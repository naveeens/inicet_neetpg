# General Anesthesia Agents

## Overview
General anesthesia (GA) has 4 components: hypnosis (unconsciousness), analgesia, amnesia, and muscle relaxation (akinesia) — no single agent achieves all four, hence "balanced anesthesia" using combinations. Classic stages of anesthesia (Guedel's, described for ether): I- Analgesia, II- Excitement/delirium, III- Surgical anesthesia, IV- Medullary paralysis/overdose.

## IV Induction Agents
| Agent | Mechanism | Key use | Key side effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Propofol | GABA-A potentiation | Induction & maintenance (TIVA); day-care surgery; antiemetic property | Pain on injection, hypotension (myocardial depression + vasodilation), apnea |
| Thiopentone | GABA-A potentiation (barbiturate) | Rapid induction; neuroprotection (↓ cerebral metabolic rate) | Laryngospasm/bronchospasm (avoid in asthma), porphyria trigger, hypotension |
| Etomidate | GABA-A potentiation | Induction in hemodynamically unstable patients (cardiac stability) | **Adrenocortical suppression** (11-beta-hydroxylase inhibition) — avoid infusion/repeated dosing; myoclonus |
| Ketamine | NMDA receptor antagonist | Induction in shock/hypovolemia, asthmatics (bronchodilator); "dissociative anesthesia"; pediatric/burns; analgesic at sub-anesthetic dose | Emergence delirium/hallucinations, ↑ HR/BP (sympathomimetic), ↑ secretions, ↑ ICP (relatively contraindicated in head injury — though modern evidence questions this) |
| Midazolam | GABA-A potentiation (benzodiazepine) | Premedication, sedation, co-induction | Slow onset/offset relative to propofol for induction; reversed by flumazenil |
| Dexmedetomidine | Alpha-2 agonist | Sedation (ICU, awake craniotomy, procedural) — preserves respiratory drive | Bradycardia, hypotension |

*High-yield: Propofol is the only induction agent with intrinsic antiemetic property. Ketamine is the only one that increases heart rate/BP and preserves airway reflexes/respiratory drive (bronchodilator — good in asthma/hemodynamically unstable trauma). Etomidate = most cardiostable but adrenal suppression limits repeat/infusion use.*

## Inhalational Agents
### Minimum Alveolar Concentration (MAC)
MAC = concentration at which 50% of patients do not respond to a standardized surgical stimulus. Lower MAC = more potent agent. MAC values are roughly additive between agents (e.g., N2O + volatile).

| Agent | Relative potency (MAC) | Blood:gas solubility (speed of onset/offset) | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrous oxide (N2O) | Very high MAC (>100%) — weak agent, needs combination | Very low solubility → fastest onset/offset | Diffusion hypoxia on discontinuation (give 100% O2 at end); expands closed air spaces (avoid in pneumothorax/bowel obstruction/middle ear surgery); megaloblastic anemia with prolonged use (inactivates B12) |
| Sevoflurane | Low MAC (~2%) | Low solubility → fast onset/offset | Preferred for inhalational induction (non-pungent, good for children); Compound A with low-flow + soda lime (theoretical nephrotoxicity) |
| Desflurane | Low MAC (~6%) | Lowest solubility of volatiles → fastest emergence | Pungent (not for induction, airway irritant → laryngospasm/cough); rapid recovery — good for day-care |
| Isoflurane | Low MAC (~1.2%) | Intermediate solubility | Pungent; good muscle relaxation; coronary "steal" (largely disproven clinically but classically tested) |
| Halothane | Low MAC (~0.75%, most potent) | High solubility → slow onset/offset | **Halothane hepatitis** (rare, immune-mediated); myocardial depression, arrhythmogenic (sensitizes heart to catecholamines); largely obsolete now |

*High-yield: Blood:gas solubility coefficient determines speed of induction/recovery — LOWER solubility = FASTER onset and offset (counterintuitive but classic trap). Oil:gas coefficient correlates with potency (Meyer-Overton rule).*

## Muscle Relaxants (Neuromuscular Blockers)
| Agent | Type | Onset/Duration | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Succinylcholine | Depolarizing | Fastest onset (30-60 sec), short duration (5-10 min) | Drug of choice for RSI; causes fasciculations, hyperkalemia, **malignant hyperthermia trigger**, bradycardia (esp. repeat dose in children), prolonged apnea in atypical/deficient pseudocholinesterase |
| Rocuronium | Non-depolarizing (aminosteroid) | Fast onset at high dose (RSI dose 1.2 mg/kg), intermediate duration | Reversed specifically by **sugammadex**; also neostigmine |
| Vecuronium | Non-depolarizing (aminosteroid) | Intermediate | Minimal cardiovascular effects |
| Atracurium | Non-depolarizing (benzylisoquinolinium) | Intermediate | Undergoes **Hofmann elimination** (organ-independent) — preferred in renal/hepatic failure; releases histamine (hypotension, bronchospasm); metabolite **laudanosine** can cause seizures in high doses/renal failure |
| Cisatracurium | Non-depolerizing (benzylisoquinolinium) | Intermediate | Hofmann elimination, minimal histamine release — preferred over atracurium in cardiac-unstable/renal failure patients |
| Pancuronium | Non-depolarizing (aminosteroid) | Long | Vagolytic → tachycardia; renal excretion |

### Reversal of Neuromuscular Blockade
- **Neostigmine** (anticholinesterase) + Glycopyrrolate/Atropine (to counter muscarinic side effects — bradycardia, secretions) — reverses non-depolarizing blockers
- **Sugammadex** — modified gamma-cyclodextrin, encapsulates rocuronium/vecuronium specifically (aminosteroids only, not benzylisoquinolinium) — rapid, reliable reversal even from deep block
- Monitor adequacy of reversal with **train-of-four (TOF) ratio >0.9**

## Malignant Hyperthermia
- Hypermetabolic crisis triggered by volatile anesthetics and succinylcholine in genetically susceptible individuals (RYR1 gene mutation — ryanodine receptor, uncontrolled Ca2+ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum)
- Features: hyperthermia (often late sign), muscle rigidity, hypercarbia (earliest reliable sign — rising EtCO2 despite increased ventilation), tachycardia, acidosis
- **Treatment: Dantrolene** (ryanodine receptor antagonist, blocks Ca2+ release) — stop trigger agents, 100% O2, cooling, treat hyperkalemia/acidosis

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Lower blood:gas solubility = faster onset AND faster recovery (N2O and desflurane fastest; halothane slowest)
- Lower MAC = more potent inhalational agent
- Sevoflurane preferred for mask induction in children (non-pungent); desflurane/isoflurane are pungent and irritate airway
- Succinylcholine is the only depolarizing muscle relaxant in clinical use; triggers malignant hyperthermia
- Sugammadex reverses only aminosteroid relaxants (rocuronium, vecuronium) — not atracurium/cisatracurium
- Atracurium/cisatracurium undergo Hofmann elimination — safest in renal and hepatic failure
- Etomidate = most hemodynamically stable induction agent but causes adrenocortical suppression
- Ketamine is the induction agent of choice in hypovolemic/hemodynamically unstable trauma patients and in asthmatics
- Malignant hyperthermia: earliest reliable sign is rising end-tidal CO2; specific antidote is dantrolene
- N2O causes diffusion hypoxia at the end of anesthesia — always give 100% O2 for a few minutes after discontinuing
