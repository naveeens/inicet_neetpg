# Abdomen GI Hepatobiliary Anatomy

## Peritoneal Reflections & Key Spaces
- **Lesser sac (omental bursa)**: behind stomach & lesser omentum; communicates with greater sac via **epiploic (omental) foramen of Winslow** — boundaries: anteriorly = free edge of lesser omentum (portal triad — portal vein, hepatic artery, common bile duct), posteriorly = IVC, superiorly = caudate lobe of liver, inferiorly = first part of duodenum
- **Greater omentum**: hangs from greater curvature of stomach, "policeman of abdomen" — walls off infection/perforation (e.g., appendicitis, perforated ulcer)
- **Ligamentum teres**: remnant of umbilical vein, in free edge of falciform ligament
- **Ligamentum venosum**: remnant of ductus venosus, separates caudate lobe from left lobe of liver

## Foregut, Midgut, Hindgut — Blood Supply & Extent
| Division | Extent | Artery | Nerve Supply (parasympathetic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foregut | Distal esophagus to 2nd part of duodenum (ampulla of Vater) — includes liver, gallbladder, pancreas, spleen (mesoderm) | Celiac trunk | Vagus |
| Midgut | 2nd part duodenum to proximal 2/3 transverse colon | Superior mesenteric artery (SMA) | Vagus |
| Hindgut | Distal 1/3 transverse colon to upper anal canal (pectinate line) | Inferior mesenteric artery (IMA) | Pelvic splanchnic (S2-S4) |

*Referred pain follows embryological origin: foregut pain → epigastrium; midgut pain → periumbilical (classic in early appendicitis before localizing to McBurney's point as parietal peritoneum becomes inflamed); hindgut pain → suprapubic/hypogastric.*

## Celiac Trunk Branches
Celiac trunk → **Left gastric, Splenic, Common hepatic** ("GHost of the Left" or simply remember the 3 branches).
- Common hepatic → hepatic artery proper (→ right & left hepatic) + gastroduodenal artery (→ supplies pancreatic head & duodenum, at risk in posterior duodenal ulcer perforation causing hemorrhage)
- Splenic artery runs along superior border of pancreas — landmark in pancreatic surgery

## SMA Branches & Midgut Rotation
- SMA branches: inferior pancreaticoduodenal, jejunal/ileal branches, ileocolic, right colic, middle colic
- Midgut undergoes **270° counterclockwise rotation** around the SMA axis during physiological herniation (weeks 6-10 gestation) and return to abdomen
- **Malrotation** → predisposes to **midgut volvulus** (surgical emergency in neonates, "double bubble" not seen — that's duodenal atresia; volvulus shows "corkscrew" sign on contrast study)

## Liver — Segments & Relations
- Couinaud classification: 8 functional segments based on portal & hepatic venous branches (independent vascular/biliary supply) — allows segmental resection
- **Portal triad** (portal vein, hepatic artery proper, common bile duct) runs in free edge of lesser omentum (hepatoduodenal ligament) — occluded during **Pringle maneuver** to control hemorrhage in liver trauma/surgery
- Porta hepatis relations (anterior to posterior): bile duct, hepatic artery, portal vein ("DAV" — duct, artery, vein anterior to posterior; but right to left it's duct-artery-vein too — commonly tested)
- Liver receives dual blood supply: ~75-80% portal vein (nutrient-rich, deoxygenated), ~20-25% hepatic artery (oxygen-rich)

## Portal Vein Formation & Portosystemic Anastomoses
Portal vein = splenic vein + superior mesenteric vein (behind neck of pancreas); inferior mesenteric vein usually drains into splenic vein.

| Site | Portal Side | Systemic Side | Clinical Correlate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower esophagus | Left gastric vein (esophageal branches) | Azygos system | **Esophageal varices** (most clinically significant — risk of massive UGI bleed) |
| Anal canal | Superior rectal vein | Middle & inferior rectal veins | Hemorrhoids (internal, above pectinate line — less painful, portal drainage) |
| Umbilicus | Paraumbilical veins (via ligamentum teres) | Superficial epigastric veins | **Caput medusae** |
| Bare area of viscera (retroperitoneal) | Portal tributaries | Retroperitoneal systemic veins | Retroperitoneal (Retzius) anastomoses |

## Gallbladder & Biliary Tree
- Gallbladder: fundus, body, neck (Hartmann's pouch — common site of gallstone impaction), spiral valves of Heister in cystic duct
- Cystic duct + common hepatic duct → **common bile duct (CBD)** → joins main pancreatic duct → **ampulla of Vater** → opens at **major duodenal papilla** in 2nd part of duodenum, controlled by **sphincter of Oddi**
- **Calot's (cystohepatic) triangle**: boundaries = cystic duct (inferior), common hepatic duct (medial), inferior surface of liver (superior) — contains cystic artery (branch of right hepatic artery) — critical landmark in laparoscopic cholecystectomy to avoid bile duct injury
- Gallbladder fundus projects at tip of **9th right costal cartilage**, where right lateral rectus border meets costal margin — Murphy's sign point (cholecystitis)

## Pancreas
- Retroperitoneal organ; head (in C-loop of duodenum, uncinate process wraps behind SMV/SMA), neck (in front of portal vein formation), body, tail (reaches spleen, at risk during splenectomy)
- Main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) joins CBD at ampulla of Vater; accessory duct (of Santorini) opens separately at minor duodenal papilla
- Exocrine (acinar, 98%) + endocrine (islets of Langerhans, 2% — alpha=glucagon, beta=insulin, delta=somatostatin)

## Spleen
- Intraperitoneal; lies along long axis of **10th rib**; related to stomach (gastrosplenic ligament — contains short gastric + left gastroepiploic vessels), kidney (splenorenal/lienorenal ligament — contains splenic vessels & tail of pancreas)
- Only spleen support ligaments containing vessels: gastrosplenic (short gastric a.) & splenorenal (splenic a./v.)
- Accessory spleens (~10-30%) most commonly near splenic hilum — relevant for failure of splenectomy to cure conditions like ITP if accessory spleen missed

## Inguinal Region & Hernias
| Feature | Direct Hernia | Indirect Hernia |
|---|---|---|
| Site relative to inferior epigastric vessels | **Medial** to inferior epigastric artery | **Lateral** to inferior epigastric artery |
| Origin | Through Hesselbach's triangle (weakness in posterior wall/transversalis fascia) | Through deep inguinal ring, follows spermatic cord/processus vaginalis |
| Covering by internal spermatic fascia | No | Yes |
| Congenital association | No (acquired, elderly, weak muscles) | Patent processus vaginalis (congenital) |
| Reaches scrotum | Rarely | Commonly (can reach scrotum) |

**Hesselbach's triangle**: bounded by inferior epigastric vessels (lateral/superior), lateral border of rectus abdominis (medial), inguinal ligament (inferior).

- Deep inguinal ring: midpoint of inguinal ligament, lateral to inferior epigastric artery (in transversalis fascia)
- Superficial inguinal ring: in external oblique aponeurosis, above pubic tubercle

## Anal Canal & Pectinate Line
| Above Pectinate Line | Below Pectinate Line |
|---|---|
| Embryology: hindgut (endoderm) | Ectoderm (proctodeum) |
| Epithelium: columnar | Squamous |
| Arterial supply: superior rectal (IMA) | Inferior rectal (internal pudendal) |
| Venous drainage: portal (superior rectal v.) | Systemic (inferior rectal v.) |
| Nerve supply: autonomic (insensitive to pain) | Somatic — inferior rectal nerve (pudendal, sensitive to pain) |
| Lymphatic drainage: internal iliac nodes | Superficial inguinal nodes |
| Hemorrhoids: internal (painless) | External (painful) |

*High-yield: internal hemorrhoids are painless (visceral innervation above pectinate line); external hemorrhoids are painful (somatic innervation below pectinate line via inferior rectal nerve).*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Referred pain: foregut = epigastrium, midgut = periumbilical, hindgut = suprapubic (embryological basis, explains early appendicitis pain pattern)
- Epiploic foramen of Winslow: only communication between greater and lesser sac; anterior boundary = portal triad
- Portal vein formed behind the neck of pancreas by union of splenic + superior mesenteric veins
- Esophageal varices are the most clinically dangerous portosystemic anastomosis (portal hypertension)
- Calot's triangle contains the cystic artery — key landmark to avoid CBD injury in cholecystectomy
- Direct inguinal hernia = medial to inferior epigastric vessels; indirect = lateral to inferior epigastric vessels (and follows the deep ring)
- Internal hemorrhoids (above pectinate line) are painless; external hemorrhoids (below) are painful
- Malrotation of midgut predisposes to volvulus — "corkscrew" sign on imaging, surgical emergency
- Gallbladder fundus surface marking: tip of right 9th costal cartilage (Murphy's point)
