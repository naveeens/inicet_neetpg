# Cerebral Blood Supply Spinal Tracts

## Circle of Willis
Formed by: 2 internal carotid arteries, 2 anterior cerebral arteries (ACA), anterior communicating artery, 2 posterior communicating arteries, 2 posterior cerebral arteries (PCA), basilar artery (formed by fusion of 2 vertebral arteries).

- **Most common site of berry (saccular) aneurysm**: anterior communicating artery > posterior communicating artery > MCA bifurcation
- Anterior communicating artery aneurysm rupture → classically presents with sudden severe headache ("thunderclap") ± bitemporal visual field defects (compression of optic chiasm) ± personality change (frontal lobe/ACA territory)
- Posterior communicating artery aneurysm → classically compresses CN III → painful third nerve palsy **with pupil involvement** (surgical/compressive third nerve palsy dilates pupil first, because parasympathetic fibers run peripherally in the nerve; contrast with diabetic/ischemic third nerve palsy which is pupil-sparing as it affects central fibers first)

## Cerebral Artery Territories & Stroke Syndromes
| Artery | Territory | Clinical Syndrome if Occluded |
|---|---|---|
| **ACA** | Medial frontal & parietal lobe (leg/foot motor-sensory cortex) | Contralateral leg > arm weakness & sensory loss; urinary incontinence; abulia (if bilateral) |
| **MCA** (most common site of stroke) | Lateral frontal, parietal, temporal (face/arm motor-sensory cortex, Broca's, Wernicke's) | Contralateral face & arm > leg weakness; aphasia (if dominant hemisphere); contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing not typical (that's PCA); gaze deviation toward the lesion |
| **PCA** | Occipital lobe, medial temporal lobe, thalamus | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia **with macular sparing** (occipital pole has dual MCA/PCA supply); alexia without agraphia if dominant side |
| **Anterior spinal artery** | Anterior 2/3 of spinal cord | Anterior spinal artery syndrome (see below) |
| **PICA** | Lateral medulla, inferior cerebellum | Wallenberg (lateral medullary) syndrome |
| **AICA** | Lateral pons, flocculus | Lateral pontine syndrome — facial palsy, deafness, ataxia |

*High-yield: Macular sparing in PCA infarcts is due to dual blood supply of the occipital pole (MCA collateral) — a classic localizing clue.*

## Watershed (Border Zone) Infarcts
Occur between ACA-MCA and MCA-PCA territories during systemic hypotension/global hypoperfusion (not embolic). ACA-MCA watershed → proximal arm/leg weakness ("man-in-a-barrel" syndrome if bilateral).

## Venous Drainage
- Superior sagittal sinus → drains into confluence of sinuses → transverse sinus → sigmoid sinus → internal jugular vein
- Cavernous sinus: contains ICA (with sympathetic plexus) and CN VI running through it; CN III, IV, V1, V2 run in its lateral wall
- Cavernous sinus thrombosis → ophthalmoplegia (III, IV, VI), ptosis, proptosis, chemosis; often from facial/nasal infection via facial vein/pterygoid plexus (dangerous area of face — infection spreads to cavernous sinus without valves)

## Blood Supply of Internal Capsule
- Genu & posterior limb (carries corticospinal fibers) supplied by **lenticulostriate branches of MCA** (Charcot's artery of cerebral hemorrhage — most common site of hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage)
- Anterior limb: recurrent artery of Heubner (branch of ACA)

## Spinal Cord Tracts — Ascending (Sensory)
| Tract | Function | Decussation Level | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dorsal columns** (fasciculus gracilis/cuneatus) | Fine touch, vibration, proprioception, 2-point discrimination | Medulla (internal arcuate fibers → medial lemniscus) | Posterior white column, ipsilateral (until medulla) |
| **Lateral spinothalamic** | Pain, temperature | Spinal cord, within 1–2 segments of entry (anterior white commissure) | Anterolateral, crossed |
| **Anterior spinothalamic** | Crude touch, pressure | Spinal cord (anterior white commissure) | Anterolateral, crossed |
| **Spinocerebellar (dorsal & ventral)** | Unconscious proprioception to cerebellum | Mostly uncrossed | Lateral, peripheral |

## Spinal Cord Tracts — Descending (Motor)
| Tract | Function | Decussation |
|---|---|---|
| **Lateral corticospinal** | Voluntary motor, fine distal limb movement (85% of fibers) | Decussate at pyramids (medulla) |
| **Anterior corticospinal** | Voluntary motor, axial/proximal (15%) | Decussate at level of termination (segmentally) |
| **Rubrospinal** | Flexor tone facilitation | Crossed (ventral tegmental decussation, midbrain) |
| **Vestibulospinal** | Extensor tone, balance | Uncrossed |
| **Reticulospinal** | Posture, muscle tone | Mixed |

## Clinically Important Cord Syndromes
| Syndrome | Cause | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Brown-Séquard (hemisection)** | Trauma, tumor | Ipsilateral: UMN weakness + loss of proprioception/vibration below lesion. Contralateral: loss of pain/temperature below lesion (1–2 segments lower due to tract ascent before crossing) |
| **Anterior spinal artery syndrome** | Aortic surgery, atherosclerosis | Bilateral loss of motor function + pain/temperature below lesion; **dorsal columns spared** (proprioception/vibration intact) — because ASA supplies anterior 2/3, posterior spinal arteries supply posterior 1/3 |
| **Subacute combined degeneration** | Vitamin B12 deficiency | Combined dorsal column (loss of proprioception/vibration, ataxia) + lateral corticospinal tract (spasticity, UMN signs) degeneration; peripheral neuropathy also common |
| **Syringomyelia** | Central canal cavitation (cervical cord) | Bilateral loss of pain/temperature in "cape" distribution (crossing spinothalamic fibers affected first); dorsal columns & corticospinal spared initially |
| **Tabes dorsalis** | Neurosyphilis | Dorsal column + dorsal root degeneration → loss of proprioception/vibration, sensory ataxia, Romberg positive, Argyll Robertson pupils |
| **Complete cord transection** | Trauma | Bilateral loss of all sensation & motor function below lesion; spinal shock acutely (areflexia) |

*High-yield distinguishing pearl: Anterior spinal artery syndrome = motor + pain/temp loss with proprioception SPARED (dorsal column supplied by posterior spinal arteries). Subacute combined degeneration = dorsal column + corticospinal BOTH affected (spares pain/temp — spinothalamic tract classically spared).*

## Blood Supply of Spinal Cord
- 1 anterior spinal artery (from vertebral arteries) supplies anterior 2/3
- 2 posterior spinal arteries supply posterior 1/3
- **Artery of Adamkiewicz** (great anterior segmental medullary artery, arises from a lower thoracic/upper lumbar intercostal/lumbar artery, typically T9–T12 on the left) — major supply to lower 2/3 of spinal cord; injury during aortic aneurysm repair → anterior spinal artery syndrome/paraplegia

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common site of berry aneurysm: anterior communicating artery
- Posterior communicating artery aneurysm → painful CN III palsy with pupil dilation (surgical) vs diabetic CN III palsy is pupil-sparing
- MCA is the most common vessel occluded in ischemic stroke
- PCA occlusion → contralateral homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing
- Charcot's artery of cerebral hemorrhage = lenticulostriate branches of MCA (supplies internal capsule/basal ganglia)
- Dorsal column pathway decussates in the medulla (internal arcuate fibers); spinothalamic tract decussates in the spinal cord itself
- Anterior spinal artery syndrome spares dorsal columns (proprioception/vibration intact)
- Artery of Adamkiewicz injury during aortic surgery → classic cause of iatrogenic paraplegia
