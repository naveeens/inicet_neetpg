# Pelvis Perineum Reproductive Anatomy

## Pelvic Diaphragm
- Formed by **levator ani** (puborectalis, pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus) + **coccygeus**
- Puborectalis: sling around anorectal junction, maintains anorectal angle, key to fecal continence
- Pudendal nerve (S2-S4) — "keeps the parts and pushes off the poo" pathway: exits pelvis via greater sciatic foramen, crosses ischial spine, re-enters via lesser sciatic foramen, runs in **pudendal (Alcock's) canal** on lateral wall of ischioanal fossa → gives inferior rectal, perineal, and dorsal nerve of penis/clitoris branches
- **Pudendal nerve block**: landmark = ischial spine, used for pain relief in labor/episiotomy

## Perineum — Triangles
Bounded by: pubic symphysis (anterior), ischial tuberosities (lateral), coccyx (posterior); divided by an imaginary line between ischial tuberosities into:
| Triangle | Contents |
|---|---|
| Urogenital triangle (anterior) | External genitalia, urethra, perineal membrane, superficial/deep perineal pouches |
| Anal triangle (posterior) | Anal canal, ischioanal fossae |

### Perineal Pouches
| Pouch | Boundaries | Contents (male) | Contents (female) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superficial perineal pouch | Between Colles' fascia and perineal membrane | Root of penis (crura, bulb), superficial perineal muscles (ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus, superficial transverse perineal) | Clitoris, bulbs of vestibule, same muscles |
| Deep perineal pouch | Between perineal membrane and superior fascia of urogenital diaphragm | Membranous urethra, external urethral sphincter, deep transverse perineal muscle, bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands | Membranous urethra, external urethral sphincter, part of vagina |

*High-yield: Straddle injury / rupture of bulbar urethra → urine extravasates into superficial perineal pouch, tracks into scrotum, penis, and up the anterior abdominal wall deep to Scarpa's fascia — limited by attachments of Colles' fascia to ischiopubic rami and perineal body (cannot spread into thigh).*

## Female Reproductive Anatomy
- **Uterus**: normally anteverted (angle between cervix & vagina) and anteflexed (angle between body & cervix) position
- Blood supply: uterine artery (branch of internal iliac/anterior division) — **crosses ABOVE and in front of ureter** near the lateral vaginal fornix/cervix ("water under the bridge") — critical relation, at risk during hysterectomy when clamping uterine vessels
- Ovarian artery arises directly from abdominal aorta (below renal arteries) — descends via **infundibulopelvic (suspensory) ligament of ovary**, crosses ureter at pelvic brim
- Broad ligament: double fold of peritoneum, contains uterine tube, round ligament, ligament of ovary, uterine/ovarian vessels, ureter (in its base)
- **Round ligament** = remnant of gubernaculum (homologous to gubernaculum testis), passes through deep inguinal ring, inguinal canal, to labium majus
- Ligament of ovary + round ligament of uterus are both derivatives of gubernaculum, continuous with each other, attach at the uterotubal junction

## Ureter Relations in Pelvis — Two Classic Crossings
| Site | Structure Crossing Ureter | Clinical Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Pelvic brim | Ovarian vessels cross ureter ("water under the bridge" applies at pelvic brim too, ovarian vessels superficial to ureter) | Ligating ovarian vessels risk during oophorectomy |
| Base of broad ligament / lateral to cervix | **Uterine artery crosses ureter** — artery passes anterior/superior to ureter | **Most common site of iatrogenic ureteric injury during hysterectomy** |

## Male Reproductive Anatomy
- **Spermatic cord** contents: vas deferens, testicular artery, pampiniform plexus, cremasteric artery, artery to vas deferens, genital branch of genitofemoral nerve, ilioinguinal nerve (outside cord proper), lymphatics, processus vaginalis remnant
- Testicular artery arises from abdominal aorta (below renal arteries, same level as ovarian artery embryologically) — long course explains vulnerability in testicular torsion
- Venous drainage: pampiniform plexus → testicular vein → **right testicular vein drains directly into IVC**; **left testicular vein drains into left renal vein** (at right angle) — explains higher incidence of **left-sided varicocele** ("bag of worms," may signal renal cell carcinoma with tumor thrombus if sudden onset in older male)
- Prostate: 5 lobes/zones — peripheral zone (most common site of **prostate cancer**, palpable on DRE), transition zone (site of **BPH**), central zone
- Prostatic urethra: contains urethral crest, prostatic utricle (Müllerian remnant), openings of ejaculatory ducts

## Testicular Torsion vs Epididymo-orchitis (clinical anatomy correlate)
| Feature | Testicular Torsion | Epididymo-orchitis |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Adolescents | Sexually active adults |
| Cremasteric reflex | Absent | Present |
| Prehn's sign (elevation relieves pain) | Negative (no relief) | Positive (relief) |
| Onset | Sudden, severe | Gradual |

## Descent of Testis & Processus Vaginalis
- Testis descends from genital ridge (retroperitoneal, near kidney) through inguinal canal into scrotum by birth, guided by gubernaculum
- **Processus vaginalis**: peritoneal diverticulum preceding testis; normally obliterates except distal part forming tunica vaginalis
- Failure of obliteration → **congenital indirect inguinal hernia** or **communicating hydrocele**
- Partial obliteration with fluid collection → **non-communicating hydrocele** or **encysted hydrocele of cord**
- **Cryptorchidism** (undescended testis) → increased risk of infertility and testicular malignancy (seminoma), most common site of arrest = inguinal canal

## Perineal Body & Episiotomy
- Perineal body (central tendon of perineum): fibromuscular node where bulbospongiosus, external anal sphincter, superficial/deep transverse perineal muscles, levator ani converge — critical for pelvic floor support; torn in perineal trauma/childbirth
- Episiotomy: mediolateral preferred over midline in many settings to avoid extension into anal sphincter/rectum (though midline gives less blood loss/dyspareunia but more risk of 3rd/4th degree tears)

## Pelvic Diameters (Obstetric Relevance)
| Diameter | Plane | Approx Value |
|---|---|---|
| Anteroposterior (true conjugate) | Pelvic inlet | ~11 cm |
| Diagonal conjugate | Clinically measured (sacral promontory to pubic symphysis) | ~12–13 cm |
| Transverse | Pelvic inlet | ~13 cm |
| Interspinous (bispinous) | Mid-pelvis (narrowest plane) | ~10.5 cm |

*High-yield: The interspinous/bispinous diameter (between ischial spines) is the narrowest diameter of the pelvis, clinically important in assessing adequacy for vaginal delivery.*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- "Water under the bridge": uterine artery crosses anterior/superior to the ureter near the cervix — most common site of iatrogenic ureteric injury in hysterectomy
- Ovarian artery arises directly from the aorta (below renal arteries); crosses ureter at the pelvic brim
- Left testicular vein drains into left renal vein (right angle) — explains left-sided predominance of varicocele
- Right testicular vein drains directly into IVC
- Pudendal nerve block landmark = ischial spine
- Straddle injuries rupturing the bulbar urethra cause urine to track into the superficial perineal pouch, scrotum, and anterior abdominal wall (deep to Scarpa's fascia) but not the thigh
- BPH arises in the transition zone; prostate cancer arises predominantly in the peripheral zone
- Failure of processus vaginalis closure → congenital indirect inguinal hernia / communicating hydrocele
- Testicular torsion: absent cremasteric reflex, negative Prehn's sign — surgical emergency
