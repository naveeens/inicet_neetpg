# Thorax Cardiac Anatomy

## Mediastinum Subdivisions
| Division | Boundaries | Key Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Superior mediastinum | Above pericardium, below thoracic inlet | Great vessels (arch of aorta, brachiocephalic veins), trachea, esophagus, thymus, phrenic & vagus nerves |
| Anterior mediastinum | Between sternum & pericardium | Thymus (in children), fat, lymph nodes |
| Middle mediastinum | Pericardium & its contents | Heart, roots of great vessels, phrenic nerves |
| Posterior mediastinum | Behind pericardium, in front of vertebral bodies T5-T12 | Esophagus, descending thoracic aorta, azygos/hemiazygos veins, thoracic duct, sympathetic trunk, vagus nerves |

## Heart — Surfaces, Borders, Chambers
| Surface | Chamber Forming It |
|---|---|
| Anterior (sternocostal) | Right ventricle (mostly) |
| Diaphragmatic (inferior) | Left ventricle (mostly), some right ventricle |
| Left (pulmonary/obtuse margin) | Left ventricle |
| Base (posterior) | Left atrium (receives 4 pulmonary veins) |

| Border | Structure |
|---|---|
| Right border | Right atrium |
| Left border | Left ventricle (+ auricle) |
| Inferior border | Right ventricle (mostly), apex of LV |
| Superior border | Both atria + great vessels |

- **Apex beat**: normally at left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line (~9 cm from midline)
- Right atrium receives SVC, IVC, coronary sinus
- Interatrial septum: fossa ovalis (remnant of foramen ovale) — septum primum on left side, septum secundum forms the limbus

## Conduction System
| Structure | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **SA node** | Upper part of right atrium, near SVC opening (crista terminalis) | Primary pacemaker; supplied by SA nodal artery (from RCA in ~60%, LCX in ~40%) |
| **AV node** | Floor of right atrium, in triangle of Koch (apex near coronary sinus, base at tricuspid annulus, hypotenuse = tendon of Todaro) | Delays impulse; supplied by AV nodal artery (branch of RCA in ~90%, "right dominant") |
| **Bundle of His** | Membranous interventricular septum | Conducts to bundle branches |
| **Purkinje fibers** | Subendocardial, ventricles | Rapid ventricular depolarization |

*High-yield: RCA supplies SA node (~60%) and AV node (~90%) — explains why inferior MI (RCA territory) commonly causes sinus bradycardia and AV blocks.*

## Coronary Arteries
| Artery | Origin | Supplies |
|---|---|---|
| **Right coronary artery (RCA)** | Right aortic sinus | Right atrium, right ventricle, SA/AV nodes, inferior wall of LV, posterior 1/3 of interventricular septum (via PDA in right-dominant) |
| **Left main → LAD** | Left aortic sinus | Anterior 2/3 of interventricular septum, anterior wall LV, apex |
| **Left main → LCX (circumflex)** | Left aortic sinus | Lateral & posterior wall of LV, left atrium |

- **"Right dominant" circulation (~70-85%)**: PDA (posterior descending artery) arises from RCA
- Coronary sinus drains into right atrium; great cardiac vein runs with LAD

## Pericardium
- Fibrous pericardium (outer, tough, attaches to central tendon of diaphragm & great vessel adventitia) + serous pericardium (parietal + visceral/epicardium layers, pericardial cavity between them)
- **Transverse pericardial sinus**: behind ascending aorta/pulmonary trunk, in front of SVC — surgical importance for cross-clamping great vessels during cardiac surgery
- **Oblique pericardial sinus**: behind left atrium, bounded by pulmonary veins & IVC
- Pericardial effusion/tamponade: Beck's triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds); pulsus paradoxus

## Great Vessels & Arch of Aorta
- Arch of aorta gives 3 branches (right to left): **Brachiocephalic trunk** (→ right subclavian + right common carotid), **Left common carotid**, **Left subclavian**
- Ligamentum arteriosum (remnant of fetal ductus arteriosus) connects arch of aorta to pulmonary trunk — left recurrent laryngeal nerve hooks around it
- Coarctation of aorta: usually just distal to left subclavian origin, near ligamentum arteriosum; notching of ribs (3rd-8th) from dilated intercostal collaterals; radiofemoral delay

## Trachea & Bronchi
- Trachea bifurcates at **carina** — level of sternal angle (T4/T5 vertebra)
- Right main bronchus: **more vertical, wider, shorter** → foreign bodies & aspirated material more commonly lodge here
- Right lung: 3 lobes, 10 segments; Left lung: 2 lobes, 8-10 segments (lingula = homolog of middle lobe)

## Lung Root/Hilum Relations
At each hilum, from anterior to posterior: **V**ein, **A**rtery, **B**ronchus (mnemonic "VAB" — anterior to posterior at both hila). From superior to inferior differs: Right — eparterial bronchus is most superior (bronchus above artery); Left — pulmonary artery is most superior (artery above bronchus).

## Diaphragm — Openings
| Level | Structure Passing |
|---|---|
| T8 | IVC, right phrenic nerve |
| T10 | Esophagus, vagus nerves (anterior & posterior), esophageal branches of left gastric vessels |
| T12 (aortic hiatus, behind diaphragm, technically retrocrural) | Aorta, thoracic duct, azygos vein |

**Mnemonic: "I 8 (ate) 10 Eggs At 12" (IVC-T8, Esophagus-T10, Aorta-T12)**

- Left phrenic nerve pierces diaphragm alone (through muscle of central tendon near apex of heart); Right phrenic nerve travels within/alongside IVC through caval opening
- Diaphragm motor supply: phrenic nerve (C3, C4, C5) — "C3, 4, 5 keep the diaphragm alive"
- Diaphragmatic hernia (congenital) — most common = **Bochdalek hernia** (posterolateral, left side more common); Morgagni hernia = anteromedial, retrosternal, rare

## Thoracic Duct
- Begins as cisterna chyli (L1-L2, behind aorta) → ascends through aortic hiatus → right of midline in lower thorax → crosses to left at T4-T5 vertebral level → ascends in superior mediastinum → drains into **left venous angle** (junction of left subclavian & left internal jugular veins)
- Drains lymph from entire body except right upper quadrant (right side of head/neck, right arm, right hemithorax) which drains via **right lymphatic duct**

## High-Yield One-Liners
- SA node supplied by RCA in ~60%, AV node by RCA in ~90% — explains bradyarrhythmias in inferior (RCA) MI
- Right dominant circulation (PDA from RCA) is most common pattern (~70-85%)
- Right main bronchus is wider, shorter, more vertical → aspirated foreign bodies lodge here more often
- Left recurrent laryngeal nerve loops around the ligamentum arteriosum/arch of aorta; right RLN loops around right subclavian artery
- Coarctation of aorta classically occurs near the ligamentum arteriosum (juxtaductal)
- Thoracic duct drains into the left venous angle; crosses midline at T4-T5 vertebral level
- Diaphragm openings mnemonic: IVC at T8, esophagus at T10, aorta at T12
- Transverse pericardial sinus lies behind aorta/pulmonary trunk and in front of SVC — used for cross-clamping in cardiac surgery
