# Embryology Congenital Anomalies

## Trilaminar Germ Layers & Derivatives
| Layer | Key Derivatives |
|---|---|
| **Ectoderm** | Epidermis, CNS/PNS (neural tube, neural crest), pituitary (anterior — Rathke's pouch, also ectoderm), lens, inner ear, tooth enamel |
| **Mesoderm** | Muscle, bone, connective tissue, cardiovascular system, kidneys/gonads (intermediate mesoderm), dermis, spleen |
| **Endoderm** | GI tract epithelium & glands (liver, pancreas), respiratory epithelium, thyroid/parathyroid, thymus, urinary bladder epithelium |

- **Neural crest** derivatives (high-yield, "ectoderm's gift"): dorsal root ganglia, autonomic ganglia, adrenal medulla, melanocytes, Schwann cells, meninges (pia/arachnoid), aorticopulmonary septum, pharyngeal arch cartilages, odontoblasts, parafollicular (C) cells of thyroid
- Neural crest cell migration defects → **Hirschsprung disease** (failure of neural crest migration to distal colon → aganglionic segment), **DiGeorge syndrome** (defective 3rd/4th pharyngeal pouch + neural crest, 22q11 deletion — cardiac defects, thymic aplasia, hypocalcemia, cleft palate — "CATCH-22")

## Folic Acid & Neural Tube Defects
- Neural tube closes by ~day 28 — folic acid supplementation (400 mcg/day, periconceptional) reduces risk of NTDs
- **Anencephaly**: failure of cranial neuropore closure
- **Spina bifida** (spectrum): occulta (vertebral defect only, often incidental) → spina bifida with meningocele (meninges herniate) → myelomeningocele (meninges + spinal cord herniate, most severe common form, associated with **Arnold-Chiari II malformation**) 
- Elevated maternal serum/amniotic **alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)** → screens for open NTDs (and abdominal wall defects); low AFP associated with Down syndrome

## Pharyngeal (Branchial) Apparatus
| Arch | Nerve | Skeletal/Muscular Derivatives | Artery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (mandibular) | CN V (trigeminal) | Muscles of mastication, Meckel's cartilage → malleus, incus; mandible | Maxillary artery |
| 2nd (hyoid) | CN VII (facial) | Muscles of facial expression, stapes, styloid process, lesser horn of hyoid | Stapedial artery (largely regresses) |
| 3rd | CN IX (glossopharyngeal) | Stylopharyngeus, greater horn of hyoid | Common carotid + proximal internal carotid |
| 4th | CN X (superior laryngeal) | Cricothyroid, pharyngeal constrictors | Arch of aorta (left), proximal right subclavian (right) |
| 6th | CN X (recurrent laryngeal) | Intrinsic laryngeal muscles (except cricothyroid) | Ductus arteriosus (left), proximal pulmonary artery (right) |

**Pharyngeal pouches (endoderm) → structures:**
| Pouch | Derivative |
|---|---|
| 1st | Middle ear cavity, eustachian tube |
| 2nd | Palatine tonsil (epithelial lining) |
| 3rd | **Inferior parathyroids** + thymus (dorsal → thymus; ventral bulb → inferior parathyroid — note the crossover: 3rd pouch structures descend further, ending up inferior) |
| 4th | **Superior parathyroids** + ultimopharyngeal body (contributes C cells to thyroid, neural crest origin) |

*High-yield: 3rd pouch gives inferior parathyroids (paired with thymus, which "drags" them down further) while 4th pouch gives superior parathyroids — a classic reversal trap.*

**Pharyngeal clefts (ectoderm)**: only 1st cleft persists → external auditory meatus; others normally obliterate — persistence → **branchial cleft cyst/fistula** (lateral neck mass, along anterior border of sternocleidomastoid).

## Cardiac Embryology & Congenital Heart Defects
- Heart tube looping and septation (atrial septum, interventricular septum, aorticopulmonary septum from neural crest) between weeks 4-8 — critical teratogenic window
- **Septum primum + septum secundum** → foramen ovale (functional right-to-left shunt in fetus, allows oxygenated placental blood to bypass lungs); closes functionally at birth (pressure change), anatomically over following months. Patent foramen ovale = common incidental finding
- **Ductus arteriosus** (connects pulmonary trunk to aortic arch) closes functionally within hours-days of birth (due to ↑ O2, ↓ prostaglandins), anatomically becomes ligamentum arteriosum; kept open with **PGE1 (alprostadil)** in duct-dependent congenital heart defects; closed pharmacologically with **indomethacin/NSAIDs** in PDA

| Defect | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **VSD** (most common congenital heart defect overall) | Membranous septum most common site; L→R shunt; small VSDs often close spontaneously |
| **ASD** | Ostium secundum most common type; L→R shunt; may be asymptomatic till adulthood |
| **PDA** | Continuous "machinery" murmur; associated with maternal rubella, prematurity; more common at high altitude |
| **Tetralogy of Fallot** (most common cyanotic CHD presenting after infancy) | 4 components: VSD, pulmonary stenosis (most important determinant of severity), overriding aorta, RVH; "boot-shaped" heart on X-ray; **Tet spells** relieved by squatting (↑ SVR → ↓ right-to-left shunt) |
| **Transposition of great arteries** (most common cyanotic CHD presenting at birth) | Aorta from RV, pulmonary artery from LV; incompatible with life unless mixing (PDA/ASD/VSD present) — "egg on a string" X-ray |
| **Coarctation of aorta** | Associated with Turner syndrome, bicuspid aortic valve; notching of ribs; differential BP/pulses upper vs lower limb |

*High-yield cyanotic mnemonic ("5 Ts"): Tetralogy of Fallot, Transposition of great arteries, Truncus arteriosus, Tricuspid atresia, Total anomalous pulmonary venous return (TAPVR).*

## Fetal Circulation Shunts (all right-to-left in fetus)
| Shunt | Connects | Postnatal Remnant |
|---|---|---|
| Ductus venosus | Umbilical vein → IVC (bypasses liver) | Ligamentum venosum |
| Foramen ovale | RA → LA | Fossa ovalis |
| Ductus arteriosus | Pulmonary trunk → aortic arch | Ligamentum arteriosum |
| Umbilical vein | Placenta → fetus (oxygenated blood) | Ligamentum teres (round ligament of liver) |
| Umbilical arteries (2) | Fetus → placenta (deoxygenated) | Medial umbilical ligaments |

## GI Congenital Anomalies
| Anomaly | Embryologic Basis | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Duodenal atresia** | Failure of recanalization of duodenal lumen (solid stage) | "Double bubble" sign on X-ray; associated with **Down syndrome** |
| **Meckel's diverticulum** | Persistent vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct | Rule of 2s: ~2% population, within 2 feet of ileocecal valve, ~2 inches long, 2 types of ectopic tissue (gastric/pancreatic); most common GI congenital anomaly |
| **Omphalocele** | Failure of intestines to return to abdomen after physiological herniation; midgut herniates through umbilical ring | Covered by peritoneum/amnion (sac present); associated with other anomalies (cardiac, chromosomal) |
| **Gastroschisis** | Defect in body wall lateral to umbilicus (not through umbilical ring) | No covering sac; bowel exposed directly to amniotic fluid; usually isolated defect |
| **Hirschsprung disease** | Failure of neural crest cell migration → aganglionic segment (rectum/distal colon, always involves anus) | Failure to pass meconium, massive distension proximal to aganglionic segment; diagnosis by rectal biopsy (absence of ganglion cells) |
| **Tracheoesophageal fistula** | Abnormal septation of foregut into trachea/esophagus | Most common type = esophageal atresia with distal TEF ("Type C"); presents with excessive drooling, choking on feeds, polyhydramnios (proximal pouch) |
| **Annular pancreas** | Abnormal rotation of ventral pancreatic bud encircles duodenum | Duodenal obstruction |

## Renal & Genital Congenital Anomalies
| Anomaly | Basis | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Horseshoe kidney** | Fusion of lower poles, trapped below inferior mesenteric artery during ascent | Kidneys unable to ascend normally to lumbar region |
| **Unilateral renal agenesis** | Failure of ureteric bud/metanephric blastema interaction | Compensatory hypertrophy of contralateral kidney |
| **Bicornuate uterus / other Müllerian anomalies** | Failure of fusion of paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts | Associated with recurrent pregnancy loss, malpresentation |
| **Uterus didelphys** | Complete failure of fusion | Two separate uteri, cervices |
| **Hypospadias** | Failure of urethral folds to fuse ventrally | Urethral opening on ventral penile surface (more common than epispadias) |
| **Epispadias** | Abnormal position of genital tubercle | Urethral opening on dorsal surface; associated with bladder exstrophy |

- Mesonephric (Wolffian) duct → male internal genitalia (epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle) — persists under influence of testosterone
- Paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct → female internal genitalia (uterine tubes, uterus, upper vagina) — regresses in males due to **Müllerian inhibiting substance (MIS/AMH)** from Sertoli cells
- **SRY gene** (Y chromosome) → testis-determining factor; drives differentiation of bipotential gonad into testis

## High-Yield One-Liners
- VSD = most common congenital heart defect overall; TOF = most common cyanotic CHD presenting after infancy; TGA = most common cyanotic CHD presenting at birth
- Tet spells relieved by squatting/knee-chest position (increases SVR, reduces right-to-left shunting)
- Meckel's diverticulum = most common GI congenital anomaly, follows "rule of 2s"
- Omphalocele has a covering sac (peritoneum/amnion); gastroschisis does not
- Duodenal atresia → "double bubble" sign, classically associated with Down syndrome
- 3rd pharyngeal pouch → inferior parathyroid + thymus; 4th pouch → superior parathyroid (reversal trap)
- DiGeorge syndrome = 22q11 deletion, 3rd/4th pouch defect = cardiac defects + thymic aplasia + hypocalcemia (CATCH-22)
- Neural crest gives rise to adrenal medulla, melanocytes, Schwann cells, and dorsal root ganglia — "the ectoderm's mesenchyme"
- Ductus arteriosus kept open with PGE1 (prostaglandin), closed with indomethacin (NSAID/prostaglandin inhibitor)
- Hirschsprung disease = failure of neural crest migration → aganglionic segment always involving the anus/rectum
