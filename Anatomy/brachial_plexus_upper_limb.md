# Brachial Plexus Upper Limb

## Formation
Ventral rami of **C5–C8, T1** → Roots → Trunks → Divisions → Cords → Branches.

**Mnemonic: "Randy Travis Drinks Cold Beer"** (Roots, Trunks, Divisions, Cords, Branches)

| Level | Components |
|---|---|
| Roots | C5, C6, C7, C8, T1 |
| Trunks | Upper (C5-C6), Middle (C7), Lower (C8-T1) |
| Divisions | Each trunk → anterior & posterior division (6 total) |
| Cords | Lateral (ant. div. of upper+middle trunk), Posterior (post. div. of all 3 trunks), Medial (ant. div. of lower trunk) — named by relation to axillary artery |
| Branches | Terminal 5 nerves + numerous proximal branches |

## Key Proximal (Pre-Cord) Branches
| Nerve | Origin | Muscles Supplied | Injury Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dorsal scapular | C5 root | Rhomboids, levator scapulae | Winging (medial border, scapula shifted laterally) |
| Long thoracic | C5-C6-C7 roots | Serratus anterior | **Winging of scapula** (classic — medial border prominent, cannot push against wall / cannot raise arm above horizontal) |
| Suprascapular | Upper trunk | Supraspinatus, infraspinatus | Weak initiation of abduction, weak lateral rotation |
| Nerve to subclavius | Upper trunk | Subclavius | — |

## Terminal Branches
| Nerve | Cord Origin | Root Value | Key Motor | Key Sensory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Musculocutaneous | Lateral cord | C5-C6-C7 | Biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis (elbow flexion, forearm supination) | Lateral forearm (lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm) |
| Median | Lateral + Medial cord | C6-C8, T1 | Most forearm flexors/pronators (except FCU, ulnar half of FDP), thenar muscles (LOAF), lumbricals 1&2 | Palmar lateral 3½ fingers |
| Ulnar | Medial cord | C8-T1 | FCU, ulnar half FDP, hypothenar, interossei, adductor pollicis, medial 2 lumbricals | Palmar + dorsal medial 1½ fingers |
| Axillary (circumflex) | Posterior cord | C5-C6 | Deltoid, teres minor | Regimental badge area (over deltoid) |
| Radial | Posterior cord | C5-C8 (T1) | Triceps, all forearm extensors, brachioradialis | Posterior arm/forearm, dorsal hand (1st web space) |

## Nerve Injury Syndromes
| Injury | Mechanism | Position/Deformity | Key Deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Erb's Palsy (upper trunk, C5-C6)** | Traction — fall on shoulder, birth trauma (shoulder dystocia), excessive lateral traction of head from shoulder | **"Waiter's tip" position** — arm adducted, medially rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated, wrist flexed | Loss of abduction (deltoid, supraspinatus), lateral rotation (infraspinatus), elbow flexion (biceps), supination (biceps) |
| **Klumpke's Palsy (lower trunk, C8-T1)** | Traction — breech delivery, grabbing a branch while falling | **Claw hand** | Loss of intrinsic hand muscles; often associated Horner's syndrome (T1 root involves sympathetic fibers to superior cervical ganglion) |
| **Axillary nerve** | Surgical neck of humerus fracture, anterior shoulder dislocation, intramuscular injection (improper site) | Flat deltoid contour | Loss of abduction beyond 15°, sensory loss regimental badge area |
| **Radial nerve — midshaft humerus fracture** | Fracture at radial (spiral) groove | **Wrist drop** | Loss of wrist/finger extension, sensory loss dorsal 1st web space |
| **Radial nerve — posterior interosseous branch** | Monteggia fracture, elbow dislocation, "Saturday night palsy" variants | Finger drop (wrist extension partially spared via ECRL) | Motor only, no sensory loss |
| **Median nerve — supracondylar fracture / elbow** | Supracondylar humerus fracture (common in children) | **"Hand of benediction"** on attempted finger flexion (index & middle finger fail to flex) | Loss of pronation, thenar LOAF muscles (in high lesions), sensory loss lateral palm |
| **Median nerve — carpal tunnel** | Compression at wrist (flexor retinaculum) | Thenar wasting, ape-hand deformity (chronic) | Sensory loss palmar lateral 3½ fingers, weak thumb opposition/abduction |
| **Ulnar nerve — medial epicondyle fracture** | Fracture/entrapment at elbow (cubital tunnel) | **Claw hand** (more marked in low lesions — "ulnar paradox": higher lesion = less clawing because FDP also paralyzed) | Loss of finger abduction/adduction (interossei), adduction of thumb |
| **Long thoracic nerve** | Axillary surgery (mastectomy), stab wounds, prolonged backpack pressure | **Winging of scapula** | Cannot push against wall, weak forward flexion above horizontal |
| **Thoracodorsal nerve** | Axillary lymph node dissection | Weak adduction/medial rotation (latissimus dorsi) | Used in swimming/climbing movements |

*High-yield "ulnar paradox": A more proximal (higher) ulnar nerve lesion produces LESS clawing than a distal lesion, because the FDP (which flexes the DIP joints, contributing to claw appearance) is also paralyzed and cannot contribute to deformity.*

## LOAF Muscles (Median Nerve, hand)
**L**ateral 2 lumbricals, **O**pponens pollicis, **A**bductor pollicis brevis, **F**lexor pollicis brevis.

## Axillary Artery — Relations to Pectoralis Minor (3 parts, 6 branches — mnemonic "Sub, Sub, Th-La-Th; Sc, Circ, Circ")
| Part | Relation | Branches |
|---|---|---|
| 1st part | Medial to pec minor | Superior thoracic |
| 2nd part | Posterior to pec minor | Thoracoacromial, Lateral thoracic |
| 3rd part | Lateral to pec minor | Subscapular, Anterior circumflex humeral, Posterior circumflex humeral |

## Quadrangular & Triangular Spaces
| Space | Boundaries | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Quadrangular space | Teres minor (above), teres major (below), long head of triceps (medial), surgical neck of humerus (lateral) | Axillary nerve, posterior circumflex humeral vessels |
| Triangular interval | Teres major, long head of triceps, lateral head of triceps/humerus | Radial nerve, profunda brachii vessels |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Cords are named by their relation to the second part of the axillary artery
- Erb's palsy = C5-C6 upper trunk = "waiter's tip"; Klumpke's = C8-T1 lower trunk = claw hand ± Horner's
- Long thoracic nerve injury = winged scapula (serratus anterior palsy)
- Axillary nerve at highest risk in surgical neck humerus fracture and shoulder dislocation
- Radial nerve at highest risk in midshaft humerus fracture (spiral groove)
- Median nerve at highest risk in supracondylar humerus fracture (anterior interosseous branch particularly)
- Ulnar nerve at highest risk at the medial epicondyle (funny bone) and in cubital tunnel syndrome
- Carpal tunnel syndrome spares the palmar cutaneous branch of median nerve (arises proximal to flexor retinaculum) — thenar eminence sensation preserved
- Winging of scapula: medial border prominent = long thoracic nerve (serratus anterior); lateral border/inferior angle prominent = trapezius (accessory nerve, CN XI)
