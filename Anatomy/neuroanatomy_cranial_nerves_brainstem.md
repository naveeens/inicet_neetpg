# Neuroanatomy Cranial Nerves Brainstem

## Cranial Nerve Overview
| No. | Nerve | Type | Foramen (exit from skull) | Brainstem Attachment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Olfactory | Sensory | Cribriform plate | Forebrain (not brainstem) |
| II | Optic | Sensory | Optic canal | Forebrain (diencephalon) |
| III | Oculomotor | Motor | Superior orbital fissure | Midbrain (crus cerebri, ventral) |
| IV | Trochlear | Motor | Superior orbital fissure | Midbrain (only CN emerging **dorsally**) |
| V | Trigeminal | Mixed | V1-Superior orbital fissure, V2-Foramen rotundum, V3-Foramen ovale | Pons |
| VI | Abducens | Motor | Superior orbital fissure | Pontomedullary junction (medial) |
| VII | Facial | Mixed | Internal acoustic meatus → facial canal → stylomastoid foramen | Pontomedullary junction (lateral) |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Sensory | Internal acoustic meatus | Pontomedullary junction (lateral) |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Mixed | Jugular foramen | Medulla (post-olivary sulcus) |
| X | Vagus | Mixed | Jugular foramen | Medulla (post-olivary sulcus) |
| XI | Accessory | Motor | Jugular foramen | Medulla (cranial root) + spinal cord C1–C5 (spinal root) |
| XII | Hypoglossal | Motor | Hypoglossal canal | Medulla (pre-olivary sulcus) |

*High-yield: Only 3 cranial nerves pass through the jugular foramen — IX, X, XI. Only CN IV emerges from the dorsal surface of the brainstem and is the only CN to decussate fully before exiting.*

## Extraocular Muscles & Nerve Supply
| Muscle | Nerve | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Superior rectus, Inferior rectus, Medial rectus, Inferior oblique | III (Oculomotor) | As per LR6SO4 rule |
| Superior oblique | IV (Trochlear) | Depresses & intorts adducted eye |
| Lateral rectus | VI (Abducens) | Abduction |

**Mnemonic: "LR6 SO4 remaining 3"** — Lateral Rectus by CN6, Superior Oblique by CN4, all others by CN3.

## Trigeminal Nerve (V) — Three Divisions
| Division | Foramen | Sensory Territory | Motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 (Ophthalmic) | Superior orbital fissure | Cornea, forehead, upper eyelid, nose tip | None |
| V2 (Maxillary) | Foramen rotundum | Midface, upper lip, upper teeth | None |
| V3 (Mandibular) | Foramen ovale | Lower face, lower teeth, anterior 2/3 tongue (general sensation only) | Muscles of mastication (masseter, temporalis, pterygoids), mylohyoid, anterior belly digastric, tensor tympani, tensor veli palatini |

*Corneal reflex: Afferent = V1 (nasociliary branch); Efferent = VII (orbicularis oculi). Loss of corneal reflex with intact blink on opposite stimulation → ipsilateral V1 lesion.*

## Facial Nerve (VII) — Course & Branches
Intracranial → internal acoustic meatus → facial canal (genu, gives geniculate ganglion) → branches within canal → stylomastoid foramen → parotid gland (5 terminal branches).

| Branch (within facial canal) | Function |
|---|---|
| Greater petrosal nerve | Parasympathetic to lacrimal gland, nasal/palatine glands (via pterygopalatine ganglion) |
| Nerve to stapedius | Dampens ossicle movement (loss → hyperacusis) |
| Chorda tympani | Taste anterior 2/3 tongue + secretomotor to submandibular/sublingual glands (via submandibular ganglion) |

**Terminal branches (mnemonic "Two Zulus Buggered My Cat"):** Temporal, Zygomatic, Buccal, Marginal mandibular, Cervical.

### Facial Nerve Lesions
| Feature | UMN (Supranuclear) Lesion | LMN (Bell's Palsy / Nuclear-Infranuclear) |
|---|---|---|
| Forehead wrinkling | Preserved (bilateral cortical representation) | Lost |
| Cause | Stroke | Bell's palsy (idiopathic, HSV reactivation), parotid tumor, trauma |
| Distribution | Contralateral lower face only | Ipsilateral whole face |

*High-yield: Bell's palsy = LMN facial palsy — forehead involved (cannot wrinkle forehead, cannot close eye = lagophthalmos), taste loss if lesion proximal to chorda tympani takeoff, hyperacusis if proximal to nerve to stapedius.*

## Vagus Nerve (X) & Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve
- Right recurrent laryngeal nerve loops under **right subclavian artery**; left loops under **arch of aorta** (around ligamentum arteriosum) — explains left RLN's greater vulnerability in thoracic pathology (aortic aneurysm, lung cancer, mitral stenosis with LA enlargement — Ortner's syndrome).
- All intrinsic laryngeal muscles supplied by RLN **except cricothyroid** (supplied by external laryngeal nerve, a branch of superior laryngeal nerve).
- Bilateral RLN injury (e.g., thyroidectomy) → bilateral vocal cord paralysis in paramedian position → **stridor, airway obstruction** (adductors overpower abductors as recurrent fibers are lost, posterior cricoarytenoid — the only abductor — most vulnerable).
- Unilateral RLN injury → hoarseness (cord fixed in paramedian position).
- Superior laryngeal nerve injury → loss of high-pitched phonation (cricothyroid paralysis), some sensory loss above cords.

## Glossopharyngeal Nerve (IX)
- Sensory: posterior 1/3 tongue (taste + general sensation), pharynx (afferent limb of **gag reflex**), carotid body/sinus (chemo/baroreceptors)
- Motor: stylopharyngeus only
- Parasympathetic: parotid gland (via otic ganglion)
- Efferent of gag reflex = X (vagus, pharyngeal muscles)

## Brainstem Cross-Sections — Key Vascular Syndromes
| Syndrome | Vessel Occluded | Structures Involved | Clinical Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wallenberg (lateral medullary)** | PICA | Spinothalamic tract, CN IX/X nucleus, inferior cerebellar peduncle, sympathetic fibers, spinal trigeminal nucleus | Contralateral pain/temp loss (body), ipsilateral pain/temp loss (face), dysphagia/hoarseness, ipsilateral cerebellar ataxia, ipsilateral Horner's |
| **Medial medullary (Dejerine)** | Anterior spinal artery / vertebral artery | Corticospinal tract, medial lemniscus, CN XII | Contralateral hemiparesis (body), contralateral loss of proprioception/vibration, ipsilateral tongue deviation |
| **Weber syndrome** | Paramedian branches of PCA | Crus cerebri, CN III fascicles | Ipsilateral CN III palsy, contralateral hemiplegia |
| **Millard-Gubler** | Basilar artery (pontine branches) | CN VI, VII fascicles, corticospinal tract | Ipsilateral CN VI + VII palsy, contralateral hemiplegia |
| **Benedikt syndrome** | PCA branches | Red nucleus, CN III | Ipsilateral CN III palsy, contralateral tremor/ataxia |

*High-yield rule: "Crossed" (alternating) hemiplegia syndromes (ipsilateral cranial nerve palsy + contralateral body weakness/sensory loss) localize the lesion to the brainstem — the cranial nerve involved tells you the level (III=midbrain, VI/VII=pons, IX/X/XII=medulla).*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- CN with the longest intracranial course and most vulnerable to raised ICP/stretching → CN VI (abducens)
- Only cranial nerve to decussate completely and exit dorsally → CN IV (trochlear)
- Jugular foramen nerves: IX, X, XI (also internal jugular vein exits here)
- Corneal reflex afferent = V1; efferent = VII
- Gag reflex afferent = IX; efferent = X
- Chorda tympani carries taste (ant 2/3 tongue) + parasympathetics to submandibular/sublingual glands, but travels *with* lingual nerve (V3) peripherally
- Left recurrent laryngeal nerve is more commonly injured/involved in mediastinal disease due to its longer intrathoracic course around the aortic arch
- Bell's palsy = LMN facial nerve palsy = forehead affected; stroke = UMN = forehead spared
