# Histology Osteology Joints

## Epithelium Types & Locations (High-Yield Table)
| Epithelium | Location |
|---|---|
| Simple squamous | Alveoli, endothelium, Bowman's capsule (parietal layer), loop of Henle (thin segment) |
| Simple cuboidal | Kidney tubules (PCT/DCT), thyroid follicles, small gland ducts |
| Simple columnar | Stomach, intestine, gallbladder, uterus |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Respiratory tract (trachea, bronchi), efferent ductules of testis |
| Stratified squamous (non-keratinized) | Esophagus, oral cavity, vagina, anal canal (below pectinate line) |
| Stratified squamous (keratinized) | Epidermis |
| Transitional (urothelium) | Renal calyces, ureter, bladder, proximal urethra — allows distension |

## Bone Histology
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Woven bone** | Immature, disorganized collagen, first formed in fracture healing & fetal development; later remodeled to lamellar |
| **Lamellar bone** | Mature, organized in layers; compact (cortical) or cancellous (trabecular/spongy) |
| **Osteon (Haversian system)** | Functional unit of compact bone — concentric lamellae around a central Haversian canal (blood vessels, nerves); Volkmann's canals connect Haversian canals transversely |

### Bone Cells
| Cell | Origin | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Osteoblast | Mesenchymal stem cell | Bone formation, secretes osteoid (type I collagen) |
| Osteocyte | Osteoblast trapped in matrix (lacuna) | Maintains bone matrix, mechanosensing |
| Osteoclast | Fusion of monocyte/macrophage precursors (hematopoietic origin) | Bone resorption (via RANKL-RANK signaling); ruffled border, secretes HCl and collagenase |

*High-yield: Osteoblasts are mesenchymal in origin; osteoclasts are hematopoietic (monocyte-macrophage lineage) — classic exam trap testing embryological origin.*

## Bone Formation (Ossification)
| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Intramembranous** | Mesenchyme directly differentiates into bone (no cartilage intermediate) | Flat bones of skull, clavicle (part), mandible |
| **Endochondral** | Cartilage model formed first, then replaced by bone | Long bones, base of skull, most of the axial skeleton |

- **Primary ossification center**: appears in diaphysis (long bones) before birth
- **Secondary ossification center**: appears in epiphysis after birth
- **Epiphyseal (growth) plate**: zone of endochondral ossification responsible for longitudinal bone growth; zones from epiphysis to diaphysis: **Resting → Proliferative → Hypertrophic → Calcification → Ossification** (mnemonic: "Reserve Proliferates Hypertrophy Calcifies Ossifies")
- Clavicle: only long bone ossifying by intramembranous ossification (also first bone to begin ossifying in utero)

## Cartilage Types
| Type | Features | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Hyaline | Type II collagen, glassy matrix | Articular surfaces, costal cartilage, trachea, epiphyseal plate |
| Fibrocartilage | Type I + II collagen, high tensile strength | Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis, menisci, TMJ, labrum |
| Elastic | Elastin fibers + collagen | Epiglottis, pinna of ear, laryngeal cartilages (some) |

## Muscle Tissue Types
| Type | Nuclei | Striations | Control | Special Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skeletal | Multinucleate, peripheral | Yes | Voluntary | T-tubules at A-I junction (2 per sarcomere) |
| Cardiac | Single/binucleate, central | Yes | Involuntary | Intercalated discs (gap junctions — desmosomes for mechanical, gap junctions for electrical coupling); T-tubules at Z-line |
| Smooth | Single, central | No | Involuntary | No troponin (uses calmodulin-myosin light chain kinase pathway); dense bodies instead of Z-discs |

## Classification of Joints
| Type | Subtype | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous (synarthrosis)** | Suture, syndesmosis, gomphosis | Skull sutures, interosseous membrane, tooth-socket |
| **Cartilaginous (amphiarthrosis)** | Primary (synchondrosis — hyaline), Secondary (symphysis — fibrocartilage) | Epiphyseal plate (primary); pubic symphysis, intervertebral disc (secondary) |
| **Synovial (diarthrosis)** | Ball & socket, hinge, pivot, saddle, condyloid, plane | Shoulder/hip (ball & socket); elbow/knee (hinge); atlantoaxial (pivot); thumb CMC (saddle) |

### Synovial Joint Components
Articular cartilage (hyaline, avascular — nourished by synovial fluid), joint capsule (fibrous outer + synovial inner membrane), synovial fluid (viscous, hyaluronic acid-rich, nourishes cartilage and lubricates), ligaments, and in some joints: articular disc/meniscus (knee, TMJ, sternoclavicular).

## Long Bone Anatomy — Regions
| Region | Description |
|---|---|
| Epiphysis | End of bone, mostly cancellous, articular cartilage covers surface |
| Metaphysis | Between epiphysis and diaphysis, contains growth plate (in growing bone) — most vascular, common site of hematogenous osteomyelitis in children |
| Diaphysis | Shaft, compact bone, medullary cavity with marrow |
| Periosteum | Outer fibrous + inner osteogenic layer, Sharpey's fibers anchor to bone |

*High-yield: Acute hematogenous osteomyelitis in children characteristically starts in the metaphysis of long bones (rich, sluggish vascular supply from nutrient artery loops) — most common organism Staphylococcus aureus.*

## Ossification Timeline Pearls (commonly tested ages)
- Anterior fontanelle closes ~18 months (largest, diamond-shaped, bregma)
- Posterior fontanelle closes ~2-3 months (triangular, lambda)
- Most secondary centers appear after birth; some (distal femur, proximal tibia) appear just before birth — used in forensic age estimation of stillbirth/live birth
- Bone age assessment: radiograph of wrist (ossification centers), used in growth/endocrine disorders

## Skull Sutures & Landmarks
| Suture | Location |
|---|---|
| Coronal | Between frontal & parietal bones |
| Sagittal | Between two parietal bones |
| Lambdoid | Between parietal & occipital bones |
| Squamous | Between temporal & parietal bones |

- **Bregma**: junction of coronal + sagittal sutures (anterior fontanelle site)
- **Lambda**: junction of sagittal + lambdoid sutures (posterior fontanelle site)
- **Pterion**: junction of frontal, parietal, temporal, sphenoid (greater wing) — thinnest part of skull, overlies **middle meningeal artery (anterior branch)** — classic site of **extradural (epidural) hematoma** after temporal trauma

## Vertebral Column — Regional Distinguishing Features
| Region | Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| Cervical (C1-C7) | Foramen transversarium (vertebral artery), bifid spinous process (C3-C6); C1 = atlas (no body), C2 = axis (dens/odontoid process) |
| Thoracic (T1-T12) | Costal facets for rib articulation |
| Lumbar (L1-L5) | Large body, no foramen transversarium/costal facets, sturdy for weight-bearing |
| Sacrum | 5 fused vertebrae |

- **Atlantoaxial joint**: pivot joint, dens of C2 held by transverse ligament of atlas against anterior arch of C1 — rupture/instability (e.g., Down syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis) risks cord compression
- **Jefferson fracture**: burst fracture of C1 (axial loading, e.g., diving injury)
- **Hangman's fracture**: bilateral C2 pedicle fracture (hyperextension, e.g., judicial hanging/high-speed deceleration)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Osteoblasts = mesenchymal origin (bone formation); osteoclasts = hematopoietic/monocyte origin (bone resorption)
- Clavicle is the only long bone formed by intramembranous ossification and the first bone to ossify in the embryo
- Epiphyseal plate zone order (epiphysis→diaphysis): resting, proliferative, hypertrophic, calcified, ossification
- Pterion overlies the middle meningeal artery — thinnest skull point, classic site of epidural hematoma after temporal blow
- Metaphysis is the most common site of acute hematogenous osteomyelitis in children due to sluggish sinusoidal blood flow
- Cardiac muscle intercalated discs provide both mechanical (desmosomes) and electrical (gap junction) coupling
- Smooth muscle contraction uses calmodulin/MLCK pathway (no troponin) unlike skeletal/cardiac muscle
- Anterior fontanelle (bregma) closes by ~18 months; posterior fontanelle (lambda) closes by ~2-3 months
- Articular (hyaline) cartilage is avascular and nourished by diffusion from synovial fluid
