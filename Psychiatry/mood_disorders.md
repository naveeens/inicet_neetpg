# Mood Disorders

## Classification (DSM-5-TR)
| Category | Disorders |
|---|---|
| Depressive disorders | Major Depressive Disorder (MDD), Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia), Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder, Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder |
| Bipolar & related disorders | Bipolar I, Bipolar II, Cyclothymic Disorder |

*High-yield: DSM-5 separated Bipolar disorders from Depressive disorders into their own chapter (placed between psychotic and depressive chapters) — reflecting their bridging position, unlike DSM-IV which grouped all "Mood Disorders" together.*

## Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

### Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5-TR)
≥5 of the following present during the same **2-week period**, representing a change from previous functioning; at least one must be **depressed mood** or **anhedonia**:
1. Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day
2. Markedly diminished interest/pleasure (anhedonia)
3. Significant weight loss/gain or appetite change
4. Insomnia or hypersomnia
5. Psychomotor agitation or retardation (observable by others)
6. Fatigue/loss of energy
7. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
8. Diminished concentration/indecisiveness
9. Recurrent thoughts of death, suicidal ideation, plan, or attempt

**Mnemonic: "SIG-E-CAPS"** — Sleep, Interest, Guilt, Energy, Concentration, Appetite, Psychomotor, Suicidality (+ depressed mood/anhedonia as core criteria)

- Symptoms cause clinically significant distress/impairment
- Not attributable to substance/another medical condition
- Not better explained by schizoaffective disorder, schizophrenia spectrum disorders
- No history of manic/hypomanic episode (else → Bipolar)

*High-yield: DSM-5 removed the "bereavement exclusion" present in DSM-IV — grief can now be diagnosed as MDD if criteria met, though clinical judgment must distinguish normal grief from depression.*

### Specifiers (frequently tested)
- **With melancholic features**: anhedonia, early morning worsening, early morning awakening (terminal insomnia), significant anorexia/weight loss, excessive guilt, psychomotor changes
- **With atypical features**: mood reactivity, hypersomnia, hyperphagia/weight gain, leaden paralysis, interpersonal rejection sensitivity
- **With psychotic features**: mood-congruent or incongruent delusions/hallucinations
- **With peripartum onset**: onset during pregnancy or within 4 weeks postpartum
- **With seasonal pattern**: regular temporal relationship with a particular time of year (winter depression, classically)
- **With catatonia**

## Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia)
- Depressed mood for **most of the day, more days than not, for ≥2 years** (≥1 year in children/adolescents)
- ≥2 of: poor appetite/overeating, insomnia/hypersomnia, low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration, hopelessness
- Never symptom-free for >2 months at a stretch
- May have superimposed MDD episodes ("**double depression**")

## Bipolar I Disorder

### Manic Episode — Diagnostic Criteria
Abnormally elevated, expansive, or irritable mood AND increased energy/activity, lasting **≥1 week** (or any duration if hospitalization required), with ≥3 (4 if mood only irritable) of:

**Mnemonic: "DIGFAST"**
- **D**istractibility
- **I**ndiscretion (risky behavior — spending, sexual)
- **G**randiosity
- **F**light of ideas / racing thoughts
- **A**ctivity increase / psychomotor agitation
- **S**leep need decreased
- **T**alkativeness / pressured speech

- Marked impairment in functioning, or hospitalization, or psychotic features
- Only **one manic episode** ever required for Bipolar I diagnosis (depressive episodes not mandatory, though almost always occur over lifetime)

### Hypomanic Episode
- Same symptoms as mania but duration **≥4 days**
- Unequivocal change in functioning observable by others, but **NOT severe enough to cause marked impairment** or require hospitalization, and **no psychotic features**
- Defines **Bipolar II** when it alternates with MDE (never a full manic episode)

### Bipolar II Disorder
- ≥1 Hypomanic episode + ≥1 Major Depressive episode
- **Never** a full manic episode (if one occurs, diagnosis becomes Bipolar I)
- Depressive episodes typically more frequent/disabling than in Bipolar I

## Cyclothymic Disorder
- Numerous periods of hypomanic symptoms AND numerous periods of depressive symptoms (not meeting full criteria for hypomanic/major depressive episode) for **≥2 years** (≥1 year children/adolescents)
- Never symptom-free for >2 months; never met full criteria for MDE/manic/hypomanic episode

## Management

### MDD
| Modality | Details |
|---|---|
| First-line pharmacotherapy | **SSRIs** (sertraline, escitalopram) — best safety/tolerability profile |
| Alternatives | SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine), Mirtazapine, Bupropion |
| Atypical depression | **MAOIs** classically preferred (or SSRIs) |
| Severe/psychotic depression, catatonia, pregnancy, treatment-refractory, high suicide risk | **ECT** — fastest and most effective |
| Psychotherapy | CBT, Interpersonal Therapy (IPT) — equally effective as pharmacotherapy in mild-moderate disease; combination best for severe/chronic |
| Treatment duration | Continue antidepressant **6–12 months after remission** (first episode); longer/indefinite for recurrent episodes |

*High-yield: TCAs (e.g., amitriptyline) are effective but poorly tolerated (anticholinergic, cardiotoxic in overdose — lethal in OD due to arrhythmia) — reserved for treatment-resistant cases. SSRIs are safer in overdose.*

*High-yield: Fluoxetine — longest half-life (activating, least withdrawal); Paroxetine — most anticholinergic/sedating SSRI, worst discontinuation syndrome; Sertraline — safest in pregnancy/cardiac disease.*

### Bipolar Disorder
| Phase | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Acute mania | **Lithium**, Valproate, or atypical antipsychotics (olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone) ± benzodiazepine for agitation |
| Acute mania (severe/psychotic) | Antipsychotic ± lithium/valproate; ECT if refractory or in pregnancy |
| Bipolar depression | **Lurasidone, quetiapine, olanzapine-fluoxetine combination** — antidepressant monotherapy avoided (risk of switching to mania) |
| Maintenance/prophylaxis | **Lithium** = gold standard (also reduces suicide risk); Valproate, Lamotrigine (better for depressive relapse prevention), atypical antipsychotics |

*High-yield: Lithium — narrow therapeutic index (0.6–1.2 mEq/L); monitor renal (creatinine) and thyroid (TSH) function; toxicity precipitated by dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics (thiazides), ACE inhibitors. Causes nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, hypothyroidism, Ebstein's anomaly (if used in first trimester pregnancy — teratogenic).*

*High-yield: Valproate — teratogenic (neural tube defects), hepatotoxic, causes PCOS-like picture; Carbamazepine — teratogenic, causes agranulocytosis/aplastic anemia, SJS (esp. in HLA-B*1502 positive, common in Asians).*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- MDD: ≥5 symptoms for ≥2 weeks, including depressed mood or anhedonia
- Manic episode: ≥1 week (or any duration if hospitalized); Hypomanic episode: ≥4 days, no marked impairment/psychosis/hospitalization
- Bipolar I needs only 1 manic episode ever; Bipolar II = hypomania + MDE, never full mania
- Dysthymia = chronic low-grade depression ≥2 years; Cyclothymia = chronic subthreshold mood swings ≥2 years
- Lithium = drug of choice for maintenance/prophylaxis of Bipolar disorder and reduces suicide risk
- SSRIs = first-line for MDD; safest in overdose compared to TCAs
- ECT = treatment of choice in severe depression with psychotic features, catatonia, high suicidality, and pregnancy
- Antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar depression risks precipitating a manic switch — avoid
- Melancholic features: anhedonia + early morning awakening + weight loss; Atypical features: mood reactivity + hypersomnia + hyperphagia + leaden paralysis
