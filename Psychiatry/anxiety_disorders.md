# Anxiety Disorders

## Classification Note (DSM-5-TR restructuring)
DSM-5 split the old DSM-IV "Anxiety Disorders" chapter into three separate chapters:
- **Anxiety Disorders**: GAD, Panic Disorder, Specific Phobia, Social Anxiety Disorder, Agoraphobia, Separation Anxiety Disorder, Selective Mutism
- **Obsessive-Compulsive & Related Disorders**: OCD, Body Dysmorphic Disorder, Hoarding Disorder, Trichotillomania, Excoriation Disorder
- **Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders**: PTSD, Acute Stress Disorder, Adjustment Disorder

*High-yield: OCD and PTSD are frequently tested "trick" questions — they are NO LONGER classified under Anxiety Disorders in DSM-5, despite anxiety being a prominent feature. Covered here together for exam completeness.*

## Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

### Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5-TR)
- Excessive anxiety and worry about multiple events/activities, occurring **more days than not for ≥6 months**
- Difficult to control the worry
- ≥3 of: restlessness, easy fatigability, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance (only 1 required in children)
- Causes significant distress/impairment; not attributable to substance/medical condition

## Panic Disorder

### Diagnostic Criteria
- Recurrent **unexpected** panic attacks: abrupt surge of intense fear/discomfort peaking within minutes, with ≥4 of: palpitations, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, choking sensation, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, chills/heat sensations, paresthesias, derealization/depersonalization, fear of losing control/dying
- ≥1 attack followed by **≥1 month** of persistent worry about additional attacks or their consequences, or maladaptive behavior change (avoidance)

*High-yield: Panic attacks can occur in other anxiety disorders too ("expected," cued) — Panic Disorder specifically requires recurrent unexpected attacks.*

### Agoraphobia
- Marked fear/anxiety in ≥2 of: public transport, open spaces, enclosed spaces, standing in line/crowds, being outside home alone
- Fear that escape might be difficult or help unavailable if panic-like symptoms occur
- Lasting ≥6 months; can occur with or without panic disorder (separate diagnosis in DSM-5)

## Specific Phobia / Social Anxiety Disorder
- Marked fear/anxiety about a specific object/situation (specific phobia) or social situations involving possible scrutiny (social anxiety disorder)
- Object/situation almost always provokes immediate fear, actively avoided or endured with intense distress
- Fear disproportionate to actual danger; lasting **≥6 months**

## Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

### Diagnostic Criteria
- Presence of obsessions, compulsions, or both:
  - **Obsessions**: recurrent, persistent, intrusive, unwanted thoughts/urges/images causing marked anxiety; individual attempts to ignore/suppress or neutralize with a compulsion
  - **Compulsions**: repetitive behaviors/mental acts performed in response to an obsession or according to rigid rules, aimed at reducing distress — not realistically connected to what they are meant to neutralize, or clearly excessive
- Time-consuming (**>1 hour/day**) or causes significant distress/impairment
- Insight specifiers: good/fair, poor, or absent insight/delusional beliefs

*High-yield: Common obsession themes — contamination, symmetry, doubt/harm; common compulsions — washing, checking, counting, ordering. Ego-dystonic (patient recognizes thoughts as unwanted/irrational) — distinguishes OCD obsessions from psychotic delusions.*

## Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

### Diagnostic Criteria
A. Exposure to actual/threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence (direct, witnessed, or learned about — close family/friend)

B. **Intrusion symptoms**: distressing memories, nightmares, flashbacks, intense psychological/physiological distress on reminder cues

C. **Avoidance** of trauma-related stimuli (thoughts, feelings, reminders)

D. **Negative alterations in cognition/mood**: amnesia for the event, persistent negative beliefs, distorted blame, negative emotional state, anhedonia, detachment

E. **Alterations in arousal/reactivity**: irritability/aggression, reckless behavior, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, concentration problems, sleep disturbance

- Duration **>1 month**; if <1 month → Acute Stress Disorder
- Delayed expression specifier if full criteria not met until ≥6 months after event

## Adjustment Disorder
- Emotional/behavioral symptoms in response to identifiable stressor, developing **within 3 months** of onset
- Marked distress out of proportion to stressor, or significant functional impairment
- Resolves within **6 months** of stressor (or its consequences) termination
- Does not meet criteria for another specific mental disorder

## Management

### Pharmacological
| Disorder | First-line | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GAD | SSRIs/SNRIs (escitalopram, venlafaxine, duloxetine) | Buspirone as adjunct/alternative (non-sedating, no dependence) |
| Panic disorder | SSRIs (first-line); benzodiazepines for acute/short-term relief | Avoid long-term benzo monotherapy — dependence risk |
| Social anxiety disorder | SSRIs/SNRIs; propranolol for performance-only ("situational") type | |
| OCD | **SSRIs at higher doses than for depression**, longer trial needed (8–12 weeks); **Clomipramine** (TCA with strong serotonergic activity) as alternative | |
| PTSD | SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine — FDA approved) | Prazosin for trauma-related nightmares |

### Psychotherapy
- **CBT with exposure** — first-line/most effective across phobias, panic disorder, social anxiety, OCD (exposure + response prevention, ERP), PTSD
- **Systematic desensitization** — graded exposure + relaxation, classic for specific phobia
- **Trauma-focused CBT / EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing)** — PTSD
- Relaxation training, biofeedback — adjuncts for GAD

## High-Yield One-Liners
- GAD: excessive worry ≥6 months + ≥3 associated symptoms
- Panic disorder: recurrent unexpected panic attacks + ≥1 month of anticipatory anxiety/avoidance
- PTSD requires symptoms >1 month; <1 month = Acute Stress Disorder
- OCD and PTSD are NOT classified as anxiety disorders in DSM-5 (separate chapters)
- Ego-dystonic obsessions (recognized as irrational) distinguish OCD from delusions (ego-syntonic, fixed false belief)
- Clomipramine and high-dose SSRIs are first-line pharmacotherapy for OCD; response takes longer (8–12 weeks) than for depression
- ERP (exposure and response prevention) = gold-standard psychotherapy for OCD
- Benzodiazepines provide rapid relief in panic disorder but are not preferred for long-term use (dependence, tolerance)
- Adjustment disorder: onset within 3 months of stressor, resolves within 6 months of stressor removal
