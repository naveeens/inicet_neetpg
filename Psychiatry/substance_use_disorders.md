# Substance Use Disorders

## Definition
DSM-5 merged "substance abuse" and "substance dependence" (DSM-IV) into a single continuum: **Substance Use Disorder**, graded by severity based on symptom count.

## Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5-TR) — Substance Use Disorder
A problematic pattern of use leading to clinically significant impairment/distress, with **≥2 of 11 criteria within a 12-month period**:

**Impaired control**: 1) taken in larger amounts/longer than intended, 2) persistent desire/unsuccessful efforts to cut down, 3) great deal of time spent obtaining/using/recovering, 4) craving

**Social impairment**: 5) failure to fulfill major role obligations, 6) continued use despite social/interpersonal problems, 7) important activities given up/reduced

**Risky use**: 8) recurrent use in hazardous situations, 9) continued use despite physical/psychological problems

**Pharmacological**: 10) tolerance, 11) withdrawal

### Severity
| Severity | Criteria met |
|---|---|
| Mild | 2–3 |
| Moderate | 4–5 |
| Severe | ≥6 |

*High-yield: DSM-5 removed "legal problems" as a criterion (present in DSM-IV abuse) and added "craving" as a new 11th criterion.*

## Alcohol Use Disorder

### Screening Tools
- **CAGE questionnaire** (≥2 positive = suggestive of problem drinking): **C**ut down (felt need to), **A**nnoyed (by others' criticism of drinking), **G**uilty (about drinking), **E**ye-opener (morning drink)
- AUDIT (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test) — WHO tool, more detailed

### Alcohol Withdrawal
| Timing (from last drink) | Feature |
|---|---|
| 6–12 h | Tremors, anxiety, sweating, insomnia, mild autonomic hyperactivity |
| 12–24 h | **Alcoholic hallucinosis** — visual/auditory hallucinations with intact sensorium (patient knows they're not real) |
| 24–48 h | **Withdrawal seizures** (generalized tonic-clonic) |
| 48–72 h (peak) | **Delirium Tremens** — coarse tremor, autonomic instability, confusion, agitation, vivid hallucinations (classically visual, e.g., "lilliputian" — tiny animals/insects); medical emergency, mortality if untreated |

- Management: **Benzodiazepines** (chlordiazepoxide/lorazepam — tapering regimen), IV thiamine (before glucose, to prevent precipitating Wernicke's), supportive care, correct electrolytes

*High-yield: Always give thiamine BEFORE/WITH glucose in a malnourished/alcoholic patient — glucose alone can precipitate Wernicke's encephalopathy by depleting remaining thiamine stores.*

### Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome (Thiamine/B1 deficiency)
- **Wernicke's encephalopathy** (acute, reversible if treated early): triad — **ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion**
- **Korsakoff syndrome** (chronic, often irreversible): **anterograde amnesia + confabulation**
- Lesion: mammillary bodies, medial thalamus, periaqueductal gray
- Treatment: parenteral thiamine (high-dose) immediately on suspicion

### Alcohol Use Disorder — Pharmacotherapy (maintenance of abstinence)
| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Disulfiram** | Inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase → accumulation of acetaldehyde on drinking → flushing, nausea, palpitations, hypotension | Aversive therapy; requires patient motivation/compliance; contraindicated in cardiac disease |
| **Naltrexone** | Opioid receptor antagonist | Reduces craving/reward from drinking; also used in opioid use disorder |
| **Acamprosate** | Modulates glutamate (NMDA)/GABA | Reduces protracted withdrawal cravings; safe in liver disease (renally cleared) |

## Opioid Use Disorder

### Intoxication
- Miosis (**pinpoint pupils**), respiratory depression, sedation, constipation, decreased pain perception, euphoria
- Overdose triad: **miosis + respiratory depression + coma**
- Treatment: **Naloxone** (opioid antagonist, short-acting — repeat dosing/infusion often needed)

### Withdrawal
- Onset depends on opioid half-life (heroin: 6–12 h; methadone: 24–48 h)
- Features: **dilated pupils (mydriasis)**, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, yawning, piloerection ("gooseflesh"/cold turkey), muscle aches, abdominal cramps, diarrhea, vomiting, insomnia, anxiety/dysphoria
- **Not life-threatening** (unlike alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal) but extremely distressing
- Management: **Methadone or buprenorphine taper** (opioid substitution therapy); **Clonidine** (alpha-2 agonist, symptomatic relief of autonomic hyperactivity); supportive care

### Opioid Substitution / Maintenance Therapy
| Drug | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Methadone | Full mu-opioid agonist | Long half-life; requires daily supervised dosing (opioid substitution clinics); risk of QT prolongation, overdose |
| **Buprenorphine** | Partial mu-agonist | Ceiling effect on respiratory depression = safer; often combined with naloxone (Suboxone) to deter IV misuse |
| Naltrexone | Antagonist | Used for relapse prevention after detox (requires complete abstinence first, else precipitates withdrawal) |

## Other Substances (high-yield withdrawal/intoxication features)

| Substance | Intoxication | Withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| **Benzodiazepines** | Sedation, ataxia, slurred speech | **Life-threatening** — anxiety, tremors, seizures, autonomic instability (similar to alcohol); requires tapering, never abrupt cessation |
| **Cannabis** | Euphoria, conjunctival injection, ↑ appetite, impaired coordination, dry mouth, tachycardia | Irritability, insomnia, decreased appetite, restlessness (rarely severe) |
| **Cocaine/Amphetamines (stimulants)** | Euphoria, ↑ BP/HR, mydriasis, paranoia, formication ("cocaine bugs") | "Crash" — dysphoria, fatigue, hypersomnia, increased appetite |
| **Hallucinogens (LSD)** | Perceptual distortions, synesthesia, mydriasis; "bad trip" — panic; flashbacks (hallucinogen persisting perception disorder) | No well-defined withdrawal syndrome |
| **PCP** | Nystagmus (vertical/horizontal), dissociation, violence, analgesia | — |
| **Nicotine** | — | Irritability, anxiety, weight gain, craving, difficulty concentrating |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- DSM-5 uses a single "Substance Use Disorder" spectrum (mild/moderate/severe) — abuse/dependence terms dropped
- CAGE ≥2 positive suggests alcohol use disorder
- Delirium tremens = medical emergency, peaks 48–72 h after last drink; treat with benzodiazepines
- Always give thiamine before/with glucose in alcoholics to prevent precipitating Wernicke's encephalopathy
- Wernicke's = ophthalmoplegia + ataxia + confusion (reversible); Korsakoff = anterograde amnesia + confabulation (often irreversible)
- Opioid overdose triad: miosis + respiratory depression + coma → treat with naloxone
- Opioid withdrawal is extremely distressing but NOT fatal; alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal CAN be fatal (seizures, DTs)
- Buprenorphine has a ceiling effect on respiratory depression, making it safer than methadone for opioid substitution
- Disulfiram works by inhibiting aldehyde dehydrogenase; Naltrexone and Acamprosate reduce craving without the disulfiram-reaction risk
