# Psychodynamic Defense Mechanisms

## Definition
Unconscious psychological processes (Freud, later elaborated by Anna Freud and Vaillant) that protect the ego from anxiety arising from unacceptable impulses, internal conflict, or external stressors. Classically graded from **immature/primitive (psychotic and borderline level)** to **mature (healthy)**, based on Vaillant's hierarchy — a favorite exam classification.

## Vaillant's Hierarchy of Defense Mechanisms

### Level 1 — Psychotic Defenses (grossly distort reality; seen in psychosis, dreams, childhood)
| Mechanism | Definition/Example |
|---|---|
| **Denial** | Refusal to accept a distressing reality despite clear evidence (e.g., a patient denying a cancer diagnosis just given) |
| **Distortion** | Reshaping external reality to suit inner needs (e.g., delusions of grandeur) |
| **Psychotic projection** | Attributing one's own unacceptable feelings/thoughts to an external, delusional source |

### Level 2 — Immature Defenses (common in personality disorders, adolescents)
| Mechanism | Definition/Example |
|---|---|
| **Projection** | Attributing one's own unacceptable feelings/impulses to another person (e.g., an unfaithful person accusing their partner of infidelity) |
| **Splitting** | Viewing self/others as all-good or all-bad, unable to integrate; classic in **Borderline Personality Disorder** |
| **Acting out** | Direct expression of an unconscious wish/impulse through action, to avoid awareness of accompanying affect (e.g., a patient with self-harm behavior instead of verbalizing distress) |
| **Passive aggression** | Indirectly expressing aggression through passivity, procrastination, or failure (e.g., "forgetting" to complete a task one resents) |
| **Somatization** | Converting psychological distress into physical/somatic symptoms |
| **Regression** | Reverting to an earlier, more childlike stage of development/behavior under stress (e.g., an adult throwing tantrums during illness) |
| **Schizoid fantasy** | Retreat into private fantasy/imagination to avoid intimacy or resolve conflict |
| **Hypochondriasis** | Excessive worry/preoccupation about having a serious illness despite reassurance |
| **Introjection** | Unconsciously incorporating qualities/values of another person into oneself (e.g., a child internalizing an abuser's harsh voice as self-criticism) |

### Level 3 — Neurotic Defenses (common in everyday adult life, "healthy neurotic")
| Mechanism | Definition/Example |
|---|---|
| **Repression** | Unconsciously excluding distressing thoughts/memories/impulses from conscious awareness (vs. suppression, which is conscious) |
| **Displacement** | Redirecting an emotion/impulse from its original object to a safer substitute (e.g., yelling at a spouse after a bad day at work) |
| **Reaction formation** | Converting an unacceptable impulse into its diametric opposite (e.g., excessive kindness toward someone one unconsciously resents) |
| **Isolation of affect** | Separating a thought/memory from its associated emotional charge (recalling a traumatic event without any emotion) |
| **Intellectualization** | Excessive use of abstract thinking/logic to distance oneself from an emotionally distressing situation |
| **Rationalization** | Creating logical-sounding but false justifications for unacceptable behavior/feelings after the fact |
| **Sexualization** | Endowing an object/behavior with sexual significance to convert a negative feeling into an exciting/pleasurable one |
| **Dissociation** | Temporary but drastic modification of one's sense of identity/consciousness to avoid emotional distress (e.g., dissociative identity disorder, depersonalization) |
| **Undoing** | Symbolically "reversing" or negating an unacceptable act/thought through a subsequent act (classic in **OCD** rituals) |
| **Controlling** | Excessive attempt to manage/regulate events or objects to minimize anxiety |

### Level 4 — Mature Defenses (adaptive, seen in psychologically healthy adults)
| Mechanism | Definition/Example |
|---|---|
| **Altruism** | Constructive service to others that also provides vicarious personal gratification (e.g., a bereaved parent starting a charity) |
| **Humor** | Overtly expressing feelings without discomfort, using comedy to focus on distressing aspects of a situation |
| **Sublimation** | Channeling an unacceptable impulse into a socially acceptable/productive activity (e.g., channeling aggression into competitive sport) |
| **Suppression** | **Conscious**, deliberate postponement of attention to a distressing thought/impulse (the only defense that is conscious rather than unconscious) |
| **Anticipation** | Realistic planning/anticipation of future discomfort or difficulty |

*High-yield exam trap: Suppression is the ONLY defense mechanism that operates consciously — every other defense mechanism (repression, denial, projection, etc.) is unconscious. This distinction is repeatedly tested.*

## Frequently Confused Pairs (classic MCQ traps)
| Mechanism | vs | Mechanism | Key distinguishing point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repression | vs | Suppression | Repression = unconscious; Suppression = conscious |
| Projection | vs | Displacement | Projection = attributing own feeling to another person; Displacement = redirecting feeling toward a different, safer object/target |
| Reaction formation | vs | Sublimation | Reaction formation = opposite behavior masking the true impulse (maladaptive-neurotic); Sublimation = same impulse channeled constructively (mature) |
| Denial | vs | Rationalization | Denial = refusal to acknowledge the fact itself; Rationalization = acknowledges the fact but justifies it with false logic |
| Isolation of affect | vs | Intellectualization | Isolation = affect stripped from the memory itself; Intellectualization = using excessive abstract/analytical thinking as a broader defensive style |
| Splitting | vs | Reaction formation | Splitting = all-or-nothing view of self/others (borderline hallmark); Reaction formation = converting one's own impulse to its opposite |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Vaillant's hierarchy: Psychotic (denial, distortion) → Immature (projection, splitting, acting out) → Neurotic (repression, displacement, reaction formation, undoing) → Mature (altruism, humor, sublimation, suppression, anticipation)
- Suppression is the only conscious defense mechanism — everything else is unconscious
- Splitting (all-good/all-bad view) is the hallmark defense of Borderline Personality Disorder
- Undoing is classically associated with OCD rituals (symbolically canceling out an unacceptable thought/act)
- Sublimation = healthiest way to channel an unacceptable impulse into a socially productive outlet (mature defense)
- Reaction formation = doing/feeling the opposite of the true unconscious impulse
- Projection = seeing one's own unacceptable trait/impulse in someone else; classic in paranoid presentations
- Denial refuses the fact itself; Rationalization accepts the fact but supplies a false justification for it
