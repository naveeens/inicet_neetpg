# Psychotic Disorders

## Definition
Disorders characterized by loss of contact with reality — hallmarked by **delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech/behavior, and negative symptoms**.

## Schizophrenia

### Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5-TR)
A. ≥2 of the following, each present for a significant portion of time during a 1-month period (at least one must be 1, 2, or 3):
1. Delusions
2. Hallucinations
3. Disorganized speech
4. Grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior
5. Negative symptoms (affective flattening, alogia, avolition)

B. Social/occupational dysfunction

C. **Continuous signs of disturbance for ≥6 months** (including prodromal/residual periods; ≥1 month of active-phase symptoms meeting criterion A)

D. Schizoaffective disorder and mood disorders with psychotic features ruled out

E. Not attributable to substance/medical condition

*High-yield: DSM-5 removed schizophrenia subtypes (paranoid, catatonic, disorganized, residual, undifferentiated) — no longer valid diagnoses, replaced by dimensional severity ratings. DSM-5 also removed the special significance of bizarre delusions/Schneiderian first-rank symptoms — now just count as one of the criterion A domains, not automatically sufficient alone.*

### Positive vs Negative Symptoms
| Positive symptoms | Negative symptoms |
|---|---|
| Delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech/behavior | Affective flattening, alogia, avolition, asociality, anhedonia |
| Respond better to antipsychotics (typical & atypical) | Respond better to **atypical** antipsychotics; often treatment-resistant |
| Dopamine hyperactivity in mesolimbic pathway | Hypofrontality / dopamine deficit in mesocortical pathway |

### Related Diagnoses (duration-based — classic exam differentiator)
| Disorder | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brief Psychotic Disorder | **<1 month** | Full recovery to premorbid level; often stress-precipitated |
| Schizophreniform Disorder | **1–6 months** | Same symptoms as schizophrenia but shorter duration; social/occupational dysfunction not required |
| Schizophrenia | **≥6 months** | As above |
| Schizoaffective Disorder | — | Uninterrupted period of illness with concurrent mood episode (major depressive or manic) AND Criterion A of schizophrenia; delusions/hallucinations for ≥2 weeks in absence of mood episode during illness course |
| Delusional Disorder | **≥1 month** | ≥1 delusion(s); **no** other Criterion A symptoms (no hallucinations, disorganized speech); functioning relatively preserved apart from delusion's impact |

*High-yield: Delusional disorder subtypes — erotomanic, grandiose, jealous (Othello syndrome), persecutory (most common), somatic. Capgras syndrome (belief that a familiar person has been replaced by an impostor) and Fregoli syndrome (belief that different people are actually a single person in disguise) are classic delusional misidentification syndromes.*

## Clinical Features
- **Prodromal phase**: social withdrawal, functional decline, odd beliefs — precedes active phase
- **Active phase**: positive + negative symptoms as above
- **Residual phase**: negative symptoms predominate, milder positive symptoms
- Insight typically poor/absent
- First-rank symptoms (Schneider): thought echo/insertion/withdrawal/broadcasting, delusional perception, made feelings/impulses/actions, somatic passivity, third-person auditory hallucinations (running commentary) — historically highly suggestive of schizophrenia, though not DSM-5 diagnostic criteria per se

## Investigations
- Diagnosis of exclusion — rule out substance-induced psychosis, medical causes (thyroid, SLE, NMDA-receptor encephalitis, temporal lobe epilepsy), delirium
- CT/MRI brain to exclude organic pathology, especially first episode
- Urine drug screen

## Management

### Pharmacological
| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Typical (first-generation) antipsychotics | Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine | Block D2 receptors; better for positive symptoms; more **EPS** (extrapyramidal side effects) and tardive dyskinesia |
| Atypical (second-generation) antipsychotics | Risperidone, Olanzapine, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole | Block D2 + 5-HT2A; effective for both positive and negative symptoms; less EPS, more metabolic side effects (weight gain, dyslipidemia, diabetes — esp. olanzapine/clozapine) |
| Treatment-resistant schizophrenia | **Clozapine** | Gold standard after failure of ≥2 antipsychotic trials; risk of **agranulocytosis** — mandatory regular CBC monitoring; also lowers seizure threshold, causes sialorrhea, myocarditis |

*High-yield: Aripiprazole = dopamine partial agonist ("dopamine stabilizer") — least metabolic/EPS burden. Clozapine — the only antipsychotic proven superior for treatment-resistant disease and reduces suicidality in schizophrenia, but requires weekly-to-monthly CBC monitoring for agranulocytosis.*

### Antipsychotic Side Effects (high-yield table)
| Side effect | Onset | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Acute dystonia | Hours-days | Muscle spasm (oculogyric crisis); treat with anticholinergics (benztropine) / antihistaminics |
| Akathisia | Days-weeks | Subjective restlessness; treat with beta-blockers (propranolol), benzodiazepines |
| Parkinsonism (EPS) | Weeks | Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia; treat with anticholinergics or dose reduction |
| Tardive dyskinesia | Months-years | Choreoathetoid orofacial movements; often **irreversible**; treat by switching to clozapine, or valbenazine/tetrabenazine |
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) | Any time (esp. early) | Fever, rigidity ("lead-pipe"), autonomic instability, ↑CK, altered sensorium — emergency; stop antipsychotic, **dantrolene/bromocriptine**, supportive cooling |

### Non-pharmacological
- Psychosocial rehabilitation, social skills training, family psychoeducation (reduces relapse — "expressed emotion" reduction)
- ECT — indicated in catatonia, treatment resistance with severe symptoms, schizoaffective disorder with mood component

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Schizophrenia requires ≥6 months of disturbance (≥1 month active-phase symptoms); Schizophreniform = 1–6 months; Brief psychotic disorder = <1 month
- Delusional disorder = isolated delusion(s) with preserved functioning, no hallucinations/disorganization
- Clozapine = drug of choice for treatment-resistant schizophrenia; major risk = agranulocytosis (mandatory CBC monitoring)
- NMS = medical emergency; hallmark = fever + rigidity + autonomic instability + ↑CK; treat with dantrolene/bromocriptine and stop the offending drug
- Tardive dyskinesia is often irreversible — switching to clozapine is the preferred management
- DSM-5 abolished schizophrenia subtypes (paranoid, catatonic, etc.)
- Capgras syndrome = impostor delusion; persecutory type = most common subtype of delusional disorder
- Atypical antipsychotics act on both D2 and 5-HT2A receptors — better negative symptom control than typicals
