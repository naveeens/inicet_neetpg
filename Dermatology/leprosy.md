# Leprosy

## Definition
Chronic granulomatous infection caused by ***Mycobacterium leprae*** (obligate intracellular, acid-fast, unculturable in vitro — grows only in mouse footpad/armadillo). Primarily affects skin, peripheral nerves, upper respiratory mucosa, eyes, and testes. Spectrum of disease determined by host cell-mediated immunity (CMI) — not organism virulence.

## Classification

### Ridley-Jopling Spectrum (immunologic/histologic — 5 groups)
| Type | CMI | Bacillary Load | Lesions |
|---|---|---|---|
| TT (Tuberculoid) | High | Paucibacillary | Few, well-defined, hypopigmented/erythematous plaques with raised edges, anesthetic |
| BT (Borderline Tuberculoid) | High | Low | Similar to TT but more numerous, less well-defined |
| BB (Mid-borderline) | Unstable | Moderate | Most unstable, "punched-out" annular lesions |
| BL (Borderline Lepromatous) | Low | High | Numerous, less symmetric than LL |
| LL (Lepromatous) | Absent (anergic) | Very high | Numerous, symmetric, ill-defined nodules/plaques; **"leonine facies"** |

*High-yield: TT is most stable pole; LL is other stable pole. Borderline forms (BT, BB, BL) are immunologically unstable and shift with reactions/treatment.*

### WHO Classification (Operational — used for treatment, India/NLEP)
| Type | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Paucibacillary (PB)** | ≤5 skin lesions, no nerve trunk involvement (or only 1 nerve), skin smear negative |
| **Multibacillary (MB)** | ≥6 skin lesions, or ≥2 nerve trunks involved, or skin smear positive (irrespective of lesion number) |

*High-yield: In India (NLEP), even a single nerve trunk involvement with smear positivity or ≥6 lesions classifies as MB. Skin smear positivity alone (even in fewer lesions) = MB.*

### Indeterminate Leprosy
Earliest, non-specific stage — single hypopigmented macule, ill-defined, may self-heal or progress to any pole.

## Etiopathogenesis
- *M. leprae* — acid-fast bacillus, cannot be cultured on artificial media
- Transmission: prolonged close contact, nasal droplets (main route); untreated MB patients are chief reservoir
- Nerve involvement due to Schwann cell tropism — superficial/cooler sites affected preferentially (ulnar, median, common peroneal, posterior auricular, facial nerves)
- Lepromin test (Mitsuda reaction) — measures host CMI, NOT diagnostic of disease (positive in TT/healthy, negative in LL); analogous to Montoux for TB

## Clinical Features
- Hypopigmented or erythematous **anesthetic** patch/plaque — hallmark (loss of sensation precedes visible changes)
- Thickened peripheral nerves — ulnar (elbow), common peroneal (fibular head), posterior tibial, great auricular, radial cutaneous
- Madarosis (loss of eyebrows, lateral first) — classic in LL
- Leonine facies, saddle nose (cartilage destruction), glove-and-stocking anesthesia
- Trophic ulcers, claw hand (ulnar nerve), foot drop (peroneal), lagophthalmos (facial nerve VII)
- Testicular atrophy, gynecomastia in LL (hypogonadism)

## Lepra Reactions (High-Yield — differentiate clearly)
| Feature | Type 1 (Reversal Reaction) | Type 2 (ENL — Erythema Nodosum Leprosum) |
|---|---|---|
| Immune mechanism | Delayed hypersensitivity (Type IV) — shift towards TT pole | Immune complex mediated (Type III) |
| Seen in | Borderline leprosy (BT, BB, BL) | Lepromatous leprosy (LL, BL) |
| Timing | Often after starting treatment (upgrading) | Any time, often during/after treatment |
| Skin lesions | Existing lesions become erythematous, edematous, tender | New crops of tender erythematous nodules |
| Systemic features | Fever, neuritis (may cause sudden nerve damage — treat urgently to prevent deformity) | Fever, malaise, arthralgia, iridocyclitis, orchitis, lymphadenitis |
| Treatment | Prednisolone (mainstay); continue MDT | **Thalidomide** (drug of choice, contraindicated in pregnancy); steroids for severe/neuritis; clofazimine for chronic ENL |

*High-yield: Type 1 reaction is a medical emergency for the nerve — sudden nerve pain/tenderness → start steroids immediately to prevent permanent damage. Lucio phenomenon is a severe necrotizing variant of ENL-like reaction seen in diffuse lepromatous (Lucio) leprosy, common in Mexico/Caribbean.*

## Diagnosis
- **Cardinal signs (any one sufficient for diagnosis):**
  1. Hypopigmented/erythematous patch with definite loss of sensation
  2. Thickened peripheral nerve with sensory/motor deficit
  3. Acid-fast bacilli on slit-skin smear
- **Slit-skin smear**: taken from ear lobes, active edge of lesions; stained with Ziehn-Neelsen; reported as **Bacterial Index (BI)** and **Morphological Index (MI, % solid-staining/viable bacilli)**
- Lepromin (Mitsuda) test — prognostic/classificatory, not diagnostic; positive in TT, negative in LL
- Histopathology: TT — well-formed epidermal granulomas, few/no bacilli; LL — diffuse macrophage (lepra cell/Virchow cell) infiltrate, numerous bacilli, foamy histiocytes
- Nerve conduction studies for subclinical neuropathy

## Management

### WHO-MDT Regimens (India follows WHO guidelines under NLEP)
| Type | Drugs | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| PB | Rifampicin 600 mg once monthly (supervised) + Dapsone 100 mg daily | 6 months |
| MB | Rifampicin 600 mg once monthly (supervised) + Clofazimine 300 mg once monthly (supervised) + 50 mg daily + Dapsone 100 mg daily | 12 months |

- Single-lesion PB leprosy: single dose ROM (Rifampicin + Ofloxacin + Minocycline) — historically used, current NLEP now treats single-lesion cases with standard PB-MDT
- Dapsone side effects: hemolysis (esp. G6PD deficiency), agranulocytosis, dapsone hypersensitivity syndrome
- Clofazimine side effects: reddish-black skin pigmentation, ichthyosis, GI upset
- Rifampicin: hepatotoxicity, orange-red discoloration of body fluids, most bactericidal of the three

## High-Yield One-Liners
- *M. leprae* cannot be cultured in vitro — grows in mouse footpad and nine-banded armadillo
- WHO classification for treatment: PB (≤5 lesions) vs MB (≥6 lesions, or ≥2 nerves, or smear positive)
- Lepromin test reflects host immunity (like Mantoux), not used for diagnosis
- Type 1 reaction = cell-mediated, seen in borderline leprosy, nerve damage risk → urgent steroids
- Type 2 reaction (ENL) = immune complex mediated, seen in lepromatous leprosy, DOC = Thalidomide (avoid in pregnancy)
- Earliest peripheral nerve affected clinically is often the ulnar nerve at the elbow
- Slit-skin smear taken from ear lobules and active lesion edges, reported as Bacterial Index and Morphological Index
- Madarosis, leonine facies, saddle nose = classic lepromatous (LL) features
