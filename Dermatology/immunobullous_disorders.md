# Immunobullous Disorders

## Overview
Autoimmune blistering diseases caused by autoantibodies against structural proteins of the epidermis/dermo-epidermal junction. Broadly divided by level of split: **intraepidermal** (pemphigus group — antibodies against desmosomal proteins) vs **subepidermal** (pemphigoid group, dermatitis herpetiformis — antibodies against basement membrane zone proteins).

## Classification by Level of Blister
| Level | Disease | Target Antigen |
|---|---|---|
| Intraepidermal (suprabasal) | Pemphigus vulgaris | Desmoglein 3 (± Dsg1) |
| Intraepidermal (subcorneal) | Pemphigus foliaceus | Desmoglein 1 |
| Subepidermal | Bullous pemphigoid | BP180 (BPAg2), BP230 (BPAg1) — hemidesmosome |
| Subepidermal | Dermatitis herpetiformis | Epidermal transglutaminase (associated with gluten sensitivity) |
| Subepidermal | Epidermolysis bullosa acquisita | Type VII collagen |

## Pemphigus Vulgaris
- Most common and most severe pemphigus variant; peak age 40-60 years
- **Mechanism**: IgG autoantibodies against Desmoglein 3 (and Dsg1 in mucocutaneous type) → loss of keratinocyte adhesion (acantholysis) → suprabasal intraepidermal bulla
- **Clinical features**: Flaccid, easily ruptured bullae on normal-appearing skin → painful erosions; **oral mucosal erosions often the FIRST and most persistent manifestation** (may precede skin lesions by months)
- Nikolsky's sign positive (see below)
- Bulla spread sign (Asboe-Hansen sign) positive — pressure on an intact bulla spreads fluid laterally into adjacent skin

## Pemphigus Foliaceus
- Antibodies against Desmoglein 1 only → superficial subcorneal split
- Scaly, crusted erosions, resembles exfoliative dermatitis; **mucosa usually spared** (Dsg3 in deep layers compensates)
- Endemic form: Fogo selvagem (Brazil)

## Bullous Pemphigoid
- Most common autoimmune subepidermal blistering disease; **elderly patients** (>60 years)
- Antibodies against BP180/BP230 (hemidesmosomal proteins) → subepidermal split → **tense bullae** (roof = full epidermis, doesn't rupture easily)
- Often preceded by intensely pruritic urticarial/eczematous plaques before bullae appear
- **Mucosal involvement uncommon and mild** (unlike pemphigus)
- Nikolsky's sign **negative**
- Generally better prognosis than pemphigus, more responsive to treatment, lower mortality

## Dermatitis Herpetiformis
- Intensely pruritic, grouped papulovesicles on extensor surfaces (elbows, knees, buttocks, scalp) — "herpetiform" clustering
- Strongly associated with **gluten-sensitive enteropathy (celiac disease)** — most patients have subclinical intestinal villous atrophy
- IgA antibodies against epidermal transglutaminase
- DIF: **granular IgA deposits at dermal papillary tips**
- Treatment: **Dapsone** (dramatic rapid symptomatic relief — near-diagnostic response) + gluten-free diet (addresses underlying cause)

## Key Bedside/Diagnostic Tests

### Nikolsky's Sign
Lateral shearing pressure on perilesional normal-appearing skin causes epidermis to separate/slide off.
- **Positive** in disorders with poor keratinocyte cohesion: Pemphigus vulgaris/foliaceus, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS)
- **Negative** in Bullous pemphigoid (subepidermal, tense bullae, strong dermo-epidermal adhesion)

### Tzanck Smear
Cytology from base of a fresh blister, Giemsa/Wright stain.
- Pemphigus: **acantholytic cells** (rounded keratinocytes with hyperchromatic nucleus, "tombstone" pattern, prominent nucleoli) — rapid bedside test
- Also used in herpes viral infections (multinucleated giant cells) — helps distinguish

### Immunofluorescence — the definitive diagnostic tool
| Test | Finding | Disease |
|---|---|---|
| Direct IF (DIF) — perilesional skin biopsy | **Intercellular ("fishnet"/"chicken-wire") IgG + C3** deposits | Pemphigus vulgaris/foliaceus |
| DIF | **Linear IgG + C3 along basement membrane zone** | Bullous pemphigoid |
| DIF | **Granular IgA at dermal papillary tips** | Dermatitis herpetiformis |
| Indirect IF (IIF) — patient serum | Circulating antibody titers correlate with disease activity (pemphigus) — useful for monitoring |
| Salt-split skin technique | Antibody binds epidermal (roof) side → pemphigoid-group vs floor side (dermal) → EBA | Differentiates BP from EBA |

*High-yield: Direct IF pattern is the single most important differentiator — intercellular/fishnet = pemphigus; linear BMZ = pemphigoid; granular papillary tip IgA = dermatitis herpetiformis.*

## Histopathology
- Pemphigus vulgaris: suprabasal acantholysis, "tombstoning" of basal cells (basal layer remains attached to dermis, "row of tombstones")
- Pemphigus foliaceus: subcorneal/granular layer acantholysis
- Bullous pemphigoid: subepidermal bulla with eosinophil-rich infiltrate, intact epidermal roof

## Management
| Disease | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Pemphigus vulgaris | **Systemic corticosteroids** (high-dose, mainstay) + adjuvant immunosuppressants (azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, cyclophosphamide); **Rituximab** now increasingly first-line/steroid-sparing in moderate-severe disease |
| Bullous pemphigoid | Topical/systemic corticosteroids (lower doses suffice than pemphigus); doxycycline + nicotinamide for milder disease |
| Dermatitis herpetiformis | Dapsone (rapid response) + lifelong gluten-free diet |

- Before oral corticosteroids/immunosuppression era, pemphigus vulgaris mortality was very high (sepsis, fluid loss from denuded skin) — now markedly improved with steroids/rituximab

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Pemphigus vulgaris = suprabasal split, anti-Dsg3; oral erosions often the first sign
- Pemphigus foliaceus = subcorneal split, anti-Dsg1, mucosa spared
- Bullous pemphigoid = subepidermal, tense bullae, elderly, Nikolsky negative, better prognosis
- Nikolsky's sign positive: pemphigus, TEN, SSSS; negative: bullous pemphigoid
- Tzanck smear shows acantholytic cells in pemphigus (bedside rapid test)
- DIF is gold standard: intercellular fishnet pattern = pemphigus; linear BMZ = pemphigoid; granular papillary IgA = dermatitis herpetiformis
- Dermatitis herpetiformis is linked to celiac disease; dapsone gives dramatic rapid relief
- Rituximab is now a preferred steroid-sparing agent in pemphigus vulgaris
