# STD Venereology

## Overview
Sexually transmitted diseases (STDs) presenting with genital ulcers, discharge, or growths. Classic exam approach: distinguish genital ulcer diseases by number, pain, edges, base, and lymphadenopathy characteristics.

## Genital Ulcer Diseases — Comparative Table (High-Yield)
| Disease | Organism | Ulcer | Pain | Lymphadenopathy | Incubation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Syphilis (chancre)** | *Treponema pallidum* | Single, indurated, clean base | Painless | Firm, non-tender, bilateral | 2-6 weeks |
| **Chancroid** | *Haemophilus ducreyi* | Multiple, ragged undermined edges, purulent base | **Painful** | Tender, may suppurate ("buboes") | 3-7 days |
| **LGV (Lymphogranuloma venereum)** | *Chlamydia trachomatis* (serovars L1-L3) | Small, transient, often unnoticed | Painless | Massive, matted, may form "groove sign" | 1-4 weeks |
| **Genital herpes** | HSV-2 (mostly), HSV-1 | Multiple, small, grouped vesicles → shallow ulcers | Painful | Tender, bilateral | 2-7 days |
| **Donovanosis (Granuloma inguinale)** | *Klebsiella granulomatis* (Calymmatobacterium) | Beefy-red, friable, rolled edges, no true lymphadenitis (pseudobuboes) | Painless | Absent true LAD (pseudobuboes = subcutaneous granulomas) | Weeks-months |

*Mnemonic for painful ulcers: "Herpes and cHancroid Hurt" — the two H's are painful; syphilis and LGV are classically painless.*

## Syphilis

### Stages
| Stage | Timing | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 3 weeks post-exposure (2-6 wks) | Painless indurated chancre at inoculation site, heals spontaneously in 3-6 weeks |
| Secondary | 4-10 weeks after chancre | Generalized rash (palms/soles classically involved), condyloma lata, mucous patches, generalized lymphadenopathy, alopecia (moth-eaten) |
| Latent | Variable | Asymptomatic, serology positive |
| Tertiary | Years later | Gummas, cardiovascular syphilis (aortitis, aortic aneurysm), neurosyphilis (tabes dorsalis, general paresis, Argyll Robertson pupil) |

### Diagnosis
- **Dark-field microscopy** of chancre exudate — direct visualization of spirochetes (gold standard for early primary syphilis, before serology turns positive)
- **Non-treponemal tests** (screening): VDRL, RPR — cheap, quantifiable (used to monitor treatment response), but non-specific (false positives in pregnancy, SLE, malaria, leprosy, TB — "**BFP**": biologic false positive)
- **Treponemal tests** (confirmatory): TPHA, FTA-ABS, TPPA — specific, remain positive for life (not useful for monitoring treatment)
- Congenital syphilis: Hutchinson's triad (Hutchinson teeth, interstitial keratitis, 8th nerve deafness), saddle nose, saber shin, mulberry molars

### Management
- **Benzathine Penicillin G** — drug of choice at all stages
  - Early syphilis (primary, secondary, early latent <1 year): single dose 2.4 MU IM
  - Late latent/tertiary (or unknown duration): 2.4 MU IM weekly × 3 doses
  - Neurosyphilis: IV aqueous crystalline penicillin G (higher CNS penetration needed)
- Doxycycline if penicillin-allergic (non-pregnant)
- Pregnancy with penicillin allergy: desensitization required (doxycycline contraindicated; erythromycin does not reliably treat fetus)
- **Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction**: fever, chills, worsening lesions within hours of starting treatment (due to massive spirochete killing/endotoxin-like release) — self-limiting, manage with antipyretics, not a reason to stop treatment

## Chancroid
- Painful, ragged, undermined ulcers with grayish-yellow purulent base — "school of fish" pattern on Gram stain (*H. ducreyi*, gram-negative coccobacilli in chains)
- Inguinal buboes may suppurate and rupture
- Treatment: Azithromycin (single dose), Ceftriaxone (single dose IM), or Ciprofloxacin

## Lymphogranuloma Venereum (LGV)
- Three stages: primary (transient painless papule/ulcer, often missed), secondary (**inguinal syndrome** — painful matted lymph nodes, "**groove sign**" — grooving above and below inguinal ligament from both inguinal and femoral node involvement), tertiary (genito-ano-rectal syndrome — strictures, fistulas, elephantiasis)
- Treatment: Doxycycline 21 days (first-line); Azithromycin weekly as alternative

## Genital Herpes
- HSV-2 causes most recurrent genital herpes; HSV-1 increasingly implicated in primary episodes
- Primary infection often most severe (fever, malaise, painful grouped vesicles/ulcers, tender bilateral lymphadenopathy); recurrences milder
- Diagnosis: PCR (most sensitive), Tzanck smear (multinucleated giant cells — non-specific for HSV vs VZV), viral culture
- Treatment: Acyclovir/Valacyclovir/Famciclovir — for primary episode, recurrent episodic therapy, or suppressive therapy in frequent recurrences

## Donovanosis (Granuloma Inguinale)
- Beefy-red, friable ulcer that bleeds easily, slowly progressive, rolled edges
- No true lymphadenopathy (pseudobuboes = subcutaneous granulomatous swellings, not true LAD)
- Diagnosis: **Donovan bodies** — intracytoplasmic organisms within macrophages on tissue smear/biopsy (Giemsa stain)
- Treatment: Azithromycin (drug of choice), doxycycline

## Gonorrhea
- *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* — gram-negative diplococci
- Men: purulent urethral discharge, dysuria (incubation 2-5 days)
- Women: often asymptomatic or mild cervicitis — important reservoir for transmission and complications (PID, infertility, ectopic pregnancy)
- Disseminated gonococcal infection: triad of dermatitis (pustules), tenosynovitis, migratory polyarthritis
- Treatment: Ceftriaxone (single dose IM) ± Azithromycin (cover possible co-infection with Chlamydia; dual therapy historically recommended, evolving due to resistance concerns)

## Syndromic Management (National AIDS Control Organisation/WHO approach — India)
Since exact etiology often cannot be confirmed at point-of-care, STDs are managed by clinical syndrome (e.g., genital ulcer syndrome, urethral discharge syndrome, vaginal discharge syndrome) with color-coded flow charts — treats most likely pathogens simultaneously without waiting for lab confirmation. Widely used in India's National AIDS Control Program.

## HIV and the Skin
- Seborrheic dermatitis — often severe/refractory, common early cutaneous marker
- Oral hairy leukoplakia (EBV-related), oral candidiasis — markers of advancing immunosuppression
- Kaposi sarcoma (HHV-8) — violaceous plaques/nodules, AIDS-defining illness
- Increased severity/atypical presentation of common infections: extensive molluscum, disseminated herpes zoster (multidermatomal), crusted/Norwegian scabies

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Painful genital ulcers: chancroid and herpes ("Hurt"); painless: syphilis and LGV
- Dark-field microscopy is the gold standard for early primary syphilis before serology becomes positive
- VDRL/RPR = non-treponemal, used for screening and monitoring treatment (titers fall with therapy); TPHA/FTA-ABS = treponemal, confirmatory, stays positive lifelong
- Benzathine penicillin G is the drug of choice at every stage of syphilis
- Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction occurs hours after starting antisyphilitic treatment — continue treatment, treat symptomatically
- LGV's "groove sign" results from combined inguinal + femoral lymph node enlargement
- Donovan bodies (Giemsa stain) = diagnostic of granuloma inguinale/donovanosis
- Chancroid Gram stain shows "school of fish" pattern of gram-negative coccobacilli
