# Psoriasis Papulosquamous Disorders

## Overview
Papulosquamous disorders present as scaly papules/plaques. High-yield group: Psoriasis, Lichen Planus, Pityriasis Rosea, Pityriasis Rubra Pilaris, Seborrheic Dermatitis. Psoriasis and Lichen Planus are the most heavily tested.

## Psoriasis

### Definition
Chronic, immune-mediated (Th1/Th17-driven), papulosquamous disease with hyperproliferation and abnormal differentiation of keratinocytes → markedly shortened epidermal turnover time (normal ~28 days → reduced to 3-4 days in psoriasis).

### Clinical Types
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Chronic plaque (psoriasis vulgaris) | Most common (~90%); well-defined erythematous plaques with silvery-white scale; extensor surfaces (elbows, knees), scalp, lumbosacral area — symmetric |
| Guttate psoriasis | Sudden eruption of small "drop-like" papules on trunk, classically **post-streptococcal** (2-3 weeks after pharyngitis), common in children/young adults |
| Pustular psoriasis (von Zumbusch) | Generalized sterile pustules on erythematous base, fever, can be life-threatening |
| Inverse/flexural psoriasis | Involves flexures (axilla, groin, inframammary) — less scale due to moisture |
| Erythrodermic psoriasis | >90% body surface area involvement, can precipitate high-output cardiac failure |
| Nail psoriasis | Pitting, oil-drop sign, onycholysis, subungual hyperkeratosis |

### Pathogenesis
- Genetic predisposition (**HLA-Cw6** strongly associated, especially early-onset/guttate type) + environmental triggers
- Triggers: streptococcal infection (guttate), trauma (Koebner phenomenon), drugs (lithium, beta-blockers, antimalarials, NSAIDs, abrupt steroid withdrawal), stress, alcohol
- Th1/Th17 mediated immune activation → keratinocyte hyperproliferation, dilated tortuous dermal papillary capillaries (explains punctate bleeding — Auspitz sign)

### Diagnostic Signs (High-Yield)
| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| **Auspitz sign** | Removal of scale reveals pinpoint bleeding points (from dilated dermal papillary capillaries close to thinned suprapapillary epidermis) |
| **Koebner phenomenon** | New psoriatic lesions appear at sites of trauma/skin injury (also seen in lichen planus, vitiligo, warts) |
| Candle grease sign | Scale resembles a scraped candle when scratched |
| Bulkeley's membrane | Thin membrane visible after scale removal, before pinpoint bleeding |
| Woronoff ring | Pale halo of vasoconstriction around a resolving psoriatic plaque |

### Histopathology
- Parakeratosis, regular acanthosis with elongated rete ridges ("test-tubes in a rack")
- Thinning of suprapapillary epidermis
- **Munro microabscesses** (neutrophils in stratum corneum) and **spongiform pustules of Kogoj** (neutrophils in stratum spinosum) — classic findings

### Associations
- **Psoriatic arthritis** (~20-30% of psoriasis patients) — patterns: asymmetric oligoarthritis (most common), DIP joint involvement (classic, associated with nail changes), symmetric RA-like, arthritis mutilans, spondylitis
- Metabolic syndrome, obesity, increased cardiovascular risk
- Nail pitting associated with psoriatic arthritis (esp. DIP involvement)

### Management
| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild-limited | Topical corticosteroids, Vitamin D analogs (calcipotriol), topical retinoids (tazarotene), coal tar |
| Moderate | Phototherapy — NB-UVB (narrowband UVB, first-line), PUVA (psoralen + UVA) |
| Severe/refractory | Systemic — Methotrexate (first-line systemic), Cyclosporine, Acitretin (retinoid, teratogenic — avoid in women of childbearing age) |
| Biologics | Anti-TNF (adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab), anti-IL-17 (secukinumab, ixekizumab), anti-IL-23 (guselkumab, risankizumab), anti-IL-12/23 (ustekinumab) |
| Pustular/erythrodermic (emergency) | Acitretin or Cyclosporine (rapid acting), supportive care |

*High-yield: Avoid abrupt systemic steroid withdrawal in psoriasis — can precipitate pustular/erythrodermic flare.*

## Lichen Planus

### Clinical Features — "6 P's"
**P**ruritic, **P**urple/Violaceous, **P**olygonal, **P**lanar (flat-topped) **P**apules and **P**laques
- Sites: flexor surfaces of wrists/forearms, shins, genitalia, oral mucosa
- **Wickham's striae**: fine white lines/network on surface of papules (best seen with magnification/oil)
- Oral LP: white reticulate ("lacy") pattern on buccal mucosa; may be erosive (higher malignant potential)
- Koebner phenomenon positive
- Nail changes: thinning, longitudinal ridging, pterygium formation
- Scalp: lichen planopilaris → scarring alopecia

### Pathogenesis
- Cell-mediated (CD8+ T cell) immune attack on basal keratinocytes; associated with Hepatitis C in some populations
- Drug-induced LP (lichenoid drug eruption): gold, antimalarials, beta-blockers, thiazides

### Histopathology
- Hyperkeratosis without parakeratosis, wedge-shaped hypergranulosis, irregular "saw-tooth" rete ridges
- **Band-like (lichenoid) lymphocytic infiltrate at dermo-epidermal junction**
- Basal cell degeneration → **Civatte/colloid bodies** (apoptotic keratinocytes)
- Max Joseph space (artifactual cleft from basal layer damage)

### Management
- Topical/intralesional corticosteroids (first-line)
- Oral LP: topical steroids, tacrolimus; systemic steroids for erosive/widespread disease
- Antihistamines for pruritus

## Pityriasis Rosea
- Acute, self-limiting eruption, likely viral (HHV-6/HHV-7 reactivation implicated)
- **Herald patch**: single, larger oval erythematous scaly plaque appears first (days to weeks before generalized eruption)
- Followed by smaller oval lesions with **collarette scaling**, distributed along skin cleavage lines on trunk — **"Christmas tree" pattern**
- Self-resolves in 6-8 weeks; treatment symptomatic (antihistamines, emollients); UVB may hasten resolution

## Pityriasis Rubra Pilaris
- Follicular hyperkeratotic papules coalescing into orange-red plaques with **"islands of sparing"** (characteristic normal skin patches within affected areas)
- Palmoplantar keratoderma (waxy, orange-yellow)
- Cephalocaudal spread pattern

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Auspitz sign = pinpoint bleeding on scale removal, classic for psoriasis
- Koebner phenomenon: seen in psoriasis, lichen planus, vitiligo, and warts
- Munro microabscess (stratum corneum) and spongiform pustule of Kogoj (stratum spinosum) = psoriasis histology
- HLA-Cw6 = strongest genetic association, especially early-onset/guttate psoriasis
- Guttate psoriasis classically follows streptococcal pharyngitis, common in children
- Psoriatic arthritis DIP involvement correlates with nail pitting
- Methotrexate = first-line systemic agent for moderate-severe psoriasis; Acitretin is teratogenic
- Lichen planus = "6 P's" + Wickham's striae + band-like lymphocytic infiltrate + Civatte bodies on histology
- Pityriasis rosea: herald patch followed by Christmas-tree pattern eruption along skin cleavage lines, self-limiting
- Pityriasis rubra pilaris: characteristic "islands of sparing" within orange-red plaques
