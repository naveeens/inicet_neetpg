# Skin Tumors Melanoma

## Overview
Skin tumors range from benign (seborrheic keratosis, dermatofibroma) to premalignant (actinic keratosis) to malignant (BCC, SCC, melanoma). BCC and SCC together = non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC) — most common cancers overall but rarely metastasize (BCC almost never). Melanoma is far less common but responsible for most skin-cancer deaths due to metastatic potential.

## Premalignant Lesions

### Actinic (Solar) Keratosis
- Chronic sun-damaged skin (face, dorsal hands, bald scalp) in fair-skinned, older individuals
- Rough, scaly, erythematous papules — "feels like sandpaper"
- Premalignant — risk of progression to **squamous cell carcinoma** (small but real risk per lesion; cumulative risk significant)
- Histology: parakeratosis with alternating orthokeratosis, atypical keratinocytes confined to lower epidermis (basal layer)
- Treatment: cryotherapy, topical 5-fluorouracil, imiquimod, photodynamic therapy

## Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)
- **Most common skin cancer overall**
- Risk factors: chronic UV (UVB) exposure, fair skin, xeroderma pigmentosum, basal cell nevus (Gorlin) syndrome
- Most common site: **face** (sun-exposed, especially nose, periorbital)
- Clinical types:
  - **Nodular** (most common) — pearly, translucent papule/nodule with rolled/telangiectatic border, central ulceration ("rodent ulcer")
  - Superficial — erythematous scaly patch, trunk
  - Morpheaform/sclerosing — scar-like, ill-defined, most aggressive/infiltrative subtype
  - Pigmented BCC
- Histology: nests of basaloid cells with **peripheral palisading** and **peripheral clefting/retraction artifact** from surrounding stroma
- Behavior: locally invasive/destructive, **metastasis is extremely rare** — mortality mainly from local tissue destruction if neglected (eyes, nose, ears)
- Treatment: surgical excision with margins; **Mohs micrographic surgery** for high-risk/cosmetically sensitive sites (best margin control, tissue-sparing); topical imiquimod/5-FU for superficial BCC; radiotherapy for non-surgical candidates; Hedgehog pathway inhibitors (vismodegib) for advanced/metastatic disease

## Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)
- Second most common skin cancer
- Risk factors: chronic UV exposure, chronic scars/ulcers/sinuses (**Marjolin's ulcer** = SCC arising in a chronic scar/burn/osteomyelitis sinus), HPV (genital SCC), immunosuppression (transplant patients), arsenic exposure, actinic keratosis, chronic non-healing wounds
- Clinical: indurated, ulcerated, or verrucous plaque/nodule, may have hyperkeratotic crust
- Higher metastatic potential than BCC — especially lip, ear, genital SCC, and those arising in scars (Marjolin's ulcer) or in immunosuppressed patients
- Histology: atypical keratinocytes invading dermis, **keratin pearls** (concentric whorls of keratinization) — hallmark of well-differentiated SCC
- Treatment: wide local excision with adequate margins, Mohs surgery for high-risk sites, radiotherapy as adjuvant/for non-surgical candidates, sentinel node evaluation in high-risk cases

## Keratoacanthoma
- Rapidly growing, dome-shaped nodule with central keratin-filled crater — can mimic SCC clinically and histologically
- Considered a low-grade/self-limiting variant of SCC by many; often regresses spontaneously over months but usually excised to rule out true SCC

## Malignant Melanoma

### Risk Factors
- Fair skin (Fitzpatrick I-II), intense intermittent UV exposure/sunburns (especially childhood), multiple/atypical/dysplastic nevi, family history, xeroderma pigmentosum, immunosuppression
- **Palmoplantar/acral and mucosal/subungual sites are the predominant presentation in darker-skinned populations (including Indians)** — unlike sun-exposed trunk/back predominance in fair-skinned populations

### Clinical Types
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Superficial spreading | Most common type overall (in fair-skinned populations); radial growth phase before vertical invasion |
| Nodular melanoma | No preceding radial growth phase — rapidly invasive, worse prognosis at presentation |
| Lentigo maligna melanoma | Elderly, chronically sun-exposed skin (face), slow radial growth (in situ phase = lentigo maligna) before invasion, best prognosis among subtypes |
| **Acral lentiginous melanoma** | Palms, soles, subungual/nail bed — **most common type in dark-skinned individuals including Indians and Asians**; often diagnosed late (mistaken for trauma/fungal infection) |

### Diagnosis — ABCDE Rule
- **A**symmetry
- **B**order irregularity
- **C**olor variation (multiple colors within one lesion)
- **D**iameter >6 mm
- **E**volving (change in size/shape/color/symptoms over time — often considered most important sign clinically)

### Prognostic Staging — Histologic Depth (High-Yield)
| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| **Breslow thickness** | Depth of invasion in mm from stratum granulosum to deepest tumor cell — **single most important prognostic factor** for localized melanoma |
| **Clark level** | Anatomic level of invasion (I: in situ, II: papillary dermis, III: fills papillary dermis, IV: reticular dermis, V: subcutaneous fat) — largely superseded by Breslow thickness in modern staging but still tested |

- Other prognostic factors: ulceration (worse prognosis), mitotic rate, lymph node involvement, presence of distant metastasis
- Sentinel lymph node biopsy — for intermediate-thickness melanomas to assess nodal micrometastasis and guide staging/further management

### Management
- Wide local excision with margins based on Breslow thickness (in situ: 0.5 cm; ≤1 mm: 1 cm; 1-2 mm: 1-2 cm; >2 mm: 2 cm)
- Sentinel lymph node biopsy for tumors with adverse features/intermediate thickness
- Advanced/metastatic disease: immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitors — anti-PD-1 like pembrolizumab/nivolumab, anti-CTLA-4 like ipilimumab), targeted therapy for **BRAF V600E mutation** (BRAF inhibitors like vemurafenib/dabrafenib + MEK inhibitors) — mutation testing guides targeted therapy eligibility

## Benign Tumors (High-Yield Distractors)
- **Seborrheic keratosis**: benign, "stuck-on" waxy/verrucous papule, common in elderly, **Leser-Trélat sign** (sudden eruption of multiple seborrheic keratoses) = paraneoplastic marker for internal malignancy (esp. GI adenocarcinoma)
- **Dermatofibroma**: firm dermal nodule, "dimple/button-hole sign" on lateral compression (retracts inward)
- **Pyogenic granuloma**: rapidly growing, friable, bleeding vascular papule/nodule, associated with pregnancy/trauma
- **Epidermal (sebaceous) cyst**: central punctum, cheesy foul-smelling contents

## High-Yield One-Liners
- BCC is the most common skin cancer overall but almost never metastasizes; nodular BCC = "rodent ulcer" with pearly rolled border
- BCC histology: peripheral palisading with peripheral clefting artifact
- SCC histology hallmark: keratin pearls; Marjolin's ulcer = SCC in a chronic scar/sinus/burn
- Mohs micrographic surgery gives the best margin control for high-risk BCC/SCC on cosmetically sensitive sites
- Acral lentiginous melanoma is the most common melanoma subtype in dark-skinned populations, including Indians — palms, soles, subungual sites
- Breslow thickness is the single most important prognostic factor in localized melanoma
- Nodular melanoma lacks a radial growth phase and carries a worse prognosis at presentation
- BRAF V600E mutation testing guides targeted therapy (vemurafenib/dabrafenib) in advanced melanoma
- Leser-Trélat sign (eruptive seborrheic keratoses) suggests an underlying internal malignancy
