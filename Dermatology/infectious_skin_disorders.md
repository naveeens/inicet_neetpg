# Infectious Skin Disorders

## Overview
Skin infections classified by causative organism — bacterial, fungal (superficial mycoses), viral, and infestations (ectoparasites). High-yield across INICET/NEET PG for classic bedside diagnostic tests: KOH mount, Tzanck smear, Gram stain, Wood's lamp examination.

## Bacterial Infections

### Impetigo
| Type | Organism | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Non-bullous (common) | *Staph. aureus* > *Strep. pyogenes* | Honey-colored crusted erosions, face/extremities, highly contagious in children |
| Bullous | *Staph. aureus* (exfoliative toxin) | Flaccid bullae, less crusting |
| Ecthyma | *Strep. pyogenes* usually | Deeper, punched-out ulcers with thick crust, scarring |

- Complication: post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (nephritogenic strains) — NOT rheumatic fever (unlike pharyngeal strep)
- Treatment: topical mupirocin/fusidic acid (localized); oral antibiotics (cloxacillin/cephalexin) for extensive disease

### Erysipelas vs Cellulitis
| Feature | Erysipelas | Cellulitis |
|---|---|---|
| Depth | Superficial dermis + lymphatics | Deeper dermis + subcutaneous fat |
| Border | Sharply demarcated, raised | Poorly demarcated |
| Organism | *Strep. pyogenes* (usually) | *Strep. pyogenes*, *Staph. aureus* |
| Site | Classically face, lower legs | Anywhere |

- Treatment: Penicillin (erysipelas), broader coverage for cellulitis (cloxacillin/cephalexin; add MRSA coverage if risk factors)

### Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS)
- Exfoliative (epidermolytic) toxin A/B from *Staph. aureus* → cleaves desmoglein 1 → superficial epidermal split
- Seen in infants/young children (immature renal clearance of toxin)
- Widespread erythema, flaccid bullae, **Nikolsky's sign positive**, perioral crusting, mucosa spared (unlike TEN)
- Treatment: anti-staphylococcal antibiotics, supportive skin care

### Cutaneous Tuberculosis (brief)
- Lupus vulgaris — most common type in India, apple-jelly nodules on diascopy
- Scrofuloderma — from underlying lymphadenitis/bone TB, breaks down to form sinuses/ulcers

## Fungal Infections (Superficial Mycoses)

### Dermatophytoses (Tinea)
| Site | Name |
|---|---|
| Scalp | Tinea capitis |
| Body | Tinea corporis |
| Groin | Tinea cruris |
| Feet | Tinea pedis |
| Hand | Tinea manuum |
| Nail | Tinea unguium (onychomycosis) |
| Face | Tinea faciei |

- Causative genera: *Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*
- Clinical: annular/ring-shaped lesion with active, scaly, raised advancing edge and central clearing
- **Tinea capitis — Wood's lamp**: *Microsporum* species fluoresce blue-green; *Trichophyton tonsurans* (common cause of endothrix, non-fluorescent) does not fluoresce — high-yield distinction
- Diagnosis: **KOH mount** — shows branching septate hyphae; fungal culture on Sabouraud's dextrose agar for species identification
- India currently facing an epidemic of **recalcitrant/recurrent tinea (steroid-modified tinea)** due to widespread misuse of topical steroid-antifungal-antibacterial combination creams — an important applied/current-affairs topic for Indian exams
- Treatment: topical antifungals (clotrimazole, terbinafine) for localized disease; oral itraconazole/terbinafine for extensive, recalcitrant, tinea capitis, or onychomycosis

### Tinea Versicolor (Pityriasis Versicolor)
- *Malassezia furfur* (formerly *Pityrosporum orbiculare*) — a normal skin commensal (not a dermatophyte), yeast form
- Hypo- or hyperpigmented macules with fine scale, trunk/upper arms, worsens with heat/humidity/sweating
- KOH mount: **"spaghetti and meatballs"** appearance (short hyphae + round yeast clusters)
- Wood's lamp: **pale yellow/gold fluorescence**
- Treatment: topical selenium sulfide/ketoconazole shampoo/cream; oral itraconazole for extensive/recurrent disease

### Candidiasis
- *Candida albicans* — intertriginous areas (moist folds), oral thrush, vulvovaginal candidiasis, angular cheilitis, chronic paronychia
- KOH mount: pseudohyphae with budding yeast cells
- Risk factors: diabetes, immunosuppression, antibiotic use, occlusion/moisture

## Viral Infections

### Herpes Simplex Virus
- HSV-1: orolabial (classically), HSV-2: genital (classically) — significant overlap now
- Grouped vesicles on erythematous base → erosions; recurrences at same site (dorsal root ganglion latency)
- Diagnosis: Tzanck smear (multinucleated giant cells), PCR (most sensitive/specific), viral culture
- Treatment: Acyclovir/valacyclovir/famciclovir

### Varicella-Zoster Virus
- Varicella (chickenpox): primary infection, vesicles in different stages of evolution simultaneously ("crops," pleomorphic — papule/vesicle/pustule/crust together — high-yield distinguishing feature from smallpox, which showed lesions in the SAME stage synchronously)
- Herpes zoster (shingles): reactivation from dorsal root ganglia, dermatomal distribution, does not cross midline; **thoracic dermatomes most common**; ophthalmic zoster (V1) risk of ocular complications; **Ramsay Hunt syndrome** = facial nerve (geniculate ganglion) zoster → facial palsy + ear vesicles + hearing loss/vertigo
- Complication: post-herpetic neuralgia (more common/severe with increasing age)
- Treatment: Acyclovir/valacyclovir (start within 72 hours ideally)

### Human Papillomavirus (Warts)
- Verruca vulgaris (common warts), verruca plana (flat warts), plantar warts, condyloma acuminata (genital warts — usually HPV 6/11, low oncogenic risk)
- High-risk oncogenic types (16, 18) associated with cervical/anogenital carcinoma
- Treatment: cryotherapy, salicylic acid, imiquimod, podophyllin (genital warts, contraindicated in pregnancy)

### Molluscum Contagiosum
- Poxvirus (MCV) — dome-shaped, pearly papules with **central umbilication**
- Common in children (direct contact) and as an STI/extensive disease in immunosuppressed/HIV patients
- Histology: Henderson-Patterson (molluscum) bodies — intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies
- Treatment: often self-limiting; curettage, cryotherapy, topical agents (podophyllotoxin, cantharidin) if treatment desired

## Infestations

### Scabies
- *Sarcoptes scabiei* var. hominis (mite) — burrows into stratum corneum
- Intense pruritus, **classically worse at night**
- Sites: finger webs, wrist flexors, axillae, areola, genitalia (in adults); face/scalp/palms/soles also involved in **infants**
- **Burrows** — pathognomonic linear/serpiginous tracks
- Diagnosis: dermoscopy/microscopy of skin scraping showing mite, eggs, or fecal pellets (scybala); "**delta wing jet with contrail**" sign on dermoscopy
- **Crusted (Norwegian) scabies**: seen in immunosuppressed/HIV patients, massive mite load, highly contagious, thick crusted plaques
- Treatment: **Permethrin 5% cream** (first-line, apply overnight, treat all close contacts simultaneously); oral Ivermectin for crusted scabies/outbreaks/treatment failure; treat entire household simultaneously to prevent reinfestation

### Pediculosis
- *Pediculus humanus capitis* (head lice), *corporis* (body lice, associated with poor hygiene, can transmit epidemic typhus/relapsing fever/trench fever), *Phthirus pubis* (pubic lice, sexually transmitted, "maculae ceruleae" — bluish macules at bite sites)
- Diagnosis: visualization of lice/nits (eggs) attached to hair shafts
- Treatment: Permethrin, malathion; nit combing

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Impetigo → post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is a recognized complication; rheumatic fever is not
- SSSS: toxin cleaves desmoglein 1, Nikolsky positive, mucosa spared — key differentiator from TEN (which involves mucosa)
- Wood's lamp: Microsporum tinea capitis fluoresces blue-green; Trichophyton tonsurans usually does not fluoresce
- Tinea versicolor KOH: "spaghetti and meatballs"; Wood's lamp: golden/pale yellow fluorescence
- India has a current epidemic of steroid-modified/recalcitrant tinea due to irrational topical steroid combination creams
- Chickenpox shows lesions in different stages simultaneously (pleomorphism); smallpox lesions were synchronous (same stage)
- Ramsay Hunt syndrome = geniculate ganglion zoster with facial palsy, ear vesicles, and vestibulocochlear symptoms
- Molluscum contagiosum shows central umbilication and Henderson-Patterson inclusion bodies on histology
- Scabies burrows are pathognomonic; pruritus is classically worse at night; permethrin 5% is first-line, treat all contacts
- Crusted (Norwegian) scabies occurs in immunosuppressed patients and is highly contagious
