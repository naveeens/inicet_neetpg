# Molecular Biology DNA Replication Repair

## Overview
DNA replication is semi-conservative, occurs during S-phase, and requires a coordinated set of enzymes at the replication fork. DNA repair mechanisms correct errors/damage to maintain genomic integrity; defects in repair pathways cause several classic inherited cancer-predisposition and premature-aging syndromes — a favorite high-yield exam theme.

## DNA Replication — Key Enzymes
| Enzyme | Function |
|---|---|
| Helicase | Unwinds DNA double helix at replication fork, ATP-dependent |
| Topoisomerase (Gyrase in bacteria) | Relieves supercoiling/torsional strain ahead of fork; Type II creates transient double-strand breaks |
| Single-strand binding proteins (SSB) | Stabilize unwound single strands, prevent reannealing |
| Primase | Synthesizes short RNA primer to initiate synthesis (DNA polymerases cannot initiate de novo) |
| **DNA Polymerase III** (prokaryotes) / Pol δ, ε (eukaryotes) | Main processive enzyme; synthesizes new strand 5'→3', has 3'→5' exonuclease (proofreading) activity |
| DNA Polymerase I (prokaryotes) | Removes RNA primers (5'→3' exonuclease) and fills gaps |
| DNA Ligase | Seals nicks between adjacent DNA fragments (joins Okazaki fragments), requires ATP/NAD+ |
| Telomerase (eukaryotes) | RNA-dependent DNA polymerase (reverse transcriptase); extends telomeres (repetitive TTAGGG in humans), solves end-replication problem; active in germ cells/stem cells/cancer cells |

## Replication Mechanics — High-Yield Points
- Synthesis is always 5'→3'; leading strand synthesized continuously, lagging strand discontinuously as **Okazaki fragments**
- Replication is bidirectional from origin(s) of replication; eukaryotes have multiple origins per chromosome, prokaryotes typically one
- Semi-discontinuous, semi-conservative (Meselson-Stahl experiment established semi-conservative nature)
- Eukaryotic DNA polymerases: **Pol α** (primer synthesis, works with primase), **Pol δ** (lagging strand, has proofreading), **Pol ε** (leading strand), Pol γ (mitochondrial DNA replication)
- Fluoroquinolones inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV — clinically relevant antibiotic mechanism

## DNA Repair Mechanisms
| Repair Type | Mechanism | Key Enzymes | Associated Disease (defect) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER)** | Removes bulky lesions (e.g., UV-induced pyrimidine dimers), bulky adducts | Excision endonucleases, helicase, DNA polymerase, ligase | **Xeroderma pigmentosum** — photosensitivity, skin cancers |
| **Base Excision Repair (BER)** | Removes single damaged/incorrect bases (oxidation, deamination, alkylation) | DNA glycosylase, AP endonuclease, DNA polymerase β, ligase | — |
| **Mismatch Repair (MMR)** | Corrects replication errors missed by proofreading (base-base mismatches) | MSH2, MLH1 (and other MutS/MutL homologs) | **Lynch syndrome (HNPCC)** — hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer |
| **Double-strand break repair** | Homologous recombination (accurate, needs sister chromatid, S/G2 phase) or Non-homologous end joining (error-prone, any phase) | BRCA1/BRCA2 (HR); Ku proteins, DNA-PK (NHEJ) | **BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations** — breast/ovarian cancer risk; Fanconi anemia — pancytopenia, cancer predisposition |

## Classic DNA Repair Disorders — Exam Table
| Disease | Defective Repair | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Xeroderma pigmentosum | NER (cannot repair UV-induced pyrimidine dimers) | Extreme photosensitivity, early skin cancers (BCC, SCC, melanoma), autosomal recessive |
| Lynch syndrome (HNPCC) | Mismatch repair (MSH2, MLH1) | Early-onset colorectal cancer without polyposis, endometrial cancer risk, autosomal dominant |
| Ataxia telangiectasia | ATM gene (DNA damage response/DSB signaling) | Cerebellar ataxia, telangiectasias, immunodeficiency, ↑radiosensitivity, ↑cancer risk |
| Fanconi anemia | DNA crosslink repair (FANC genes) | Pancytopenia, aplastic anemia, congenital anomalies, ↑risk of AML/cancers |
| Bloom syndrome | BLM helicase (DNA repair/replication) | Short stature, photosensitivity, ↑cancer risk, genomic instability |
| BRCA1/BRCA2 mutation | Homologous recombination repair | Hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome |
| Werner syndrome | WRN helicase | Premature aging (progeria-like), ↑cancer risk |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- DNA synthesis always proceeds 5'→3'; leading strand continuous, lagging strand discontinuous (Okazaki fragments)
- Primase makes the RNA primer; DNA polymerase cannot initiate synthesis de novo
- DNA polymerase III (prokaryotes)/Pol δ,ε (eukaryotes) = main replicative enzymes with proofreading (3'→5' exonuclease)
- Telomerase = reverse transcriptase, uses its own RNA template, active in stem/germ/cancer cells — reactivation is a hallmark of many cancers
- Xeroderma pigmentosum = defective Nucleotide Excision Repair = cannot fix UV-induced thymine dimers
- Lynch syndrome (HNPCC) = defective Mismatch Repair (MSH2/MLH1)
- BRCA1/BRCA2 = defective homologous recombination repair of double-strand breaks
- Fluoroquinolones target bacterial DNA gyrase/topoisomerase — a classic drug-mechanism link to replication machinery
- Ataxia telangiectasia and Fanconi anemia are both DNA-repair-defect syndromes with marked cancer predisposition and radiosensitivity
