# Carbohydrate Metabolism Glycolysis Gluconeogenesis

## Overview
Glycolysis (cytoplasm, all cells, especially RBCs which depend on it exclusively) converts glucose → 2 pyruvate, net yield 2 ATP + 2 NADH (anaerobic) or up to ~30-32 ATP total after complete oxidation (aerobic). Gluconeogenesis is the reverse synthetic pathway (liver, kidney cortex) making glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors (lactate, glycerol, glucogenic amino acids, propionate) — essential during fasting.

## Glycolysis — Key Steps and Regulation
| Step | Enzyme | Regulation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose → Glucose-6-P | Hexokinase (most tissues) / **Glucokinase** (liver, β-cell) | Hexokinase inhibited by G-6-P (product inhibition); Glucokinase NOT inhibited by G-6-P, induced by insulin, has high Km (glucose sensor) | Irreversible, uses 1 ATP |
| Fructose-6-P → Fructose-1,6-BP | **Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)** — rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis | Activated by AMP, F-2,6-BP, Pi; Inhibited by ATP, citrate | Committed step of glycolysis |
| Phosphoenolpyruvate → Pyruvate | Pyruvate kinase | Activated by F-1,6-BP (feed-forward); Inhibited by ATP, alanine; Inactivated by glucagon-mediated phosphorylation (liver) | Irreversible, generates ATP |
| Glyceraldehyde-3-P dehydrogenase step | GAPDH | NAD+ dependent | Only oxidation step producing NADH in glycolysis |

- Net ATP: 2 (aerobic, per glucose, after subtracting 2 used initially)
- Net NADH: 2 (must be shuttled into mitochondria via malate-aspartate shuttle [liver/heart, yields 2.5 ATP/NADH] or glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle [muscle/brain, yields 1.5 ATP/NADH])
- RBCs: lack mitochondria → glycolysis is sole ATP source; also generates 2,3-BPG via Rapoport-Luebering shunt (shifts O2 dissociation curve right)

## Fructose-2,6-Bisphosphate — The Master Regulator
- Synthesized by PFK-2 (bifunctional enzyme PFK-2/FBPase-2)
- **Fed state (high insulin):** PFK-2 dephosphorylated → active → ↑F-2,6-BP → activates PFK-1, inhibits F-1,6-BPase → favors glycolysis
- **Fasting (high glucagon):** PFK-2 phosphorylated (via PKA) → FBPase-2 domain active → ↓F-2,6-BP → favors gluconeogenesis

## Gluconeogenesis — Key Steps (Bypass Reactions)
| Bypass Step | Enzyme | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pyruvate → Oxaloacetate | Pyruvate carboxylase | Mitochondria | Requires biotin, ATP; activated by acetyl-CoA |
| Oxaloacetate → PEP | PEP carboxykinase (PEPCK) | Cytosol (OAA shuttled out as malate) | **Rate-limiting/key regulatory enzyme of gluconeogenesis**; induced by glucagon/cortisol, repressed by insulin |
| Fructose-1,6-BP → Fructose-6-P | Fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase | Cytosol | Inhibited by AMP, F-2,6-BP; activated by citrate |
| Glucose-6-P → Glucose | Glucose-6-phosphatase | ER, liver/kidney (absent in muscle/brain) | Allows glucose release into blood; deficient in **von Gierke disease (GSD I)** |

- Precursors: lactate (Cori cycle from muscle/RBC), glycerol (from lipolysis), glucogenic amino acids (esp. alanine via Cori/alanine cycle), propionyl-CoA (odd-chain fatty acid oxidation)
- Key concept: Fatty acids CANNOT be net converted to glucose (acetyl-CoA cannot bypass pyruvate dehydrogenase step irreversibility)

## Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex (Link Reaction)
- Pyruvate + CoA + NAD+ → Acetyl-CoA + CO2 + NADH (mitochondrial matrix, irreversible)
- Requires **5 cofactors**: TPP (thiamine, B1), Lipoic acid, CoA (pantothenic acid, B5), FAD (B2), NAD+ (niacin, B3) — mnemonic "**T**uck **L**ovely **C**oA **F**or **N**AD"
- Inhibited by product (Acetyl-CoA, NADH) and by arsenic/mercury (inhibit lipoic acid-dependent enzymes)
- Activated by: insulin (dephosphorylation), ADP, Ca2+, pyruvate
- Inactivated by: PDH kinase (phosphorylation) — activated by ATP, NADH, Acetyl-CoA (fed vs starved switching)
- Deficiency: PDH deficiency → lactic acidosis, neurologic impairment (X-linked, most common cause of congenital lactic acidosis)

## Clinically Relevant Enzyme Deficiencies
| Enzyme Deficient | Disease | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose-6-phosphatase | von Gierke disease (GSD I) | Severe fasting hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis, hyperuricemia, hyperlipidemia |
| Pyruvate kinase | Hereditary hemolytic anemia | Second most common cause of hereditary hemolytic anemia after G6PD deficiency; rigid RBCs, extravascular hemolysis |
| Pyruvate dehydrogenase | Congenital lactic acidosis | X-linked; neurological symptoms, worsens with carbohydrate load |
| Fructokinase | Essential fructosuria | Benign, asymptomatic |
| Aldolase B | Hereditary fructose intolerance | Severe hypoglycemia, vomiting, hepatomegaly after fructose/sucrose ingestion; fructose-1-P accumulation traps phosphate |
| Galactose-1-P uridyltransferase (GALT) | Classic galactosemia | Cataracts, hepatomegaly, mental retardation, E. coli sepsis risk; galactose-1-P accumulates |
| Galactokinase | Galactokinase deficiency | Cataracts only (milder, no liver/brain involvement) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Rate-limiting enzyme of glycolysis: **PFK-1**; key regulatory point of gluconeogenesis: **PEPCK**
- Only glycolytic enzyme deficiency causing hemolytic anemia: **pyruvate kinase**
- Glucokinase = glucose sensor of liver/pancreas, high Km, induced by insulin, not inhibited by G6P (unlike hexokinase)
- RBCs are obligate anaerobes for ATP — no mitochondria, fully dependent on glycolysis
- PDH complex needs 5 cofactors: TPP, lipoic acid, CoA, FAD, NAD+
- Cori cycle: muscle lactate → liver glucose (via gluconeogenesis) — links anaerobic muscle metabolism to hepatic glucose output
- Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate is the key allosteric switch between glycolysis (↑ in fed state) and gluconeogenesis (↓, fasting/glucagon)
- Von Gierke disease = classic "cannot fast" glycogen storage disease with severe hypoglycemia + lactic acidosis + hyperuricemia
