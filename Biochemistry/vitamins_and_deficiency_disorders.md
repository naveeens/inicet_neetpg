# Vitamins and Deficiency Disorders

## Overview
Vitamins are organic micronutrients required in small amounts for normal metabolism; classified as fat-soluble (A, D, E, K — stored in body, deficiency develops slowly, toxicity risk on excess) or water-soluble (B-complex, C — not stored much, excreted in urine, deficiency develops faster, toxicity rare except B6/niacin).

## Fat-Soluble Vitamins
| Vitamin | Active Form/Role | Deficiency | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (Retinol) | Vision (retinal — rhodopsin), epithelial differentiation, immunity | Night blindness (earliest sign), xerophthalmia, Bitot spots, follicular hyperkeratosis | Hypervitaminosis A — pseudotumor cerebri, teratogenic (isotretinoin) |
| D (Calcitriol) | Ca2+/phosphate absorption from gut, bone mineralization | Rickets (children), Osteomalacia (adults) | Hypercalcemia, metastatic calcification |
| E (Tocopherol) | Antioxidant (protects membrane lipids from peroxidation) | Hemolytic anemia (esp. premature infants), ataxia, areflexia (mimics Friedreich ataxia) | Relatively non-toxic; may impair vitamin K-dependent clotting at high doses |
| K | Cofactor for γ-carboxylation of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X, protein C & S | Bleeding diathesis, ↑PT (extrinsic pathway first affected); hemorrhagic disease of newborn | Rare |

## Water-Soluble Vitamins
| Vitamin | Coenzyme Form | Function | Deficiency Disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 (Thiamine) | TPP | Cofactor for PDH, α-KGDH, transketolase (HMP shunt), branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase | **Beriberi** (dry — neuropathy; wet — high-output cardiac failure), **Wernicke-Korsakoff** (ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion + confabulation/amnesia) |
| B2 (Riboflavin) | FAD, FMN | Redox reactions, ETC Complex II | Angular stomatitis, cheilosis, glossitis, corneal vascularization |
| B3 (Niacin) | NAD+, NADP+ | Redox reactions (glycolysis, TCA, fatty acid synthesis) | **Pellagra** — "3 D's": Dermatitis (photosensitive), Diarrhea, Dementia (± Death); synthesized from tryptophan (needs B6); Hartnup disease and carcinoid syndrome deplete tryptophan → pellagra |
| B5 (Pantothenic acid) | CoA | Acyl group carrier (fatty acid synthesis/oxidation, PDH, TCA) | Rare; paresthesia |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) | PLP | Transamination, decarboxylation, heme synthesis (ALA synthase), neurotransmitter synthesis | Peripheral neuropathy (classic with isoniazid use — INH inhibits B6), sideroblastic anemia, seizures (infants) |
| B7 (Biotin) | Biotin | CO2 carrier for carboxylases (pyruvate carboxylase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase, propionyl-CoA carboxylase) | Rare; seen with excess raw egg white intake (avidin binds biotin) — dermatitis, alopecia |
| B9 (Folate) | THF | One-carbon transfer, purine/thymidylate (dTMP) synthesis, needed for homocysteine → methionine | **Megaloblastic anemia**, neural tube defects in pregnancy (must supplement before conception) |
| B12 (Cobalamin) | Methylcobalamin, Adenosylcobalamin | Homocysteine→methionine (methylcobalamin); Methylmalonyl-CoA→Succinyl-CoA (adenosylcobalamin) | Megaloblastic anemia + **subacute combined degeneration** (neurological, unlike folate deficiency); pernicious anemia (autoimmune, anti-intrinsic factor/anti-parietal cell antibodies) |
| C (Ascorbic acid) | — (antioxidant, cofactor) | Prolyl/lysyl hydroxylase (collagen synthesis), iron absorption (reduces Fe3+ to Fe2+), antioxidant | **Scurvy** — bleeding gums, poor wound healing, perifollicular hemorrhages, corkscrew hairs |

## Key Differentiating Points (Classic Exam Traps)
| Comparison | Key Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| Folate vs B12 deficiency | Both cause megaloblastic anemia; only B12 deficiency causes neurological symptoms (subacute combined degeneration) — folate never does |
| B12 deficiency mechanisms | Pernicious anemia (autoimmune gastritis, most common cause), strict vegan diet, ileal disease/resection (site of absorption), fish tapeworm (Diphyllobothrium latum), bacterial overgrowth |
| Wet vs Dry beriberi | Wet = cardiovascular (high-output failure, edema); Dry = neurological (peripheral neuropathy) |
| Isoniazid neuropathy | Due to B6 deficiency — INH is structurally similar to pyridoxine and inhibits pyridoxine kinase; prevent with prophylactic pyridoxine |
| Rickets vs Osteomalacia | Same defect (Vitamin D deficiency), different age of presentation — rickets in growing bone (children, before epiphyseal closure) vs osteomalacia in adults |
| Vitamin A vs Vitamin C in wound healing | Vitamin A needed for epithelialization; Vitamin C needed for collagen cross-linking — both relevant post-surgery |

## Biochemical/Lab Correlates
| Test | Vitamin Assessed |
|---|---|
| Schilling test (largely historical) | Vitamin B12 absorption pathway localization |
| Erythrocyte transketolase activity | Thiamine (B1) status |
| Methylmalonic acid (elevated) + Homocysteine (elevated) | B12 deficiency |
| Homocysteine elevated, Methylmalonic acid normal | Folate deficiency |
| Prothrombin time (prolonged) | Vitamin K status |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Earliest sign of Vitamin A deficiency: night blindness (nyctalopia)
- Pellagra's "3 D's": Dermatitis, Diarrhea, Dementia (± Death, the 4th D)
- B12 deficiency = megaloblastic anemia + neuropathy (subacute combined degeneration); folate deficiency = megaloblastic anemia only, no neuropathy
- Isoniazid causes peripheral neuropathy via Vitamin B6 depletion — always co-prescribe pyridoxine
- TPP (B1) is cofactor for PDH, α-KGDH, and transketolase — thiamine deficiency causes lactic acidosis and impairs HMP shunt
- Biotin deficiency classically from excessive raw egg white consumption (avidin binds biotin irreversibly)
- Vitamin K deficiency prolongs PT first (Factor VII has shortest half-life)
- Elevated methylmalonic acid distinguishes B12 deficiency from folate deficiency (both have elevated homocysteine)
- Scurvy = defective collagen cross-linking due to non-functional prolyl/lysyl hydroxylase (Vitamin C-dependent)
- Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) — deficiency more likely with fat malabsorption (e.g., celiac disease, cystic fibrosis, cholestasis)
