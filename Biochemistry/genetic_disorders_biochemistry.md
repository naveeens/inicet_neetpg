# Genetic Disorders Biochemistry

## Overview
Genetic disorders relevant to biochemistry span single-gene enzyme defects (inborn errors of metabolism, usually autosomal recessive), trinucleotide repeat disorders, mitochondrial (maternal) inheritance, and chromosomal disorders. Understanding inheritance pattern + biochemical defect + diagnostic marker is the recurring exam pattern.

## Modes of Inheritance — Quick Reference
| Pattern | Key Features | Classic Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Autosomal Recessive | Most inborn errors of metabolism; parents usually carriers (unaffected); consanguinity increases risk | PKU, Galactosemia, most glycogen storage diseases, most lysosomal storage diseases |
| Autosomal Dominant | One copy sufficient; often structural protein defects | Familial hypercholesterolemia, Marfan syndrome, Huntington disease |
| X-linked Recessive | Males affected, females carriers; no father-to-son transmission | G6PD deficiency, OTC deficiency (urea cycle), Fabry disease, Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, Hemophilia A/B |
| Mitochondrial (maternal) | Transmitted only from mother; variable severity (heteroplasmy) | MELAS, MERRF, Leber hereditary optic neuropathy |

## Trinucleotide Repeat Disorders
| Disease | Repeat | Gene | Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Huntington disease | CAG | HTT | Chorea, dementia, psychiatric symptoms; anticipation with paternal transmission |
| Fragile X syndrome | CGG | FMR1 | Most common cause of inherited intellectual disability; macroorchidism, long face, large ears |
| Myotonic dystrophy | CTG | DMPK | Myotonia, cataracts, cardiac conduction defects |
| Friedreich ataxia | GAA | FXN (frataxin) | Exception — autosomal recessive (most repeat disorders are AD); ataxia, cardiomyopathy, diabetes |

- **Anticipation**: increasing severity/earlier onset in successive generations due to repeat expansion — classic for Huntington disease (CAG repeats), especially with paternal transmission

## Genomic Imprinting Disorders
| Disorder | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Prader-Willi syndrome | Deletion/loss of function of paternal chromosome 15q11-13 (maternal allele imprinted/silenced) — hyperphagia, obesity, hypotonia, intellectual disability |
| Angelman syndrome | Deletion/loss of function of maternal chromosome 15q11-13 (paternal allele imprinted/silenced) — severe intellectual disability, seizures, inappropriate laughter ("happy puppet") |

## Purine/Pyrimidine Metabolism Disorders
| Disorder | Enzyme Defect | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Lesch-Nyhan syndrome | **HGPRT deficiency** (X-linked) — purine salvage pathway | Hyperuricemia, gout, self-mutilation, choreoathetosis, intellectual disability |
| Gout | Overproduction/underexcretion of uric acid; PRPP synthetase overactivity (rare primary cause) | Monosodium urate crystals, negatively birefringent, podagra |
| Von Gierke disease (see carb metabolism) | Glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency | Secondary hyperuricemia from ↑purine degradation |
| SCID (Adenosine deaminase deficiency) | ADA deficiency | Accumulation of dATP toxic to lymphocytes → severe combined immunodeficiency |
| Orotic aciduria (hereditary) | UMP synthase deficiency (pyrimidine synthesis) | Megaloblastic anemia unresponsive to B12/folate, orotic acid crystals in urine, growth retardation; NO hyperammonemia (distinguishes from OTC deficiency which also causes ↑orotic acid but WITH hyperammonemia) |

## Chromosomal Disorders — Key Biochemical/Screening Correlates
| Disorder | Karyotype | Screening/Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Down syndrome | Trisomy 21 | ↓PAPP-A, ↓AFP, ↑β-hCG, ↑inhibin A (quad screen); ↑nuchal translucency |
| Edwards syndrome | Trisomy 18 | ↓AFP, ↓β-hCG, ↓estriol, ↓PAPP-A (all low) |
| Patau syndrome | Trisomy 13 | Similar pattern to Edwards; severe multiple malformations |
| Turner syndrome | 45,XO | ↓estrogen, ↑FSH/LH; webbed neck, streak ovaries |
| Klinefelter syndrome | 47,XXY | ↑FSH/LH, ↓testosterone; tall, gynecomastia, infertility |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most inborn errors of metabolism are autosomal recessive; most urea-cycle/lysosomal X-linked exceptions: OTC deficiency, Fabry disease, Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, Hunter syndrome
- Anticipation (worsening across generations) is classic for Huntington disease (CAG repeat expansion)
- Fragile X = most common inherited (X-linked) cause of intellectual disability; Down syndrome remains the most common genetic cause of intellectual disability overall
- Prader-Willi = paternal deletion; Angelman = maternal deletion — same chromosomal region (15q11-13), opposite parent-of-origin
- HGPRT deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan) → hyperuricemia + self-mutilation + choreoathetosis — classic triad
- Hereditary orotic aciduria: ↑orotic acid WITHOUT hyperammonemia (pyrimidine pathway defect) vs OTC deficiency: ↑orotic acid WITH hyperammonemia (urea cycle defect) — key differentiator
- ADA deficiency = one cause of SCID — toxic dATP accumulation kills lymphocytes
- Quad screen: Down syndrome → low AFP, low PAPP-A, high hCG, high inhibin A; Edwards syndrome → everything low
