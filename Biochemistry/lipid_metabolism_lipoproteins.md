# Lipid Metabolism Lipoproteins

## Overview
Lipid metabolism encompasses fatty acid oxidation (energy production), fatty acid/triglyceride/cholesterol synthesis (storage and membrane/hormone precursors), and transport of lipids in blood via lipoproteins (since lipids are insoluble in plasma).

## Fatty Acid Synthesis (Cytoplasm, Fed State)
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Rate-limiting enzyme | **Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)** — converts Acetyl-CoA → Malonyl-CoA |
| Regulation of ACC | Activated by citrate, insulin (dephosphorylation); Inhibited by palmitoyl-CoA (product), glucagon/epinephrine (phosphorylation via AMPK) |
| Key synthetic enzyme | Fatty acid synthase (FAS) — multienzyme complex, requires NADPH (from HMP shunt/malic enzyme) |
| Citrate shuttle | Mitochondrial Acetyl-CoA exported as citrate → cleaved in cytosol by ATP-citrate lyase back to Acetyl-CoA + OAA |
| Elongation/Desaturation | Occurs in ER; humans lack Δ12 and Δ15 desaturases → cannot synthesize linoleic acid (ω-6) or α-linolenic acid (ω-3) → essential fatty acids |

## Fatty Acid Oxidation (β-Oxidation, Mitochondria, Fasting State)
| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Activation | Fatty acid + CoA → Fatty acyl-CoA (fatty acyl-CoA synthetase, uses ATP) |
| Transport into mitochondria | **Carnitine shuttle**: CPT-I (outer membrane, rate-limiting, inhibited by malonyl-CoA) → carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase → CPT-II (inner membrane, regenerates fatty acyl-CoA) |
| Oxidation cycle | Each cycle: Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (FADH2) → Enoyl-CoA hydratase → 3-Hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase (NADH) → Thiolase (releases Acetyl-CoA, shortens chain by 2C) |
| Regulation | CPT-I inhibited by malonyl-CoA — key reciprocal control ensuring synthesis and oxidation don't occur simultaneously (fed state: ↑malonyl-CoA blocks oxidation; fasting: ↓malonyl-CoA permits oxidation) |
| Odd-chain fatty acids | Final propionyl-CoA converted to succinyl-CoA (requires **Vitamin B12** + biotin) → enters TCA cycle → only route by which fat can contribute (indirectly, minor) to gluconeogenesis |

- Ketone body formation (ketogenesis): occurs in liver mitochondria during prolonged fasting/starvation/DM (uncontrolled) — Acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA (via HMG-CoA synthase) → Acetoacetate → β-hydroxybutyrate/acetone
- Ketone bodies are a major fuel for brain during prolonged starvation (brain cannot use fatty acids directly, crosses BBB via ketones)

## Cholesterol Synthesis
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Rate-limiting enzyme | **HMG-CoA reductase** (ER, liver) — converts HMG-CoA → Mevalonate |
| Regulation | Inhibited by cholesterol (feedback), glucagon, statins (competitive inhibitors — drug class); Activated by insulin, thyroid hormone |
| Rhythm | Enzyme activity peaks at night — statins with short half-life dosed at bedtime |
| Fate of cholesterol | Bile acids (rate-limiting: 7α-hydroxylase), steroid hormones, vitamin D, membrane component |

## Lipoprotein Classification
| Lipoprotein | Origin | Major Lipid Carried | Key Apoprotein | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chylomicrons | Intestine | Dietary triglycerides | ApoB-48 (structural), ApoC-II (activates LPL), ApoE | Transport dietary fat to peripheral tissues |
| VLDL | Liver | Endogenous triglycerides | ApoB-100, ApoC-II, ApoE | Transport hepatic TG to tissues |
| IDL | VLDL remnant | Cholesterol + TG | ApoB-100, ApoE | Intermediate; taken up by liver or converted to LDL |
| LDL | IDL remnant | Cholesterol (highest %) | ApoB-100 | Delivers cholesterol to peripheral tissues; "bad" — atherogenic |
| HDL | Liver/intestine | Phospholipid + cholesterol | **ApoA-I** (activates LCAT) | Reverse cholesterol transport — "good" cholesterol |

- **Lipoprotein lipase (LPL)**: endothelial surface enzyme, hydrolyzes TG in chylomicrons/VLDL, activated by ApoC-II; deficiency → Type I hyperlipoproteinemia (chylomicronemia)
- **LCAT** (lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase): esterifies free cholesterol on HDL, activated by ApoA-I
- **CETP** (cholesteryl ester transfer protein): transfers cholesteryl esters between lipoproteins
- LDL receptor recognizes ApoB-100/ApoE; defective/absent in Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## Clinically Relevant Disorders
| Disorder | Defect | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Familial Hypercholesterolemia | LDL receptor defect (AD) | Very high LDL, premature CAD, tendon xanthomas, corneal arcus; homozygotes — MI in childhood |
| Type I hyperlipoproteinemia | LPL or ApoC-II deficiency | Chylomicronemia, eruptive xanthomas, pancreatitis, creamy layer on standing plasma |
| Abetalipoproteinemia | MTP defect (no ApoB synthesis) | No chylomicrons/VLDL/LDL; fat malabsorption, acanthocytosis, ataxia (vitamin E deficiency) |
| Tangier disease | ABCA1 defect (no HDL formation) | Very low HDL, orange tonsils, cholesteryl ester accumulation in tissues |
| Carnitine deficiency / CPT deficiency | Impaired fatty acid entry into mitochondria | Hypoketotic hypoglycemia, muscle weakness/cramps with exercise (CPT-II), cardiomyopathy |
| MCAD deficiency | Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency | Most common fatty acid oxidation disorder; hypoketotic hypoglycemia, vomiting, lethargy on fasting; risk of sudden infant death |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Rate-limiting enzyme of fatty acid synthesis: **Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)**; of cholesterol synthesis: **HMG-CoA reductase**; of β-oxidation transport: **CPT-I**
- Malonyl-CoA is the key link inhibiting CPT-I — prevents simultaneous synthesis and oxidation of fat
- Statins competitively inhibit HMG-CoA reductase — best given at night due to enzyme's nocturnal peak activity
- ApoB-48 = chylomicrons (intestine); ApoB-100 = VLDL/IDL/LDL (liver) — same gene, different mRNA editing in intestine
- ApoC-II activates LPL; ApoE mediates remnant uptake by liver
- HDL's ApoA-I activates LCAT for reverse cholesterol transport
- Ketone bodies (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone) are the brain's alternative fuel in prolonged starvation
- Humans cannot synthesize linoleic acid or α-linolenic acid — essential fatty acids must come from diet
- Vitamin B12 required for propionyl-CoA → methylmalonyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA conversion (odd-chain fatty acid metabolism)
- MCAD deficiency = most common inherited disorder of fatty acid oxidation
