# Lysosomal Storage Diseases

## Overview
Lysosomal storage diseases (LSDs) result from deficiency of lysosomal enzymes (or transport proteins) needed to degrade macromolecules (sphingolipids, mucopolysaccharides, glycogen), leading to substrate accumulation within lysosomes of various tissues. Mostly autosomal recessive; key X-linked exceptions are Fabry disease and Hunter syndrome (MPS II).

## Sphingolipidoses (Lipid Storage Disorders)
| Disease | Enzyme Deficient | Substrate Accumulated | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tay-Sachs disease** | Hexosaminidase A | GM2 ganglioside | Cherry-red spot on macula, progressive neurodegeneration, developmental regression by ~6 months, **no hepatosplenomegaly** (key differentiator from Gaucher/Niemann-Pick), high prevalence in Ashkenazi Jews |
| **Gaucher disease** | Glucocerebrosidase (β-glucosidase) | Glucocerebroside | **Most common lysosomal storage disease overall**; hepatosplenomegaly, bone crises/pathologic fractures, "crumpled tissue paper" macrophages (Gaucher cells), pancytopenia; Ashkenazi Jews; Type 1 (adult, non-neuronopathic) most common |
| **Niemann-Pick disease (Type A/B)** | Sphingomyelinase | Sphingomyelin | Hepatosplenomegaly, cherry-red spot (Type A, like Tay-Sachs but WITH hepatosplenomegaly), foam cells, progressive neurodegeneration (Type A); Ashkenazi Jews |
| **Fabry disease** | α-galactosidase A (**X-linked**) | Ceramide trihexoside (globotriaosylceramide) | Angiokeratomas, peripheral neuropathy (burning pain in extremities), renal failure, corneal opacities (cornea verticillata), cardiovascular disease |
| **Krabbe disease** | Galactocerebrosidase | Galactocerebroside | Peripheral neuropathy, optic atrophy, globoid cells, severe developmental delay |
| **Metachromatic leukodystrophy** | Arylsulfatase A | Cerebroside sulfate | Central and peripheral demyelination, ataxia, dementia |

## Mucopolysaccharidoses (MPS)
| Disease | Enzyme Deficient | GAG Accumulated | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hurler syndrome (MPS I)** | α-L-iduronidase | Heparan sulfate + Dermatan sulfate | Corneal clouding, coarse facial features, hepatosplenomegaly, developmental delay, gargoylism |
| **Hunter syndrome (MPS II)** | Iduronate sulfatase (**X-linked**) | Heparan sulfate + Dermatan sulfate | Similar to Hurler but MILDER, NO corneal clouding, X-linked (mnemonic: Hunters need to see, so no corneal clouding) |

- Both present with organomegaly, coarse facies, joint stiffness; MPS are the classic LSDs with mucopolysacchariduria (excess GAGs in urine — used for diagnosis)

## Glycogen Storage Disease with Lysosomal Involvement
| Disease | Enzyme Deficient | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Pompe disease (GSD II)** | Lysosomal α-1,4-glucosidase (acid maltase) | Cardiomegaly, hypotonia ("floppy baby"), hepatomegaly; unique among GSDs as a LYSOSOMAL storage disease (others are cytoplasmic enzyme defects) |

## Diagnostic Approach
- Enzyme assay on leukocytes/fibroblasts = definitive diagnosis for most LSDs
- Prenatal diagnosis via chorionic villus sampling/amniocentesis enzyme assay in known familial cases
- Urinary GAG electrophoresis for suspected MPS
- Bone marrow biopsy: characteristic macrophages — Gaucher cells (crumpled tissue paper), foam cells (Niemann-Pick)
- Enzyme replacement therapy available for several LSDs (e.g., imiglucerase for Gaucher, agalsidase for Fabry, laronidase for Hurler)

## Key Differentiating Points (Classic Exam Traps)
| Comparison | Distinguishing Feature |
|---|---|
| Tay-Sachs vs Niemann-Pick (both cherry-red spot, both Ashkenazi Jewish) | Tay-Sachs has NO hepatosplenomegaly; Niemann-Pick HAS hepatosplenomegaly |
| Gaucher vs Niemann-Pick (both hepatosplenomegaly) | Gaucher = glucocerebrosidase deficiency, no cherry-red spot typically, bone crises; Niemann-Pick = sphingomyelinase deficiency, cherry-red spot (Type A), foam cells |
| Hurler vs Hunter (MPS I vs II) | Hunter = X-linked, milder, NO corneal clouding; Hurler = autosomal recessive, severe, WITH corneal clouding |
| Pompe vs other GSDs | Pompe is the only glycogen storage disease that is a lysosomal enzyme defect (acid maltase); others (von Gierke, McArdle, Cori) are cytosolic/other organelle enzyme defects |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common lysosomal storage disease overall: **Gaucher disease**
- Tay-Sachs = Hexosaminidase A deficiency, cherry-red spot, NO organomegaly (key trap vs Niemann-Pick)
- Fabry disease and Hunter syndrome = the two classic X-linked lysosomal storage diseases (most LSDs are autosomal recessive)
- Gaucher cells = "crumpled tissue paper" macrophages; Niemann-Pick = foam cells
- Hurler (corneal clouding, AR) vs Hunter (no corneal clouding, X-linked) — mnemonic: Hunters can see fine
- Pompe disease = only glycogen storage disease caused by a lysosomal enzyme (acid α-glucosidase/acid maltase) deficiency
- Ashkenazi Jewish population has increased carrier frequency for Tay-Sachs, Gaucher, and Niemann-Pick disease
- Fabry disease: angiokeratomas + burning extremity pain + renal failure + corneal verticillata — classic tetrad
- Enzyme replacement therapy is available and effective for Gaucher disease (imiglucerase) and several other LSDs, though it does not cross the blood-brain barrier (no benefit for neurological manifestations)
