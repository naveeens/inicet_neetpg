# Sudden Infant Death Syndrome

## Definition
Sudden, unexpected death of an infant younger than 1 year, occurring typically during sleep, that remains **unexplained after a thorough investigation** — including complete autopsy, examination of the death scene, and review of clinical history. SIDS is a diagnosis of exclusion (a "diagnosis by exclusion of all other causes").

## Epidemiology (High Yield)
- Peak incidence: **2-4 months of age** (most cases occur between 1-6 months; rare after 6 months and before 2 weeks)
- More common in **males**
- Most deaths occur during **sleep, typically at night**
- Leading cause of death between 1 month and 1 year of age in many Western countries (verify current epidemiological data/registry statistics if precise figures are asked, as rates have declined substantially since "Back to Sleep" campaigns)

## Risk Factors

### Infant-Related
- **Prone sleeping position** — single most important modifiable risk factor
- Prematurity and low birth weight
- Male sex
- Prior sibling with SIDS (increases risk, though most cases are not genetic/familial)
- Overheating/overwrapping (excess bedding, high room temperature)
- Soft sleeping surface, loose bedding, pillows, soft toys in crib (co-suffocation risk overlaps with SIDS risk factors)
- Co-sleeping/bed-sharing with parents (increases risk, especially with additional risk factors like parental smoking/alcohol or very soft bedding)

### Maternal/Environmental
- **Maternal smoking during pregnancy** (and secondhand smoke exposure after birth) — major independent risk factor
- Maternal drug/alcohol use during pregnancy
- Young maternal age, low socioeconomic status, inadequate prenatal care
- Lack of breastfeeding (breastfeeding is associated with reduced risk)

## Pathophysiology (Proposed — Not Fully Understood)
- Believed to be **multifactorial**, often explained by the **"Triple Risk Model"**:
  1. Vulnerable infant (e.g., brainstem abnormality in arousal/cardiorespiratory control, genetic susceptibility)
  2. Critical developmental period (2-4 months, when sleep-arousal and cardiorespiratory control mechanisms are still maturing)
  3. Exogenous stressor(s) (prone sleeping, overheating, soft bedding, co-sleeping)
- Proposed mechanism involves failure of arousal response and protective reflexes to hypoxia/hypercapnia during sleep (possible abnormalities in brainstem serotonergic system have been implicated in research)

## Prevention — "Safe Sleep" Recommendations (High Yield, Frequently Tested)
- **Supine sleeping position** ("Back to Sleep") for every sleep, until 1 year of age — the single most effective preventive measure
- Firm, flat sleep surface; avoid soft mattresses/couches/sofas for infant sleep
- **Avoid loose bedding, pillows, soft toys, and bumper pads** in the crib
- **Room-sharing without bed-sharing** — infant sleeps in parents' room but in own crib/bassinet, ideally for at least the first 6 months
- Avoid overheating/overwrapping the infant
- Avoid smoking during pregnancy and secondhand smoke exposure after birth
- Breastfeeding is protective (encourage exclusive breastfeeding)
- Pacifier use during sleep has been associated with reduced risk (once breastfeeding is well established)
- Routine immunization does NOT increase SIDS risk (important myth-busting exam point — vaccines are NOT a cause of SIDS)
- Avoid alcohol/illicit drug use by caregivers, especially when co-sleeping

## Differential Diagnosis / Mimics to Exclude at Autopsy
- Accidental suffocation/asphyxiation in bed (overlapping risk factors but distinct mechanism)
- Undiagnosed congenital metabolic disorders (e.g., fatty acid oxidation defects like MCAD deficiency can mimic SIDS)
- Undiagnosed congenital heart disease/arrhythmia (e.g., long QT syndrome)
- Infections (occult sepsis, myocarditis)
- Non-accidental injury/child abuse — must always be considered and excluded in sudden unexplained infant death
- Metabolic/electrolyte abnormalities

## Approach After a Sudden Infant Death
- Thorough death scene investigation (sleep environment, position found, bedding)
- Complete autopsy including toxicology and metabolic screening
- Review of clinical and family history (including any prior sibling deaths)
- Diagnosis of SIDS is only made once ALL other explanations have been excluded
- Bereavement support for family; investigation of surviving siblings' risk in familial contexts if indicated

## High-Yield One-Liners
- SIDS is a diagnosis of exclusion — made only after thorough autopsy, scene investigation, and history review find no other cause
- Peak age of occurrence is 2-4 months; rare before 2 weeks or after 6 months
- Prone sleeping position is the single most important modifiable risk factor
- Supine ("Back to Sleep") positioning is the single most effective preventive measure
- Maternal smoking during pregnancy is a major independent risk factor
- Room-sharing (not bed-sharing) with the infant in a separate crib is recommended
- Vaccination does NOT cause or increase the risk of SIDS — a common exam myth-buster
- Breastfeeding and pacifier use during sleep are both associated with reduced SIDS risk
