# Pediatric Infectious Diseases

## Exanthematous Fevers — Classic Comparison Table (High Yield)

| Disease | Agent | Prodrome/Rash Pattern | Key Signs | Rash Spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measles (Rubeola) | Measles virus (Paramyxovirus) | Fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis ("3 C's") → Koplik spots (buccal mucosa, pathognomonic, appear 1-2 days before rash) | Maculopapular rash, appears day 3-4 of fever | Face → trunk → extremities (cephalocaudal), rash coincides with fever peak |
| Rubella (German Measles) | Rubella virus | Mild fever, posterior auricular/suboccipital lymphadenopathy | Forchheimer spots (soft palate, not specific) | Face → trunk, spreads and clears fast (1 rash "day" per body part), milder than measles |
| Chickenpox (Varicella) | VZV | Fever + rash together (no significant prodrome in kids) | **Pleomorphism** — lesions in different stages simultaneously ("crops"): macule→papule→vesicle→pustule→crust | Centripetal (trunk → face/extremities), "dew drop on rose petal" vesicles |
| Scarlet Fever | Group A Streptococcus (erythrogenic toxin) | Sore throat, fever | **Strawberry tongue**, circumoral pallor, Pastia's lines (skin folds), sandpaper rash | Trunk → extremities, desquamation in 2nd week |
| Roseola Infantum (Exanthem Subitum) | HHV-6 | High fever (3-4 days) that resolves abruptly, THEN rash appears | Febrile seizures common (high fever in young infant) | Rash appears as fever breaks — trunk → face/extremities |
| Erythema Infectiosum (Fifth Disease) | Parvovirus B19 | Mild fever | **"Slapped cheek"** appearance, lacy/reticular rash on extremities | Face first (slapped cheek), then lace-like rash on limbs/trunk |
| Hand-Foot-Mouth Disease | Coxsackie A16 (enterovirus) | Mild fever, oral ulcers | Vesicles on palms, soles, and mouth | Hands, feet, mouth (buttocks sometimes) |

*High-yield: Koplik spots = pathognomonic for measles, appear BEFORE the rash. Slapped cheek = parvovirus B19 (also causes aplastic crisis in sickle cell/hemolytic anemia, and hydrops fetalis if maternal infection). Roseola = "fever first, rash after fever breaks" — reverse pattern from most exanthems.*

## Measles — Complications (Exam Favorites)
- Otitis media (most common complication overall)
- Pneumonia (most common cause of death in measles)
- Acute encephalitis (rare, within days-weeks)
- **Subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (SSPE)** — delayed (years later), fatal degenerative CNS disease from persistent defective measles virus
- Immunosuppression can persist for weeks after infection

## Diphtheria
- Corynebacterium diphtheriae (exotoxin-mediated)
- **Grayish-white pseudomembrane** over tonsils/pharynx, bleeds on attempted removal, does not wipe off easily
- "Bull neck" appearance from cervical lymphadenopathy + edema
- Complications: myocarditis (most common cause of death), toxic neuropathy (palatal palsy, later peripheral neuropathy), airway obstruction
- Treatment: **Diphtheria antitoxin (DAT) is priority** — given on clinical suspicion without waiting for culture confirmation, plus antibiotics (penicillin/erythromycin)

## Pertussis (Whooping Cough)
- Bordetella pertussis
- Catarrhal stage (most infectious) → Paroxysmal stage (paroxysms of cough + inspiratory "whoop," post-tussive vomiting) → Convalescent stage
- Young infants (<6 months) may present with apnea instead of classic whoop
- Marked lymphocytosis characteristic
- Treatment: macrolides (azithromycin); prophylaxis for close contacts

## Dengue
- Aedes aegypti mosquito (day-biting)
- Fever + severe myalgia/arthralgia ("break-bone fever"), retro-orbital pain, maculopapular rash
- Warning signs (WHO): abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, lethargy, liver enlargement, rising hematocrit with rapid platelet drop
- **Dengue hemorrhagic fever/shock syndrome**: plasma leakage → hemoconcentration (rising hematocrit ≥20%), thrombocytopenia (<100,000), bleeding manifestations
- Tourniquet test positive (capillary fragility)
- Management: supportive, careful fluid management (avoid fluid overload), platelet transfusion only if active bleeding + very low counts (not prophylactic for number alone per current guidance)
- NS1 antigen (early, first 5 days), IgM/IgG serology (later)

## Enteric Fever (Typhoid)
- Salmonella Typhi/Paratyphi
- Stepladder fever, relative bradycardia, rose spots (trunk), hepatosplenomegaly, coated tongue
- Widal test (paired sera, rising titer more significant than single value — limited utility, high false positive in endemic areas)
- Blood culture gold standard in first week; bone marrow/stool culture more useful later in illness
- Complications: intestinal perforation, GI bleed (2nd-3rd week), typhoid encephalopathy
- Treatment: ceftriaxone/azithromycin (increasing fluoroquinolone resistance is a known issue in India)

## Tuberculosis in Children
- Primary complex (Ghon focus + regional lymphadenitis) most common presentation
- Mantoux test (Tuberculin skin test): induration ≥10 mm positive in immunocompetent; ≥5 mm in immunocompromised/close contacts (thresholds per standard national guidelines — verify current RNTCP/NTEP criteria)
- BCG vaccination can cause false-positive Mantoux (usually smaller induration)
- Chest X-ray: hilar lymphadenopathy characteristic in primary TB (vs cavitary disease in adults/reactivation)
- DOTS therapy — directly observed treatment, short course; pediatric fixed-dose combinations per weight bands (RNTEP/NTEP guidance)
- Miliary TB and TB meningitis are severe disseminated forms seen especially in young/malnourished children

## Acute Rheumatic Fever (Post-Streptococcal)
- Follows Group A streptococcal pharyngitis (2-4 weeks latency)
- **Modified Jones Criteria**: 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor (plus evidence of preceding GAS infection)
  - Major: Carditis, Polyarthritis (migratory, large joints), Chorea (Sydenham), Erythema marginatum, Subcutaneous nodules ("CANCER — Carditis, Arthritis, Nodules, Chorea, Erythema marginatum, Rheumatic")
  - Minor: Fever, arthralgia, ↑ESR/CRP, prolonged PR interval
- Mitral valve most commonly affected (mitral regurgitation acutely, mitral stenosis chronically)
- Secondary prophylaxis: **Benzathine penicillin G IM every 3-4 weeks**, continued for years (duration depends on carditis severity — 5 years/until age 21, or lifelong if severe valve disease)

## Acute Bacterial Meningitis — Age-Specific Organisms (High Yield)
| Age Group | Common Organisms |
|---|---|
| Neonates (0-3 months) | Group B Streptococcus, E. coli, Listeria monocytogenes |
| 1 month - 5 years | Streptococcus pneumoniae, Neisseria meningitidis, Haemophilus influenzae type b (declining with Hib vaccination) |
| >5 years/adolescents | S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis |

- CSF findings: ↑WBC (neutrophilic), ↑protein, ↓glucose (CSF:blood glucose ratio <0.4)
- Empirical therapy: 3rd generation cephalosporin (cefotaxime/ceftriaxone) ± vancomycin (pneumococcal resistance) ± ampicillin (neonates, for Listeria coverage)
- Dexamethasone (before or with first antibiotic dose) reduces neurological complications, especially in Hib meningitis

## Congenital TORCH Infections (Brief)
- **T**oxoplasmosis, **O**ther (syphilis, varicella, parvovirus), **R**ubella, **C**MV, **H**erpes/HIV
- Classic triad of congenital rubella: cataract, deafness (sensorineural), cardiac defects (PDA most common)
- CMV: most common congenital infection overall; periventricular calcifications, microcephaly, sensorineural hearing loss
- Toxoplasmosis: chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, diffuse intracranial calcifications

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Koplik spots = pathognomonic for measles, appear before rash
- Roseola: fever resolves THEN rash appears (unique reverse pattern)
- Slapped cheek rash = Parvovirus B19; causes aplastic crisis in hemolytic anemias
- Diphtheria: give antitoxin on clinical suspicion, do not wait for culture
- Dengue warning signs mandate close monitoring — rising hematocrit + falling platelets = plasma leakage
- Mitral valve is the most commonly affected valve in acute rheumatic fever
- Secondary prophylaxis for rheumatic fever = monthly benzathine penicillin G, continued for years
- CMV is the most common congenital infection; classic triad of congenital rubella = cataract, deafness, PDA
- Neonatal meningitis: cover for GBS, E. coli, and Listeria (ampicillin + 3rd gen cephalosporin)
