# Pediatric Genetic Metabolic Disorders

## Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21)
- Most common chromosomal disorder / most common cause of intellectual disability due to a chromosomal abnormality
- **Cause**: nondisjunction (~95%, associated with advanced maternal age), translocation (~4%, often Robertsonian t(14;21), can be inherited/familial — check parental karyotype if translocation type), mosaicism (~1%, often milder phenotype)
- **Features**: upslanting palpebral fissures, epicanthic folds, flat nasal bridge, Brushfield spots (iris), protruding tongue, single palmar crease (simian crease), **sandal gap** (wide gap between 1st-2nd toe), hypotonia, short stature, clinodactyly
- **Associations**: Congenital heart disease (~40-50%, **endocardial cushion defect/AV canal defect most characteristic**, also VSD), duodenal atresia ("double bubble" sign), Hirschsprung disease, hypothyroidism (screen periodically), atlantoaxial instability, increased risk of **acute leukemia** (both ALL and AML, especially transient myeloproliferative disorder in neonates), early-onset Alzheimer's disease in adulthood
- Diagnosis: karyotype confirms; antenatal screening (double/triple/quadruple marker test, NT scan, NIPT) followed by confirmatory testing

## Turner Syndrome (45,X)
- Monosomy X (or mosaic 45,X/46,XX); phenotypically female
- Features: short stature, **webbed neck**, shield chest with widely spaced nipples, low posterior hairline, lymphedema of hands/feet (especially at birth), **primary amenorrhea/streak gonads** (gonadal dysgenesis → infertility)
- Associations: **coarctation of aorta** (and bicuspid aortic valve), horseshoe kidney, hypothyroidism (autoimmune), normal intelligence generally (verbal IQ typically normal, some visuospatial difficulty)
- Management: growth hormone therapy for short stature, estrogen replacement at appropriate age for secondary sexual characteristics

## Klinefelter Syndrome (47,XXY)
- Phenotypically male; most common cause of male hypogonadism / male infertility due to chromosomal abnormality
- Features: tall stature, gynecomastia, small firm testes, infertility (azoospermia), decreased facial/body hair, learning difficulties may be present
- ↑FSH/LH, low testosterone

## Fragile X Syndrome
- Most common inherited cause of intellectual disability (X-linked, CGG trinucleotide repeat expansion in FMR1 gene)
- Features: long face, large everted ears, macroorchidism (post-pubertal), mitral valve prolapse, autism spectrum features, intellectual disability (more severe in males)
- Shows **anticipation** (repeat expansion worsens across generations)

## Inborn Errors of Metabolism — Key High-Yield Conditions

### Phenylketonuria (PKU)
- Deficiency of **phenylalanine hydroxylase** → phenylalanine accumulation, tyrosine deficiency
- Autosomal recessive
- Clinical: normal at birth → progressive intellectual disability if untreated, **"musty/mousy" odor of urine/sweat**, fair skin/hair/blue eyes (deficient melanin from tyrosine lack), eczema, seizures, microcephaly
- Screening: newborn screening (Guthrie test) universal in many countries — high-yield exam point even where universal NBS is not yet standard practice in India
- Management: dietary phenylalanine restriction (lifelong or at least through critical brain development), tyrosine supplementation — early dietary treatment prevents intellectual disability

### Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD)
- Deficiency of branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase → accumulation of leucine, isoleucine, valine
- Sweet, "maple syrup" smelling urine/cerumen
- Presents in neonatal period with poor feeding, lethargy, seizures, encephalopathy — metabolic emergency
- Management: dietary restriction of branched-chain amino acids, thiamine (cofactor) in some variants

### Galactosemia
- Classic form: deficiency of **galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase (GALT)**
- Presents after starting milk feeds: vomiting, jaundice, hepatomegaly, cataracts, hypoglycemia, **E. coli sepsis** (classic association — galactosemia predisposes to gram-negative sepsis), failure to thrive
- Management: lactose/galactose-free diet (soy-based formula) — reverses most acute manifestations if started early

### Glycogen Storage Diseases (Key Types)
| Type | Enzyme Deficiency | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Type I (von Gierke) | Glucose-6-phosphatase | Severe fasting hypoglycemia, massive hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis, "doll-like face" |
| Type II (Pompe) | Acid alpha-glucosidase (lysosomal) | Cardiomegaly, hypotonia ("floppy baby"), muscle weakness — the glycogenosis with cardiac involvement |
| Type V (McArdle) | Muscle phosphorylase | Exercise intolerance, muscle cramps, myoglobinuria (adult/adolescent onset typically) |

### Mucopolysaccharidoses (Key Types)
| Type | Enzyme Deficiency | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Hurler syndrome (MPS I) | Alpha-L-iduronidase | Coarse facies, corneal clouding, intellectual disability, hepatosplenomegaly, dysostosis multiplex — most severe |
| Hunter syndrome (MPS II) | Iduronate sulfatase | Similar to Hurler but **X-linked** (only MPS that is X-linked) and **no corneal clouding** |

*High-yield: Hunter = "no cloudy cornea, X-linked" — classic distinguishing pair against Hurler (autosomal recessive, corneal clouding present).*

### Lysosomal Storage Disorders — Sphingolipidoses
| Disease | Enzyme Deficiency | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Tay-Sachs disease | Hexosaminidase A | **Cherry-red spot on macula**, progressive neurodegeneration, exaggerated startle response, NO hepatosplenomegaly (distinguishes from Niemann-Pick/Gaucher), common in Ashkenazi Jewish population |
| Niemann-Pick disease | Sphingomyelinase | Cherry-red spot (can occur) + hepatosplenomegaly (unlike Tay-Sachs) |
| Gaucher disease | Glucocerebrosidase | Hepatosplenomegaly, bone pain/pathologic fractures, **Gaucher cells** (crumpled-tissue-paper appearance) on marrow, pancytopenia — most common lysosomal storage disorder overall |
| Krabbe disease | Galactocerebrosidase | Severe infantile neurodegeneration, irritability, globoid cells |

*High-yield: Cherry-red spot + hepatosplenomegaly ABSENT = Tay-Sachs; cherry-red spot + hepatosplenomegaly PRESENT = Niemann-Pick.*

### Wilson Disease
- Autosomal recessive, ATP7B gene defect → impaired copper excretion into bile → copper accumulation in liver, brain, cornea
- Features: hepatic dysfunction (can present as acute liver failure or chronic liver disease), neuropsychiatric symptoms (tremor, dystonia, behavioral change), **Kayser-Fleischer rings** (corneal copper deposition, seen on slit-lamp)
- Labs: low serum ceruloplasmin, low serum copper, high urinary copper excretion
- Management: copper chelation (D-penicillamine, trientine), zinc (reduces copper absorption), liver transplant in fulminant failure

### Congenital Hypothyroidism
- Most common preventable cause of intellectual disability worldwide (hence importance of newborn screening where available)
- Causes: thyroid dysgenesis (most common — agenesis/ectopic/hypoplastic gland), dyshormonogenesis, maternal antithyroid drugs/iodine deficiency
- Features (often subtle at birth, hence screening importance): prolonged jaundice, lethargy, poor feeding, constipation, large posterior fontanelle, macroglossia, umbilical hernia, hoarse cry, hypotonia — untreated leads to **cretinism** (irreversible intellectual disability, short stature)
- Management: **levothyroxine started as early as possible** (ideally within first 2 weeks of life) — early treatment prevents neurodevelopmental damage; delay worsens prognosis

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Down syndrome: most common chromosomal cause of intellectual disability; AV canal defect is the most characteristic cardiac lesion
- Turner syndrome: webbed neck, coarctation of aorta, streak gonads, normal intelligence generally
- Klinefelter syndrome: most common chromosomal cause of male infertility
- Fragile X: most common INHERITED cause of intellectual disability, shows anticipation
- PKU: musty odor, fair pigmentation, treat with dietary phenylalanine restriction — prevents intellectual disability if started early
- Galactosemia classically predisposes to E. coli sepsis
- Tay-Sachs: cherry-red spot WITHOUT hepatosplenomegaly; Niemann-Pick: cherry-red spot WITH hepatosplenomegaly
- Gaucher disease is the most common lysosomal storage disorder
- Hunter syndrome (unlike Hurler) is X-linked and lacks corneal clouding
- Wilson disease: low ceruloplasmin + Kayser-Fleischer rings + hepatic/neuropsychiatric features
- Congenital hypothyroidism is the most common PREVENTABLE cause of intellectual disability — early levothyroxine is critical
