# Pediatric Nutritional Disorders Rickets Scurvy

## Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM) — Kwashiorkor vs Marasmus

| Feature | Kwashiorkor | Marasmus |
|---|---|---|
| Primary deficiency | Protein deficiency (relatively adequate calories) | Total caloric (energy) deficiency |
| Edema | Present (pitting, bilateral, classically starts in feet) | Absent |
| Muscle wasting | Present but masked by edema | Severe, obvious ("skin and bones") |
| Subcutaneous fat | Preserved (relatively) | Markedly lost |
| Hair changes | Sparse, depigmented, easily pluckable, **"flag sign"** (bands of alternating pigmentation reflecting nutritional status over time) | Present but less prominent |
| Skin changes | **"Flaky paint" dermatosis**, hyperpigmented patches that desquamate | Dry, wrinkled, "old man" facies |
| Mental changes | Apathetic, irritable, lethargic | Alert (relatively), but weak |
| Serum albumin | Low (hypoalbuminemia — contributes to edema) | Normal or mildly low |
| Fatty liver | Present | Absent |
| Appetite | Poor | Preserved (until severe) |

*High-yield: Edema + hypoalbuminemia + skin/hair changes = Kwashiorkor. Marasmus = pure calorie deficit, severe wasting without edema. Marasmic-kwashiorkor = mixed picture (edema + severe wasting).*

## WHO Classification of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM)
- Weight-for-height/length Z-score < -3 SD, OR
- Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) < 11.5 cm (6-59 months), OR
- Bilateral pitting edema (nutritional edema) — automatically classifies as SAM regardless of anthropometry
- IAP/WHO growth charts and Z-scores preferred over % of expected weight (older classifications like IAP/Gomez classification are largely historical now)

## Management of SAM (WHO 10-Step / Facility-Based Approach)
- Treat/prevent **hypoglycemia and hypothermia** first (immediate life threats)
- Treat/prevent dehydration cautiously — SAM children should NOT be rehydrated as aggressively as normally nourished (use ReSoMal, not standard ORS, due to altered electrolyte handling)
- Correct electrolyte imbalances (SAM children have low total body potassium/magnesium despite possibly normal serum levels)
- Treat infection — broad-spectrum antibiotics (often given even without overt signs of infection, as classic signs may be masked)
- Correct micronutrient deficiencies — **avoid iron in initial stabilization phase** (can worsen infection/oxidative stress; add iron only in rehabilitation phase after appetite returns)
- Start cautious feeding — F-75 formula (stabilization phase, lower protein/energy) → F-100 (rehabilitation phase, higher energy) or Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF)
- Achieve catch-up growth (rehabilitation phase)
- Provide sensory stimulation and emotional support
- Prepare for follow-up after discharge

*High-yield: Refeeding syndrome — reintroducing calories/carbohydrate too fast in a severely malnourished child causes a shift of phosphate/potassium/magnesium intracellularly → hypophosphatemia, arrhythmia, heart failure. This is why feeding starts cautiously (F-75) rather than aggressively.*

## Vitamin D Deficiency Rickets

### Etiology
- Deficient vitamin D (dietary/sunlight lack) → impaired intestinal calcium/phosphate absorption → secondary hyperparathyroidism → defective mineralization of growing bone (osteoid formed normally but fails to mineralize)
- Affects growing bones — hence a disease of children (adult equivalent = osteomalacia, no growth plates involved)

### Clinical Features (Age-Dependent Presentation — High Yield)
| Age | Classic Sign |
|---|---|
| Infants | Craniotabes (soft skull, "ping-pong ball" feel), delayed fontanelle closure, frontal bossing |
| 6-12 months | **Rachitic rosary** (costochondral junction widening), Harrison's sulcus (indrawing along diaphragm attachment) |
| Toddlers/walking age | **Bow legs (genu varum)** — most common in weight-bearing age; widened wrists/ankles |
| Older children | Genu valgum can also occur, kyphoscoliosis, delayed dentition, dental enamel defects |
| General | Widened epiphyses (wrists — "double malleoli" sign), muscle hypotonia, delayed motor milestones, delayed closure of fontanelles |

- Craniotabes is a fairly early and sensitive (though nonspecific) sign
- Rachitic rosary = beading at costochondral junctions (compare with scurvy's rosary, which is sharper/angulated)

### Diagnosis
- Serum **25-hydroxyvitamin D** = best test for vitamin D status (low)
- Serum calcium: normal/low; serum phosphate: low; **Alkaline phosphatase markedly elevated** (best biochemical marker of disease activity in rickets)
- PTH: elevated (secondary hyperparathyroidism)
- X-ray wrist (most commonly imaged site): cupping, fraying, and splaying of metaphysis; widened epiphyseal plate; "double contour" of diaphysis

### Management
- Vitamin D supplementation (stoss therapy or daily dosing per standard protocols) + adequate calcium intake
- Sunlight exposure, dietary correction
- Nutritional rickets generally shows good radiological and clinical response within weeks of adequate therapy

## Scurvy (Vitamin C Deficiency)

### Etiology
- Vitamin C is required as a cofactor for **collagen synthesis** (hydroxylation of proline and lysine) — deficiency causes defective collagen → capillary fragility, poor wound healing, defective osteoid formation
- Seen in infants on unsupplemented boiled/canned milk formulas (vitamin C is heat-labile), rarely in exclusively breastfed infants beyond 6-12 months without complementary feeding

### Clinical Features
- **Irritability + pseudoparalysis** (child avoids moving limbs due to pain, mimicking paralysis but no true neurological deficit) — classic early presentation
- Subperiosteal hemorrhage (severe tenderness over long bones, especially lower limbs)
- Gum bleeding/swelling (especially where teeth have erupted)
- Perifollicular hemorrhages, "corkscrew hairs"
- **Scorbutic rosary** — costochondral beading, but sharper and more lateral/angulated than the rachitic rosary of rickets (exam distinguishing point)
- Poor wound healing

### Diagnosis
- Clinical + dietary history (inadequate vitamin C intake)
- X-ray long bones: **"ground glass" osteopenia**, **"pencil-thin cortex"**, **Frankel's line** (dense line of provisional calcification at metaphysis), **Trümmerfeld zone** (lucent zone beneath Frankel's line — zone of scurvy), **Pelkan spur** (lateral spike at metaphysis), subperiosteal hemorrhage/elevation
- Low plasma/leukocyte ascorbic acid levels

### Management
- Vitamin C supplementation — dramatic and rapid clinical improvement (pain relief within days) — a hallmark diagnostic-therapeutic clue

## Rickets vs Scurvy — Quick Differentiator (High Yield)
| Feature | Rickets | Scurvy |
|---|---|---|
| Deficiency | Vitamin D | Vitamin C |
| Defect | Mineralization defect | Collagen synthesis defect |
| Rosary | Blunt, rounded (costochondral) | Sharp, angulated |
| Pain | Less prominent | Severe (pseudoparalysis) |
| X-ray | Cupping/fraying/splaying of metaphysis | Ground-glass osteopenia, Frankel's line, Pelkan spur |
| Alk phos | Markedly elevated | Normal |

## Vitamin A Deficiency (Xerophthalmia)
- Leading preventable cause of childhood blindness in developing countries
- Progression: night blindness → conjunctival xerosis → **Bitot's spots** (foamy patches, conjunctiva) → corneal xerosis → keratomalacia (corneal liquefaction/necrosis — vision-threatening emergency)
- Management: Vitamin A supplementation per national guidelines (given with measles vaccine at 9 months, then 6-monthly to 5 years, as part of the national prophylaxis programme)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Kwashiorkor = edema + hypoalbuminemia + flaky skin/hair changes; Marasmus = severe wasting, no edema
- Bilateral pitting edema alone qualifies a child as Severe Acute Malnutrition regardless of anthropometry
- Avoid iron in the initial stabilization phase of SAM management; start after appetite returns
- Rachitic rosary (rickets) is blunt/rounded; scorbutic rosary (scurvy) is sharp/angulated
- Alkaline phosphatase is markedly elevated in rickets, normal in scurvy
- Genu varum (bow legs) is the classic deformity in toddlers with rickets
- Pseudoparalysis + gum bleeding + subperiosteal hemorrhage = scurvy; dramatic response to vitamin C is diagnostic
- Bitot's spots = vitamin A deficiency; keratomalacia is a vision-threatening ocular emergency
- 25-hydroxyvitamin D is the test of choice for vitamin D status
