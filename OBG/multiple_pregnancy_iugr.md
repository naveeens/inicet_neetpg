# Multiple Pregnancy & IUGR

## Multiple Pregnancy

### Types & Zygosity
| Type | Origin | Chorionicity/Amnionicity |
|---|---|---|
| Dizygotic (fraternal) | Two separate ova fertilized | Always **dichorionic diamniotic (DCDA)** |
| Monozygotic (identical) | Single fertilized ovum splits | Depends on timing of split (see below) |

### Monozygotic Twinning — Timing of Division
| Division Timing | Placentation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 days (morula) | Dichorionic diamniotic (DCDA) | ~1/3 of MZ twins |
| 4–8 days (blastocyst) | **Monochorionic diamniotic (MCDA)** | Most common MZ pattern (~2/3) |
| 8–13 days | **Monochorionic monoamniotic (MCMA)** | Rare, high risk (cord entanglement) |
| >13 days (after primitive streak forms) | **Conjoined twins** | Incomplete division |

### Diagnosis of Chorionicity
- Best determined by **first-trimester ultrasound** (before 14 weeks)
- **Lambda/twin-peak sign** = dichorionic
- **T-sign** = monochorionic
- Chorionicity is more prognostically important than zygosity

### Complications
| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Twin-to-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS)** | Occurs only in monochorionic pregnancies (shared placental circulation via vascular anastomoses) — donor twin: oligohydramnios, growth restriction, anemia; recipient twin: polyhydramnios, hydrops, polycythemia. Staged by **Quintero staging**. Treatment: **fetoscopic laser ablation of anastomoses** |
| Preterm labor | Very common — leading complication overall in multiples |
| Malpresentation | Higher incidence, especially of second twin |
| Hypertensive disorders, GDM, anemia | Increased maternal risk |
| Postpartum hemorrhage | Uterine atony from overdistension |
| IUGR/discordant growth | More common in monochorionic twins |
| Monoamniotic twins | Cord entanglement — high fetal mortality, close inpatient surveillance, early delivery |

### Management
- Serial growth scans (monochorionic twins monitored more frequently, every 2 weeks, given TTTS risk)
- Antenatal corticosteroids if preterm delivery anticipated
- Mode of delivery: depends on presentation of both twins — vertex/vertex favors vaginal delivery; malpresentation of first twin generally mandates cesarean
- Timing: uncomplicated DCDA twins ~37–38 weeks; MCDA ~36 weeks; MCMA earlier with elective cesarean (per current knowledge — verify against latest guideline)

## Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR) / Fetal Growth Restriction (FGR)

### Definition
Estimated fetal weight <10th percentile for gestational age (some definitions <3rd percentile for FGR specifically), often with evidence of pathological growth restriction rather than constitutionally small fetus.

### Classification
| Type | Onset | Cause pattern | Head-to-body ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Symmetric (Type I)** | Early (first/early second trimester) | Intrinsic — chromosomal anomalies, congenital infection (TORCH), genetic syndromes | Proportionate — head and body both reduced |
| **Asymmetric (Type II)** | Late (third trimester) | Extrinsic — placental insufficiency (pre-eclampsia, chronic hypertension), maternal malnutrition | Head spared relative to abdomen ("head-sparing" — brain-sparing effect) |

### Etiology
- Maternal: hypertensive disorders, chronic renal disease, smoking, substance use, malnutrition, thrombophilia, autoimmune disease
- Fetal: chromosomal abnormalities, structural anomalies, congenital infections (TORCH), multiple pregnancy
- Placental: insufficiency, infarcts, abnormal cord insertion, previa

### Diagnosis & Surveillance
- Serial fundal height measurement (screening), confirmed by ultrasound biometry (AC most sensitive single parameter, combined with HC, FL, BPD)
- **Doppler studies**: umbilical artery Doppler is key — raised resistance, absent/reversed end-diastolic flow (AREDF) signifies severe placental insufficiency and impending decompensation
- Sequence of deterioration in severe placental insufficiency: ↑ umbilical artery resistance → absent → reversed end-diastolic flow → MCA changes (brain-sparing) → ductus venosus abnormal waveform (pre-terminal) → biophysical profile decline
- Amniotic fluid volume — oligohydramnios common (reduced renal perfusion)
- Karyotyping/TORCH workup if symmetric IUGR or structural anomaly noted

### Management
- Treat/optimize reversible causes (BP control, smoking cessation)
- Serial growth scans + Doppler surveillance to time delivery
- Antenatal corticosteroids if preterm delivery anticipated
- Delivery timing individualized based on severity of Doppler changes, gestational age, and biophysical status — earlier delivery for AREDF/severe compromise, balancing prematurity risk

### Complications
- Perinatal asphyxia, meconium aspiration, hypoglycemia, hypothermia, polycythemia
- Increased perinatal mortality and morbidity
- Long-term: neurodevelopmental impairment, and per fetal-origins-of-adult-disease hypothesis, increased adult risk of metabolic syndrome/cardiovascular disease

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Chorionicity (not zygosity) determines pregnancy risk and management in twins — assess by first-trimester USG (lambda sign = dichorionic, T-sign = monochorionic)
- TTTS occurs exclusively in monochorionic twin pregnancies due to shared placental vascular anastomoses; treated with fetoscopic laser ablation
- Monoamniotic twins carry the highest risk due to cord entanglement
- Symmetric IUGR = early, intrinsic (chromosomal/infectious) cause; asymmetric IUGR = late, extrinsic (placental insufficiency) with brain-sparing
- Umbilical artery Doppler is the primary surveillance tool in IUGR; absent/reversed end-diastolic flow signals severe compromise
- Abdominal circumference is the most sensitive single ultrasound biometric parameter for detecting IUGR
