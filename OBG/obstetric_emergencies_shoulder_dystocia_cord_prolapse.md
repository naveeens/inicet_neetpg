# Obstetric Emergencies: Shoulder Dystocia & Cord Prolapse

## Shoulder Dystocia

### Definition
Failure of the fetal shoulders to deliver spontaneously after delivery of the head, requiring additional obstetric maneuvers beyond gentle downward traction — due to impaction of the anterior shoulder behind the maternal symphysis pubis.

### Risk Factors
- Fetal macrosomia (especially with maternal diabetes — disproportionate shoulder/trunk growth), maternal obesity, excessive maternal weight gain
- Post-term pregnancy, prior shoulder dystocia, operative vaginal delivery (forceps/vacuum), prolonged second stage
- *Many cases occur with no identifiable risk factors — cannot reliably predict or prevent, so all birth attendants must be prepared*

### Recognition
- **"Turtle sign"** — fetal head delivers then retracts back against the perineum
- Failure of restitution/external rotation

### Management — Stepwise (HELPERR mnemonic concept)
1. **Call for Help** (senior obstetrician, anesthetist, neonatologist, extra staff)
2. **Evaluate for Episiotomy** (to allow room for maneuvers, not to relieve bony obstruction itself)
3. **Legs** — **McRoberts maneuver**: hyperflex maternal thighs onto abdomen (first-line, most effective single maneuver — straightens sacrum, rotates symphysis, increases functional pelvic diameter)
4. **Pressure** — Suprapubic pressure (not fundal pressure — fundal pressure worsens impaction and is contraindicated) to dislodge anterior shoulder
5. **Enter** — internal maneuvers: **Rubin's maneuver** (rotate anterior shoulder), **Woods' corkscrew maneuver** (rotate posterior shoulder progressively)
6. **Remove** posterior arm — deliver the posterior arm manually to reduce bisacromial diameter
7. **Roll** the patient (Gaskin all-fours maneuver) — changes pelvic dimensions, can facilitate delivery
- Last resort (rare): deliberate clavicle fracture, Zavanelli maneuver (cephalic replacement + cesarean), symphysiotomy

### Complications
- Maternal: PPH, perineal/vaginal lacerations, uterine rupture (rare)
- Fetal: **brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy — most common)**, clavicle/humerus fracture, hypoxic brain injury, death

## Umbilical Cord Prolapse

### Definition
Descent of the umbilical cord through the cervix alongside or past the presenting part after rupture of membranes — obstetric emergency due to risk of cord compression and fetal hypoxia.

### Risk Factors
- Malpresentation (breech, especially footling; transverse lie), unengaged head, polyhydramnios, prematurity, multiple pregnancy (second twin), long cord, artificial rupture of membranes with unengaged/high presenting part

### Diagnosis
- Sudden fetal bradycardia/variable decelerations after membrane rupture
- Cord felt/visible on vaginal examination or at introitus

### Management — Immediate
1. **Call for help** — obstetric emergency, mobilize for immediate cesarean
2. **Avoid handling the cord** unnecessarily (minimize vasospasm); if cord is outside vagina, may gently replace into vagina to keep warm/moist — do not push back through cervix
3. **Relieve pressure on the cord**:
   - Manually elevate the presenting part off the cord (examiner's hand kept in vagina until delivery)
   - Position: **knee-chest position** or **exaggerated Sims'/left lateral position with hips elevated** or steep **Trendelenburg**
   - Fill the maternal bladder (retrograde bladder filling with ~500 mL saline) to elevate the presenting part off the cord during transfer to theatre
   - Tocolysis (e.g., terbutaline) may be considered to reduce contractions while preparing for delivery
4. **Immediate delivery** — Category 1 (crash) cesarean section in most cases; vaginal delivery only if fully dilated and delivery is imminent/rapid (e.g., low forceps/vacuum)

### Complications
- Fetal hypoxia, acidosis, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, stillbirth if prolonged compression before delivery
- Outcome is time-critical — decision-to-delivery interval is a key determinant of fetal outcome

## Mnemonics
- Shoulder dystocia stepwise maneuvers: **HELPERR** — Help, Episiotomy, Legs (McRoberts), Pressure (suprapubic), Enter (internal rotation), Remove posterior arm, Roll
- Cord prolapse immediate actions: elevate presenting part, position (knee-chest/Trendelenburg), bladder filling, expedite delivery

## High-Yield One-Liners
- McRoberts maneuver is the single most effective first-line maneuver for shoulder dystocia
- Fundal pressure is contraindicated in shoulder dystocia — worsens impaction and risks uterine rupture
- Most common neonatal injury from shoulder dystocia = brachial plexus (Erb's) palsy
- Cord prolapse: never push the cord back through the cervix; keep it warm/moist if exposed and elevate the presenting part manually
- Cord prolapse with cervix fully dilated and delivery imminent may allow rapid vaginal/instrumental delivery instead of cesarean
- In cord prolapse, decision-to-delivery time is the key determinant of fetal outcome — treat as a crash cesarean emergency
