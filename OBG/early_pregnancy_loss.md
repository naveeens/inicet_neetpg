# Early Pregnancy Loss

## Definition
Spontaneous loss of pregnancy before **20 weeks** of gestation (or below a viability weight threshold, e.g., <500 g, per some definitions). "Miscarriage" and "spontaneous abortion" are used synonymously.

## Etiology
- **Chromosomal abnormalities** — most common cause overall, especially in first-trimester losses (majority are aneuploid, most commonly autosomal trisomies)
- Uterine anomalies (septate uterus — highest miscarriage association among Müllerian anomalies), fibroids, Asherman syndrome
- Endocrine: luteal phase defect, poorly controlled diabetes, thyroid dysfunction, PCOS
- **Antiphospholipid syndrome** and other thrombophilias — classically associated with recurrent pregnancy loss, especially later first/second trimester losses
- Infections (TORCH, listeria), cervical insufficiency (mid-trimester painless loss)
- Maternal age (risk rises sharply after 35), lifestyle (smoking, alcohol), environmental toxins

## Classification & Clinical Types
| Type | Features | Cervical Os | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Threatened abortion** | Bleeding ± mild pain, viable pregnancy on USG | Closed | Expectant, rest, follow-up USG; pregnancy may continue |
| **Inevitable abortion** | Bleeding, pain, membrane rupture possible | Open | Miscarriage cannot be prevented; proceed per completeness |
| **Incomplete abortion** | Partial expulsion of products of conception | Open | Evacuation (medical or surgical) if bleeding/retained tissue |
| **Complete abortion** | Total expulsion of products, bleeding subsides | Closed | Observation, confirm with USG (empty uterus) |
| **Missed abortion** | Fetal demise, no expulsion, may be asymptomatic (no bleeding) | Closed | Confirm with USG (no cardiac activity); medical or surgical evacuation |
| **Septic abortion** | Infected products of conception — fever, foul discharge, tenderness | Variable | IV antibiotics + evacuation of uterus; can progress to sepsis/DIC |
| **Recurrent pregnancy loss** | **≥2–3 consecutive** miscarriages (definitions vary by guideline) | — | Detailed workup for underlying cause |

## Diagnosis
- **Transvaginal ultrasound** — key modality: confirms viability (fetal cardiac activity), gestational sac appearance, crown-rump length
- Serial serum β-hCG — useful when USG is equivocal (e.g., pregnancy of unknown location); normal early pregnancy shows a rise of ≥53% (approx.) over 48 hours — suboptimal rise suggests non-viable/ectopic pregnancy
- **Pregnancy of Unknown Location (PUL)**: no intrauterine or extrauterine gestational sac seen on TVS despite positive β-hCG — requires serial hCG/USG follow-up to distinguish very early pregnancy, complete miscarriage, or ectopic

## Management
| Approach | Details |
|---|---|
| **Expectant management** | Appropriate for incomplete miscarriage/missed abortion in stable, motivated patients willing for follow-up; success rate variable and lower for missed abortion |
| **Medical management** | **Misoprostol** (± mifepristone priming where available) — effective for incomplete/missed abortion, avoids surgery |
| **Surgical management** | **Suction/vacuum aspiration** (preferred, safer than sharp curettage) or dilatation and curettage — for incomplete abortion, heavy bleeding, infected/septic abortion, or failed medical/expectant management |
| Septic abortion | Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics + prompt evacuation of retained infected tissue; monitor for sepsis/DIC |
| Rh-negative women | **Anti-D immunoglobulin** should be given after any miscarriage (especially with instrumentation) to prevent sensitization |

## Cervical Insufficiency
- Painless cervical dilatation typically in the **second trimester**, leading to recurrent mid-trimester losses
- Diagnosis: history of prior painless mid-trimester losses, short cervical length on TVS in current pregnancy
- Management: **cervical cerclage** (e.g., McDonald or Shirodkar procedure) — typically placed prophylactically around 12–14 weeks in women with a strong history, or "rescue" cerclage if cervical shortening/dilation detected in current pregnancy before viability

## Recurrent Pregnancy Loss — Workup
- Parental karyotype, uterine cavity assessment (HSG/sonohysterography/hysteroscopy), antiphospholipid antibody panel (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2 glycoprotein I), thyroid function, diabetes screening, thrombophilia workup as indicated
- Management targets identified cause: **low-dose aspirin + heparin/LMWH** for confirmed antiphospholipid syndrome, surgical correction for uterine anomalies, cerclage for cervical insufficiency

## Complications
- Hemorrhage, infection (septic abortion → sepsis, DIC, renal failure)
- Asherman syndrome (post-curettage intrauterine adhesions)
- Rh sensitization if prophylaxis omitted
- Psychological impact — counseling is an important part of management

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Chromosomal abnormalities are the most common cause of sporadic first-trimester miscarriage
- Threatened abortion has a closed cervical os and a viable pregnancy on ultrasound; inevitable/incomplete abortion has an open os
- Suction/vacuum aspiration is preferred over sharp curettage for surgical management of incomplete abortion — lower complication rate
- Cervical insufficiency causes painless second-trimester pregnancy loss and is managed with cerclage
- Antiphospholipid syndrome is a key treatable cause of recurrent pregnancy loss, managed with low-dose aspirin plus heparin/LMWH
- All Rh-negative women should receive anti-D immunoglobulin after miscarriage to prevent alloimmunization
- Missed abortion may present without bleeding — diagnosed incidentally on ultrasound showing absent fetal cardiac activity with a closed cervix
