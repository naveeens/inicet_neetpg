# PCOS

## Definition
Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome — most common endocrine disorder in reproductive-age women. Core defect: hyperandrogenism + chronic anovulation, often with insulin resistance.

## Diagnostic Criteria (Rotterdam, 2003) — need 2 of 3
1. Oligo/anovulation
2. Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism
3. Polycystic ovaries on USG (≥12 follicles 2–9 mm, and/or ovarian volume >10 mL, per unilateral ovary) — after exclusion of other causes (CAH, androgen-secreting tumor, Cushing's, hyperprolactinemia, thyroid dysfunction)

*High-yield: 2018 international guideline raised follicle threshold to ≥20/ovary if using newer high-resolution transvaginal probes (>8 MHz) — know both numbers.*

## Etiopathogenesis
- Insulin resistance → compensatory hyperinsulinemia → ↑ ovarian androgen synthesis + ↓ hepatic SHBG → ↑ free testosterone
- ↑ LH:FSH ratio (classically >2:1, no longer a diagnostic criterion but still tested)
- Hyperandrogenism → arrest of follicular growth → multiple small antral follicles ("string of pearls")

## Clinical Features
- Oligomenorrhea/amenorrhea, infertility (anovulatory)
- Hirsutism (Ferriman-Gallwey score ≥8 in Indian/South Asian women)
- Acne, androgenic alopecia
- Obesity (~50%, but lean PCOS exists)
- Acanthosis nigricans (marker of insulin resistance)

## Investigations
- LH/FSH ratio, total & free testosterone, DHEAS
- TSH, prolactin (rule out mimickers)
- 17-OH progesterone (rule out late-onset CAH)
- OGTT/HOMA-IR — insulin resistance workup
- Lipid profile
- TVS: polycystic ovarian morphology

## Complications
- Infertility, endometrial hyperplasia/carcinoma (unopposed estrogen from anovulation)
- Type 2 DM, metabolic syndrome, dyslipidemia
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- ↑ risk of gestational diabetes if pregnant

## Management
| Goal | Treatment |
|---|---|
| First-line (all) | Lifestyle modification — weight loss (even 5–10%) |
| Menstrual regulation / endometrial protection | Combined OCPs (also treats hirsutism/acne) or cyclic progestins |
| Infertility (ovulation induction) | **Letrozole = first-line** (superior to clomiphene for live birth rate — landmark trial changed practice); Clomiphene citrate second-line; Metformin as adjunct especially if insulin resistant; Gonadotropins/laparoscopic ovarian drilling if resistant |
| Hirsutism | OCPs, spironolactone (antiandrogen), eflornithine cream |
| Insulin resistance | Metformin |

*High-yield flip: Older exams keyed Clomiphene as first-line for ovulation induction — updated evidence (and current guidelines) favor Letrozole. Know both for MCQ traps depending on question framing ("classically" vs "current evidence").*

## Mnemonic
**"PCOS = Periods irregular, Cysts (string of pearls), Obesity/insulin resistance, Skin changes (acne/hirsutism/acanthosis)"**

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Rotterdam criteria: 2 of 3 (anovulation, hyperandrogenism, PCO morphology on USG)
- Letrozole > Clomiphene for ovulation induction (live birth rate)
- Long-term unopposed estrogen risk → endometrial carcinoma
- Acanthosis nigricans = cutaneous marker of insulin resistance, not androgen excess directly
