# Rh Isoimmunization & Fetal Surveillance

## Pathophysiology
- Rh-negative mother carrying Rh-positive fetus → fetal RBCs (bearing D antigen) enter maternal circulation (delivery, abortion, trauma, invasive procedures) → maternal sensitization → **anti-D IgG antibodies** form
- In subsequent pregnancies, anti-D IgG crosses placenta → attaches to fetal RBCs → extravascular hemolysis (spleen) → **hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN)**
- First affected pregnancy is usually the sensitizing event itself (unaffected), unless sensitized by prior transfusion/abortion — severity typically worsens with each subsequent affected pregnancy

## Risk Factors for Sensitization
- Delivery (vaginal > cesarean risk of large fetomaternal hemorrhage in some scenarios), miscarriage/abortion, ectopic pregnancy, antepartum hemorrhage (abruption, previa), external cephalic version, invasive procedures (amniocentesis, CVS, cordocentesis), abdominal trauma, molar pregnancy

## Diagnosis & Monitoring in Sensitized Pregnancy
| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Indirect Coombs test (ICT) on mother | Detects and quantifies maternal anti-D antibody titer; repeated serially |
| Paternal/fetal Rh genotyping | Determines if fetus at risk (if father Rh-negative, fetus unaffected) |
| **Critical titer** (commonly ≥1:16 or 1:32, per lab/protocol) | Threshold to proceed with further fetal surveillance |
| **Middle cerebral artery (MCA) peak systolic velocity (PSV) Doppler** | Non-invasive method of choice to detect fetal anemia — raised PSV suggests anemia |
| Amniotic fluid spectrophotometry (ΔOD 450, Liley/Queenan charts) | Older method, largely replaced by MCA Doppler |
| **Cordocentesis** | Invasive, direct fetal Hb/Hct measurement — used to confirm anemia before intervention, carries procedural risk |

## Management
### Prevention (Rh Prophylaxis)
- **Anti-D immunoglobulin** given to all Rh-negative, unsensitized women (indirect Coombs test negative) at:
  - **28 weeks** gestation (antenatal prophylaxis)
  - **Within 72 hours of delivery** if baby is Rh-positive
  - After any sensitizing event: miscarriage, ectopic, abortion (medical/surgical), antepartum hemorrhage, invasive prenatal procedures, external cephalic version, abdominal trauma
- Dose: standard 300 μg (1500 IU) IM covers up to ~30 mL fetomaternal hemorrhage; **Kleihauer-Betke test** used to quantify fetomaternal hemorrhage and calculate additional dose needed if large bleed suspected
- Anti-D is NOT given if mother is already sensitized (ICT positive) — no benefit once alloimmunized

### Management of Affected Fetus
| Severity | Management |
|---|---|
| Mild, rising titers only | Serial monitoring (titers, MCA Doppler) |
| Significant fetal anemia (MCA-PSV elevated) confirmed | **Intrauterine intraperitoneal/intravascular transfusion** with Rh-negative, cross-matched, irradiated blood |
| Severe/hydrops fetalis | Intrauterine transfusion; deliver when fetal lung maturity allows or condition mandates |
| At/near term | Deliver, manage neonate for hemolytic disease |

## Neonatal Hemolytic Disease
- **Hydrops fetalis** (severe, in-utero heart failure from anemia — ascites, pleural/pericardial effusion, skin edema)
- **Icterus gravis neonatorum** — severe jaundice, risk of **kernicterus** (bilirubin encephalopathy) if untreated
- Neonatal management: phototherapy, **exchange transfusion** for severe hyperbilirubinemia, IVIG in select cases

## Fetal Surveillance — General (Beyond Rh Disease)
- Applicable broadly for high-risk pregnancies (IUGR, diabetes, hypertension, post-dates, decreased fetal movements)
- **Non-Stress Test (NST)**: reactive = ≥2 accelerations (≥15 bpm for ≥15 sec) in 20 min — reassuring
- **Biophysical Profile (BPP)**: 5 components (NST, fetal breathing, movement, tone, amniotic fluid volume), score /10, ≤4 concerning
- **Doppler studies**: umbilical artery (raised resistance/absent-reversed end-diastolic flow = placental insufficiency), MCA (fetal anemia/redistribution), ductus venosus (late/pre-terminal changes)
- **Kick counts** — maternal perception of fetal movement, simple screening tool

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Anti-D immunoglobulin is given prophylactically — it has no role once the mother is already sensitized (positive indirect Coombs)
- Routine antenatal anti-D at 28 weeks + postnatal dose within 72 hours if baby Rh-positive
- MCA-PSV Doppler is the current non-invasive investigation of choice for detecting fetal anemia, superseding amniotic fluid spectrophotometry
- Kleihauer-Betke test quantifies fetomaternal hemorrhage to guide additional anti-D dosing
- Cordocentesis carries direct procedural risk and is reserved to confirm anemia before invasive therapy
- Severe untreated fetal/neonatal hemolysis can progress to hydrops fetalis in utero or kernicterus after birth
