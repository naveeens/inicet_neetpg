# Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)

## Definition
Carbohydrate intolerance with onset or first recognition during pregnancy, due to increasing insulin resistance from placental hormones (human placental lactogen, progesterone, cortisol, prolactin) that peak in the second/third trimester.

## Risk Factors
- Obesity/BMI ≥25–30, prior GDM, prior macrosomic baby (>4 kg), family history of T2DM
- Prior unexplained stillbirth/congenital anomaly, PCOS, glycosuria, advanced maternal age, ethnicity (South Asians at higher risk — relevant to Indian population)

## Screening & Diagnosis
- **Universal screening in India/high-prevalence populations** (as opposed to risk-based screening in low-prevalence populations) — done at first antenatal visit and repeated at 24–28 weeks if initial negative
- Screening timing: 24–28 weeks (universal window); earlier testing in high-risk women to detect pre-existing/early GDM

| Test | Protocol | Diagnostic Cutoff (per current knowledge — verify against latest FOGSI/WHO/ADA guideline in use) |
|---|---|---|
| **DIPSI (India-specific)** | 75 g OGTT, non-fasting, single 2-hour value | 2-hr plasma glucose ≥140 mg/dL |
| **WHO/IADPSG criteria** | 75 g OGTT, fasting | Fasting ≥92, 1-hr ≥180, 2-hr ≥153 mg/dL — **any one abnormal value diagnostic** |
| **Carpenter-Coustan (2-step, US)** | 50 g screening → if positive, 100 g 3-hr OGTT | **≥2 abnormal values** required: Fasting ≥95, 1-hr ≥180, 2-hr ≥155, 3-hr ≥140 mg/dL |

- *High-yield: DIPSI is preferred in India as it is a single-step, non-fasting test — practical for high patient volume/OPD setting.*

## Pathophysiology
- Placental hormones → progressive insulin resistance through pregnancy → pancreatic beta-cell reserve inadequate to compensate → hyperglycemia
- Fetal hyperinsulinemia (in response to maternal hyperglycemia crossing placenta) → **macrosomia** (Pedersen hypothesis) and neonatal hypoglycemia after cord clamping

## Complications
| Maternal | Fetal/Neonatal |
|---|---|
| Pre-eclampsia, polyhydramnios | **Macrosomia** (shoulder dystocia risk) |
| Increased cesarean/operative delivery rate | Neonatal hypoglycemia |
| Recurrent UTIs, candidiasis | Respiratory distress syndrome (delayed lung maturation) |
| Progression to overt T2DM postpartum (~50% within years) | Hypocalcemia, hyperbilirubinemia, polycythemia |
| — | Higher perinatal mortality if poorly controlled |
| — | **Congenital anomalies** — only if hyperglycemia present in first trimester (pre-existing/undiagnosed diabetes, NOT typical GDM which develops later); classic anomaly = **caudal regression syndrome** (sacral agenesis) — highly specific to diabetic embryopathy |

## Management
| Step | Approach |
|---|---|
| First-line | Medical Nutrition Therapy (diet) + exercise, self-monitoring of blood glucose |
| Targets | Fasting <95 mg/dL, 1-hr postprandial <140 mg/dL, 2-hr postprandial <120 mg/dL (per current knowledge — verify local targets) |
| Pharmacotherapy (if diet fails) | **Insulin** = gold standard (does not cross placenta) |
| Oral agents | **Metformin** and **glyburide/glibenclamide** used as alternatives in some settings — cross placenta to varying degrees; insulin remains preferred/gold standard per most current guidelines |
| Fetal surveillance | Serial growth scans, antenatal fetal monitoring especially if insulin-requiring or poorly controlled |
| Delivery timing | Individualized; well-controlled GDM on diet alone may go to term (39–40 weeks); insulin-requiring/poorly controlled — earlier delivery considered (~37–39 weeks) |
| Intrapartum | Glucose-insulin monitoring during labor to prevent neonatal hypoglycemia |

## Postpartum Follow-up
- **75 g OGTT at 6 weeks postpartum** to reclassify (normal / prediabetes / overt diabetes)
- Lifelong increased risk of developing type 2 DM — counsel on lifestyle, periodic screening
- Recurrence risk in future pregnancies is high

## High-Yield One-Liners
- GDM is diagnosed for the first time during pregnancy; hyperglycemia detected in first trimester = likely pre-existing diabetes, not GDM
- DIPSI test = single-step, non-fasting 75 g OGTT with 2-hr cutoff ≥140 mg/dL — India-specific and practical
- Congenital anomalies in diabetic pregnancy are linked to first-trimester hyperglycemia, not classic (later-onset) GDM
- Caudal regression syndrome (sacral agenesis) is the most specific (though not most common) anomaly of diabetic embryopathy
- Insulin is the gold-standard pharmacologic agent in pregnancy — does not cross the placenta
- All women with GDM need a 75 g OGTT at 6 weeks postpartum to reclassify glycemic status
- Fetal macrosomia in GDM predisposes to shoulder dystocia and birth trauma
