# Contraception

## Classification & Overview
| Method | Mechanism | Typical-Use Failure Rate (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCP) | Inhibits ovulation (suppresses FSH/LH surge), thickens cervical mucus | Low (~0.3% perfect use; ~7–9% typical use) |
| Progestin-only pills (POP/mini-pill) | Thickens cervical mucus; inconsistent ovulation suppression | Similar range to COCP, more user-dependent |
| **Depot medroxyprogesterone acetate (DMPA) injectable** | Inhibits ovulation | Very low (<1% with correct use) |
| **Copper IUD (Cu-T)** | Copper ions are spermicidal/toxic to sperm and ova; local inflammatory reaction prevents fertilization/implantation | Very low (~0.6–0.8%), long-acting (10 years for Cu-T 380A) |
| **Levonorgestrel IUS (LNG-IUS)** | Local progestin — thickens mucus, thins endometrium, partial ovulation suppression | Very low, also reduces menstrual blood loss (used therapeutically for menorrhagia) |
| **Implants (etonogestrel/levonorgestrel)** | Inhibits ovulation | Very low, long-acting (3–5 years depending on product) |
| Barrier methods (condom, diaphragm) | Mechanical/chemical barrier to sperm | Higher failure rate, but condoms uniquely protect against STIs |
| Natural methods (rhythm, withdrawal, LAM) | Avoidance of fertile window / prevents ejaculation in vagina / lactation-induced anovulation | Highest failure rates among reversible methods |
| Permanent — Tubal ligation / Vasectomy | Mechanical occlusion of tubes/vas deferens | Very low, considered permanent |

## Combined Oral Contraceptives (COCP)
### Mechanism
- Estrogen: suppresses FSH → prevents follicular development; Progestin: suppresses LH surge → prevents ovulation; also thickens cervical mucus and alters endometrium

### Non-Contraceptive Benefits
- Regulates cycles, reduces menorrhagia/dysmenorrhea
- Reduces risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer (protective effect increases with duration of use)
- Improves acne/hirsutism (antiandrogenic progestins), used in PCOS management
- Reduces functional ovarian cysts, benign breast disease

### Contraindications (WHO Medical Eligibility Criteria — Category 4/Absolute, key exam favorites)
- Current/history of venous thromboembolism, known thrombophilia
- Migraine with aura (stroke risk)
- Uncontrolled hypertension, ischemic heart disease, history of stroke
- Smokers **≥35 years** (age + smoking combination significantly raises cardiovascular risk)
- Breast cancer (current), severe liver disease, undiagnosed abnormal vaginal bleeding
- **Breastfeeding <6 weeks postpartum** — avoid estrogen-containing methods (progestin-only preferred during lactation)

## Emergency Contraception
| Method | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Levonorgestrel (1.5 mg single dose)** | Within 72 hours (most effective closer to intercourse) | Most commonly used; efficacy decreases with delay |
| **Ulipristal acetate** | Within 120 hours | More effective than LNG later in the window per current evidence |
| **Copper IUD insertion** | Within 120 hours | **Most effective emergency contraceptive method overall** — also provides ongoing contraception |
| Yuzpe method (combined estrogen-progestin) | Within 72 hours | Older method, largely superseded, higher nausea incidence |

## Intrauterine Devices (IUD)
- **Copper IUD**: safe in breastfeeding, no hormonal effect, can increase menstrual bleeding/cramping
- **LNG-IUS**: reduces menstrual bleeding, used therapeutically for menorrhagia/adenomyosis, endometrial protection in HRT
- Contraindications: active PID, pregnancy, unexplained abnormal uterine bleeding, distorted uterine cavity, known pelvic TB (relevant in Indian context), current cervical/endometrial malignancy
- Insertion timing: anytime if pregnancy reasonably excluded; particularly convenient immediately postpartum or post-abortion in many protocols

## Postpartum & Lactation Considerations
- **Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM)**: effective only if exclusive breastfeeding, amenorrheic, and <6 months postpartum
- Progestin-only methods (POP, DMPA, implants, LNG-IUS) preferred over estrogen-containing methods during breastfeeding, especially <6 weeks postpartum

## Sterilization
- **Female**: tubal ligation (minilap or laparoscopic) — commonly done postpartum or as interval procedure; considered permanent, low failure rate but not zero (subsequent pregnancy, if it occurs, has increased ectopic risk)
- **Male**: vasectomy — simpler, lower complication rate than female sterilization; **not immediately effective** — requires backup contraception until confirmed azoospermia (commonly ~3 months/20 ejaculations, per current knowledge — verify local protocol)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Combined OCPs reduce the risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer with increasing duration of use
- Smokers aged ≥35 years should avoid combined estrogen-containing contraceptives due to elevated cardiovascular risk
- Migraine with aura is an absolute contraindication to combined OCPs due to stroke risk
- Copper IUD is the most effective form of emergency contraception and can be inserted up to 120 hours after unprotected intercourse
- Progestin-only methods are preferred during breastfeeding, particularly in the first 6 weeks postpartum
- LNG-IUS is used both for contraception and as a therapeutic option for menorrhagia
- Vasectomy is not immediately effective — confirm azoospermia before relying on it as sole contraception
- Lactational amenorrhea method is reliable only with exclusive breastfeeding, amenorrhea, and within 6 months postpartum
