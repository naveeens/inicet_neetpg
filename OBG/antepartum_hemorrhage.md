# Antepartum Hemorrhage

## Definition
Bleeding from the genital tract after 20/24 weeks of gestation (varies by convention) and before delivery of the fetus. Two major causes: **Placenta Previa** and **Abruptio Placentae**; also consider vasa previa, local causes (cervical polyp, ectropion, carcinoma).

## Placenta Previa vs Abruptio Placentae
| Feature | Placenta Previa | Abruptio Placentae |
|---|---|---|
| Bleeding | Painless, bright red | Painful, may be dark/concealed |
| Uterus | Soft, non-tender, relaxed | Tense, tender, "woody hard" |
| Fetal parts | Easily palpable | Difficult to palpate |
| Fetal heart | Usually normal | Often abnormal/absent (severe cases) |
| Shock | Proportional to visible blood loss | May be **out of proportion** to visible loss (concealed hemorrhage) |
| Coagulopathy | Rare | Common (DIC risk) |
| Presentation | Malpresentation common | Usually normal presentation |
| USG | Diagnostic (localizes placenta) | May show retroplacental clot but often normal — clinical diagnosis |

## Placenta Previa
### Classification
- **Type I (low-lying)**: placenta encroaches lower segment, doesn't reach os
- **Type II (marginal)**: edge reaches internal os
- **Type III (partial)**: partially covers os
- **Type IV (complete/central)**: completely covers os
- Modern classification often simplified to: **placenta previa** (covers os) vs **low-lying placenta** (edge within 2 cm of os on TVS)

### Risk Factors
- Previous C-section/uterine surgery, multiparity, advanced maternal age, multiple pregnancy, previous placenta previa, smoking, assisted reproduction

### Diagnosis
- **Transvaginal ultrasound** (safe, more accurate than transabdominal) — avoid digital vaginal examination until previa excluded ("no PV exam until USG done")
- Placental migration: apparent low-lying placenta in 2nd trimester may resolve by term as lower segment develops

### Management
| Scenario | Management |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic, previa on early scan | Rescan at 32 & 36 weeks; pelvic rest, avoid intercourse |
| Preterm, stable minor bleed | Expectant management, corticosteroids if <34 weeks, tocolysis cautiously |
| Term or major bleed/unstable | **Cesarean section** (mandatory for major degree/covering os) |
| Placenta accreta spectrum risk | Anticipate in previa + previous C-section; plan for possible hysterectomy, arrange blood products, senior team |

## Abruptio Placentae
### Etiology
- Hypertension/pre-eclampsia (strongest association), trauma, cocaine use, sudden uterine decompression (polyhydramnios rupture, after delivery of first twin), smoking, thrombophilia, previous abruption

### Types
- **Revealed**: blood escapes vaginally
- **Concealed**: blood retained behind placenta — more dangerous (shock out of proportion, higher DIC risk)
- **Mixed**: both

### Clinical Features
- Painful bleeding, tense tender "woody hard" uterus, backache
- Signs of shock, fetal distress/absent heart sounds
- **Couvelaire uterus**: blood infiltrates myometrium — uterus appears bruised/purple; can still contract, not itself an indication for hysterectomy

### Management
| Scenario | Management |
|---|---|
| Fetus alive, mild abruption, stable | Continuous monitoring, may allow vaginal delivery if progressing well |
| Fetal distress / severe abruption | Emergency cesarean section |
| Fetal death | Vaginal delivery preferred if maternal condition stable (avoid surgery risk with coagulopathy); induce labor, correct coagulopathy first |
| Any case | Aggressive resuscitation, blood products, monitor for DIC/renal failure |

## Complications
- **Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)** — especially with abruption + fetal death
- Postpartum hemorrhage (Couvelaire uterus, atony)
- Renal failure (acute tubular/cortical necrosis)
- Sheehan syndrome (postpartum pituitary necrosis due to severe hemorrhagic shock)
- Fetal: hypoxia, IUGR (chronic abruption), death

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Never perform a digital vaginal examination in suspected placenta previa before ultrasound localization — can provoke catastrophic hemorrhage ("no touch technique")
- Transvaginal sonography is safe and superior to transabdominal for placenta previa diagnosis
- Concealed abruption → shock disproportionate to visible blood loss — classic exam trap
- Couvelaire uterus does not by itself mandate hysterectomy — uterus usually retains contractility
- Strongest risk factor for abruption = hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
- Placenta previa + prior cesarean → high suspicion for placenta accreta spectrum — plan multidisciplinary delivery
