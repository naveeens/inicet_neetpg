# Postpartum Hemorrhage

## Definition
- **Primary PPH**: blood loss ≥500 mL after vaginal delivery or ≥1000 mL after cesarean, within 24 hours of delivery (some definitions use ≥1000 mL for "clinically significant" PPH regardless of route, per current guidance)
- **Secondary PPH**: excessive bleeding from 24 hours to 6–12 weeks postpartum
- **Severe PPH**: blood loss ≥1000–1500 mL or any amount causing hemodynamic instability

## Etiology — "4 T's"
| T | Cause | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **T**one | Uterine atony | ~70% — most common cause |
| **T**issue | Retained placenta/products, placenta accreta | ~10% |
| **T**rauma | Genital tract lacerations, uterine rupture, inversion | ~20% |
| **T**hrombin | Coagulopathy (DIC, inherited bleeding disorders, anticoagulant use) | ~1% |

## Risk Factors for Uterine Atony
- Overdistended uterus (multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, macrosomia)
- Prolonged/rapid labor, grand multiparity, chorioamnionitis
- Uterine relaxants (general anesthesia, magnesium sulfate, tocolytics)
- Prior PPH

## Prevention — Active Management of Third Stage of Labor (AMTSL)
1. **Uterotonic** immediately after delivery of baby (oxytocin drug of choice)
2. **Controlled cord traction**
3. **Uterine massage** after placental delivery
- AMTSL reduces incidence of PPH significantly and is recommended for all deliveries

## Management — Stepwise Approach
### 1. Call for help, resuscitate (ABC), two large-bore IV lines, crossmatch blood
### 2. Identify and treat cause ("4 T's")

### Uterine Atony
| Step | Measure |
|---|---|
| Mechanical | **Bimanual uterine massage/compression**, empty bladder (Foley catheter) |
| Pharmacological (first-line) | **Oxytocin** IV infusion |
| Second-line uterotonics | **Methylergometrine** (avoid in hypertension/pre-eclampsia), **Carboprost/15-methyl PGF2α** (avoid in asthma), **Misoprostol** (rectal/sublingual, useful where others contraindicated/unavailable) |
| Refractory | Uterine tamponade (**Bakri balloon**/condom catheter tamponade), uterine artery embolization if stable & available |
| Surgical (escalating) | Uterine compression sutures (**B-Lynch suture**), stepwise uterine devascularization (uterine/ovarian artery ligation), internal iliac artery ligation, **hysterectomy** (last resort/life-saving) |

### Retained Tissue
- Manual removal of placenta, exploration under anesthesia, curettage for retained fragments; treat placenta accreta per anticipated management plan

### Trauma
- Inspect and repair cervical/vaginal/perineal lacerations; correct uterine inversion (**manual replacement — Johnson's method**; do NOT give oxytocics until uterus repositioned); manage uterine rupture (laparotomy, repair or hysterectomy)

### Thrombin (Coagulopathy)
- Fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate, platelets; treat underlying DIC; tranexamic acid — reduces mortality from bleeding, give early (per current evidence)

## Uterotonic Drugs — Quick Reference
| Drug | Dose/Route | Key Contraindication |
|---|---|---|
| Oxytocin | IV infusion/IM | Rare — safest, first-line |
| Methylergometrine | IM/IV | Hypertension, pre-eclampsia, cardiac disease |
| Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α) | IM | Asthma, cardiac/pulmonary/hepatic/renal disease |
| Misoprostol | Sublingual/rectal/oral | Relatively few — useful in low-resource settings |
| Tranexamic acid | IV | Adjunct antifibrinolytic, not a uterotonic — give within 3 h of bleeding onset |

## Balloon Tamponade & Surgical Sutures
- **Bakri balloon**: intrauterine tamponade, "tamponade test" — if bleeding stops, avoid further surgery
- **B-Lynch suture**: compresses uterus vertically, fertility-preserving, used when medical management fails and hysterectomy wants to be avoided

## Complications
- Hypovolemic shock, DIC, **Sheehan syndrome** (postpartum pituitary necrosis from severe hemorrhagic hypotension → hypopituitarism, failure of lactation, amenorrhea)
- Death — PPH is a leading cause of maternal mortality globally and in India

## Mnemonic
**"4 T's"** — Tone, Tissue, Trauma, Thrombin (in decreasing order of frequency)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common cause of PPH = uterine atony
- First-line drug for PPH prevention and treatment = oxytocin
- Avoid methylergometrine in hypertensive disorders; avoid carboprost in asthma
- Uterine inversion: reposition uterus manually BEFORE giving uterotonics
- Tranexamic acid given early reduces bleeding-related mortality — give as adjunct, not a substitute for uterotonics
- B-Lynch suture is fertility-preserving; hysterectomy is the last resort for life-threatening uncontrolled PPH
- Sheehan syndrome results from severe postpartum hemorrhagic shock causing pituitary infarction
