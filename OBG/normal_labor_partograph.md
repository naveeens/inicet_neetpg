# Normal Labor & Partograph

## Definition of Labor
Onset of regular, painful uterine contractions associated with progressive cervical dilatation and effacement, leading to expulsion of the fetus and placenta.

## Stages of Labor
| Stage | Definition | Duration (primigravida) | Duration (multigravida) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First stage** | Onset of true labor to full cervical dilatation (10 cm) | ~10–12 h | ~6–8 h |
| — Latent phase | Onset to ~4–6 cm (per current definitions) | Variable, up to 20 h | Up to 14 h |
| — Active phase | ~4–6 cm to 10 cm | Faster, ≥1 cm/h | Faster, ≥1.5 cm/h |
| **Second stage** | Full dilatation to delivery of baby | ~30 min–2 h (up to 3 h with epidural) | ~15–30 min (up to 2 h with epidural) |
| **Third stage** | Delivery of baby to delivery of placenta | Up to 30 min | Up to 30 min |
| **Fourth stage** | First hour postpartum (observation for hemorrhage) | 1 h | 1 h |

*High-yield: WHO/current partograph guidance redefined active labor as starting around 5–6 cm rather than the classically taught 4 cm — expect exam framing of both older ("4 cm") and updated cutoffs.*

## Mechanism of Normal Labor (Cardinal Movements) — Vertex Presentation
1. **Engagement**
2. **Descent**
3. **Flexion**
4. **Internal rotation**
5. **Extension**
6. **External rotation (restitution)**
7. **Expulsion**

## Partograph
A graphical record of labor progress used to detect abnormal labor early and prompt timely intervention (WHO tool, especially valuable in resource-limited settings).

### Components
| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Cervical dilatation** | Plotted against time; **Alert line** — drawn from 4 cm at 1 cm/h; **Action line** — parallel, 4 hours to the right of alert line |
| Descent of head | Plotted in fifths palpable per abdomen |
| Uterine contractions | Frequency and duration per 10 minutes |
| Fetal heart rate | Monitored every 30 min in first stage |
| Amniotic fluid | Color/consistency (clear, meconium-stained, blood-stained) noted at each vaginal exam |
| Cervical dilatation/molding | Degree of molding of fetal skull recorded |
| Maternal vitals | BP, pulse, temperature, urine (protein/acetone), oxytocin if used |

- Crossing of the **action line** signals need for active intervention (augmentation, reassessment, possible operative delivery) — key exam point
- Partograph plotting typically begins once the woman is in the **active phase**

## Friedman's Labor Curve (Classic Teaching)
- Describes labor as sigmoid curve: latent phase → acceleration → phase of maximum slope (active) → deceleration → second stage
- Historically the basis for defining abnormal labor patterns (protraction, arrest disorders)
- *High-yield flip: Newer labor curve data (Zhang et al. and WHO updates) show labor progresses slower than Friedman's classic curve, especially before 6 cm — contemporary definitions of "active labor" and diagnosis of arrest have been revised accordingly. Exams may test either the classic Friedman framework or the updated framework — read the question stem carefully.*

## Abnormal Labor Patterns
| Pattern | Definition |
|---|---|
| Prolonged latent phase | >20 h (primi) / >14 h (multi) |
| Protracted active phase | Dilatation <1 cm/h (primi) / <1.5 cm/h (multi) |
| Arrest of dilatation | No cervical change for ≥2 h despite adequate contractions |
| Arrest of descent | No descent for ≥1 h in second stage |
| Prolonged second stage | >3 h (primi with epidural) / >2 h (multi with epidural) — shorter without epidural |

## Active Management of Labor
- Amniotomy (artificial rupture of membranes) and oxytocin augmentation for slow progress with adequate assessment of pelvis/contractions
- Continuous support, adequate hydration, encourage mobility/upright position

## High-Yield One-Liners
- The partograph's action line lies 4 hours to the right of the alert line — crossing it mandates active intervention
- Cardinal movements of labor (vertex): engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, external rotation, expulsion
- Classic teaching: active phase begins at 4 cm; current WHO/updated definitions place it closer to 5–6 cm — know both for exam traps
- Arrest of dilatation = no cervical change for ≥2 hours despite adequate uterine contractions
- Full cervical dilatation = 10 cm; marks the end of the first stage and start of the second stage
- Fourth stage of labor (first postpartum hour) is the critical window for detecting and managing postpartum hemorrhage
