# Uterine Fibroids (Leiomyoma)

## Definition
Benign monoclonal tumors arising from the smooth muscle of the myometrium — the **most common benign tumor of the female genital tract** and most common pelvic tumor overall in women.

## Risk Factors
- Nulliparity, early menarche, obesity, family history, African ancestry (higher incidence/earlier onset)
- Estrogen and progesterone dependent — grow during reproductive years and pregnancy, regress after menopause
- Protective factors: multiparity, smoking (antiestrogenic effect), combined OCP use (some protective association)

## Classification by Location
| Type | Location |
|---|---|
| **Intramural** | Within the myometrium — most common type |
| **Subserosal** | Bulges outward under the serosa; may become pedunculated |
| **Submucosal** | Bulges into the endometrial cavity — **most symptomatic** (menorrhagia, infertility) despite being least common |
| **Pedunculated** | Subserosal or submucosal on a stalk — risk of torsion |
| **Cervical fibroid** | Arises from the cervix — rare, can cause outflow obstruction |
| **FIGO classification (0–8)** | Standardized system for submucosal-to-subserosal spectrum, used to guide treatment planning especially for fertility |

## Clinical Features
- Often **asymptomatic** (incidental finding)
- **Menorrhagia** (most common symptom — especially with submucosal/intramural fibroids distorting cavity)
- Pressure symptoms: urinary frequency (bladder pressure), constipation, backache
- Pelvic pain/pressure, dysmenorrhea
- Infertility/recurrent pregnancy loss (especially with cavity-distorting submucosal fibroids)
- Abdominal mass/pelvic mass on examination — firm, irregular, mobile with cervix

## Degenerative Changes
| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Hyaline degeneration** | Most common degeneration overall |
| **Red (carneous) degeneration** | Classic in **pregnancy** — acute infarction, presents with sudden pain and fever, managed conservatively (analgesia, rest) |
| **Cystic degeneration** | Softening, may mimic ovarian cyst |
| **Calcific degeneration** | Common postmenopausally ("womb stone") |
| **Sarcomatous change** | Rare (<1%) — suspect with rapid growth, especially postmenopausal |

## Diagnosis
- Clinical examination + **transvaginal/abdominal ultrasound** (first-line imaging)
- **MRI** — most accurate for mapping size, number, location, especially for pre-surgical planning (myomectomy) or to differentiate from adenomyosis/sarcoma
- Hysteroscopy — direct visualization/treatment of submucosal fibroids
- Sonohysterography — assesses cavity distortion

## Management
| Scenario | Approach |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic, small | Observation/expectant management |
| Symptomatic, wants fertility preserved | Medical management first; **myomectomy** (hysteroscopic for submucosal, laparoscopic/open for others) if surgery needed |
| Symptomatic, fertility not desired / definitive treatment | **Hysterectomy** — definitive cure |
| Medical (symptom/size reduction, often pre-operative) | GnRH agonists (shrink fibroid, correct anemia pre-op — but rebound growth on stopping; limited duration due to hypoestrogenic effects), Ulipristal acetate (selective progesterone receptor modulator — where available), tranexamic acid/NSAIDs for bleeding, levonorgestrel IUS for menorrhagia without significant cavity distortion |
| Minimally invasive alternatives | Uterine artery embolization (fertility-sparing alternative to myomectomy in selected patients, though fertility data mixed), MRI-guided focused ultrasound surgery (in select settings) |

## Fibroids & Pregnancy
- May cause infertility (submucosal/cavity-distorting), recurrent miscarriage, malpresentation, preterm labor, obstructed labor (cervical fibroid), red degeneration (pain in pregnancy), postpartum hemorrhage (impaired uterine contraction)
- Myomectomy during cesarean generally avoided unless pedunculated/easily accessible (risk of severe hemorrhage)

## Complications
- Menorrhagia → iron-deficiency anemia
- Infertility, recurrent pregnancy loss
- Torsion (pedunculated fibroid) — acute abdomen
- Rare malignant transformation to leiomyosarcoma

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Uterine fibroids are the most common benign tumor of the female genital tract, estrogen/progesterone dependent
- Submucosal fibroids cause the most symptoms (menorrhagia, infertility) despite being the least common location
- Red (carneous) degeneration is classically associated with pregnancy, presenting as acute pain and low-grade fever
- MRI is the most accurate imaging modality for pre-surgical mapping of fibroids
- Myomectomy preserves fertility; hysterectomy is the definitive cure once fertility is no longer desired
- GnRH agonists shrink fibroids preoperatively but cause rebound growth on discontinuation — used short-term only
- Rapid fibroid growth, especially postmenopausally, should raise suspicion for leiomyosarcoma
