# Endometrial Cancer

## Epidemiology
- **Most common gynecological malignancy in developed countries** (in India, cervical cancer historically more common overall, but endometrial cancer incidence is rising)
- Typically presents in **postmenopausal women** (peak age 55–65 years)

## Classification (Bokhman Dualistic Model)
| Type | Type I (Estrogen-dependent) | Type II (Estrogen-independent) |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | ~80% | ~10–20% |
| Histology | **Endometrioid adenocarcinoma** | Serous, clear cell carcinoma |
| Precursor | Endometrial hyperplasia (atypical) | Endometrial intraepithelial carcinoma, arises in atrophic endometrium |
| Risk factors | Unopposed estrogen exposure, obesity | Not clearly estrogen-related |
| Grade/behavior | Usually low-grade, better prognosis | High-grade, aggressive, worse prognosis |
| Molecular | PTEN, K-ras mutations, microsatellite instability | **p53 mutations** |

## Risk Factors (Unopposed Estrogen Exposure — Type I)
- Obesity (peripheral aromatization of androgens to estrone in adipose tissue)
- Nulliparity, early menarche, late menopause
- **PCOS** (chronic anovulation → unopposed estrogen)
- **Unopposed estrogen therapy** (estrogen without progestin in a woman with a uterus)
- **Tamoxifen** use (estrogen agonist effect on endometrium despite antagonist effect on breast)
- Estrogen-secreting tumors (e.g., granulosa cell tumor)
- Diabetes mellitus, hypertension (associated via metabolic syndrome/obesity link)
- **Lynch syndrome (HNPCC)** — significantly increased lifetime risk, also associated with colorectal/ovarian cancer

## Protective Factors
- Multiparity, combined OCP use, smoking (antiestrogenic, though harmful overall), levonorgestrel IUS

## Clinical Features
- **Postmenopausal bleeding = cardinal symptom** — any postmenopausal bleeding must be investigated to exclude endometrial cancer until proven otherwise
- Premenopausal: abnormal uterine bleeding, intermenstrual bleeding
- Pelvic pain/pressure (advanced disease), vaginal discharge

## Diagnosis
| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| First-line imaging | **Transvaginal ultrasound** — endometrial thickness measurement; **≤4 mm in a postmenopausal woman has high negative predictive value** for cancer (per current knowledge — verify local cutoff) |
| Definitive diagnosis | **Endometrial biopsy** (office/outpatient pipelle biopsy) — first-line tissue sampling method |
| If biopsy inconclusive/insufficient or high suspicion | **Hysteroscopy with dilatation & curettage (D&C)** |
| Staging workup | Pelvic/abdominal imaging (CT/MRI), chest imaging — endometrial cancer is surgically staged |

## Staging (FIGO) — Surgical
- Unlike cervical cancer, endometrial cancer is staged **surgically** (based on hysterectomy specimen), not clinically
| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Confined to uterine corpus |
| II | Involves cervical stroma |
| III | Local/regional spread (serosa, adnexa, vagina, pelvic/para-aortic nodes) |
| IV | Bladder/bowel mucosa invasion or distant metastasis |

## Management
| Stage | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Early stage (I/II), operable | **Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (TAH+BSO)** ± pelvic/para-aortic lymphadenectomy — mainstay |
| High-risk histology/advanced stage | Surgery + adjuvant radiotherapy (pelvic external beam/vaginal brachytherapy) ± chemotherapy |
| Advanced/metastatic/recurrent | Chemotherapy, hormonal therapy (progestins) in select low-grade ER/PR-positive disease, palliative care |
| Fertility-sparing (select young patients, well-differentiated, stage IA, no myometrial invasion) | High-dose progestin therapy with close surveillance — atypical/highly selected cases only |

## Endometrial Hyperplasia (Precursor Lesion)
| Type (WHO classification) | Risk of progression to carcinoma |
|---|---|
| Hyperplasia without atypia | Low (~1–3%) |
| **Atypical hyperplasia / Endometrial Intraepithelial Neoplasia (EIN)** | High (up to ~30%, considered a direct precursor) |
- Management: without atypia — progestin therapy (e.g., levonorgestrel IUS, oral progestins), with atypia — hysterectomy generally recommended (progestin therapy + surveillance only if fertility strongly desired/surgical risk high)

## Complications
- Local spread to myometrium, cervix, adnexa; lymphatic spread to pelvic/para-aortic nodes; hematogenous spread (lung, liver) in advanced/aggressive histology
- Recurrence — vaginal vault most common site

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Postmenopausal bleeding must always be investigated to rule out endometrial cancer
- Unopposed estrogen (no progestin) is the central risk factor for Type I endometrioid endometrial cancer
- Endometrial biopsy (pipelle) is the first-line diagnostic test; D&C/hysteroscopy if inconclusive
- Endometrial cancer is staged surgically, unlike cervical cancer which is staged clinically
- Tamoxifen increases endometrial cancer risk despite its antiestrogenic action on breast tissue
- Atypical endometrial hyperplasia is a direct precursor with a high risk of progression to carcinoma — generally managed with hysterectomy
- Lynch syndrome carriers have significantly elevated lifetime risk of endometrial and colorectal cancer
