# Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy

## Classification
| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Gestational hypertension | BP ≥140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks, **no proteinuria/end-organ damage**, resolves by 12 weeks postpartum |
| Pre-eclampsia | Gestational hypertension + proteinuria (≥300 mg/24h, or ACR ≥30 mg/mmol, or dipstick ≥2+) OR other end-organ dysfunction even without proteinuria |
| Eclampsia | Pre-eclampsia + new-onset generalized tonic-clonic seizures (not attributable to other cause) |
| Chronic hypertension | BP ≥140/90 mmHg **before 20 weeks** or pre-pregnancy, or persisting >12 weeks postpartum |
| Chronic HTN with superimposed pre-eclampsia | New/worsening proteinuria or end-organ dysfunction in a woman with pre-existing hypertension |

## Pre-eclampsia — Diagnostic Criteria
- BP ≥140/90 on two occasions ≥4 h apart, after 20 weeks, in a previously normotensive woman
- **Severe features** (any one): BP ≥160/110, thrombocytopenia (<100,000), impaired liver function (transaminases ≥2× normal, severe RUQ/epigastric pain), renal insufficiency (creatinine >1.1 mg/dL or doubling), pulmonary edema, new-onset headache unresponsive to medication, visual disturbances
- *High-yield: Proteinuria is no longer mandatory for diagnosis per current guidelines if severe features are present — classically taught as essential, but current ACOG/ISSHP criteria do not require it. Know both for exam framing.*

## Etiopathogenesis
- Abnormal **trophoblastic invasion** of spiral arteries → failure of physiological vasodilation → placental ischemia
- Release of anti-angiogenic factors (**sFlt-1**, soluble endoglin) and ↓ pro-angiogenic **PlGF** → endothelial dysfunction
- Endothelial dysfunction → vasospasm, ↑ vascular permeability, activation of coagulation → the multisystem disease

## Risk Factors
- Primigravida, extremes of maternal age, multiple pregnancy, molar pregnancy
- Pre-existing HTN, diabetes, renal disease, obesity, antiphospholipid syndrome
- Prior pre-eclampsia, family history
- New paternity (immunological theory)

## Clinical Features
- Often asymptomatic initially (screening-detected)
- Headache, visual disturbance (blurring, scotomata), epigastric/RUQ pain (liver capsule stretch), nausea/vomiting, sudden edema/rapid weight gain, decreased urine output
- Hyperreflexia, clonus — impending eclampsia

## Investigations
- BP monitoring, urine protein (dipstick/24h/ACR)
- CBC (platelets, hemoconcentration), LFT, RFT, uric acid (early marker, not diagnostic)
- Coagulation profile if severe/HELLP suspected
- USG for fetal growth, Doppler (umbilical artery, MCA), NST/BPP for fetal surveillance
- sFlt-1/PlGF ratio — emerging tool to predict/rule out pre-eclampsia in select settings (per current knowledge; verify local guideline uptake)

## HELLP Syndrome
**H**emolysis, **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, **L**ow **P**latelets — a severe variant of pre-eclampsia
- Presents with RUQ/epigastric pain, malaise, nausea; may occur without marked hypertension/proteinuria
- Diagnosis: schistocytes on smear, ↑ LDH, ↑ AST/ALT, platelets <100,000
- Management: deliver (definitive treatment) regardless of gestational age if severe/deteriorating

## Management
| Aspect | Approach |
|---|---|
| Mild gestational HTN/pre-eclampsia without severe features | Close monitoring, may continue pregnancy with fetal surveillance; deliver by 37 weeks |
| Severe pre-eclampsia | Stabilize (antihypertensives, MgSO4), deliver — timing depends on gestational age & severity, generally by 34 weeks with steroids for lung maturity if <34 weeks, immediate delivery if uncontrollable severe features/HELLP/eclampsia |
| Antihypertensives (acute severe HTN) | IV **Labetalol**, IV **Hydralazine**, oral **Nifedipine** — first-line agents |
| Antihypertensives (long-term/oral) | Labetalol, Methyldopa, Nifedipine — **avoid ACE inhibitors/ARBs** (teratogenic — fetal renal agenesis, oligohydramnios) |
| Seizure prophylaxis/treatment | **Magnesium sulfate** — Pritchard/Zuspan/Sibai regimens |
| Definitive treatment | Delivery of placenta — only cure |

### Magnesium Sulfate — Regimens & Toxicity
- **Pritchard regimen**: 4 g IV loading + 10 g IM (5 g each buttock), then 5 g IM q4h
- **Zuspan regimen**: 4 g IV loading, then 1–2 g/h IV infusion
- Monitor: deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate (≥12/min), urine output (≥25–30 mL/h) — toxicity screen before each dose
- Toxicity order: loss of DTR (first sign, ~9–12 mg/dL) → respiratory depression (~12–15 mg/dL) → cardiac arrest (>15 mg/dL)
- **Antidote: 10% Calcium gluconate 10 mL IV** slowly

## Prevention
- **Low-dose aspirin (75–150 mg)** starting before 16 weeks in high-risk women (prior pre-eclampsia, chronic HTN, multiple pregnancy, etc.) — reduces incidence
- Calcium supplementation in low-calcium-intake populations (relevant in Indian population per national guidelines)

## Complications
- Maternal: eclampsia, HELLP, abruptio placentae, DIC, acute kidney injury, pulmonary edema, cerebral hemorrhage, liver rupture
- Fetal: IUGR, oligohydramnios, placental insufficiency, preterm birth, stillbirth

## Mnemonic
**"HELLP me, my liver and platelets are failing"** — Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Only definitive cure for pre-eclampsia/eclampsia = delivery of the placenta
- Drug of choice for eclamptic seizure prophylaxis and treatment = **MgSO4** (superior to phenytoin/diazepam — landmark trial changed practice)
- MgSO4 toxicity antidote = 10% calcium gluconate; first sign of toxicity = loss of deep tendon reflexes
- Avoid ACEI/ARBs throughout pregnancy — fetotoxic
- HELLP syndrome can occur without significant hypertension or proteinuria — high index of suspicion needed
- Low-dose aspirin prophylaxis started ideally before 16 weeks in high-risk pregnancies
