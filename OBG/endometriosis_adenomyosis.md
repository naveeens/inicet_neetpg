# Endometriosis & Adenomyosis

## Endometriosis

### Definition
Presence of functioning endometrial glands and stroma outside the uterine cavity, most commonly on the ovaries, uterosacral ligaments, pouch of Douglas, and pelvic peritoneum.

### Theories of Pathogenesis
- **Sampson's theory (retrograde menstruation)** — most widely accepted
- Coelomic metaplasia theory
- Lymphatic/vascular dissemination (for distant sites — lung, umbilicus)
- Immunological/genetic predisposition

### Common Sites
- Ovaries (most common site) — **chocolate cyst/endometrioma**
- Uterosacral ligaments, pouch of Douglas, rectovaginal septum, pelvic peritoneum
- Rare: umbilicus (post-surgical scar), lung, cesarean scar

### Clinical Features
- **Classic triad**: **dysmenorrhea** (secondary, progressive), **dyspareunia** (deep), **infertility**
- Chronic pelvic pain, cyclical pain worsening with menses
- Dyschezia (pain on defecation) if rectovaginal involvement
- Examination: fixed retroverted uterus, tender nodularity in pouch of Douglas/uterosacral ligaments, adnexal mass (endometrioma)

### Diagnosis
- **Laparoscopy = gold standard** — direct visualization of lesions ("gunpowder"/"powder burn" spots, chocolate cysts, adhesions) ± biopsy
- Transvaginal ultrasound — "ground glass" echogenicity of endometrioma
- MRI — useful for deep infiltrating disease mapping
- **CA-125** may be elevated but is nonspecific — not diagnostic, occasionally used to monitor treatment response

### Staging
- **American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ASRM) staging** — Stage I (minimal) to Stage IV (severe), based on laparoscopic findings (implants, adhesions, cul-de-sac obliteration)

### Management
| Approach | Details |
|---|---|
| Medical (symptom control, suppresses but does not cure) | NSAIDs for pain; hormonal suppression — combined OCPs (continuous), progestins, **GnRH agonists** (with add-back therapy to limit hypoestrogenic side effects), **Danazol** (androgenic side effects limit use), Dienogest, aromatase inhibitors in refractory cases |
| Surgical (conservative) | Laparoscopic excision/ablation of lesions, adhesiolysis, cystectomy for endometrioma — preferred in women desiring fertility |
| Surgical (definitive) | Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy — reserved for severe disease/failed conservative treatment, no further fertility desire |
| Infertility associated | Laparoscopic surgery to improve fertility; consider ART (IUI/IVF) if severe/longstanding |

### Complications
- Infertility, chronic pelvic pain, endometrioma rupture, dense adhesions, rare malignant transformation (endometrioid/clear cell ovarian carcinoma — small but recognized risk with long-standing endometriosis)

## Adenomyosis

### Definition
Presence of ectopic endometrial glands and stroma **within the myometrium**, with surrounding smooth muscle hyperplasia — sometimes termed "endometriosis interna" (distinct disease from endometriosis, though they may coexist).

### Risk Factors
- Multiparity, advancing age (typically presents in 40s), prior uterine surgery

### Clinical Features
- **Secondary dysmenorrhea** (progressive, severe) and **menorrhagia**
- Chronic pelvic pain, dyspareunia
- Examination: **uniformly enlarged, globular, "boggy" tender uterus** (typically not more than 12-week size)

### Diagnosis
- **MRI** — most accurate imaging modality (widened junctional zone >12 mm, T2 hyperintense myometrial foci)
- Transvaginal ultrasound — heterogeneous myometrium, myometrial cysts, poorly defined endometrial-myometrial junction
- Definitive diagnosis historically only by **histopathology after hysterectomy**

### Management
| Approach | Details |
|---|---|
| Symptomatic/medical | NSAIDs, hormonal therapy (levonorgestrel IUS, combined OCPs, GnRH agonists) — especially if fertility desired or surgery undesired |
| Definitive | **Hysterectomy** — curative, treatment of choice for women who have completed childbearing and have significant symptoms |
| Fertility-sparing surgical options | Adenomyomectomy (technically difficult, limited data), uterine artery embolization in select cases |

## Endometriosis vs Adenomyosis — Quick Comparison
| Feature | Endometriosis | Adenomyosis |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Outside the uterus (ovary, peritoneum) | Within the myometrium |
| Typical age | Younger, reproductive age | Older, typically 40s, multiparous |
| Uterus on exam | Often normal size, fixed/retroverted | Diffusely enlarged, boggy, tender |
| Key symptom pattern | Dysmenorrhea + dyspareunia + infertility | Dysmenorrhea + menorrhagia |
| Gold standard diagnosis | Laparoscopy | MRI (clinically); histopathology (definitive) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Sampson's retrograde menstruation theory is the most accepted explanation for pelvic endometriosis
- Ovary is the most common site of endometriosis; presents as a chocolate cyst/endometrioma
- Laparoscopy is the gold standard for diagnosing endometriosis; MRI is preferred for adenomyosis
- Classic endometriosis triad: dysmenorrhea, dyspareunia, infertility
- Adenomyosis classically presents with a diffusely enlarged, tender, "boggy" uterus in a multiparous woman in her 40s
- CA-125 may be elevated in both endometriosis and ovarian malignancy — nonspecific, not used for diagnosis
- Hysterectomy is definitive/curative treatment for adenomyosis in women who have completed childbearing
