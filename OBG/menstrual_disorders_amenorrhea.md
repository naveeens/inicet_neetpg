# Menstrual Disorders & Amenorrhea

## Normal Menstrual Cycle
- Average cycle length: 21–35 days; average flow duration 3–7 days; average blood loss ~30–40 mL (>80 mL considered menorrhagia)
- Regulated by hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis: GnRH (pulsatile) → FSH/LH → follicular development, ovulation, corpus luteum → estrogen/progesterone feedback

## Amenorrhea

### Classification
| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Primary amenorrhea** | No menarche by age 15 in presence of normal secondary sexual characteristics, OR no menarche by age 13 with absent secondary sexual characteristics |
| **Secondary amenorrhea** | Absence of menses for ≥3 consecutive cycles (or 6 months) in a woman with previously normal menstruation |

### Causes — Compartment-Based Approach
| Compartment | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Hypothalamic** | Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (stress, excessive exercise, weight loss/eating disorders), Kallmann syndrome (anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism) |
| **Pituitary** | Hyperprolactinemia (prolactinoma), Sheehan syndrome, other pituitary tumors/hypopituitarism |
| **Ovarian** | **PCOS** (most common cause of secondary amenorrhea overall), premature ovarian insufficiency, Turner syndrome (45,X — primary amenorrhea with streak gonads) |
| **Uterine/Outflow tract** | **Asherman syndrome** (intrauterine adhesions, classically post-curettage/endometritis), Müllerian agenesis (**Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome** — absent uterus/vagina, normal ovaries/secondary sexual characteristics, 46,XX), imperforate hymen/transverse vaginal septum (cause of primary amenorrhea with cyclical pain — hematocolpos) |
| Physiological | Pregnancy (most common cause of secondary amenorrhea overall in reproductive-age women — always exclude first), lactation, menopause |

### Approach to Primary Amenorrhea
1. Exclude pregnancy
2. Assess secondary sexual characteristics and pelvic anatomy (USG — presence/absence of uterus)
3. Uterus absent → karyotype ± testosterone level (differentiate MRKH syndrome from complete androgen insensitivity syndrome — both present with absent uterus, normal external female phenotype, but karyotype differs: 46,XX in MRKH vs 46,XY in androgen insensitivity)
4. Uterus present → hormonal workup (FSH/LH, prolactin, TSH) to localize hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis defect

### Approach to Secondary Amenorrhea
1. Exclude pregnancy (urine/serum β-hCG) — always first step
2. TSH, prolactin
3. FSH/LH — high FSH suggests ovarian failure; low/normal FSH with low estrogen suggests hypothalamic-pituitary cause
4. Progesterone challenge test — withdrawal bleed suggests adequate estrogen and patent outflow tract (anovulation as cause); no bleed suggests estrogen deficiency or outflow tract obstruction

## Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB)

### PALM-COEIN Classification (FIGO)
| Structural (PALM) | Non-Structural (COEIN) |
|---|---|
| **P**olyp | **C**oagulopathy |
| **A**denomyosis | **O**vulatory dysfunction |
| **L**eiomyoma (fibroid) | **E**ndometrial (primary endometrial disorder) |
| **M**alignancy and hyperplasia | **I**atrogenic |
| — | **N**ot yet classified |

### Dysfunctional Uterine Bleeding (DUB)
- Abnormal bleeding without identifiable structural/systemic pathology — most commonly due to **anovulation** (common at extremes of reproductive life: adolescence, perimenopause)
- Anovulatory DUB: unopposed estrogen → irregular endometrial proliferation and breakdown
- Management: correct anemia; hormonal management (COCP, cyclic progestins, high-dose progestins for acute bleeding); NSAIDs/tranexamic acid for ovulatory heavy bleeding; LNG-IUS as effective long-term option; D&C for acute uncontrolled bleeding (diagnostic + therapeutic) or if malignancy needs exclusion (especially perimenopausal/postmenopausal)

## Terminology of Abnormal Bleeding
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Menorrhagia (heavy menstrual bleeding) | Regular cycles, excessive amount/duration |
| Metrorrhagia | Irregular intermenstrual bleeding |
| Menometrorrhagia | Irregular, excessive bleeding |
| Polymenorrhea | Cycles <21 days |
| Oligomenorrhea | Cycles >35 days |
| Hypomenorrhea | Regular cycles, scanty flow |

## Dysmenorrhea
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | No pelvic pathology; due to excess prostaglandins (PGF2α) causing uterine hypercontractility/ischemia; onset with ovulatory cycles (adolescence); pain begins with/just before menses |
| **Secondary** | Underlying pelvic pathology (endometriosis, adenomyosis, fibroids, PID); onset later in life, progressive |
- Management primary dysmenorrhea: NSAIDs (first-line, inhibit prostaglandin synthesis), COCPs (suppress ovulation)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Pregnancy is the most common cause of secondary amenorrhea in reproductive-age women — always exclude first
- PCOS is the most common endocrine/ovarian cause of secondary amenorrhea
- MRKH syndrome (46,XX, absent uterus, normal ovaries) vs androgen insensitivity syndrome (46,XY) both present with primary amenorrhea and absent uterus but differ by karyotype and testosterone level
- Asherman syndrome (intrauterine adhesions) classically follows aggressive uterine curettage and causes secondary amenorrhea with a normal hormonal axis
- PALM-COEIN is the FIGO classification system for abnormal uterine bleeding, separating structural from non-structural causes
- Primary dysmenorrhea is due to excess prostaglandin-mediated uterine contraction; NSAIDs are first-line treatment
- High FSH in secondary amenorrhea points to ovarian failure; low/normal FSH points to a hypothalamic-pituitary cause
