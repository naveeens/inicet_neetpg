# BPH Prostate Disease

## Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

### Definition & Pathophysiology
Age-related non-malignant hyperplasia of the **transition zone** of the prostate (periurethral glands), leading to bladder outlet obstruction. Driven by dihydrotestosterone (DHT), produced from testosterone via 5-alpha-reductase.

### Clinical Features — LUTS (Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms)
| Category | Symptoms |
|---|---|
| Obstructive (voiding) | Hesitancy, straining, weak/intermittent stream, terminal dribbling, incomplete emptying, sense of residual urine |
| Irritative (storage) | Frequency, urgency, nocturia (often the most bothersome symptom to patients) |

- DRE: smooth, firm, enlarged prostate with preserved median sulcus (contrast with malignancy — hard, irregular, nodular, sulcus may be obliterated)
- **International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS)** — validated tool to grade symptom severity and guide treatment

### Investigations
- PSA — can be mildly elevated in BPH (large gland); markedly elevated/rapidly rising PSA raises suspicion for malignancy
- USG (abdominal/transrectal) — estimates prostate volume, post-void residual urine, assesses for hydronephrosis if chronic retention
- Uroflowmetry — objective assessment of urinary flow rate

### Complications
- Acute urinary retention, recurrent UTI, bladder calculi (from stasis), **bladder diverticula**, hydronephrosis/renal impairment (chronic retention with overflow incontinence — "high-pressure chronic retention")

### Management
| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild symptoms | Watchful waiting, lifestyle modification (avoid evening fluids, caffeine, anticholinergics) |
| Moderate symptoms | **Alpha-blockers** (tamsulosin, silodosin — relax prostatic smooth muscle, faster symptom relief); **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors** (finasteride, dutasteride — shrink gland, slower onset, useful in larger prostates); combination therapy for larger glands with bothersome symptoms |
| Severe/refractory/complications (retention, recurrent UTI, stones, renal impairment) | Surgical: **TURP (transurethral resection of prostate)** = gold standard surgical treatment; open prostatectomy for very large glands; laser techniques (HoLEP, PVP) increasingly used |

*High-yield: 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors lower PSA levels by roughly half — must account for this when interpreting PSA in patients on finasteride/dutasteride.*

### TURP Complications
- **TURP syndrome**: dilutional hyponatremia from absorption of hypotonic irrigation fluid — confusion, nausea, seizures, bradycardia; less common now with use of isotonic (saline-based) irrigation in bipolar TURP
- Retrograde ejaculation (most common long-term sexual side effect), urinary incontinence, urethral stricture, bleeding

## Prostate Cancer (Brief Surgical Overview — see also Urologic Oncology)
- Arises predominantly in the **peripheral zone** of the prostate (contrast with BPH — transition zone) — this is why prostate cancer is often palpable on DRE while remaining asymptomatic for urinary symptoms until advanced/locally invasive
- DRE: hard, nodular, irregular prostate with loss of median sulcus
- Screening and management detailed separately (see Urologic Oncology notes)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- BPH arises from the transition zone; prostate cancer arises predominantly from the peripheral zone
- DRE in BPH: smooth, firm, enlarged, median sulcus preserved; in cancer: hard, nodular, irregular, sulcus may be lost
- Alpha-blockers give faster symptomatic relief; 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors shrink the gland over months and are more effective in larger prostates
- Finasteride/dutasteride reduce PSA by about 50% — adjust interpretation accordingly
- TURP is the surgical gold standard for BPH; TURP syndrome = dilutional hyponatremia from hypotonic irrigant absorption
- Nocturia is often the most bothersome LUTS symptom for patients despite not always being the most "severe" objectively
- Acute urinary retention, recurrent UTI, bladder stones, and renal impairment are absolute indications for surgical intervention in BPH
