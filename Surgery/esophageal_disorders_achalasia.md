# Esophageal Disorders Achalasia

## Achalasia Cardia

### Definition
Primary esophageal motility disorder due to loss of inhibitory neurons (containing NO/VIP) in the myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus of the lower esophageal sphincter (LES) → failure of LES relaxation + loss of peristalsis in the esophageal body.

### Etiology
- Idiopathic (most common in Western/Indian population)
- Secondary (pseudoachalasia): Chagas disease (*T. cruzi* — destroys myenteric plexus, common in South America), esophageal/gastric malignancy infiltrating the LES

### Clinical Features
- Progressive dysphagia to **both solids and liquids** (differentiates from mechanical/malignant strictures, which typically start with solids and progress to liquids)
- Regurgitation of undigested food, weight loss
- Nocturnal cough/aspiration pneumonia (regurgitated contents)
- Chest pain/discomfort (variable)

### Diagnosis
| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Barium esophagogram | **"Bird's beak" appearance** — dilated esophageal body tapering smoothly to a narrowed LES; may show air-fluid level |
| Esophageal manometry | **Gold standard** — incomplete/absent LES relaxation + absent or simultaneous (non-peristaltic) contractions of esophageal body |
| Upper GI endoscopy | Mandatory to rule out pseudoachalasia (malignancy) — done in all patients before treatment |

### Management
| Modality | Details |
|---|---|
| Pneumatic dilatation | Balloon dilation of LES — effective, less invasive, risk of perforation |
| Heller's myotomy (surgical) | Division of LES muscle fibers, typically laparoscopic; combined with partial fundoplication (Dor/Toupet) to prevent reflux — considered most durable treatment |
| POEM (Per-Oral Endoscopic Myotomy) | Newer endoscopic technique gaining preference in many centers — verify against latest guideline on first-line positioning |
| Botulinum toxin injection into LES | Temporary relief — reserved for poor surgical candidates, short-lived effect |

*High-yield: Achalasia is a risk factor for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (long-standing stasis and chronic mucosal irritation) — periodic surveillance is advised in long-standing disease.*

## Other Esophageal Disorders (Exam Essentials)

### GERD
- LES incompetence → acid reflux → heartburn, regurgitation; complications: esophagitis, stricture, **Barrett's esophagus** (intestinal metaplasia — premarker for esophageal **adenocarcinoma**)
- Diagnosis: 24-h pH monitoring (gold standard for reflux quantification), endoscopy for complications
- Management: PPI first-line; Nissen fundoplication for refractory/complicated GERD

### Esophageal Carcinoma
| Type | Key Association |
|---|---|
| Squamous cell carcinoma | Upper/mid esophagus; risk factors — smoking, alcohol, hot beverages, achalasia, corrosive stricture; more common in India/Asia |
| Adenocarcinoma | Lower esophagus/GE junction; arises from **Barrett's esophagus** (chronic GERD); increasing incidence in Western countries |

- Progressive dysphagia (solids → liquids), weight loss — classic presentation
- Diagnosis: endoscopy + biopsy; staging with CT/EUS/PET
- Management: esophagectomy (curative in early disease) ± neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy for locally advanced disease

### Zenker's Diverticulum
- Pharyngoesophageal pulsion diverticulum through Killian's dehiscence (area of weakness between cricopharyngeus and inferior pharyngeal constrictor)
- Elderly, dysphagia, regurgitation of undigested food, halitosis, gurgling in neck
- Diagnosis: barium swallow (avoid endoscopy first — perforation risk)
- Treatment: surgical/endoscopic diverticulectomy with cricopharyngeal myotomy

### Mallory-Weiss Tear
- Mucosal tear at GE junction from forceful vomiting/retching (classically post-alcohol binge)
- Presents with hematemesis after repeated retching; usually self-limiting, managed conservatively/endoscopically

### Boerhaave Syndrome
- Full-thickness esophageal rupture from forceful vomiting — surgical emergency
- Mackler's triad: vomiting, chest pain, subcutaneous emphysema
- High mortality if delayed diagnosis — urgent surgical repair

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Achalasia: dysphagia to solids AND liquids from onset; malignant stricture/carcinoma: dysphagia to solids first, progressing to liquids
- "Bird's beak" on barium swallow = achalasia; manometry is the gold standard for diagnosis
- Barrett's esophagus → risk factor for esophageal **adenocarcinoma**; achalasia and chronic irritation → risk factor for **squamous cell carcinoma**
- Zenker's diverticulum arises through Killian's dehiscence — get barium swallow, not endoscopy, first
- Boerhaave syndrome = spontaneous esophageal rupture from vomiting — surgical emergency, Mackler's triad
- Chagas disease is a classic secondary cause of achalasia-like picture in endemic areas
