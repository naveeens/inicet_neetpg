# Renal Transplant Tumors

## Renal Transplantation

### Indications
End-stage renal disease (ESRD) requiring renal replacement therapy — transplantation offers superior survival and quality of life compared to long-term dialysis when feasible.

### Donor Types
| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| Living related donor | Better HLA matching typically, best long-term graft survival, avoids waitlist time |
| Living unrelated donor | Legally regulated (in India, governed by Transplantation of Human Organs Act) to prevent commercial exploitation |
| Deceased donor | Brain-dead donor (heart-beating) most common source; longer cold ischemia time |

### Immunology & Matching
- **HLA matching** (A, B, DR loci) — better matching associated with improved graft survival, though modern immunosuppression has reduced its absolute importance
- **ABO compatibility** — generally required (ABO-incompatible transplant possible with specialized desensitization protocols)
- Crossmatch test (recipient serum + donor lymphocytes) — must be negative before transplant to exclude preformed donor-specific antibodies (positive crossmatch = high hyperacute rejection risk)

### Rejection Types
| Type | Timing | Mechanism | Key Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperacute rejection | Minutes to hours | Preformed antibodies against donor antigens (ABO/HLA) | Irreversible, graft must be removed; prevented by pre-transplant crossmatching |
| Acute rejection | Days to weeks (up to first few months) | Cellular (T-cell mediated) or antibody-mediated | Reversible with augmented immunosuppression if caught early; diagnosed on biopsy |
| Chronic rejection | Months to years | Combination of immune and non-immune mechanisms | Progressive graft dysfunction/fibrosis, largely irreversible, most common cause of long-term graft loss |

### Immunosuppression
- Induction: often includes lymphocyte-depleting or IL-2 receptor antibodies at time of transplant
- Maintenance: typically a **calcineurin inhibitor** (tacrolimus/cyclosporine) + **antimetabolite** (mycophenolate mofetil) + **corticosteroids** — standard triple therapy backbone
- Complications of long-term immunosuppression: increased infection risk (opportunistic — CMV, BK virus), increased malignancy risk (skin cancers, post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder — often EBV-driven), nephrotoxicity (calcineurin inhibitors), metabolic effects (new-onset diabetes, hypertension)

### Surgical Considerations
- Graft typically placed extraperitoneally in the iliac fossa (heterotopic — native kidneys usually left in situ)
- Vascular anastomosis: donor renal artery to recipient external/internal iliac artery, donor renal vein to external iliac vein
- Ureteroneocystostomy for urinary drainage

## Renal Tumors

### Benign Renal Tumors
| Tumor | Key Points |
|---|---|
| Angiomyolipoma | Benign hamartoma (fat, smooth muscle, vessels); associated with tuberous sclerosis; risk of spontaneous hemorrhage if large (>4 cm) |
| Oncocytoma | Benign, can mimic RCC on imaging, definitive distinction often requires histology |
| Simple renal cyst | Very common, usually incidental and asymptomatic; Bosniak classification used to stratify malignant potential of cystic renal lesions on CT |

### Malignant Renal Tumors
- **Renal cell carcinoma** — most common renal malignancy in adults (detailed separately in Urologic Oncology notes)
- **Wilms' tumor (nephroblastoma)** — most common renal malignancy of childhood (typically 2–5 years); presents as asymptomatic abdominal mass; associated with WT1 gene mutations, WAGR syndrome, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome; excellent prognosis with combined surgery + chemotherapy ± radiotherapy
- **Transitional cell carcinoma of renal pelvis** — less common, associated with analgesic abuse (phenacetin) and aristolochic acid (Balkan/Chinese herb nephropathy), same risk factors as bladder urothelial cancer

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Hyperacute rejection is due to preformed antibodies, occurs within minutes-hours, and is irreversible — prevented by crossmatching
- Chronic rejection is the leading cause of long-term graft loss in renal transplantation
- Standard maintenance immunosuppression = calcineurin inhibitor + antimetabolite + corticosteroid
- Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder is typically EBV-driven and related to the degree of immunosuppression
- Angiomyolipoma is associated with tuberous sclerosis and can bleed spontaneously if large
- Wilms' tumor is the most common renal malignancy of childhood; RCC is the most common in adults
- Bosniak classification stratifies renal cystic lesions by malignant potential on CT
