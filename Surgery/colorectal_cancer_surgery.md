# Colorectal Cancer Surgery

## Definition
Malignancy arising from colonic/rectal mucosa, overwhelmingly adenocarcinoma, typically progressing through the adenoma-carcinoma sequence (APC → KRAS → p53 mutations — classic molecular pathway).

## Risk Factors
- Adenomatous polyps (villous > tubulovillous > tubular — increasing malignant potential), especially large (>2 cm) or high-grade dysplasia
- Hereditary syndromes: **FAP** (APC gene, chromosome 5q, autosomal dominant, 100% risk of CRC by 40s if untreated), **Lynch syndrome/HNPCC** (mismatch repair genes — MLH1, MSH2, etc.; right-sided cancers, younger age, synchronous/metachronous tumors)
- Inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis > Crohn's; risk correlates with extent and duration of disease — surveillance colonoscopy after 8–10 years)
- Diet: low fiber, high red/processed meat; obesity, smoking, alcohol
- Family history, personal history of prior CRC/polyps

## Clinical Features (Site-Dependent)
| Location | Presentation |
|---|---|
| Right colon (cecum, ascending) | Occult bleeding → iron-deficiency anemia, vague abdominal pain, mass; lumen wide so obstruction is late |
| Left colon (descending, sigmoid) | Altered bowel habits, colicky pain, obstruction (narrower lumen, semi-solid stool), hematochezia |
| Rectum | Bleeding per rectum, tenesmus, mucus discharge, altered bowel habit, mass on DRE |

*High-yield: New-onset iron-deficiency anemia in a male or postmenopausal woman must be worked up for occult GI malignancy (colonoscopy) until proven otherwise.*

## Diagnosis & Staging
- Colonoscopy with biopsy — gold standard for diagnosis
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis — staging, metastasis workup (liver, lung)
- **CEA** — tumor marker; not for screening/diagnosis, useful for baseline, monitoring recurrence, and prognosis post-resection
- Rectal cancer: MRI pelvis for local staging (mesorectal involvement, circumferential resection margin) ± endorectal USG

### TNM/Staging Essentials
- Depth of invasion (T), nodal involvement (N), and distant metastasis (M) determine stage and prognosis
- Liver = most common site of distant metastasis (portal venous drainage)

## Surgical Management
| Tumor Location | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Cecum/ascending colon | Right hemicolectomy |
| Transverse colon | Extended right hemicolectomy |
| Descending colon | Left hemicolectomy |
| Sigmoid colon | Sigmoid colectomy |
| Upper/mid rectum | Anterior resection (with total mesorectal excision, TME) |
| Low rectum (near anal sphincter, not amenable to sphincter-sparing surgery) | Abdominoperineal resection (APR) — permanent colostomy |

- **Total mesorectal excision (TME)** = key oncologic principle for rectal cancer — dramatically reduces local recurrence
- Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy for locally advanced rectal cancer (T3/T4 or node-positive) before surgery — downstages tumor, improves sphincter preservation
- Adjuvant chemotherapy (FOLFOX-based) for stage III (node-positive) and select high-risk stage II colon cancer

## Screening (Average-Risk Population)
- Colonoscopy — gold standard; per current guidance start around age 45–50 and repeat every 10 years (verify against latest national/exam-specific guideline for exact starting age, as this has trended earlier in recent years)
- FOBT/FIT (annual), flexible sigmoidoscopy — alternative/adjunct strategies
- FAP: screening sigmoidoscopy from early teens; consider prophylactic colectomy once polyps develop
- Lynch syndrome: colonoscopy from ~20–25 years, every 1–2 years

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Adenoma-carcinoma sequence: normal mucosa → adenoma → carcinoma (APC → KRAS → p53)
- Right-sided colon cancer classically presents with **anemia**; left-sided with **obstruction/altered bowel habit**
- Liver is the most common site of hematogenous metastasis (portal drainage)
- CEA is for monitoring/prognosis, NOT for screening or diagnosis
- TME is the standard oncologic principle in rectal cancer surgery
- FAP → APC gene, chromosome 5; Lynch syndrome → mismatch repair genes, right-sided predominance
- Ulcerative colitis carries higher colorectal cancer risk than Crohn's disease, correlating with disease duration and extent
