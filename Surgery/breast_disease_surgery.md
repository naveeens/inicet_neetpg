# Breast Disease Surgery

## Benign Breast Disease
| Condition | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Fibroadenoma | Most common benign breast tumor, young women (<30 yrs); "breast mouse" — mobile, firm, painless; estrogen-sensitive |
| Fibrocystic disease | Most common cause of breast lumps/pain overall; cyclical pain, multiple lumps varying with menstrual cycle |
| Breast cyst | Smooth, mobile, fluctuant; aspirate — if clear fluid and resolves, no further workup; if bloody/recurrent/residual mass, biopsy |
| Phyllodes tumor | Can be benign or malignant (rare); large, rapidly growing; treated by wide local excision with margins (not enucleation) |
| Duct ectasia | Perimenopausal women; greenish nipple discharge, subareolar mass |
| Intraductal papilloma | Most common cause of **bloody nipple discharge** in young/middle-aged women; usually benign, solitary |
| Fat necrosis | History of trauma; can mimic carcinoma clinically and on imaging — biopsy often needed to exclude malignancy |
| Mastalgia | Cyclical (fibrocystic change, most common) vs non-cyclical; reassurance, evening primrose oil, danazol for severe cases |

*High-yield: Bloody nipple discharge — most common cause is intraductal papilloma, but malignancy must always be excluded, especially in older patients.*

## Breast Infections
- **Acute mastitis/breast abscess**: lactational (Staph aureus, cracked nipple as portal of entry) — antibiotics ± drainage; continue breastfeeding/expression from affected side
- **Periductal mastitis/duct ectasia-related infection**: non-lactational, associated with smoking
- Any non-resolving "abscess" in a non-lactating woman, especially with skin changes → must exclude **inflammatory breast carcinoma**

## Breast Cancer

### Risk Factors
- Female sex, increasing age, early menarche/late menopause, nulliparity/late first pregnancy, hormone replacement therapy, family history, **BRCA1/BRCA2** mutations, prior chest radiation, obesity (postmenopausal), alcohol

### Clinical Features
- Painless breast lump (most common presentation) — hard, irregular, fixed
- Nipple retraction, skin dimpling (Cooper's ligament involvement) — "peau d'orange" (skin edema from lymphatic blockage)
- Axillary lymphadenopathy
- **Inflammatory breast cancer**: erythema, warmth, peau d'orange without a discrete mass — aggressive, often mistaken for mastitis
- **Paget's disease of the nipple**: eczematous change of nipple-areolar complex — underlying ductal carcinoma in situ/invasive cancer

### Diagnosis
- **Triple assessment**: clinical examination + imaging (mammography/USG) + pathology (core needle biopsy/FNAC)
- Mammography: screening and diagnostic tool; USG preferred in younger women (<40, denser breasts) and for guided biopsy
- Core needle biopsy preferred over FNAC for definitive histology, receptor status (ER/PR/HER2)

### Molecular Subtypes & Receptor Status
| Receptor Status | Implication |
|---|---|
| ER/PR positive | Hormonal therapy (tamoxifen premenopausal, aromatase inhibitors postmenopausal) — better prognosis |
| HER2 positive | Trastuzumab (anti-HER2 targeted therapy) |
| Triple negative (ER-, PR-, HER2-) | No targeted therapy available; relies on chemotherapy; generally more aggressive |

### Surgical Management
- Breast-conserving surgery (lumpectomy) + adjuvant radiotherapy — equivalent survival to mastectomy for early-stage disease
- Modified radical mastectomy — for larger tumors, multicentric disease, or patient preference
- **Sentinel lymph node biopsy** — standard for axillary staging in clinically node-negative disease (avoids morbidity of full axillary dissection); axillary lymph node dissection if sentinel node positive or clinically node-positive disease
- Neoadjuvant chemotherapy — for locally advanced disease to downstage before surgery

### Screening
- Mammography — per current knowledge generally recommended starting around age 40–50 with periodic screening; exact starting age and interval vary by guideline body — verify against latest applicable guideline for exam purposes
- Clinical breast examination and breast self-awareness as adjuncts

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common benign breast tumor: **fibroadenoma** (young women); most common cause of breast lump overall: **fibrocystic disease**
- Most common cause of bloody nipple discharge: **intraductal papilloma**
- Peau d'orange = dermal lymphatic blockage by tumor cells — sign of locally advanced/inflammatory breast cancer
- Paget's disease of nipple = eczematous nipple change signaling underlying ductal carcinoma
- Triple-negative breast cancer has no targeted therapy and tends to be more aggressive
- Sentinel lymph node biopsy has replaced routine axillary dissection for clinically node-negative early breast cancer
- Triple assessment (clinical + imaging + pathology) is mandatory for any suspicious breast lump before management decisions
