# Acute Abdomen

## Definition
Sudden onset of severe abdominal pain of <7 days duration requiring urgent diagnosis and often surgical decision-making. Not a single disease — a clinical presentation with a broad differential.

## Etiology by Region (High-Yield Localization)
| Region | Common Causes |
|---|---|
| RUQ | Acute cholecystitis, cholangitis, liver abscess, perforated duodenal ulcer |
| Epigastric | Acute pancreatitis, perforated PUD, MI (always rule out), gastritis |
| LUQ | Splenic rupture/infarct, splenic flexure diverticulitis |
| RIF | Acute appendicitis (most common surgical cause overall), Meckel's diverticulitis, ectopic pregnancy, right ureteric colic, mesenteric adenitis (children) |
| Periumbilical | Early appendicitis (referred), mesenteric ischemia, ruptured AAA |
| LIF | Acute diverticulitis (sigmoid), ovarian torsion, ectopic pregnancy, ureteric colic |
| Diffuse | Perforation with peritonitis, mesenteric ischemia, intestinal obstruction, DKA, acute intermittent porphyria |

*High-yield: In any woman of reproductive age with lower abdominal pain, ectopic pregnancy must be excluded first (β-hCG) before other workup.*

## Clinical Assessment
- **Character of pain**: visceral (dull, poorly localized, midline) → somatic/parietal (sharp, localized, aggravated by movement) as peritoneum gets involved — classic in appendicitis (periumbilical → RIF migration)
- **Peritonitis signs**: guarding, rigidity, rebound tenderness, absent bowel sounds, patient lying still (vs colic — patient writhes in pain)
- **Referred pain**: shoulder tip pain (diaphragmatic irritation — subphrenic abscess, splenic rupture, ruptured ectopic with hemoperitoneum)
- Special signs: Murphy's sign (cholecystitis), McBurney's point tenderness/Rovsing's/Psoas/Obturator sign (appendicitis), Cullen's/Grey-Turner's sign (hemorrhagic pancreatitis), Kehr's sign (splenic injury)

## Investigations
| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Erect chest X-ray / abdominal X-ray | Free gas under diaphragm (perforation), dilated bowel loops, air-fluid levels (obstruction), sentinel loop (pancreatitis) |
| USG abdomen | First-line for gallbladder, appendix (especially children/pregnant), free fluid, ovarian/adnexal pathology |
| Contrast CT abdomen | Gold standard for most causes when diagnosis unclear — appendicitis, diverticulitis, pancreatitis, ischemia, obstruction |
| Serum amylase/lipase | Pancreatitis (lipase more specific) |
| β-hCG | Exclude ectopic/pregnancy-related causes in women |
| Serum lactate | Marker of bowel ischemia/hypoperfusion |

## Management Principles
- Resuscitate first (IV fluids, analgesia — old teaching of "no analgesia before diagnosis" is outdated; adequate analgesia does not mask peritonism and is now standard of care)
- NG decompression if obstruction/ileus, NPO, correct electrolytes
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics if suspected perforation/sepsis
- Indications for emergency laparotomy: generalized peritonitis, free gas on imaging (perforation), uncontrolled hemorrhage, bowel ischemia/gangrene, failed conservative management

## Special Scenarios
| Condition | Key Point |
|---|---|
| Perforated peptic ulcer | Sudden severe epigastric pain, board-like rigidity, free gas on erect CXR; Graham's omental patch repair for perforation |
| Acute appendicitis | Alvarado score for clinical diagnosis; migratory pain (periumbilical → RIF) is classic; imaging preferred in atypical presentations (children, elderly, pregnant, women) |
| Mesenteric ischemia | Pain out of proportion to exam — classic exam trap; elderly with AF/embolic source; high mortality if missed |
| Ruptured AAA | Elderly male, sudden abdominal/back pain, hypotension, pulsatile mass — immediate surgery, no time for extensive imaging if unstable |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common surgical cause of acute abdomen overall: **acute appendicitis**
- Pain out of proportion to physical findings → think **mesenteric ischemia**
- Erect CXR showing free gas under diaphragm → **perforated hollow viscus** (duodenal ulcer most common cause)
- Reproductive-age woman with acute lower abdominal pain → always exclude **ectopic pregnancy** first
- Adequate analgesia does NOT delay diagnosis or mask peritonism — outdated teaching of withholding analgesia has been abandoned
- Old, sick, and immunosuppressed patients can have minimal signs despite severe intra-abdominal pathology — low threshold for imaging
