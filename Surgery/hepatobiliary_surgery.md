# Hepatobiliary Surgery

## Gallstone Disease

### Types of Gallstones
| Type | Composition | Risk Factors |
|---|---|---|
| Cholesterol stones | Cholesterol supersaturation | **"5 F's"** — Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile, Family history; also OCPs, rapid weight loss, TPN |
| Pigment stones (black) | Calcium bilirubinate | Hemolytic conditions (sickle cell, thalassemia, hereditary spherocytosis), cirrhosis |
| Pigment stones (brown) | Bacterial infection related | Biliary stasis/infection, seen in bile duct stones |
| Mixed stones | Most common type overall | — |

### Clinical Spectrum
| Condition | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Biliary colic | Episodic RUQ pain post-fatty meal, resolves spontaneously, no fever/systemic signs |
| Acute cholecystitis | Persistent RUQ pain, fever, **Murphy's sign positive**, leukocytosis; USG — gallbladder wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid, sonographic Murphy's sign |
| Choledocholithiasis | Stone in CBD — obstructive jaundice, pain, may have deranged LFTs (↑ conjugated bilirubin, ALP) |
| Cholangitis | **Charcot's triad**: fever, jaundice, RUQ pain (+ hypotension + altered mentation = **Reynold's pentad** — suppurative cholangitis, surgical/endoscopic emergency) |
| Gallstone ileus | Cholecystoenteric fistula → stone impacts distal ileum (narrowest point) → small bowel obstruction; **Rigler's triad** on imaging: pneumobilia, ectopic gallstone, small bowel obstruction |
| Mirizzi syndrome | Stone impacted in cystic duct/Hartmann's pouch compresses common hepatic duct → obstructive jaundice |

### Management
- Asymptomatic gallstones: usually observation (exceptions: porcelain gallbladder, large stones >3 cm, in some high-risk ethnic groups — cholecystectomy considered for cancer risk)
- Symptomatic/acute cholecystitis: **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** — early (within 72 h/1 week of symptom onset per current practice) preferred over delayed interval surgery
- Choledocholithiasis: **ERCP** with sphincterotomy and stone extraction ± subsequent cholecystectomy
- Acute cholangitis: urgent biliary decompression (ERCP) + IV antibiotics — emergency

## Liver Abscess
| Feature | Amoebic Liver Abscess | Pyogenic Liver Abscess |
|---|---|---|
| Organism | *Entamoeba histolytica* | Polymicrobial (E. coli, Klebsiella, anaerobes) — often biliary source |
| Location | Usually right lobe, single | Can be multiple |
| Content | "Anchovy sauce" pus — sterile, odorless | Foul-smelling, frank pus |
| Serology | Amoebic serology positive | Negative |
| Management | Metronidazole/tinidazole (most respond to medical therapy alone); aspiration if large (>5 cm), no response, or impending rupture | Antibiotics + percutaneous/surgical drainage (source control essential) |

## Portal Hypertension
- Causes: cirrhosis (most common, "pre-sinusoidal/sinusoidal/post-sinusoidal" classification), portal vein thrombosis, Budd-Chiari syndrome
- Complications: esophageal/gastric varices (bleeding — most feared), ascites, splenomegaly, hepatic encephalopathy, caput medusae
- Variceal bleed management: resuscitation, IV octreotide/terlipressin, endoscopic band ligation, balloon tamponade (Sengstaken-Blakemore tube) as bridge, TIPS for refractory cases

## Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)
- Risk factors: cirrhosis (any cause), chronic HBV/HCV infection, aflatoxin exposure
- **AFP (alpha-fetoprotein)** — tumor marker
- Diagnosis: multiphase contrast CT/MRI showing arterial enhancement with venous washout (characteristic imaging can be diagnostic without biopsy in cirrhotic liver per current criteria)
- Management: surgical resection/liver transplant (early stage), locoregional therapy (RFA, TACE) for unresectable disease, systemic therapy for advanced disease

## Cholangiocarcinoma
- Risk factors: primary sclerosing cholangitis, choledochal cysts, liver fluke infection (*Clonorchis*, endemic areas), chronic biliary stasis
- **Klatskin tumor** — cholangiocarcinoma at the confluence of right and left hepatic ducts (most common site)
- Presents with painless progressive obstructive jaundice
- Poor prognosis; surgical resection is only curative option in resectable disease

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Charcot's triad (fever, jaundice, RUQ pain) = cholangitis; add hypotension + altered mentation = Reynold's pentad (suppurative cholangitis)
- Mirizzi syndrome: cystic duct stone compressing common hepatic duct → obstructive jaundice
- Rigler's triad (pneumobilia, ectopic gallstone, SBO) = gallstone ileus
- Amoebic liver abscess: "anchovy sauce" pus, right lobe, responds to metronidazole
- Klatskin tumor = cholangiocarcinoma at hepatic duct confluence
- AFP = HCC tumor marker; CEA/CA 19-9 = more associated with cholangiocarcinoma/pancreatic malignancy
- Early laparoscopic cholecystectomy (within about a week of symptom onset) is now preferred over delayed interval cholecystectomy for acute cholecystitis
