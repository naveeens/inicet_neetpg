# Hernias

## Definition
Protrusion of a viscus (or part of it) through an abnormal opening in the wall of the cavity that normally contains it.

## Anatomy — Key Differentiator: Inguinal Hernias
| Feature | Indirect Inguinal Hernia | Direct Inguinal Hernia |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Lateral to inferior epigastric vessels; through deep inguinal ring | Medial to inferior epigastric vessels; through **Hesselbach's triangle** (bounded by inguinal ligament, rectus abdominis, inferior epigastric vessels) |
| Cause | Congenital — patent processus vaginalis | Acquired — weakness of posterior wall (transversalis fascia) |
| Age | Younger patients, can occur in children | Older patients, straining/raised IAP |
| Course | Oblique, can descend into scrotum | Direct bulge, rarely reaches scrotum |
| Relation to vessels | Lateral to inferior epigastric artery | Medial to inferior epigastric artery |
| Occlusion test (deep ring) | Controlled (does not reappear) | Not controlled (still bulges — weak posterior wall) |

*High-yield: Indirect = lateral to inferior epigastric vessels ("look laterally, think indirect"); Direct = medial.*

## Types by Site
| Hernia | Key Points |
|---|---|
| Inguinal | Most common hernia overall; indirect > direct |
| Femoral | More common in females (but inguinal is still overall the most common hernia even in females); passes through femoral canal, medial to femoral vein; **highest risk of strangulation** (rigid boundaries — femoral ring) |
| Umbilical | Congenital (children, often resolves spontaneously by age 2–4) vs paraumbilical (adults, acquired) |
| Epigastric | Through linea alba, above umbilicus |
| Incisional | At site of previous surgical incision; risk factors: wound infection, obesity, poor technique |
| Spigelian | Through semilunar line, lateral to rectus — rare, difficult to diagnose |
| Obturator | Elderly thin females; presents with small bowel obstruction + medial thigh pain (Howship-Romberg sign) — notoriously missed |
| Richter's hernia | Only antimesenteric border of bowel herniates — can strangulate without causing complete obstruction (no obstructive symptoms) — dangerous, easily missed |

## Complications
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Reducible | Contents can be pushed back into the abdominal cavity |
| Irreducible (incarcerated) | Cannot be reduced, but blood supply intact |
| Obstructed | Bowel loop within hernia sac is obstructed (lumen occluded) but blood supply intact |
| Strangulated | Blood supply compromised → ischemia → gangrene — **surgical emergency** |
| Sliding hernia | Part of the wall of the sac is formed by a viscus (e.g., cecum, sigmoid colon, bladder) — risk of injury during repair |
| Maydl's hernia ("hernia-in-W") | Two loops of bowel in sac, with a third (connecting) loop lying intra-abdominally — this intra-abdominal loop is often the one that strangulates first |

## Clinical Features
- Reducible swelling that increases with cough/straining (cough impulse), disappears on lying down
- Strangulation: sudden severe pain, irreducibility, tenderness, signs of obstruction (vomiting, distension), systemic toxicity, erythema/skin changes over hernia

## Management
- **Elective**: definitive surgical repair for all clinically evident, symptomatic hernias to prevent complications
- **Mesh repair (tension-free)** — Lichtenstein technique = gold standard for open inguinal hernia repair; laparoscopic (TAPP/TEP) increasingly preferred, faster recovery
- **Strangulated hernia** = surgical emergency — resuscitate and proceed to emergency surgery; assess bowel viability, resect if non-viable
- Truss/watchful waiting: only in poor surgical candidates or minimally symptomatic direct hernias in the very elderly — not definitive treatment
- Congenital umbilical hernia in children: observe till 2–4 years, most close spontaneously; repair if persists beyond 4–5 years or symptomatic

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common hernia overall (including in women): **inguinal hernia** (indirect type)
- Hernia with highest risk of strangulation: **femoral hernia**
- Femoral canal boundaries make femoral ring rigid → poor distensibility → strangulation risk
- Richter's hernia can strangulate without bowel obstruction symptoms — high index of suspicion needed
- Obturator hernia → Howship-Romberg sign (pain along medial thigh, obturator nerve distribution) — classic in thin elderly women
- Indirect inguinal hernia sac lies within the cremasteric fibers (external to spermatic cord structures proper only after passing through internal ring, following the same path as testicular descent)
- Direct hernias almost never strangulate (wide neck through Hesselbach's triangle) — indirect and femoral hernias are more prone to strangulation
