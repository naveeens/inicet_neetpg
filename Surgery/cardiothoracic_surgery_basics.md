# Cardiothoracic Surgery Basics

## Pneumothorax
| Type | Key Points |
|---|---|
| Simple/spontaneous pneumothorax | Primary (tall, thin young males, ruptured apical bleb) vs secondary (underlying lung disease — COPD, TB) |
| Open (communicating) pneumothorax | "Sucking chest wound" — air enters pleural space through chest wall defect; seal with occlusive dressing (three-sided/vented) to prevent conversion to tension pneumothorax |
| **Tension pneumothorax** | Life-threatening — air enters pleural space, cannot escape, progressively raises intrapleural pressure → mediastinal shift, compresses great vessels/contralateral lung → obstructive shock; clinical diagnosis — do NOT wait for X-ray |

### Tension Pneumothorax — Diagnosis & Management
- Clinical features: severe respiratory distress, absent breath sounds on affected side, hyperresonant percussion, tracheal deviation **away** from affected side, distended neck veins, hypotension
- **Immediate needle decompression** (traditionally 2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line; some current protocols favor 4th/5th intercostal space, anterior/mid-axillary line — verify against latest ATLS edition) followed by **tube thoracostomy (chest tube)** as definitive management
- This is a clinical diagnosis — treat immediately, do not delay for imaging confirmation

## Chest Tube / Tube Thoracostomy
- Indications: pneumothorax (especially tension after decompression, or significant simple pneumothorax), hemothorax, empyema, post-thoracic surgery drainage
- Insertion site: **5th intercostal space, anterior to mid-axillary line** (within the "safe triangle") — avoids injury to internal mammary vessels, breast tissue, and major muscles
- Insert just above the rib (neurovascular bundle runs along the inferior border of each rib) to avoid intercostal vessel/nerve injury

## Hemothorax
- Massive hemothorax: >1500 mL blood loss into pleural space (or >200 mL/hour ongoing) — indication for thoracotomy
- Sources: intercostal/internal mammary vessels, lung parenchyma, great vessels, heart
- Management: chest tube drainage (also therapeutic and allows monitoring of ongoing blood loss), thoracotomy if massive or ongoing significant output

## Flail Chest
- Segment of chest wall (≥2 adjacent ribs fractured in ≥2 places each) that moves independently — **paradoxical movement** (inward on inspiration, outward on expiration)
- Underlying pulmonary contusion is usually the primary driver of respiratory compromise, not the mechanical flail segment itself
- Management: adequate analgesia (epidural often helpful), pulmonary toilet, oxygen, positive pressure ventilation if respiratory failure develops; surgical fixation in select cases

## Cardiac Tamponade
- Fluid/blood accumulation in pericardial sac → impaired diastolic filling → decreased cardiac output/obstructive shock
- **Beck's triad**: hypotension, distended neck veins (raised JVP), muffled/distant heart sounds
- **Pulsus paradoxus** — exaggerated fall (>10 mmHg) in systolic BP during inspiration
- Kussmaul's sign — paradoxical rise in JVP with inspiration (also seen in constrictive pericarditis)
- Diagnosis: bedside echocardiography (FAST in trauma) — pericardial effusion with diastolic collapse of right ventricle
- Management: pericardiocentesis (temporizing) → definitive surgical drainage/pericardial window or thoracotomy, especially in trauma

## Basics of Cardiopulmonary Bypass & Cardiac Surgery (Overview)
- CPB allows a motionless, bloodless surgical field by diverting blood from the heart/lungs through an extracorporeal oxygenator circuit
- Requires systemic heparinization (reversed with protamine post-bypass)
- Common indications for cardiac surgery: coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) for multivessel/left main coronary disease, valve replacement/repair (rheumatic, degenerative, endocarditis), correction of congenital heart defects

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis treated immediately — never wait for a chest X-ray
- Tracheal deviation in tension pneumothorax is AWAY from the affected side; mediastinal structures shift away from the high-pressure side
- Chest tube insertion site: 5th intercostal space, anterior to mid-axillary line ("safe triangle"), just above the rib to avoid the neurovascular bundle
- Beck's triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds) = cardiac tamponade
- Massive hemothorax (>1500 mL or >200 mL/h) is an indication for thoracotomy
- Pulmonary contusion (not the flail segment itself) is usually the main driver of respiratory failure in flail chest
- Open pneumothorax should be covered with a three-sided occlusive dressing to prevent progression to tension pneumothorax
