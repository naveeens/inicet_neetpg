# Pancreatic Surgery Tumors

## Acute Pancreatitis

### Etiology
- **"I GET SMASHED"** mnemonic: **I**diopathic, **G**allstones, **E**thanol, **T**rauma, **S**teroids, **M**umps (and other infections), **A**utoimmune, **S**corpion sting, **H**ypercalcemia/**H**ypertriglyceridemia, **E**RCP, **D**rugs
- Gallstones and alcohol account for the vast majority of cases

### Pathophysiology
Premature activation of trypsinogen within acinar cells → autodigestion of pancreatic tissue → local inflammation → systemic inflammatory response (can progress to SIRS, ARDS, multiorgan failure).

### Diagnosis (need 2 of 3)
1. Characteristic abdominal pain (epigastric, radiating to back)
2. Serum lipase/amylase >3x upper limit of normal (lipase more specific, remains elevated longer)
3. Characteristic findings on CT/MRI/USG

### Severity Assessment
- **Ranson's criteria** — classic scoring system (at admission and at 48 h); higher score = worse prognosis
- **APACHE II**, **BISAP score** — also used, BISAP simpler and can be calculated early
- CT Severity Index (Balthazar) — grades necrosis/inflammation on imaging

### Local Complications
| Complication | Timing | Key Points |
|---|---|---|
| Acute peripancreatic fluid collection | <4 weeks | No defined wall |
| Pancreatic pseudocyst | >4 weeks | Encapsulated fluid collection, no necrotic debris; managed conservatively if asymptomatic/<6cm, drainage (endoscopic/percutaneous/surgical) if symptomatic/large/complicated |
| Walled-off necrosis | >4 weeks | Encapsulated necrotic tissue; step-up approach (drainage before necrosectomy) preferred over early open surgery |
| Infected necrosis | Any time, suspect if clinical deterioration | Needs source control — minimally invasive step-up approach favored over immediate open necrosectomy |

### Management
- Supportive: aggressive IV fluid resuscitation, analgesia, NPO initially with early enteral (not parenteral) feeding once tolerated — shown to reduce infectious complications and improve outcomes
- Antibiotics: NOT routinely indicated prophylactically; reserved for confirmed infected necrosis/extrapancreatic infection
- Gallstone pancreatitis: cholecystectomy during same admission for mild cases (after resolution of pancreatitis) to prevent recurrence
- Severe/necrotizing pancreatitis: step-up approach (percutaneous/endoscopic drainage first, necrosectomy only if needed) — reduces morbidity vs early open surgery

## Chronic Pancreatitis
- Most common cause: chronic alcohol use (also idiopathic, tropical pancreatitis — notably relevant in Indian patients, hereditary pancreatitis)
- Triad: abdominal pain, steatorrhea (exocrine insufficiency), diabetes (endocrine insufficiency)
- Imaging: pancreatic calcifications on X-ray/CT, "chain of lakes" appearance of duct on ERCP/MRCP
- Management: pain control, pancreatic enzyme replacement, endoscopic/surgical drainage procedures (e.g., Puestow procedure — lateral pancreaticojejunostomy) for dilated duct disease

## Pancreatic Cancer
- Adenocarcinoma (ductal) — most common pancreatic malignancy; head of pancreas most common site
- Risk factors: smoking (strongest modifiable risk factor), chronic pancreatitis, diabetes, obesity, family history/hereditary syndromes
- Clinical: painless progressive obstructive jaundice (head of pancreas tumors), weight loss, new-onset diabetes, **Trousseau's sign** (migratory thrombophlebitis), **Courvoisier's law** (palpable non-tender gallbladder + jaundice suggests malignant, not gallstone, obstruction)
- Tumor marker: **CA 19-9** (not for screening, used for monitoring/prognosis)
- Diagnosis: contrast CT (pancreatic protocol) for staging/resectability; EUS-guided biopsy for tissue diagnosis
- Surgery: **Whipple's procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy)** for resectable head-of-pancreas tumors — only potentially curative option; most patients present at unresectable/metastatic stage due to late symptoms

## Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors (Exam Favorites)
| Tumor | Hormone | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Insulinoma | Insulin | Whipple's triad (hypoglycemia symptoms, low glucose, relief with glucose); most common functional pancreatic NET; usually benign |
| Gastrinoma | Gastrin | Zollinger-Ellison syndrome — recurrent/refractory peptic ulcers, often associated with MEN 1 |
| VIPoma | VIP | WDHA syndrome (Watery Diarrhea, Hypokalemia, Achlorhydria) |
| Glucagonoma | Glucagon | Necrolytic migratory erythema, diabetes, weight loss |
| Somatostatinoma | Somatostatin | Diabetes, gallstones, steatorrhea (inhibitory triad) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Gallstones and alcohol are the two most common causes of acute pancreatitis
- Lipase is more specific and stays elevated longer than amylase in acute pancreatitis
- Early enteral feeding (not bowel rest/TPN) improves outcomes in acute pancreatitis
- Step-up (minimally invasive) approach preferred over early open necrosectomy in infected pancreatic necrosis
- Courvoisier's law: painless palpable gallbladder + jaundice = think malignancy (periampullary/pancreatic head), not stones
- CA 19-9 = pancreatic cancer marker (not for screening); Whipple's procedure = surgery for resectable head-of-pancreas cancer
- Insulinoma = most common functional pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor, usually benign; classic triad = Whipple's triad
