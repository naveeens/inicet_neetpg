# GI Surgery PUD Bleeding

## Definition
Peptic ulcer disease (PUD) — mucosal breach in stomach/duodenum extending through muscularis mucosae, most commonly due to *H. pylori* infection or NSAID use. Surgical relevance centers on complications: bleeding, perforation, obstruction (gastric outlet obstruction).

## Etiology
- **H. pylori infection** — most common cause of duodenal ulcers (>90%) and gastric ulcers (~70–80%)
- **NSAIDs** — inhibit mucosal prostaglandin synthesis (COX-1) → impaired mucosal defense
- Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (gastrinoma) — rare but classic cause of recurrent/refractory ulcers, especially multiple/atypical location ulcers
- Stress ulcers: Cushing's ulcer (head injury/raised ICP), Curling's ulcer (burns)

## Duodenal vs Gastric Ulcer
| Feature | Duodenal Ulcer | Gastric Ulcer |
|---|---|---|
| Incidence | More common | Less common |
| Acid secretion | Normal to increased | Normal to decreased |
| Pain pattern | Relieved by food ("hunger pain," night pain) | Worsened/precipitated by food |
| Malignant potential | Essentially never malignant | Can be malignant — biopsy mandatory |
| Age | Younger (30–50) | Older (>50) |

*High-yield: All gastric ulcers require biopsy (multiple quadrant biopsies from ulcer edge) to exclude malignancy; duodenal ulcers do not routinely require biopsy.*

## Upper GI Bleeding — Approach
- Most common cause of upper GI bleed: **peptic ulcer disease** (duodenal > gastric); other causes — esophageal varices, Mallory-Weiss tear, gastritis, malignancy
- Resuscitation first: IV access (2 large-bore), crystalloid, blood transfusion as needed, correct coagulopathy
- **Endoscopy** — diagnostic and therapeutic (within 24 h); allows risk stratification

### Forrest Classification (Endoscopic — Predicts Rebleeding Risk)
| Class | Finding | Rebleed Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Ia | Active spurting bleed | Highest |
| Ib | Active oozing bleed | High |
| IIa | Non-bleeding visible vessel | High |
| IIb | Adherent clot | Intermediate |
| IIc | Flat pigmented spot | Low |
| III | Clean base ulcer | Lowest |

- Endoscopic therapy (adrenaline injection + thermal/mechanical — clips): indicated for Forrest Ia, Ib, IIa (and often IIb)
- IV proton pump inhibitor (high-dose) reduces rebleeding — start empirically before endoscopy in suspected UGI bleed

## Complications of PUD
| Complication | Key Points |
|---|---|
| Perforation | Sudden severe epigastric pain, board-like rigidity, free gas on erect CXR/CT; **Graham's omental patch repair** (definitive acid-reducing surgery not routinely done at emergency surgery — H. pylori eradication treats underlying cause) |
| Bleeding | Most common complication; posterior duodenal ulcers erode into **gastroduodenal artery** — high-risk for massive bleed |
| Gastric outlet obstruction | Chronic pyloric channel/duodenal ulcer scarring → succussion splash, copious non-bilious vomiting, hypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis |
| Penetration | Posterior duodenal ulcer → pancreas (pancreatitis, back pain) |

*High-yield: Posterior duodenal ulcers bleed (erode gastroduodenal artery); anterior duodenal ulcers perforate (into peritoneal cavity, less well protected anteriorly).*

## Management of PUD
- Medical: PPI, *H. pylori* eradication (triple therapy: PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin, or per local resistance patterns), stop NSAIDs
- Indications for surgery: failed endoscopic hemostasis/recurrent bleeding, perforation, gastric outlet obstruction unresponsive to dilation, suspicion of malignancy (gastric ulcer not healing on medical therapy)
- Bleeding duodenal ulcer surgery: duodenotomy + under-running of the bleeding vessel (gastroduodenal artery) ± truncal vagotomy and pyloroplasty
- Elective surgery for PUD is now rare given effective medical therapy (H. pylori eradication, PPIs) — surgery mainly reserved for complications

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common cause of upper GI bleed: **peptic ulcer disease**
- Posterior duodenal ulcer → erodes gastroduodenal artery → **massive bleeding**
- Anterior duodenal ulcer → **perforation** (into peritoneal cavity)
- All gastric ulcers must be biopsied to rule out malignancy; duodenal ulcers are almost never malignant
- Forrest Ia/Ib/IIa endoscopic findings → high rebleed risk → need endoscopic therapy
- Gastric outlet obstruction → hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with paradoxical aciduria
- Zollinger-Ellison syndrome → suspect in recurrent/refractory or atypically located (post-bulbar, multiple) ulcers
