# Trauma ATLS Head Injury

## ATLS — Primary Survey ("ABCDE")
| Step | Focus |
|---|---|
| **A** — Airway (with C-spine protection) | Ensure patent airway, assume C-spine injury until cleared, jaw thrust/chin lift, definitive airway if compromised |
| **B** — Breathing | Assess ventilation/oxygenation; identify and treat life-threats: tension pneumothorax, open pneumothorax, massive hemothorax, flail chest |
| **C** — Circulation (with hemorrhage control) | Control external bleeding (direct pressure), assess for shock, 2 large-bore IV lines, identify sources of internal hemorrhage |
| **D** — Disability (neurologic status) | **GCS**, pupillary response, gross motor/sensory exam |
| **E** — Exposure/Environment | Fully expose patient to find injuries, prevent hypothermia |

- Secondary survey (head-to-toe examination) only after primary survey completed and life-threats addressed
- **"Treat the greatest threat to life first"** — do not proceed to next step until preceding one addressed

## Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)
| Component | Best Score | Worst Score |
|---|---|---|
| Eye opening | 4 (spontaneous) | 1 (none) |
| Verbal response | 5 (oriented) | 1 (none) |
| Motor response | 6 (obeys commands) | 1 (none) |

- Total score range: 3–15
- Severity: Mild TBI (13–15), Moderate (9–12), Severe (≤8 — **GCS ≤8 = indication for intubation/definitive airway**, "GCS 8, intubate")

## Head Injury — Types of Intracranial Hemorrhage
| Type | Source | Classic Features |
|---|---|---|
| Extradural (epidural) hematoma | Middle meningeal artery (temporal bone fracture) | **Lucid interval** followed by rapid deterioration; biconvex/lentiform shape on CT; does not cross suture lines |
| Subdural hematoma | Bridging veins | Crescent/concave shape on CT, crosses suture lines; common in elderly/alcoholics/anticoagulated patients (even minor trauma), can be acute or chronic |
| Subarachnoid hemorrhage (traumatic) | Cortical vessels | Blood in CSF spaces/sulci on CT; also caused by aneurysm rupture (non-traumatic) |
| Diffuse axonal injury | Shearing forces (deceleration injury) | Normal/subtle CT findings but severe clinical impairment; MRI more sensitive |

*High-yield: Lucid interval is classic for extradural hematoma — patient initially conscious, then deteriorates as hematoma expands (arterial bleed = rapid).*

## Cushing's Triad (Raised ICP — Herniation Risk)
- **Bradycardia + Hypertension (widened pulse pressure) + Irregular respiration**
- Signals critically raised ICP — impending brain herniation, medical/surgical emergency

## Management of Raised ICP / Head Injury
- Maintain cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP = MAP − ICP); avoid hypotension and hypoxia (secondary brain injury prevention is the priority — "the injured brain cannot tolerate a second insult")
- Head elevation 30°, avoid tight cervical collar/neck constriction
- Osmotherapy: mannitol or hypertonic saline for acutely raised ICP
- Hyperventilation — only as a temporary bridging measure for impending herniation, not routine/prolonged (causes cerebral vasoconstriction and can worsen ischemia)
- Avoid hyperglycemia and hyperthermia
- Surgical decompression (craniotomy/evacuation) for significant extradural/subdural hematomas with mass effect or midline shift

## Damage Control & Massive Transfusion
- **Damage control surgery** — abbreviated initial surgery (control hemorrhage/contamination only) in unstable/exsanguinating trauma patients, followed by resuscitation in ICU and definitive repair later
- **Massive transfusion protocol** — balanced ratio transfusion (roughly 1:1:1 of packed RBCs:FFP:platelets) for major hemorrhage
- **Lethal triad of trauma**: hypothermia, coagulopathy, acidosis — each worsens the others, must be actively corrected

## High-Yield One-Liners
- GCS ≤8 = indication for definitive airway (intubation)
- Extradural hematoma: lucid interval, biconvex on CT, middle meningeal artery, does not cross suture lines
- Subdural hematoma: crosses suture lines, bridging veins, crescent-shaped on CT
- Cushing's triad (bradycardia, hypertension, irregular breathing) = impending herniation — surgical emergency
- Avoid hypotension and hypoxia at all costs in TBI — prevention of secondary brain injury is the guiding principle
- Prolonged/prophylactic hyperventilation is harmful in head injury — only for acute bridging in impending herniation
- Lethal triad of trauma: hypothermia, coagulopathy, acidosis — each perpetuates the others
