# Health Administration Economics

## Health Care System Levels in India
| Level | Facility | Population Norm (per current knowledge — verify latest RHS/IPHS norms) |
|---|---|---|
| Village level | Sub-centre (SC) / Health & Wellness Centre (HWC) | ~5000 (plain areas) / ~3000 (hilly/tribal/difficult areas) |
| Primary | Primary Health Centre (PHC) | ~30,000 (plain areas) / ~20,000 (hilly/tribal/difficult areas) |

| Secondary/Tertiary | District Hospital | District-level referral |

*Note: Population norms per Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS) are periodically revised — verify current figures against the latest IPHS guideline.*

## Referral System (Chain of Care — Bottom-Up)
Sub-centre/HWC (health promotion, first contact, basic curative care) → PHC (outpatient care, minor procedures, referral point for SC) → CHC (specialist care — surgery, obstetrics, medicine, pediatrics — the "First Referral Unit" for many programs) → District Hospital → Medical College/Tertiary Care Centre

## Ayushman Bharat — Two Pillars
| Pillar | Description |
|---|---|
| Health & Wellness Centres (HWCs) | Upgraded SCs/PHCs delivering Comprehensive Primary Health Care (12 packages including NCD screening, maternal-child health, mental health, etc.) |
| Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY) | Health insurance cover for hospitalization for economically vulnerable families, cashless/paperless access at empanelled hospitals — verify current cover amount and eligibility criteria against latest scheme notification |

## Health Financing — Key Concepts
- **Out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE)**: direct payment by patients at the point of service, without insurance/reimbursement — historically high in India, a major driver of catastrophic health expenditure and impoverishment
- **Catastrophic health expenditure**: health spending that exceeds a defined threshold proportion of household income/capacity to pay, pushing households into financial distress
- **Universal Health Coverage (UHC)**: ensuring all people have access to needed health services of sufficient quality without financial hardship — WHO framework built on 3 dimensions: population coverage, service coverage, financial protection
- **National Health Policy** — periodically revised (most recent framework referenced in teaching is NHP 2017); sets aspirational targets for health spending as % of GDP, IMR/MMR targets, etc. — verify current specific targets against the latest policy document

## Health Insurance Models (Conceptual)
| Model | Description |
|---|---|
| Social health insurance | Contributory scheme, typically for organized-sector workers (e.g., ESI) |
| Community-based health insurance | Voluntary, community-pooled risk, common in informal sector |
| Government-funded insurance | Tax-funded scheme covering defined population (e.g., PM-JAY) |
| Private health insurance | Premium-based, individual/employer purchased |

## Health Planning and Five-Year Plans (Historical Context)
- India's health planning historically operated through Five-Year Plans (now replaced by NITI Aayog's policy frameworks after the Planning Commission was replaced)
- Key historical committees/reports relevant to Indian public health system evolution: **Bhore Committee (1946)** — laid foundation for India's primary healthcare-oriented health system, recommended integration of preventive and curative services and one health worker per limited population

## Hospital Administration — Key Indicators
| Indicator | Formula/Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bed Occupancy Rate | (Total inpatient days / (Number of beds × number of days in period)) × 100 |
| Average Length of Stay (ALOS) | Total inpatient days / Total discharges (including deaths) |
| Bed Turnover Rate | Number of discharges / Average number of beds available |
| Death Rate (hospital) | (Total deaths / Total discharges) × 100 |

## Health Management Information System (HMIS)
- Routine reporting system aggregating health facility-level data upward (sub-centre → PHC → district → state → national) for monitoring program indicators
- Basis for tracking most national program indicators (immunization coverage, institutional delivery rates, disease notifications, etc.)

## Primary Health Care — Alma-Ata Declaration (1978)
- Landmark declaration establishing "Health for All" as a goal, with **Primary Health Care (PHC)** as the key strategy
- Core principles: equitable distribution, community participation, appropriate technology, intersectoral coordination, focus on health promotion and prevention alongside curative care
- **Essential elements of PHC** (classic list, adapted over time): health education, maternal-child health & family planning, immunization, control of locally endemic diseases, provision of essential drugs, nutrition, safe water and sanitation, treatment of common diseases/injuries

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Bhore Committee (1946) laid the foundation of India's primary healthcare-focused health system structure
- Alma-Ata Declaration (1978) established "Health for All" through Primary Health Care as the core strategy
- CHC typically functions as a First Referral Unit with specialist (surgery, OBG, medicine, pediatrics) coverage
- Out-of-pocket expenditure remains a major driver of catastrophic health spending in India, motivating UHC-oriented reforms like PM-JAY
- Ayushman Bharat has two pillars — Health & Wellness Centres (primary care) and PM-JAY (hospitalization insurance)
- Bed occupancy rate, average length of stay, and bed turnover rate are the key hospital efficiency indicators
- Universal Health Coverage rests on three dimensions: who is covered, what services are covered, and how well people are financially protected
