# Occupational Health

## Definitions
- **Occupational disease**: disease caused directly by conditions/exposures of the work environment, with a clear causal relationship (e.g., silicosis from silica dust exposure)
- **Work-related disease**: condition that may be caused/aggravated by work but is multifactorial in origin (e.g., low back pain, hypertension aggravated by stress)

## Classic Occupational Diseases and Causative Agents
| Disease | Occupation/Exposure | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Silicosis | Mining, stone cutting, sandblasting | Fibrosis, ↑ risk of TB (silicotuberculosis); "egg-shell" calcification of hilar lymph nodes on X-ray |
| Asbestosis | Asbestos manufacturing/insulation work | Progressive fibrosis; strongly associated with mesothelioma and bronchogenic carcinoma |
| Byssinosis | Cotton textile/ginning workers | "Monday morning fever/chest tightness" — symptoms worse at start of work week, improve over the week |
| Coal Worker's Pneumoconiosis (CWP) | Coal mining | Simple CWP vs complicated (progressive massive fibrosis) |
| Anthracosis | Coal dust exposure (urban air pollution too) | Deposition of carbon pigment in lungs |
| Lead poisoning | Battery manufacturing, printing, paint industry | Basophilic stippling of RBCs, wrist/foot drop, lead line on gums (Burton's line), abdominal colic |
| Mercury poisoning | Thermometer/mining industry | Erethism (irritability, tremor), "mad hatter" |
| Mesothelioma | Asbestos exposure | Malignant pleural tumor, strongly linked to asbestos |
| Byssinosis, occupational asthma | Various dust/chemical exposures | Reversible airway obstruction with removal from exposure (early stage) |
| Noise-Induced Hearing Loss (NIHL) | Industrial/factory noise, aviation | Sensorineural hearing loss, classically affects **4000 Hz** frequency first ("noise notch") |
| Caisson disease (Decompression sickness) | Divers, tunnel workers (compressed air work) | Nitrogen bubble formation on rapid decompression; joint pain ("the bends") |
| Writer's cramp / Repetitive strain injury | Occupations with repetitive fine hand movements | Focal dystonia |

## Pneumoconiosis Group — Comparison
| Feature | Silicosis | Asbestosis | CWP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent | Free silica (SiO2) | Asbestos fibers | Coal dust |
| Predominant lung zone | Upper zone | Lower zone | Upper zone |
| TB association | Strong (silicotuberculosis) | Weaker | Weaker |
| Malignancy association | Lung cancer (weaker) | Strong (mesothelioma, bronchogenic CA) | Weak |

*High-yield: Asbestosis characteristically affects the LOWER lung zones, unlike silicosis and CWP which affect UPPER zones — classic exam differentiator.*

## Occupational Health Legislation and Institutions (India)
- **Factories Act, 1948**: regulates health, safety, welfare of workers in factories (defines working hours, safety provisions, provisions for canteens/creches, appointment of factory medical officers, etc.)
- **Employees' State Insurance (ESI) Act, 1948**: social security scheme providing medical care, cash benefits (sickness, maternity, disability, dependent benefits) to organized-sector workers earning below a wage ceiling — verify current wage ceiling against latest ESI notification
- **Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)**: retirement benefit scheme (distinct from ESI, focuses on savings/pension rather than health care)

## Threshold Limit Value (TLV)
- **TLV**: maximum concentration of a substance in workplace air to which a worker can be exposed repeatedly (typically 8 hr/day, 40 hr/week) without adverse health effects
- **TLV-TWA** (Time-Weighted Average): average concentration over a normal 8-hour workday
- **TLV-STEL** (Short-Term Exposure Limit): maximum concentration for a short period (typically 15 min) without adverse effect
- **TLV-C** (Ceiling): concentration that should never be exceeded

## Biological Monitoring (Key Exam Correlations)
| Exposure | Biological Marker |
|---|---|
| Lead | Blood lead level, urinary delta-ALA, zinc protoporphyrin |
| Organophosphates | Serum/RBC cholinesterase level |
| Benzene | Urinary phenol |
| Carbon monoxide | Carboxyhemoglobin level |
| Mercury | Urinary mercury |

## Ergonomics
- Study of fitting the job/workplace to the worker to minimize strain, injury, and fatigue
- Addresses posture, workstation design, repetitive movement, load handling to prevent musculoskeletal disorders

## Occupational Health Services — Levels of Prevention
- **Primary prevention**: engineering controls (ventilation, substitution of hazardous material), administrative controls (job rotation, exposure limits), personal protective equipment (PPE)
- **Secondary prevention**: periodic medical examination, biological monitoring, early detection screening
- **Tertiary prevention**: treatment, rehabilitation, compensation for established occupational disease

*High-yield hierarchy of control: Elimination/Substitution > Engineering controls > Administrative controls > PPE (PPE is the last line of defense, not the first).*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Asbestosis affects lower lung zones; silicosis and CWP affect upper lung zones
- Silicosis has a strong association with pulmonary TB (silicotuberculosis); asbestosis has a strong association with mesothelioma
- Noise-induced hearing loss classically first affects the 4000 Hz frequency
- Byssinosis presents with symptoms worse at the start of the work week ("Monday morning fever")
- Lead poisoning: basophilic stippling, wrist drop, Burton's line, abdominal colic
- Hierarchy of hazard control: elimination > substitution > engineering controls > administrative controls > PPE
- ESI Act provides health/cash benefits to organized-sector workers; Factories Act regulates workplace safety/welfare conditions
- Organophosphate poisoning is biologically monitored via serum/RBC cholinesterase levels
