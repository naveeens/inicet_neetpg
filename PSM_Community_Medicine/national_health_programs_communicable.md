# National Health Programs Communicable

*Note: National program names, targets, and strategies in India are revised periodically (renaming, merging, target-year updates). Core strategies and classic exam facts below are stable; verify specific current targets/cutoffs/years against the latest Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) guideline before high-stakes use.*

## National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP, earlier RNTCP)
- Goal (per current knowledge): eliminate TB in India — target year has been stated as **2025** in national strategic plans; verify against latest MoHFW/Central TB Division communication
- Strategy historically built on **DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course)** — now integrated into NTEP's broader diagnostic-treatment-prevention framework
- Diagnosis: sputum microscopy (AFB smear), **CBNAAT/Truenat** (rapid molecular tests, also detect rifampicin resistance), chest X-ray
- Treatment regimen: multi-drug (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol — "HRZE") given as fixed-dose combinations; daily regimen is current standard (replaced earlier intermittent thrice-weekly regimen)
- **Category-based regimens** (historical RNTCP Cat I/II) have been phased out in favor of a unified daily regimen with drug-susceptibility testing–guided treatment — verify current regimen structure
- **Nikshay** portal — case-based, web-enabled surveillance system for TB notification (mandatory notification of all TB cases in India by both public and private practitioners)
- **Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana** — direct benefit transfer (nutritional support) to TB patients during treatment — verify current amount against latest guideline
- DR-TB (drug-resistant TB) management: MDR-TB (resistant to at least Isoniazid + Rifampicin), managed with second-line drugs (bedaquiline-based regimens increasingly standard) — verify current regimen

## National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP)
Covers: Malaria, Dengue, Chikungunya, Japanese Encephalitis, Lymphatic Filariasis, Kala-azar (Visceral Leishmaniasis)

| Disease | Vector | Key Control Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Malaria | *Anopheles* mosquito (female) | Early diagnosis + complete treatment (ACT for *P. falciparum*), vector control (IRS, ITNs/LLINs), larval source management |
| Dengue/Chikungunya | *Aedes aegypti* (day-biting) | Source reduction (breeding site elimination), no vaccine widely used in national program |
| Lymphatic Filariasis | *Culex* mosquito | Mass Drug Administration (MDA) with DEC ± Albendazole (± Ivermectin in some areas) in endemic districts |
| Kala-azar | Sandfly (*Phlebotomus*) | Elimination target pursued via case detection + vector control (IRS) + treatment (miltefosine/amphotericin B) |
| Japanese Encephalitis | *Culex* mosquito | JE vaccination in endemic districts + vector control |

*High-yield: Malaria — P. vivax has a shorter incubation but relapses (hypnozoites in liver); P. falciparum causes severe/complicated malaria (cerebral malaria) and does not relapse (no hypnozoite stage) but can recrudesce.*

## National AIDS Control Programme (NACP) — now under NACO
- Current phase strategy (verify latest phase number/name) focuses on "90-90-90" or updated **95-95-95** UNAIDS targets: 95% of people with HIV know their status, 95% of those diagnosed on ART, 95% of those on ART virally suppressed — verify current phase specifics
- Free ART provided through ART centers; **Test and Treat** policy — ART initiated immediately upon diagnosis regardless of CD4 count (removed earlier CD4-based eligibility threshold)
- PPTCT (Prevention of Parent-to-Child Transmission) — universal testing of pregnant women, ART to HIV-positive mothers to prevent vertical transmission
- Targeted interventions for high-risk groups (FSW, MSM, IDU, transgender)

## National Leprosy Eradication Programme (NLEP)
- India achieved "elimination" (prevalence <1/10,000) at national level in 2005; sub-national pockets persist — sub-national elimination remains ongoing goal
- Classification: **Paucibacillary (PB)** vs **Multibacillary (MB)** based on number of skin lesions/nerves involved and skin smear
- Treatment: **Multi-Drug Therapy (MDT)** — PB: Rifampicin + Dapsone; MB: Rifampicin + Clofazimine + Dapsone; duration differs (PB shorter, MB longer) — verify exact current duration against latest WHO/NLEP guideline
- Detection strategies: Active Case Detection Campaign, contact tracing, self-reporting

## National Vector-Borne + Zoonotic — Rabies
- **National Rabies Control Programme**: post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) with anti-rabies vaccine (intradermal/intramuscular regimens) ± Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) for category III bites/high-risk exposures
- Category of animal bite determines management (WHO categories I/II/III) — category III (transdermal bites/scratches, licks on broken skin/mucosa) needs vaccine + RIG

## Other Key Communicable Disease Programs (Brief)
- **Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP)** — surveillance backbone (see outbreak investigation notes)
- **Pulse Polio Programme** — India certified polio-free in 2014 (WHO South-East Asia Region); continues surveillance (AFP surveillance) and Polio National Immunization Days as part of the Global Polio Eradication Initiative's endgame strategy
- **National Programme for Yaws Eradication** — India declared yaws-free in 2006

## High-Yield One-Liners
- India was certified polio-free in 2014; AFP surveillance continues as a sensitive indicator for any polio reintroduction
- NTEP mandates notification of all TB cases (public and private sector) via the Nikshay portal
- Daily DOTS-based regimen with drug-susceptibility testing has replaced the older intermittent Category-based regimens in TB treatment (verify current specifics)
- ART is now initiated at diagnosis regardless of CD4 count under the "Test and Treat" policy
- Leprosy MDT differs by classification: PB gets Rifampicin+Dapsone; MB gets Rifampicin+Clofazimine+Dapsone
- P. falciparum malaria causes severe/cerebral malaria; P. vivax causes relapse due to hepatic hypnozoites
- Category III animal bites require both vaccine and rabies immunoglobulin
