# Immunization National Schedule Cold Chain

*Note: The Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) schedule (vaccines, ages, doses) is periodically revised by MoHFW/IAP. The structure and general timing below reflect standard teaching — verify exact current ages/doses/vaccine additions against the latest UIP/National Immunization Schedule before relying on specific cutoffs.*

## Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) — General Schedule Structure (per current knowledge)
| Age | Vaccines (typical UIP schedule — verify current specifics) |
|---|---|
| At birth | BCG, OPV-0, Hepatitis B (birth dose) |
| 6, 10, 14 weeks | OPV 1/2/3, Pentavalent (DPT+Hep B+Hib) 1/2/3, Rotavirus 1/2/3, fIPV (fractional IPV) doses |
| 9–12 months | Measles-Rubella (MR) 1st dose, Japanese Encephalitis (in endemic districts), Vitamin A 1st dose |
| 16–24 months | DPT booster 1, OPV booster, MR 2nd dose, Vitamin A 2nd dose |
| 5–6 years | DPT booster 2 |
| 10 years & 16 years | TT/Tdap (per current program — verify) |

*High-yield: Exact ages/doses (e.g., number of Vitamin A doses up to age 5, whether it's IPV or fIPV, PCV/Rotavirus rollout status in a given state) are frequently updated — always cross-check against the current year's official UIP schedule before an exam or clinical use.*

## Vaccine Types (Live vs Killed — classic distinction)
| Live Attenuated | Killed/Inactivated | Toxoid | Subunit/Conjugate |
|---|---|---|---|
| BCG, OPV, MR/MMR, Varicella, Rotavirus, Yellow Fever | IPV, Rabies (cell culture), Hepatitis A, Influenza (injectable) | Diphtheria toxoid (DT), Tetanus toxoid (TT) | Hepatitis B, Hib, Pneumococcal (PCV), HPV |

- Live vaccines: generally single dose or fewer doses needed, longer immunity, contraindicated in pregnancy and immunocompromised
- Killed/subunit vaccines: usually need multiple doses/boosters, safe in pregnancy and immunocompromised

## Cold Chain
- **Cold chain**: system of storing and transporting vaccines within recommended temperature range from manufacturer to point of use
- Storage temperatures (standard teaching):
  - **State/Regional vaccine stores**: −15°C to −25°C (for freeze-sensitive vaccines like OPV) and +2°C to +8°C (for others)
  - **PHC/Sub-centre level (ILR — Ice Lined Refrigerator, Deep Freezer)**: +2°C to +8°C for ILR (vaccine storage); Deep Freezer for ice pack freezing
  - **Vaccine carrier (field level)**: +2°C to +8°C

## Vaccine Vial Monitor (VVM)
- Heat-sensitive label on vaccine vial that changes color irreversibly with cumulative heat exposure — indicates whether vaccine is still usable
- Read the VVM before checking expiry date — a vaccine with an intact VVM but past a color-change point should be discarded, even if not expired

## "10 Cell Chain" / Vaccine Storage Hierarchy (Conceptual)
Manufacturer → Primary vaccine store (national/regional, walk-in coolers) → State vaccine store → Regional/District vaccine store → PHC/CHC (ILR) → Sub-centre → Session site (vaccine carrier with ice packs)

## Vaccines Most Sensitive to Heat vs Freezing (High-Yield Trap)
| Sensitive to Freezing (do NOT freeze) | Sensitive to Heat (most heat-labile) |
|---|---|
| DPT, TT, Hepatitis B, Pentavalent | OPV (most heat-sensitive) |

*High-yield: OPV is most sensitive to heat; DPT/TT/Hepatitis B are damaged by freezing — never place these directly against ice/frozen packs.*

## Adverse Events Following Immunization (AEFI)
- Reporting and surveillance system for any untoward medical event following immunization, not necessarily causally related to the vaccine
- Classified as: vaccine product-related, vaccine quality defect-related, immunization error-related (programmatic error), immunization anxiety-related, or coincidental
- BCG-related: local abscess/lymphadenitis if given with wrong technique/needle depth (should be given intradermally)

## Contraindications to Immunization (Key Exam Points)
- True contraindications: severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) to a previous dose or vaccine component, immunodeficiency (for live vaccines), pregnancy (for live vaccines)
- NOT contraindications (common exam traps): mild illness/low-grade fever, diarrhea, malnutrition, being on antibiotics, prematurity, family history of adverse events, allergy not related to vaccine components

## High-Yield One-Liners
- OPV is the vaccine most sensitive to heat; DPT/TT/Hep B are damaged by freezing — never allow direct contact with ice
- Vaccine Vial Monitor (VVM) must be checked before administering any vaccine, in addition to expiry date
- Live vaccines are contraindicated in pregnancy and immunocompromised states; killed/subunit vaccines are generally safe
- Mild fever, diarrhea, and malnutrition are NOT contraindications to immunization — common exam trap
- BCG must be given intradermally; wrong technique risks local abscess/regional lymphadenitis
- Cold chain temperature at peripheral storage (ILR) and vaccine carriers is maintained at +2°C to +8°C
