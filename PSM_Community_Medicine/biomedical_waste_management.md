# Biomedical Waste Management

*Note: Biomedical Waste Management Rules (India) have been amended multiple times (notably 2016, with subsequent amendments in 2018/2019/2021). The color-coding, category numbers, and container material below reflect standard teaching per the Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (as amended) — verify exact current category list/color scheme against the latest official rule text, as amendments have adjusted category numbering and consolidated categories over time.*

## Categories and Color Coding (Per Current Knowledge — Verify Latest Rule)
| Color Code | Waste Type | Examples | Treatment/Disposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yellow** | Human anatomical waste, soiled waste, expired/discarded medicines, chemical waste, microbiology/biotechnology waste | Body parts, tissues, dressings, blood-soaked items, cytotoxic drugs | Incineration or plasma pyrolysis (non-plastic); deep burial only where incineration not available |
| **Red** | Contaminated (non-sharp) recyclable waste | Tubing, IV sets, catheters, urine bags, gloves (non-metallic) | Autoclaving/microwaving/hydroclaving followed by shredding, then recycling |
| **White (translucent)** | Sharps — waste requiring puncture/cutting-resistant containers | Needles, syringes (with fixed needles), scalpels, blades | Autoclaving/dry heat sterilization followed by shredding/mutilation and encapsulation or disposal in secure landfill |
| **Blue** | Glassware and metallic body implants | Broken/discarded glass vials, ampoules, metallic implants | Disinfection followed by disposal at secure landfill or recycling |

*High-yield: The color-coding scheme has been revised over successive amendments (e.g., prior 2016 scheme had 4 categories with different groupings than the earlier 1998 rules' 10 categories) — always confirm current scheme in force before an exam cycle.*

## General Principles of Segregation
- Segregation at the point of generation is mandatory — mixing of biomedical waste with general municipal solid waste is prohibited
- Different color-coded bags/containers/bins used based on waste category as above
- Bags should be filled to only about three-fourths capacity to allow secure sealing

## Treatment Methods
| Method | Used For | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Incineration** | Anatomical waste, pathological waste, cytotoxic drugs | High-temperature combustion; destroys pathogens completely; generates ash |
| **Autoclaving** | Microbiological/lab waste, sharps, some plastics | Steam sterilization under pressure |
| **Microwaving/Hydroclaving** | Similar to autoclaving | Heat-based disinfection |
| **Plasma pyrolysis** | Alternative to incineration for certain plastic/anatomical waste | High-temperature plasma-based destruction with lower emissions |
| **Chemical disinfection** | Liquid waste, some sharps | Disinfectant treatment before disposal |
| **Deep burial** | Only in rural/remote areas with no access to incineration facility, and only for permitted categories | Buried in pits meeting specified depth/lining criteria |

## Occupational Hazards of Improper BMW Handling
- Needlestick injuries → transmission risk of Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV to healthcare/sanitation workers
- Improper segregation/disposal → environmental contamination, spread of infection to community (rag-pickers, waste handlers)

## Post-Exposure Management for Needlestick Injury (Occupational Exposure)
- Immediate first aid: wash the wound with soap and water (do not scrub aggressively, do not apply antiseptics/bleach)
- Report the exposure; risk assessment based on source patient's status
- Post-exposure prophylaxis considered for Hepatitis B (vaccine/immunoglobulin per vaccination status) and HIV (ART-based PEP, ideally started within 72 hours, as early as possible)

## Regulatory Bodies
- **State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)** — grants authorization to healthcare facilities for biomedical waste generation, handling, and disposal in India
- Healthcare establishments are required to maintain records and submit annual reports on biomedical waste generated and disposed

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Yellow bag/container is for human anatomical, soiled, and pathological/cytotoxic waste, mainly disposed by incineration
- Red is for contaminated recyclable non-sharp waste, treated by autoclaving/microwaving then recycled
- White (translucent), puncture-proof containers are used for sharps like needles and syringes
- Blue is for glassware and metallic implants
- Segregation at the source of generation is the cornerstone principle of biomedical waste management
- Deep burial for biomedical waste is permitted only in specific rural/remote settings lacking incineration facilities, and only for allowed waste categories
- Needlestick injury first aid is simple wash with soap and water — do not squeeze or apply antiseptic/bleach to the wound
- State Pollution Control Boards are the regulatory authority for biomedical waste authorization in India
