# Demography Vital Statistics

## Key Rates and Formulas
| Measure | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Crude Birth Rate (CBR)** | (Total live births in a year / Mid-year population) × 1000 | Per 1000 population per year |
| **Crude Death Rate (CDR)** | (Total deaths in a year / Mid-year population) × 1000 | Per 1000 population per year |
| **General Fertility Rate** | (Live births / Women aged 15–49 years) × 1000 | Refines CBR to reproductive-age women |
| **Total Fertility Rate (TFR)** | Sum of age-specific fertility rates across reproductive age span | Average number of children a woman would have in her lifetime at current fertility rates |
| **Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)** | (Deaths <1 year of age / Live births) × 1000 | Per 1000 live births; sensitive indicator of overall health/development status |
| **Neonatal Mortality Rate (NMR)** | (Deaths <28 days / Live births) × 1000 | |
| **Perinatal Mortality Rate** | (Stillbirths + deaths in first 7 days / Total births [live + still]) × 1000 | Reflects both obstetric and neonatal care quality |
| **Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR)** | (Maternal deaths / Live births) × 100,000 | Per 1,00,000 live births; deaths during pregnancy/childbirth/42 days postpartum from pregnancy-related causes |
| **Under-5 Mortality Rate (U5MR)** | (Deaths <5 years / Live births) × 1000 | Key child health/MDG-SDG indicator |

*High-yield distinction: MMR is a RATIO (denominator = live births, a proxy for pregnancies, not the population at risk) — technically not a true "rate," though conventionally still called MMR.*

## Growth and Balance Indicators
- **Natural increase** = CBR − CDR
- **Population growth rate** accounts for natural increase + net migration
- **Doubling time** ≈ 70/annual growth rate (%) (rule of 70)
- **Dependency ratio** = [(Population <15 yrs + Population >65 yrs) / Population 15–64 yrs] × 100 — measures economic burden of dependents on working-age group

## Replacement Level Fertility
- **TFR ≈ 2.1** is generally considered replacement level (accounts for mortality before reproductive age and slight male excess at birth) — verify current national TFR against latest NFHS/SRS data as India's TFR has been declining and may already be at/below replacement in many states

## Demographic Transition Theory (Stages)
| Stage | Birth Rate | Death Rate | Population Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (High stationary) | High | High | Stable/slow (high birth balanced by high death) |
| 2 (Early expanding) | High | Falling (rapidly) | Rapid growth |
| 3 (Late expanding) | Falling | Low | Growth slowing |
| 4 (Low stationary) | Low | Low | Stable/near-zero growth |
| 5 (Declining, proposed) | Very low | Low (may rise as population ages) | Population decline |

- India is generally taught as transitioning through stage 3 towards stage 4, with substantial inter-state variation (southern states more advanced in transition than several northern states)

## Census and Sample Registration System (SRS)
- **Census**: complete enumeration of population, conducted every 10 years in India (most recent completed census was 2011; subsequent census has faced delays — verify current status)
- **SRS (Sample Registration System)**: large-scale demographic survey providing annual estimates of birth/death rates, IMR, etc. between censuses — considered the most reliable ongoing source for vital rates in India
- **NFHS (National Family Health Survey)**: periodic large-scale household survey providing indicators on fertility, family planning, maternal/child health, nutrition — verify latest round (NFHS-5 was conducted 2019–21; a subsequent round may follow per periodic schedule)

## Sex Ratio
- **Sex ratio** in India conventionally = number of females per 1000 males
- **Child sex ratio** (0–6 years) is a specific, closely monitored indicator of sex-selective practices — verify latest census/SRS figures

## Population Pyramid
- Graphical representation of age-sex distribution
- **Broad base, narrow apex** = young/expanding population (high birth rate) — typical of developing countries historically
- **Rectangular/barrel shape** = stationary population with low birth and death rates
- **Constrictions at base** = declining birth rate (aging population trend)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- IMR is considered the most sensitive indicator of a community's overall health and socioeconomic development
- MMR denominator is live births (a proxy for pregnancies), expressed per 1,00,000 live births
- TFR ~2.1 is considered replacement level fertility
- SRS is the most reliable continuous source of vital statistics in India between decennial censuses
- Doubling time of population ≈ 70 / annual growth rate (%)
- Broad-based population pyramid indicates a young, rapidly growing population; narrowing base indicates declining fertility
- Perinatal mortality rate combines stillbirths and early neonatal deaths, reflecting both obstetric and neonatal care quality
