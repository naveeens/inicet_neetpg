# Head Neck Tumors

## Relevant Anatomy
- Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) arises from mucosal lining of oral cavity, oropharynx, hypopharynx, larynx, nasopharynx, and paranasal sinuses.
- **Waldeyer's ring** (lymphoid tissue: adenoids, palatine tonsils, lingual tonsil) — sites of lymphoma as well as SCC (tonsil).
- Cervical lymph node **levels I–VI** (surgical/oncologic classification) guide neck dissection planning:
  - Level I: submental/submandibular
  - Level II–IV: upper, mid, lower jugular chain (along internal jugular vein)
  - Level V: posterior triangle
  - Level VI: central compartment (pretracheal, paratracheal — thyroid cancer drainage)
- Lymphatic drainage patterns predict primary site from a neck node (occult primary workup): e.g., level II from oropharynx/oral cavity, level VI from thyroid.

## General Risk Factors for Head & Neck SCC
- **Tobacco** (smoking and smokeless/chewing) and **alcohol** — strong synergistic risk, most important overall
- Betel nut/paan chewing (oral submucous fibrosis → oral SCC) — very relevant in Indian population
- HPV (esp. type 16) — increasingly recognized cause of **oropharyngeal (tonsil/base of tongue) SCC**, better prognosis than HPV-negative disease
- EBV — strongly associated with **nasopharyngeal carcinoma**
- Chronic sun exposure — lip SCC (lower lip)
- Plummer-Vinson syndrome (iron deficiency, esophageal web, dysphagia) — increased risk of post-cricoid/hypopharyngeal SCC

## Oral Cavity Carcinoma
- Most common site in India: **lateral border of tongue and buccal mucosa** (linked to tobacco chewing habits)
- Precursor/premalignant lesions: leukoplakia (white patch), erythroplakia (red patch — higher malignant potential), oral submucous fibrosis (irreversible, progressive; strongly linked to areca nut)
- Presents as non-healing ulcer, pain, referred otalgia (via lingual/glossopharyngeal nerve), difficulty in speech/swallowing, trismus (if buccal/retromolar with pterygoid involvement)
- Management: primarily surgical (wide local excision with margins) ± neck dissection ± adjuvant radiotherapy/chemoradiotherapy for advanced/high-risk features (positive margins, extranodal extension, perineural/lymphovascular invasion)

## Oropharyngeal Carcinoma (tonsil, base of tongue, soft palate)
- Rising incidence linked to **HPV** in non-smokers/younger patients — HPV-positive tumors have better response to treatment and prognosis than HPV-negative (tobacco-related)
- Often presents with a painless neck mass (nodal metastasis) even before obvious primary symptoms, due to rich lymphatics
- p16 immunohistochemistry used as surrogate marker for HPV status
- Management: surgery (transoral robotic/laser) or (chemo)radiotherapy depending on stage and HPV status; treatment de-escalation strategies being studied in HPV-positive disease

## Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC)
- Strongly associated with **EBV**; higher incidence in Southern Chinese/Southeast Asian populations; also linked to salted fish/nitrosamine intake
- Classic presentation (often first sign is nodal, not local):
  - Painless upper neck mass (nodal metastasis) — most common presenting complaint
  - Nasal obstruction/epistaxis
  - Unilateral serous otitis media/conductive hearing loss (tumor blocking Eustachian tube opening in fossa of Rosenmüller) — high-yield: unilateral OME in an adult, especially from endemic regions, must raise suspicion for NPC
  - Cranial nerve palsies (skull base erosion, foramen lacerum) — especially CN VI (abducens)
  - **Trotter's triad** (conductive deafness + palatal immobility/trigeminal pain + trismus) with parapharyngeal extension
- Investigations: nasopharyngoscopy with biopsy, MRI (soft tissue/skull base extent), EBV serology/titers
- Management: **radiotherapy is the mainstay** (NPC is highly radiosensitive) ± concurrent chemotherapy for advanced disease; surgery has a limited role

## Hypopharyngeal Carcinoma
- Post-cricoid region carcinoma linked to **Plummer-Vinson syndrome** especially in women
- Presents late (silent region) — dysphagia, odynophagia, referred otalgia, hoarseness (laryngeal invasion), neck node (often bilateral due to rich, crossing lymphatics) — poor prognosis due to late presentation

## Salivary Gland Tumors
- **Parotid gland**: most common site of salivary gland tumors overall; most parotid tumors are **benign** — **pleomorphic adenoma** is the most common benign tumor (mixed tumor, recurrence risk if capsule breached during surgery — treated by superficial/total parotidectomy preserving facial nerve, NOT enucleation)
- **Warthin's tumor** (papillary cystadenoma lymphomatosum): second most common benign parotid tumor; bilateral in some cases; strongly associated with smoking; classic on imaging/scan — hot on Tc-99m pertechnetate scan
- **Mucoepidermoid carcinoma** = most common **malignant** salivary gland tumor overall (and most common malignant tumor in parotid and in children)
- **Adenoid cystic carcinoma**: most common malignant tumor of **submandibular and minor salivary glands**; classic for **perineural invasion** and late distant metastasis (lung) with indolent but relentless local course
- General rule: smaller the gland, higher the chance a tumor is malignant (parotid tumors mostly benign; minor salivary gland tumors mostly malignant)
- Facial nerve palsy with a parotid mass strongly suggests malignancy with nerve invasion (benign tumors virtually never cause facial palsy)
- FNAC is used for preoperative diagnosis; **incision biopsy is avoided in parotid masses (risk of tumor seeding/facial nerve injury)** — comprehensive excision (with nerve preservation as feasible) is standard

## Thyroid Nodule / Carcinoma (Head & Neck Surgical Relevance)
- Papillary carcinoma = most common thyroid malignancy overall, spreads via lymphatics (nodal), excellent prognosis
- FNAC (Bethesda system) is first-line investigation for a thyroid nodule

## Approach to Neck Mass / TNM Staging Principles
- "Rule of 80" (teaching heuristic): in adult neck mass, ~80% of persistent masses are neoplastic, ~80% of neoplastic are malignant, ~80% of malignant are metastatic (not primary), and ~80% of metastases are from a primary above the clavicle
- TNM staging integrates: **T** (primary tumor size/extent), **N** (nodal involvement — number, size, laterality, extranodal extension), **M** (distant metastasis)
- Triple assessment for any suspicious neck mass: clinical exam, imaging (USG/CT/MRI), and tissue diagnosis (FNAC preferred initial test; core/open biopsy if inconclusive)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Tobacco + alcohol = strongest synergistic risk factors for head and neck SCC
- Lateral border of tongue and buccal mucosa are the most common oral cavity SCC sites in India (chewing tobacco/paan-linked)
- Erythroplakia has higher malignant potential than leukoplakia
- HPV-positive oropharyngeal SCC has better prognosis than HPV-negative
- EBV is strongly linked to nasopharyngeal carcinoma; radiotherapy is the mainstay of treatment (highly radiosensitive)
- Unilateral serous otitis media in an adult should raise suspicion for nasopharyngeal carcinoma until proven otherwise
- Pleomorphic adenoma = most common benign salivary tumor; mucoepidermoid carcinoma = most common malignant salivary tumor overall
- Adenoid cystic carcinoma is notorious for perineural invasion and late hematogenous (lung) metastasis
- Facial nerve palsy with a parotid mass suggests malignant invasion
- Never do an incision biopsy on a parotid mass — FNAC is the preferred preoperative test
