# Ear Anatomy Hearing Loss

## Relevant Anatomy
**External ear**: Pinna + external auditory canal (EAC, ~2.5 cm) — outer 1/3 cartilaginous (skin with hair/glands, boils occur here), inner 2/3 bony (skin tightly adherent, very sensitive). EAC has two narrowings: bony-cartilaginous junction and isthmus (near tympanic membrane) — common sites for foreign body/wax impaction.

**Middle ear (tympanic cavity)**: Contains ossicles — **malleus, incus, stapes** (smallest bone in body = stapes; longest = malleus... "MIS" from lateral to medial). Tensor tympani (nerve to medial pterygoid, CN V3) and stapedius (facial nerve, CN VII) dampen loud sounds (acoustic reflex). Eustachian tube connects middle ear to nasopharynx — equalizes pressure; shorter, wider, more horizontal in children → more prone to acute otitis media.

**Facial nerve** runs through middle ear (horizontal/tympanic segment) close to oval window and horizontal semicircular canal — vulnerable in middle ear surgery/CSOM erosion.

**Inner ear**: Bony labyrinth (cochlea, vestibule, semicircular canals) containing membranous labyrinth (perilymph outside, endolymph inside). **Cochlea** = hearing (organ of Corti on basilar membrane); base detects high frequency, apex detects low frequency. **Vestibular apparatus** (utricle, saccule, semicircular canals) = balance.

**Blood supply**: Inner ear via labyrinthine artery (branch of AICA) — end artery, no collaterals → ischemia causes sudden sensorineural hearing loss.

## Sound Conduction Pathway
Sound → pinna → EAC → TM vibration → ossicular chain (malleus-incus-stapes) → oval window → perilymph (scala vestibuli) → basilar membrane displacement → organ of Corti hair cells → CN VIII → cochlear nuclei → auditory pathway → auditory cortex (temporal lobe).

- Middle ear acts as an **impedance-matching transformer** (air to fluid) via area ratio of TM to stapes footplate and ossicular lever action — loss of this mechanism (e.g., TM perforation) causes conductive hearing loss.

## Classification of Hearing Loss
| Type | Site of Lesion | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Conductive | External/middle ear | Wax, TM perforation, otosclerosis, otitis media, ossicular discontinuity |
| Sensorineural (SNHL) | Cochlea or CN VIII/central pathway | Presbycusis, noise-induced, ototoxicity, Meniere's, acoustic neuroma, congenital |
| Mixed | Both | Advanced otosclerosis, chronic suppurative otitis media with cochlear involvement |

## Tuning Fork Tests (High-Yield, use 512 Hz fork)
| Test | Method | Normal | Conductive HL | SNHL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rinne** | Compare air conduction (AC) vs bone conduction (BC) | AC > BC (Rinne positive) | BC > AC (**Rinne negative**) | AC > BC (Rinne positive, but both reduced) |
| **Weber** | Fork on vertex/forehead, ask where sound is louder | Midline (no lateralization) | Lateralizes to **affected (worse-hearing) ear** | Lateralizes to **normal (better-hearing) ear** |
| **Absolute Bone Conduction (ABC)** | Compare patient's BC to examiner's (normal) BC | Equal | Normal (equal) | **Reduced** compared to examiner |
| **Schwabach** | Compare patient's BC duration to examiner's | Equal (~ 20 sec) | Prolonged | Reduced |

- **False negative Rinne**: In severe unilateral SNHL, bone-conducted sound crosses skull and is heard by contralateral normal ear → mimics conductive pattern; confirmed by masking the opposite ear (Barany noise box).
- Weber lateralizes to the *worse* ear in conductive loss (ambient noise masking is reduced there) and to the *better* ear in SNHL (nonfunctional cochlea on affected side).

## Pure Tone Audiometry (PTA)
- Plots hearing threshold (dB) across frequencies (250–8000 Hz) for AC (○ right, × left) and BC ([ right, ] left).
- **Air-bone gap (ABG)** present → conductive component; ABG >10 dB is significant.
- Grades of hearing loss: Normal 0–20 dB (varies by source, commonly <25 dB); Mild 26–40; Moderate 41–55; Moderately severe 56–70; Severe 71–90; Profound >90 dB.
- **Carhart notch**: dip in bone conduction at 2000 Hz — classic finding in **otosclerosis** (artifact from stapes fixation, not true SNHL).

## Other Investigations
- **Impedance audiometry (Tympanometry)**: assesses middle ear compliance/pressure.
  - Type A: normal
  - Type B: flat curve — middle ear effusion (OME) or perforation
  - Type C: negative peak pressure — Eustachian tube dysfunction/retracted TM
- **Speech audiometry**: Speech Discrimination Score (SDS) — poor SDS disproportionate to PTA loss suggests retrocochlear (CN VIII) pathology e.g. acoustic neuroma.
- **BERA (Brainstem Evoked Response Audiometry)**: objective test, used in infants/malingering, and for retrocochlear lesion localization; also used for **neonatal hearing screening** along with OAE.
- **OAE (Otoacoustic Emissions)**: screens cochlear (outer hair cell) function — used in universal newborn hearing screening.
- **Stapedial reflex/Acoustic reflex**: absent in significant conductive loss and in facial nerve palsy proximal to the branch to stapedius.

## Common Causes by Type
| Conductive | Sensorineural |
|---|---|
| Impacted cerumen, foreign body | Presbycusis (age-related, high frequency first) |
| TM perforation (trauma, infection) | Noise-induced hearing loss (classic notch at **4000 Hz**) |
| Acute/Chronic otitis media, OME | Ototoxic drugs (aminoglycosides, loop diuretics, cisplatin, salicylates) |
| **Otosclerosis** (stapes fixation; young adults, F>M, worsens in pregnancy, may have paracusis willisii) | Meniere's disease |
| Ossicular discontinuity | Mumps, measles, meningitis (congenital/acquired) |
| Congenital atresia | Acoustic neuroma (unilateral SNHL + tinnitus) |
| | Sudden SNHL (viral, vascular — emergency, treat with steroids ASAP) |

## Management Principles
- Conductive loss: treat underlying cause (wax removal, myringoplasty for perforation, stapedotomy for otosclerosis, hearing aids)
- SNHL: hearing aids if residual hearing useful; **cochlear implant** if severe-profound bilateral SNHL with poor benefit from hearing aids (esp. prelingual children — ideally implanted early, by 1–2 years, for best speech development)
- Sudden SNHL = **otologic emergency** — oral/intratympanic corticosteroids within 2 weeks (ideally 72 hours) of onset

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Rinne negative = conductive hearing loss (bone conduction better than air conduction in that ear)
- Weber lateralizes to the bad ear in conductive loss, to the good ear in sensorineural loss
- Carhart's notch (dip at 2 kHz on bone conduction) = otosclerosis
- Noise-induced hearing loss classically notches at 4000 Hz
- Universal newborn hearing screening uses OAE and BERA
- Stapes = smallest bone in the body; malleus = largest ossicle
- Labyrinthine artery is an end artery — no collateral supply, hence vulnerability to sudden ischemic SNHL
- Sudden sensorineural hearing loss is an emergency — start steroids as early as possible
