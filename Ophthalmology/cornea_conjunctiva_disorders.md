# Cornea Conjunctiva Disorders

## Conjunctivitis — Types & Differentiation
| Feature | Bacterial | Viral | Allergic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discharge | Purulent/mucopurulent | Watery | Stringy/ropy mucoid |
| Onset | Acute, often unilateral → bilateral | Acute, usually bilateral | Chronic/recurrent, bilateral |
| Lymphadenopathy | Uncommon | Preauricular LN common (esp. adenoviral) | Absent |
| Itching | Minimal | Minimal | Prominent (hallmark) |
| Common organism | *S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, S. aureus* | Adenovirus (most common) | Type I hypersensitivity |
| Treatment | Topical antibiotics (e.g., moxifloxacin) | Supportive; cold compress; avoid steroids unless severe | Topical antihistamine/mast cell stabilizer (olopatadine); avoid rubbing |

- **Ophthalmia neonatorum**: conjunctivitis in first 28 days of life
  - Chemical (silver nitrate, day 1) → *Gonococcal* (day 2–5, hyperacute, sight-threatening, needs systemic + topical treatment) → *Chlamydial* (day 5–14, most common overall, follicular)
  - Prophylaxis: 1% tetracycline/erythromycin ointment or povidone-iodine at birth
- **Trachoma** (*Chlamydia trachomatis* serotypes A, B, Ba, C) — leading infectious cause of blindness worldwide
  - WHO grading (FISTO): **F**ollicles, **I**ntense inflammation, **S**carring, **T**richiasis, **O**pacity (corneal)
  - Herbert's pits = pathognomonic (limbal follicles healing with scarring)
  - Complications: entropion → trichiasis → corneal scarring → blindness
  - Treatment: Azithromycin (single dose) — mainstay of **SAFE** strategy (Surgery, Antibiotics, Facial cleanliness, Environmental improvement)

## Corneal Ulcer
- **Bacterial keratitis**: Pseudomonas (rapid, associated with contact lens use), Pneumococcus (serpiginous ulcer, associated with dacryocystitis)
- **Fungal keratitis**: history of vegetative/agricultural trauma; slow progressive course; **satellite lesions**, feathery margins, hypopyon may be present — Fusarium, Aspergillus common
- **Acanthamoeba keratitis**: contact lens wearers (especially with tap water exposure); severe pain out of proportion to signs; ring infiltrate; radial keratoneuritis
- **Herpes simplex keratitis (HSK)**: **dendritic ulcer** (branching, with terminal bulbs) stains brightly with fluorescein; recurrent; reduced corneal sensation
  - Treatment: topical acyclovir/ganciclovir gel; **steroids contraindicated in active epithelial dendritic disease** (worsens ulcer) — steroids used cautiously only in stromal (disciform) keratitis under antiviral cover
- **Herpes zoster ophthalmicus (HZO)**: involves V1 (ophthalmic division of trigeminal)
  - **Hutchinson's sign** = vesicles on tip of nose → strong predictor of ocular involvement
  - Treatment: oral acyclovir/valacyclovir within 72 h of rash onset

## Keratoconus
- Progressive bilateral (usually asymmetric) corneal ectasia — cone-shaped cornea, thinning
- Associated with: allergic eye disease/atopy, eye rubbing, Down syndrome, connective tissue disorders
- Signs: **Munson's sign** (V-shaped conjunctival bulge on downgaze), **Vogt's striae**, **Fleischer ring** (iron deposition at cone base), scissoring reflex on retinoscopy
- Diagnosis: corneal topography (gold standard) — shows inferior steepening
- Management: spectacles/RGP contact lenses (mild-moderate) → **corneal collagen cross-linking (CXL)** to halt progression → penetrating keratoplasty (advanced/scarred)
- **Acute hydrops**: sudden stromal edema from Descemet's membrane rupture — presents with sudden pain, photophobia, decreased vision

## Pterygium & Pinguecula
- **Pterygium**: fibrovascular triangular growth of conjunctiva onto cornea, typically nasal side; associated with UV exposure/outdoor occupation; excise if encroaching visual axis/progressive (recurrence common, esp. without conjunctival autografting)
- **Pinguecula**: yellowish nodule on bulbar conjunctiva near limbus (nasal > temporal), does NOT encroach cornea — no treatment needed unless inflamed

## Xerophthalmia (Vitamin A Deficiency) — WHO Classification
| Stage | Finding |
|---|---|
| XN | Night blindness |
| X1A | Conjunctival xerosis |
| X1B | **Bitot's spots** (foamy, triangular, keratinized conjunctival patches — pathognomonic) |
| X2 | Corneal xerosis |
| X3A | Corneal ulceration/keratomalacia <1/3 corneal surface |
| X3B | Keratomalacia ≥1/3 corneal surface |
| XS | Corneal scar |
| XF | Xerophthalmic fundus |

- Treatment: Vitamin A 200,000 IU oral (100,000 IU if <1 year) on day 0, day 1, and day 14 (WHO protocol)
- Earliest reversible symptom: night blindness

## Dry Eye Disease (Keratoconjunctivitis Sicca)
- Sjögren syndrome = classic association (with dry mouth — sicca complex)
- Schirmer's test: <10 mm in 5 min = abnormal (severe if <5 mm)
- Tear film breakup time (TBUT) <10 sec = abnormal
- Treatment: artificial tears, punctal plugs, topical cyclosporine

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common cause of infectious blindness worldwide: **trachoma**
- Hyperacute purulent conjunctivitis in a neonate on day 2–5 → think gonococcal → same-day ophthalmology emergency
- Dendritic ulcer + reduced corneal sensation → HSV keratitis; never give steroids alone in active epithelial disease
- Hutchinson's sign (nose-tip vesicles) predicts corneal/ocular involvement in HZO
- Fleischer ring + Vogt's striae + Munson's sign → keratoconus triad
- Bitot's spots = pathognomonic for vitamin A deficiency; earliest symptom is night blindness
- Fungal corneal ulcer → satellite lesions, feathery margins, history of vegetable matter injury
- Acanthamoeba keratitis: contact lens wearer + pain out of proportion to clinical signs
