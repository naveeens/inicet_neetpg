# Refractive Errors

## Definition
Refractive error = mismatch between the eye's optical power and axial length such that parallel light rays do not focus precisely on the retina in an unaccommodated state (ametropia). Emmetropia = normal, no refractive error.

## Types

### Myopia (Near-sightedness)
- Light focuses **in front of** the retina; distant objects blurred, near vision typically better
- Causes: **axial myopia** (eyeball too long — most common), curvature myopia (steep cornea/lens)
- Correction: **concave (minus/diverging) lens**
- Types: simple (physiological, usually stable), pathological/degenerative myopia (progressive, high axial length, associated with retinal complications)
- Complications of high myopia: retinal detachment, lattice degeneration, posterior staphyloma, myopic choroidal neovascularization, glaucoma, **early cataract**
- Fundus finding in high myopia: **myopic crescent** (peripapillary atrophy), tessellated/tigroid fundus

### Hypermetropia/Hyperopia (Far-sightedness)
- Light focuses **behind** the retina; near vision blurred more than distant; accommodation compensates partially (esp. in young)
- Causes: **axial hypermetropia** (eyeball too short — most common), curvature (flat cornea), index (low refractive index e.g. in some cataracts — but note nuclear sclerotic cataract usually causes myopic shift)
- Correction: **convex (plus/converging) lens**
- Associations: shallow anterior chamber → predisposes to **angle closure glaucoma**; uncorrected hypermetropia in children → **accommodative esotropia**, amblyopia

### Astigmatism
- Unequal corneal (or lenticular) curvature in different meridians → light does not focus at a single point
- Types: regular (correctable with cylindrical lens — with-the-rule most common, more corneal curvature vertically) vs irregular (e.g., keratoconus — needs RGP contact lens)
- Correction: **cylindrical lens**

### Presbyopia
- Age-related physiological loss of accommodation due to ↓ lens elasticity (not a true refractive error, but a common exam topic)
- Onset typically ~40 years; near point recedes progressively
- Correction: convex lens (reading glasses) added to distance correction; near add typically increases with age (roughly +1.00 D at 40 up to +3.00 D by 60, individual variation)

## Amblyopia ("Lazy Eye")
- Reduced best-corrected visual acuity without detectable organic pathology, due to abnormal visual development in early childhood (functional, potentially reversible if caught early — **critical period up to ~7–9 years**)
- Causes:
  - **Strabismic** — misalignment leads to suppression of the deviating eye (most common type)
  - **Refractive/Anisometropic** — significant difference in refractive error between eyes
  - **Ametropic** — high bilateral uncorrected refractive error
  - **Deprivation** — form vision deprivation (e.g., congenital cataract, ptosis) — most severe/worst prognosis type
- Diagnosis: reduced vision not improving with pinhole/refraction to expected level, presence of a risk factor
- Treatment: correct underlying cause + **occlusion therapy (patching the good/normal eye)** to force use of amblyopic eye; earlier treatment = better outcome; atropine penalization as alternative to patching

## Accommodation & Convergence
- Near reflex triad: **accommodation (lens rounding via ciliary muscle contraction/zonular relaxation), convergence (medial rectus), and miosis (pupillary constriction)**
- Presbyopia = failure of accommodation component

## Strabismus (Squint) — Brief Overview
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Esotropia | Inward deviation; associated with uncorrected hypermetropia (accommodative esotropia) |
| Exotropia | Outward deviation |
| Comitant (concomitant) squint | Deviation same in all directions of gaze — typically childhood/refractive origin |
| Incomitant (non-concomitant) squint | Deviation varies with gaze direction — suggests neurological/muscle paresis (CN III/IV/VI palsy) |

- Cover-uncover test distinguishes latent (phoria) vs manifest (tropia) squint
- Any childhood squint warrants prompt refraction and correction to prevent amblyopia

## Correction Methods
- Spectacles (most common, no risk), contact lenses (better for high anisometropia, aphakia, keratoconus)
- **LASIK (Laser-Assisted in Situ Keratomileusis)**: reshapes corneal stroma under a flap; suitable for stable refractive error, adequate corneal thickness
- **PRK (Photorefractive Keratectomy)**: no flap, epithelium removed; used when cornea too thin for LASIK
- Refractive surgery contraindications: unstable refraction, keratoconus/ectatic disorders, thin cornea, active ocular disease
- Intraocular lens (phakic IOL) or refractive lens exchange for very high refractive errors unsuitable for corneal laser surgery

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Myopia → concave (diverging/minus) lens; Hypermetropia → convex (converging/plus) lens; Astigmatism → cylindrical lens
- Axial length changes are the most common cause of both myopia and hypermetropia
- High myopia predisposes to retinal detachment, myopic degeneration, and early cataract
- Uncorrected hypermetropia in a shallow AC eye predisposes to angle closure glaucoma
- Deprivation amblyopia (e.g., congenital cataract) has the worst prognosis among amblyopia types
- Amblyopia treatment window is time-critical — occlusion therapy works best before ~7-9 years of age
- Near reflex triad: accommodation, convergence, miosis
- Comitant squint (same in all gazes) = childhood/refractive; incomitant (varies with gaze) = neurogenic/paralytic — always evaluate incomitant squint for cranial nerve palsy
- Presbyopia is due to loss of lens elasticity/accommodation, not a classic refractive error
