# Glaucoma

## Definition
Progressive optic neuropathy characterized by retinal ganglion cell death and characteristic optic disc/visual field changes, most often (but not always) associated with raised intraocular pressure (IOP). Normal IOP: 10–21 mmHg (mean ~15–16 mmHg).

## Classification
| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Primary Open Angle Glaucoma (POAG) | Open angle but ↑ resistance to aqueous outflow at trabecular meshwork |
| Primary Angle Closure Glaucoma (PACG) | Mechanical blockage of angle by peripheral iris (pupillary block most common) |
| Secondary glaucoma | Due to another ocular/systemic condition (uveitis, trauma, steroids, neovascular, lens-induced) |
| Congenital/Developmental glaucoma | Maldevelopment of angle structures (trabeculodysgenesis) |

## Primary Open Angle Glaucoma (POAG)
- Most common type of glaucoma overall; bilateral, chronic, asymptomatic until advanced (**"sneak thief of sight"**)
- Risk factors: age >40, family history, myopia, diabetes, black race (higher prevalence in African descent populations), corticosteroid use
- Gonioscopy: open angle (Grade III–IV / Shaffer)
- Diagnosis requires 2 of 3: raised IOP, glaucomatous disc changes, glaucomatous visual field defects (can have normal-tension glaucoma with normal IOP)

### Optic Disc Changes (Glaucomatous Cupping)
- ↑ Cup:disc ratio (normal <0.3; suspicious if >0.6 or asymmetry >0.2 between eyes)
- Notching of neuroretinal rim (**ISNT rule violation** — normally rim thickness Inferior > Superior > Nasal > Temporal; loss of this pattern is early sign)
- **Bayonetting sign**, nasalization of vessels, **laminar dots sign**, disc hemorrhages (splinter hemorrhages — sign of progression)

### Visual Field Defects (in order of progression)
1. Paracentral scotoma
2. **Bjerrum's/arcuate scotoma** (arcs around fixation, respecting horizontal meridian — corresponds to nerve fiber bundle damage)
3. **Seidel's scotoma** (extension of arcuate scotoma)
4. Ring scotoma
5. **Nasal step** (Roenne's step)
6. Tunnel vision (only small central island of vision remains) → temporal crescent may persist last

### Management of POAG
| Drug class | Example | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prostaglandin analogs | Latanoprost, Bimatoprost, Travoprost | ↑ uveoscleral outflow | **First-line**; once daily; side effects: iris pigmentation, eyelash growth, hyperemia |
| Beta-blockers | Timolol | ↓ aqueous production | Avoid in asthma/COPD, heart block, bradycardia |
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors | Dorzolamide (topical), Acetazolamide (oral) | ↓ aqueous production | Oral CAI: metabolic acidosis, hypokalemia, renal stones, paresthesia |
| Alpha-2 agonists | Brimonidine | ↓ production + ↑ outflow | Avoid in young children (CNS depression risk) |
| Miotics | Pilocarpine | ↑ outflow via ciliary muscle contraction (opens trabecular meshwork) | Now largely historical/limited use |
| Rho-kinase inhibitors | Netarsudil | ↑ trabecular outflow | Newer class |

- Laser trabeculoplasty (SLT/ALT) or trabeculectomy (filtering surgery) if medical therapy fails/non-compliant

## Primary Angle Closure Glaucoma (PACG)
- Mechanism: pupillary block → iris bombé → peripheral iris apposition to trabecular meshwork → outflow obstruction
- Risk factors: hypermetropia (shallow anterior chamber), female sex, age >40, family history, shallow AC, plateau iris, Asian/Inuit ethnicity

### Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma (Ophthalmic Emergency)
- Precipitated by pupillary dilation (dim light, mydriatics, stress, anticholinergics)
- Clinical features: sudden severe ocular pain, headache, **colored halos around lights**, nausea/vomiting, blurred vision
- Signs: markedly ↑ IOP (often >40–50 mmHg), **mid-dilated fixed/sluggish pupil**, hazy/steamy cornea (epithelial edema), shallow AC, congested eye (circumcorneal congestion), stony-hard eye on palpation
- Gonioscopy: closed angle (once corneal edema settles); fellow eye also has narrow angle — always examine both eyes

### Management of Acute Attack
1. **Medical (initial, to break the attack)**: IV Mannitol/oral Glycerol (osmotic agent), topical Timolol, topical Pilocarpine (once IOP falls enough for sphincter to respond — miotic ineffective if iris ischemic), oral/IV Acetazolamide
2. **Definitive**: **Laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI)** — treatment of choice, once eye is quiet; done prophylactically in the fellow (asymptomatic) eye too since it shares the same anatomical risk
3. If LPI not feasible/fails → surgical peripheral iridectomy or trabeculectomy

## Congenital Glaucoma (Buphthalmos)
- Due to trabeculodysgenesis; presents in infancy
- Classic triad: **epiphora (watering), photophobia, blepharospasm**
- Signs: corneal enlargement/haze, **Haab's striae** (breaks in Descemet's membrane), buphthalmos ("ox eye" — enlarged globe), ↑ corneal diameter (>12 mm suspicious)
- Associated: Sturge-Weber syndrome, neurofibromatosis, Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome, aniridia, rubella
- Treatment: primarily surgical — **goniotomy/trabeculotomy** (first-line); trabeculectomy if fails

## Secondary Glaucomas — High-Yield Associations
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Neovascular glaucoma | Rubeosis iridis; follows CRVO/proliferative diabetic retinopathy; very poor prognosis |
| Phacomorphic glaucoma | Intumescent/mature cataract pushes iris forward → angle closure |
| Phacolytic glaucoma | Leaking lens proteins from hypermature cataract → open angle blocked by macrophages |
| Pigmentary/pseudoexfoliation glaucoma | Pigment/exfoliative material clogs trabecular meshwork |
| Steroid-induced glaucoma | Chronic steroid use (topical > systemic) → ↓ outflow — mimics POAG |
| Uveitic glaucoma | Trabeculitis, synechiae, steroid response |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Glaucoma = 2nd leading cause of blindness worldwide (after cataract); leading cause of **irreversible** blindness
- POAG is asymptomatic until late — "sneak thief of sight"; central vision preserved till very late (tunnel vision)
- First visual field defect in glaucoma: paracentral/Bjerrum (arcuate) scotoma
- ISNT rule violation on disc examination = early sign of glaucomatous damage
- Acute angle closure: mid-dilated fixed pupil + steamy cornea + halos + severe pain — ophthalmic emergency
- Definitive treatment for angle closure glaucoma = laser peripheral iridotomy (do prophylactically in fellow eye too)
- Congenital glaucoma triad: epiphora, photophobia, blepharospasm; Haab's striae characteristic
- Prostaglandin analogs are first-line medical therapy for POAG (once-daily dosing improves compliance)
- Avoid systemic beta-blockers/timolol in asthma, COPD, and heart block
- Pilocarpine ineffective in acute angle closure if iris sphincter is ischemic (need to lower IOP first)
