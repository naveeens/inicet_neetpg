# Retina Diabetic Retinopathy

## Diabetic Retinopathy — Pathophysiology
Chronic hyperglycemia → pericyte loss + basement membrane thickening → capillary microaneurysms, occlusion → retinal ischemia → VEGF release → neovascularization. Leading cause of blindness in working-age adults (20–64 years) worldwide.

## Classification
### Non-Proliferative DR (NPDR)
| Severity | Findings |
|---|---|
| Mild | Microaneurysms only |
| Moderate | Microaneurysms + dot-blot hemorrhages + hard exudates + cotton wool spots (some, not severe) |
| Severe | **4-2-1 rule**: severe hemorrhages in 4 quadrants, OR venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, OR intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) in ≥1 quadrant |

### Proliferative DR (PDR)
- Neovascularization at disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE) — response to VEGF-driven retinal ischemia
- Complications: **vitreous hemorrhage**, tractional retinal detachment, **neovascular glaucoma** (rubeosis iridis)
- High-risk PDR (needs urgent treatment): NVD >1/3 disc area, or any NVD with vitreous hemorrhage, or NVE >1/2 disc area with vitreous hemorrhage

## Diabetic Macular Edema (DME)
- Can occur at any stage of DR (NPDR or PDR) — most common cause of visual impairment in diabetics
- **Clinically significant macular edema (CSME)** criteria (any one):
  - Retinal thickening within 500 μm of fovea
  - Hard exudates within 500 μm of fovea with adjacent thickening
  - Zone of thickening ≥1 disc area, part of which is within 1 disc diameter of fovea
- Diagnosis: OCT (gold standard for monitoring), fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA)

## Management
| Stage | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild-moderate NPDR | Glycemic/BP/lipid control; monitor (annual dilated exam) |
| Severe NPDR / early PDR | **Panretinal photocoagulation (PRP)** — laser to peripheral retina to reduce VEGF drive |
| High-risk PDR | PRP ± anti-VEGF (bevacizumab/ranibizumab/aflibercept) |
| CSME/DME | **Anti-VEGF intravitreal injection = first-line** (superior to focal laser for center-involving DME); focal/grid laser for non-center-involving |
| Vitreous hemorrhage/tractional RD | Pars plana vitrectomy |

- Screening: all Type 2 DM at diagnosis, Type 1 DM within 5 years of diagnosis, then annually; more frequent in pregnancy
- Rapid tight glycemic control can transiently worsen retinopathy (still continue — long-term benefit outweighs)

## Hypertensive Retinopathy
### Keith-Wagener-Barker Classification
| Grade | Findings |
|---|---|
| I | Mild arteriolar narrowing/attenuation |
| II | Grade I + AV nicking (copper wiring) |
| III | Grade II + flame-shaped hemorrhages, cotton wool spots, hard exudates |
| IV | Grade III + **papilledema** (malignant hypertension) |

- Silver wiring = more severe/chronic narrowing than copper wiring
- Macular star = exudates radiating from fovea (severe HTN)

## Retinal Vascular Occlusions
| Feature | CRAO | CRVO |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Sudden, painless, profound vision loss | Sudden, painless, variable severity |
| Fundus | **Cherry-red spot** at macula, pale/opaque retina, boxcarring of blood in vessels | **"Blood and thunder"** fundus — diffuse hemorrhages, dilated tortuous veins, disc edema |
| Cause | Embolism (carotid, cardiac), GCA in elderly | Hypercoagulable states, glaucoma, HTN, DM |
| Complication | Poor visual prognosis; rule out GCA (ESR/CRP) | **Neovascular glaucoma** (90-day glaucoma) especially in ischemic CRVO |
| Treatment | Emergency — ocular massage, anterior chamber paracentesis, IOP-lowering, (limited efficacy; window <4–6h) | Anti-VEGF for macular edema; PRP if neovascularization develops |

- Branch retinal artery/vein occlusion (BRAO/BRVO) = same pathophysiology, sectoral involvement, better prognosis

## Retinal Detachment (RD)
| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Rhegmatogenous | Retinal break/tear (most common) → vitreous fluid seeps beneath retina; associated with myopia, lattice degeneration, trauma, prior cataract surgery |
| Tractional | Fibrovascular membranes pull retina (e.g., proliferative diabetic retinopathy) |
| Exudative/Serous | Fluid accumulation without a break (e.g., choroidal tumor, severe uveitis, malignant HTN) — shifting fluid on positional change |

- Symptoms: sudden flashes (photopsia), floaters, progressive curtain-like/shadow field loss
- **Shafer's sign** (tobacco dust — pigment cells in anterior vitreous) suggests retinal break
- Treatment: Rhegmatogenous → scleral buckling/pneumatic retinopexy/vitrectomy with laser/cryo to seal break; urgent if macula still attached ("macula-on" = emergency to prevent macular involvement)

## Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP)
- Risk factors: prematurity (<32 weeks/<1500 g most important), uncontrolled supplemental oxygen therapy
- Screening: all infants <30–32 weeks gestation or <1500 g birth weight, starting at 4 weeks postnatal age (or 31 weeks postmenstrual age)
- International Classification: Zone (I central, most severe if involved) and Stage (I–V, with **plus disease** = vascular dilation/tortuosity indicating activity)
- Treatment: laser photocoagulation of avascular retina for threshold disease; anti-VEGF (bevacizumab) increasingly used for zone I disease

## Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Dry (non-exudative) | More common (~90%); drusen, RPE atrophy; slow progressive central vision loss |
| Wet (exudative/neovascular) | Choroidal neovascularization; sudden vision loss, metamorphopsia; more severe but treatable |
- Amsler grid used for monitoring/self-detection of metamorphopsia
- Treatment: Anti-VEGF (ranibizumab/aflibercept/bevacizumab) for wet AMD; AREDS2 vitamin formulation (vit C, E, zinc, copper, lutein/zeaxanthin) may slow progression of intermediate/advanced dry AMD

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Diabetic retinopathy = leading cause of blindness in working-age adults
- 4-2-1 rule defines severe NPDR (high risk of progression to PDR within 1 year)
- Anti-VEGF is first-line for center-involving diabetic macular edema and wet AMD
- PRP is treatment of choice for high-risk PDR (reduces VEGF drive from ischemic peripheral retina)
- CRAO → cherry-red spot at macula; CRVO → "blood and thunder" fundus
- CRVO's most feared complication: neovascular glaucoma (90-day glaucoma)
- Rhegmatogenous RD strongly associated with high myopia and lattice degeneration
- Shafer's sign (tobacco dust in vitreous) indicates a retinal break
- ROP screening based on birth weight/gestational age, not chronological age alone
- Dry AMD = drusen/slow progression; Wet AMD = neovascularization/sudden loss — both use Amsler grid for monitoring
