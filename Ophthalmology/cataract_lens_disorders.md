# Cataract Lens Disorders

## Definition
Any opacification of the crystalline lens. Leading cause of blindness worldwide (reversible, treatable — biggest contributor to global blindness burden overall, though glaucoma leads irreversible blindness).

## Classification by Morphology
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Nuclear cataract** | Most associated with **myopic shift** (index myopia, due to ↑ refractive index of nucleus); yellow-brown discoloration; most common in elderly, age-related |
| **Cortical cataract** | Spoke-like/wedge opacities from periphery; associated with **glare** and difficulty in bright light |
| **Posterior subcapsular cataract (PSC)** | Classic in **steroid use, diabetes, and radiation exposure**; causes early/disproportionate difficulty with near vision and glare/bright light (affects visual axis early) |
| **Christmas tree cataract** | Polychromatic crystalline deposits — myotonic dystrophy |
| **Sunflower cataract** | Wilson's disease (copper deposition) |

## Age-Related (Senile) Cataract — Stages
1. Lamellar separation of fibers
2. **Incipient** — spokes/wedges, vision minimally affected
3. **Immature (intumescent)** — partial opacity, lens swollen (risk of phacomorphic glaucoma)
4. **Mature** — complete opacity, lens normal size, no red reflex
5. **Hypermature (Morgagnian)** — liquefied cortex, nucleus sinks inferiorly; risk of **phacolytic glaucoma** (leaking proteins trigger macrophage response clogging trabecular meshwork)

## Congenital Cataract
- Causes: TORCH infections (**rubella classic** — also associated with congenital cataract + PDA + deafness), galactosemia, Down syndrome, maternal diabetes, hereditary
- Types: zonular/lamellar (most common), polar, total
- Urgency: **visually significant congenital cataract must be operated within first 6–8 weeks of life** to prevent irreversible amblyopia (deprivation amblyopia) — critical period of visual development
- Leukocoria (white pupillary reflex) in an infant — differentials: congenital cataract, retinoblastoma, ROP, persistent fetal vasculature, Coats disease, toxocariasis — **retinoblastoma must always be ruled out first** (life-threatening)

## Other Etiological Types
| Type | Association |
|---|---|
| Traumatic cataract | Blunt/penetrating trauma; **Vossius ring** (imprint of iris pigment on anterior lens capsule) |
| Diabetic (true diabetic) cataract | Rare, rapidly progressive, bilateral, "snowflake" cortical opacities in young uncontrolled diabetics; osmotic changes via sorbitol pathway |
| Complicated cataract | Secondary to intraocular disease (uveitis — classically starts posterior subcapsular; also high myopia, retinitis pigmentosa) |
| Drug-induced | Corticosteroids (PSC), phenothiazines, miotics (long-term pilocarpine) |
| Electric/lightning cataract | Star/flower shaped anterior subcapsular opacity |

## Clinical Features
- Painless, progressive diminution of vision
- Glare (esp. cortical, PSC), monocular diplopia, colored halos, **"second sight"** (transient myopic shift improving near vision in early nuclear sclerosis, before eventual worsening)
- Absent red reflex / **leukocoria** in mature cataract
- Diagnosis: slit-lamp exam, distant direct ophthalmoscopy showing black area against red reflex

## Management
- **Definitive treatment = surgery** — no medical treatment reverses cataract
- Indication: visually significant cataract affecting patient's functional needs (not solely visual acuity number)
- **Phacoemulsification with posterior chamber IOL implantation = current standard of care** (small incision, faster recovery)
- Other techniques: Extracapsular cataract extraction (ECCE), Manual Small Incision Cataract Surgery (MSICS — widely used in high-volume settings/India), Intracapsular cataract extraction (ICCE, largely historical)
- Congenital cataract: lensectomy + anterior vitrectomy (often without primary IOL in very young infants) + early optical correction/amblyopia therapy

### Post-Operative Complications
| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Posterior capsular opacification (PCO)** | Most common late complication; "secondary cataract"; treated with **Nd:YAG laser posterior capsulotomy** |
| Endophthalmitis | Most feared complication; sudden severe pain, redness, hypopyon, vision loss — emergency (intravitreal antibiotics ± vitrectomy) |
| Cystoid macular edema (Irvine-Gass syndrome) | Presents weeks after surgery |
| Retained lens matter, IOL dislocation, corneal edema | — |

## Ectopia Lentis (Lens Subluxation/Dislocation)
| Cause | Direction/Association |
|---|---|
| **Marfan syndrome** | Superotemporal dislocation (classic) |
| **Homocystinuria** | Inferonasal dislocation (classic) — opposite direction from Marfan, key exam differentiator |
| Trauma | Any direction |
| Weill-Marchesani syndrome | Microspherophakia, lens may dislocate anteriorly |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Cataract = leading cause of blindness worldwide overall (reversible)
- Nuclear cataract → myopic shift; PSC → early glare/near-vision difficulty; classic in steroid use and diabetes
- Congenital cataract surgery must be done within 6–8 weeks of life to prevent deprivation amblyopia
- Leukocoria in a child → always rule out retinoblastoma first
- Vossius ring = pathognomonic sign of blunt trauma to the lens
- Hypermature (Morgagnian) cataract risks phacolytic glaucoma; intumescent/immature cataract risks phacomorphic glaucoma
- Most common late complication of cataract surgery = posterior capsular opacification, treated with Nd:YAG capsulotomy
- Marfan = superotemporal lens dislocation; Homocystinuria = inferonasal lens dislocation
- Endophthalmitis post-cataract surgery = ophthalmic emergency
