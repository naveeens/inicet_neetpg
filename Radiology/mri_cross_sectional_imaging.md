# MRI & Cross-Sectional Imaging

## Basic Sequence Logic
| Sequence | Water | Fat | Key Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1-weighted** | Dark | Bright | Anatomy, fat, subacute blood (methemoglobin) |
| **T2-weighted** | Bright | Bright | Pathology (edema, most lesions are bright) |
| **FLAIR** | Dark (CSF suppressed) | Bright | Periventricular/cortical lesions (MS plaques) obscured by CSF on plain T2 |
| **DWI** | Restricted diffusion = bright | — | **Acute infarct** (bright within minutes), abscess, epidermoid, CJD |
| **ADC map** | Corresponding dark = true restriction | — | Confirms true diffusion restriction (dark ADC) vs T2 shine-through (bright ADC despite bright DWI) |
| **GRE/SWI** | — | — | "Blooming" of hemorrhage, calcification, iron/hemosiderin — most sensitive for microbleeds |
| **STIR** | Bright | Suppressed | Bone marrow edema, MSK fluid-sensitive imaging without contrast fat suppression artifact |

*High-yield DWI/ADC rule: True acute infarct = DWI bright + ADC dark (restricted). T2 shine-through (e.g., chronic infarct, some tumors) = DWI bright but ADC also bright/iso.*

## Classic Neuro-MRI Named Signs
| Sign | Description | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Dawson's fingers** | Periventricular ovoid lesions oriented perpendicular to the ventricles, along medullary veins | **Multiple sclerosis** |
| **Central vein sign** | A single vein running through the center of a white matter lesion | Helps distinguish MS plaques from other white matter lesions (vascular, migraine) |
| **Hot cross bun sign** | Cruciform T2 hyperintensity in the pons from selective degeneration of pontocerebellar fibers | **Multiple system atrophy (cerebellar type, MSA-C)** |
| **Hummingbird sign (penguin sign)** | Midbrain atrophy with flattening giving a hummingbird silhouette on midsagittal T1 | **Progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP)** |
| **Mickey Mouse sign** | Thin cerebral peduncles ("mouse ears") with reduced midbrain AP diameter | PSP (axial correlate of hummingbird sign) |
| **Face of the giant panda sign** | Midbrain T2 hyperintensity sparing the red nucleus and lateral SN, giving a "panda face" | **Wilson's disease** |
| **Eye of the tiger sign** | Central hyperintensity within a hypointense (iron-laden) globus pallidus | **PKAN / Hallervorden–Spatz disease** |
| **Pulvinar sign** | Bilateral symmetric T2/DWI hyperintensity in the pulvinar (posterior) thalamus | **Variant CJD** |
| **Cortical ribboning** | Gyriform DWI hyperintensity along the cerebral cortex | Sporadic CJD |
| **Molar tooth sign** | Elongated, thickened superior cerebellar peduncles + deep interpeduncular fossa + vermian hypoplasia on axial midbrain images | **Joubert syndrome** |
| **Dural tail sign** | Enhancing thickened dura tapering away from an extra-axial mass | **Meningioma** (not specific, also seen in other dural pathology) |
| **Target sign** | Central enhancing scolex within a cystic lesion | **Neurocysticercosis** |
| **Empty delta sign** | Triangular filling defect in a dural sinus on post-contrast imaging | Venous sinus thrombosis |
| **Butterfly glioma** | Bilateral, symmetric infiltration across the corpus callosum | **Glioblastoma multiforme** |

## Ring-Enhancing Lesion — Differential (classic MCQ list)
**M-A-G-I-C-A-L**: **M**etastasis, **A**bscess, **G**lioblastoma, **I**nfarct (subacute), **C**ontusion, **A**IDS-related (toxoplasmosis, lymphoma), **L**ymphoma/demyelination (tumefactive MS)
- Rim of ring-enhancing abscess is typically **thin, smooth, and hypointense on T2** (unlike thick irregular malignant rims) — DWI restriction inside the cavity favors abscess over necrotic tumor

## Spinal Cord Signs
| Sign | Description | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Owl's eyes sign** | Bilateral symmetric hyperintensity of the anterior horns on axial cord images | Anterior spinal artery infarct, poliomyelitis |
| **Snake eyes sign** | Similar bilateral anterior horn hyperintensity, chronic | Chronic compressive/spondylotic myelomalacia |
| **Trident sign** | Y/trident-shaped central signal change on axial pons | Central pontine myelinolysis |

## Renal & Abdominal Cross-Sectional Signs
| Sign | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Claw sign** | Normal organ parenchyma "claws" around an exophytic mass | Confirms organ of origin (renal vs adrenal/retroperitoneal mass) |
| **Beak sign** | Organ parenchyma tapers to a beak at the edge of a mass arising from within it | Same purpose — localizing mass origin (renal, splenic, hepatic) |
| **India ink artifact (chemical shift artifact)** | Signal dropout at fat-water interfaces on opposed-phase imaging | Helps confirm intralesional fat, e.g. differentiating **AML from RCC** |

## MSK MRI Highlights
- **Meniscal tear** — linear/globular high T2 signal extending to the articular surface (a signal not reaching the surface is a degenerative change, not a tear)
- **ACL tear** — discontinuity of fibers, "empty notch sign," anterior tibial translation, bone bruise pattern (kissing contusions of lateral femoral condyle + posterolateral tibia)
- **Segond fracture** (associated avulsion at lateral tibial condyle on radiograph) — strongly associated with **ACL tear**
- **Bone marrow edema** — best seen on **STIR/fat-suppressed T2** — earliest sign of stress fracture, osteomyelitis, AVN before radiographs turn positive

## High-Yield One-Liners
- True acute infarct: DWI bright + ADC dark; T2 shine-through: DWI bright + ADC bright/normal
- SWI/GRE is the most sensitive sequence for old hemorrhage/microbleeds and diffuse axonal injury — CT is often falsely normal in DAI
- Dawson's fingers + central vein sign = classic MS plaque morphology on FLAIR/T2
- Hot cross bun sign = MSA-C; Hummingbird sign = PSP; Panda sign = Wilson's disease; Eye of the tiger = PKAN — classic movement-disorder MRI sign set
- Pulvinar sign = variant CJD (vs cortical ribboning in sporadic CJD)
- Molar tooth sign = Joubert syndrome
- Claw/beak sign and chemical-shift (India ink) artifact both help characterize/localize renal masses on cross-sectional imaging
