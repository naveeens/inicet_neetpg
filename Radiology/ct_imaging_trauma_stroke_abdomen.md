# CT Imaging: Trauma, Stroke, Abdomen

## Modalities Overview
- **NCCT head** — first investigation in acute stroke (to exclude hemorrhage before thrombolysis) and in head trauma
- **CTA/CTP** — vessel occlusion + penumbra assessment for mechanical thrombectomy candidacy
- **CT (whole-body trauma protocol)** — polytrauma work-up; NCCT + contrast-enhanced phases
- **CECT abdomen (triple phase)** — arterial, portal venous, delayed — for solid organ injury/tumor characterization

## Acute Ischemic Stroke — Early CT Signs
| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hyperdense MCA sign** | Hyperattenuating thrombus within the MCA — earliest sign, can appear within minutes |
| **Insular ribbon sign** | Loss of grey-white differentiation at the insular cortex — one of the earliest parenchymal signs of MCA infarct |
| **Obscured lentiform nucleus sign** | Loss of definition of the lentiform nucleus from early cytotoxic edema |
| **Loss of grey-white differentiation** | Generalized early ischemic change |
| **Fogging phenomenon** | Subacute infarct (~1–3 weeks) transiently appears pseudo-normal on NCCT due to resolving edema before cavitation — pitfall on follow-up imaging |

- **ASPECTS (Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score)** — 10-point NCCT score in MCA territory; score ≤7 associated with worse outcome/higher hemorrhage risk after thrombolysis
- **DWI-ADC on MRI remains more sensitive** than NCCT for hyperacute infarct, but NCCT is done first to exclude bleed

## Intracranial Hemorrhage
| Sign | Description | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Spot sign** | Foci of contrast extravasation within an intracerebral hematoma on CTA | Predicts **hematoma expansion** — poor prognosis marker |
| **Swirl sign** | Areas of low/mixed attenuation within a hyperdense hematoma (unretracted clot) | Active/ongoing bleeding (used in both intracranial and splenic/hepatic hematomas) |

### Epidural vs Subdural Hematoma (classic differentiator)
| Feature | Epidural Hematoma | Subdural Hematoma |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Biconvex / lentiform | Crescent-shaped |
| Crosses suture lines? | **No** | **Yes** |
| Crosses midline/falx? | Yes (can) | No (limited by falx/tentorium) |
| Cause | Middle meningeal artery tear, associated with **temporal bone fracture** | Bridging vein tear |

## Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis
| Sign | Modality | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Dense triangle sign** | NCCT | Hyperdense clotted superior sagittal sinus (triangular on axial) |
| **Empty delta sign** | Contrast CT / MR venography | Triangular filling defect (non-enhancing thrombus) surrounded by enhancing dural walls in the sinus |
| **Cord sign / string sign** | NCCT / MRV | Linear hyperdensity along a thrombosed cortical vein |

## Traumatic Brain Injury
- **Epidural hematoma** — lentiform, does not cross sutures; classically from **pterion fracture → MMA injury**; "lucid interval" clinically
- **Subdural hematoma** — crescentic, crosses sutures, does not cross midline; bridging vein rupture, common in elderly/atrophy and shaken baby syndrome
- **Contrecoup injury** — injury opposite the site of direct impact
- **Diffuse axonal injury** — best seen on **MRI (SWI/GRE)** as multiple petechial hemorrhages at grey-white junction, corpus callosum, brainstem — CT often looks deceptively normal
- **Hinge fracture** — skull base fracture through sella, high mortality

## Blunt Chest/Abdominal Trauma
| Sign | Description | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Seatbelt sign** | Linear ecchymosis across abdomen in seatbelt pattern (clinical) | High association with **bowel/mesenteric injury, Chance fracture** |
| **Sentinel clot sign** | Highest-density (most adherent) clot localizes closest to the bleeding source in hemoperitoneum | Localizes site of solid-organ injury |
| **Contrast blush** | Focal area of high attenuation approaching arterial density within/around a solid organ | **Active arterial extravasation** — spleen/liver/kidney injury, may need embolization |
| **Hypoperfusion complex (shock bowel)** | Diffusely fluid-filled, enhancing, thickened bowel loops with a small "flat" IVC and hyperenhancing adrenals/kidneys | Pediatric hypovolemic shock |
| **Calcium/intimal sign** | Displaced (medially placed) intimal calcification from the aortic wall | Traumatic aortic injury |

## Pneumoperitoneum
| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| **Rigler sign (double wall sign)** | Both serosal (outer) and mucosal (inner) surfaces of bowel wall visualized due to free air on both sides |
| **Football sign** | Massive pneumoperitoneum outlining the abdomen as a large oval lucency — classically in **neonatal perforation** |
| **Falciform ligament sign** | Free air outlining the falciform ligament as a linear density |

## Bowel & Mesenteric Signs
| Sign | Description | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Whirl sign** | Swirled appearance of mesenteric vessels/fat twisting around a point | **Volvulus** (midgut, sigmoid, cecal) |
| **Coffee bean sign** | Two adjacent dilated bowel loops resembling a coffee bean (plain film) | **Sigmoid volvulus** |
| **Bird's beak sign** | Smooth tapering narrowing at the point of torsion on contrast studies | Volvulus (sigmoid/cecal) |
| **Target/doughnut sign** | Concentric rings of bowel-within-bowel on cross-section | **Intussusception**; also generic bowel wall thickening |
| **Comb sign** | Engorged, prominent straight vasa recta supplying an inflamed bowel segment | **Active Crohn's disease** |
| **Fat halo sign** | Low-attenuation submucosal fat ring in thickened bowel wall | Chronic Crohn's disease / chronic UC |
| **Thumbprinting** | Thick, rounded, thumb-like indentations of bowel wall from submucosal edema/hemorrhage | **Ischemic colitis** |
| **Pneumatosis intestinalis** | Gas within the bowel wall | Bowel ischemia/necrosis (also benign causes) |
| **Portal venous gas** | Branching lucencies extending to liver periphery | Ominous — bowel necrosis/ischemia |
| **String of pearls sign** | Row of small gas bubbles trapped between valvulae conniventes in fluid-filled dilated loops (erect film) | **Small bowel obstruction** |
| **Small bowel feces sign** | Particulate, feces-like material within dilated small bowel | Chronic/high-grade small bowel obstruction |

## Hepatobiliary, Renal & Pancreatic Signs
| Sign | Description | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **WES sign (wall-echo-shadow)** | Gallbladder wall + stone echo + posterior shadow (mainly USG but referenced on CT correlation) | Contracted, stone-filled gallbladder |
| **Rim sign** | Gas or discontinuity in gallbladder wall | Gangrenous cholecystitis |
| **Misty mesentery / sandwich sign** | Hazy increased attenuation of mesenteric fat, engulfing vessels | Mesenteric edema/panniculitis, lymphoma |
| **Cortical rim sign** | Thin peripheral rim of preserved enhancing cortex with non-enhancing infarcted parenchyma | Renal infarction |
| **Sentinel loop sign** | Localized dilated bowel loop adjacent to inflamed organ | Acute pancreatitis (also other localized peritonitis) |
| **Colon cut-off sign** | Abrupt gaseous distension ending at splenic flexure | Acute pancreatitis (spasm of descending colon from adjacent inflammation) |

- **Balthazar (CT severity index) grading** — used for acute pancreatitis severity/necrosis on CECT

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Hyperdense MCA sign is the earliest visible NCCT sign of ischemic stroke (within minutes), but insular ribbon sign is the earliest **parenchymal** sign
- ASPECTS ≤7 predicts poor outcome / higher hemorrhagic transformation risk after thrombolysis
- Epidural = biconvex, does not cross sutures; Subdural = crescent, crosses sutures but not midline — classic MCQ differentiator
- Spot sign on CTA = risk of hematoma expansion in intracerebral hemorrhage
- Empty delta sign = venous sinus thrombosis on contrast CT/MRV
- Whirl sign = volvulus (mesenteric twist); Rigler sign = pneumoperitoneum
- Contrast blush on trauma CT = active bleeding requiring urgent intervention/embolization
- Comb sign + fat halo sign = classic CT findings differentiating active from chronic Crohn's disease
