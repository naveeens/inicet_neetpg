# Mycology

## Overview
Medically important fungi are grouped as: **Superficial/cutaneous mycoses** (dermatophytes), **Subcutaneous mycoses**, **Systemic dimorphic fungi**, and **Opportunistic fungi** (Candida, Aspergillus, Cryptococcus, Mucor) — the last group being the highest-yield for PG exams given rising immunocompromised populations.

## Candida
- Yeast, part of normal flora (mouth, GI tract, vagina); dimorphic (yeast + pseudohyphae)
- *C. albicans*: germ tube positive (differentiates from most non-albicans Candida)
- Diseases: oral thrush (white curd-like plaques), vulvovaginal candidiasis (cottage-cheese discharge), esophageal candidiasis (AIDS-defining if CD4 low), candidemia/invasive candidiasis (ICU, catheters, immunosuppressed), diaper rash, chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis
- Diagnosis: KOH mount/Gram stain showing budding yeast + pseudohyphae; culture on Sabouraud's dextrose agar; germ tube test for *C. albicans*
- Treatment: Fluconazole (mucosal), Echinocandins (caspofungin — invasive candidiasis, first-line in many ICU settings), Amphotericin B (severe systemic)
- Non-albicans species (*C. glabrata*, *C. krusei*) — increasing fluconazole resistance, esp. *C. krusei* (intrinsically resistant)

## Aspergillus
- Mold, septate hyphae with **acute-angle (45°) branching** (key differentiator from Mucor)
- *A. fumigatus* most common pathogenic species
- Clinical spectrum:
  - **Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA)**: hypersensitivity reaction in asthma/cystic fibrosis patients, ↑ IgE, central bronchiectasis
  - **Aspergilloma ("fungus ball")**: colonizes pre-existing lung cavities (old TB cavities classic), crescent sign on imaging
  - **Invasive aspergillosis**: angioinvasive, in severely immunocompromised (neutropenic, transplant) — halo sign on CT
- Diagnosis: Galactomannan antigen (serum/BAL), culture, biopsy showing septate acute-angle branching hyphae
- Treatment: Voriconazole (drug of choice for invasive aspergillosis), Amphotericin B alternative; surgery for aspergilloma if symptomatic

## Mucor / Rhizopus (Mucormycosis)
- Mold, **non-septate (aseptate) hyphae with wide-angle (90°) branching** — key differentiator from Aspergillus
- Risk factors: uncontrolled diabetes (esp. with ketoacidosis), neutropenia, immunosuppression, iron overload
- **Rhino-orbital-cerebral mucormycosis**: classic in poorly controlled diabetics — black necrotic eschar on palate/nasal turbinates, rapidly angioinvasive, can extend to orbit/brain
- Notably increased incidence associated with COVID-19 (steroid use + diabetes) — recent high-yield clinical association
- Treatment: Aggressive surgical debridement + Amphotericin B (liposomal); poor prognosis if delayed

## Cryptococcus neoformans
- Encapsulated yeast, found in soil/pigeon droppings
- **India ink negative staining**: shows clear halo (capsule) around yeast cells — classic diagnostic image
- Disease: **Cryptococcal meningitis** — major opportunistic infection in AIDS (CD4 <100), subacute presentation
- Diagnosis: CSF India ink stain, **Cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) test** (latex agglutination/lateral flow — more sensitive than India ink), culture on Sabouraud's agar (mucoid colonies)
- Treatment: Induction with Amphotericin B + Flucytosine, followed by Fluconazole consolidation/maintenance

## Dimorphic Fungi (Systemic Mycoses)
| Organism | Disease | Geographic Association | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Histoplasma capsulatum* | Histoplasmosis | Ohio/Mississippi river valleys (USA); bat/bird droppings, caves | Intracellular yeast in macrophages; mimics TB (pulmonary, disseminated in AIDS) |
| *Coccidioides immitis* | Coccidioidomycosis (Valley fever) | Southwestern USA (San Joaquin Valley) | Spherules containing endospores in tissue |
| *Blastomyces dermatitidis* | Blastomycosis | Central/Eastern USA (river valleys) | Broad-based budding yeast |
| *Paracoccidioides brasiliensis* | Paracoccidioidomycosis | South/Central America | "Pilot's wheel" / "Mariner's wheel" multiple budding yeast |
| *Sporothrix schenckii* | Sporotrichosis ("Rose gardener's disease") | Worldwide, soil/plant matter (thorn pricks) | Subcutaneous nodules along lymphatics (sporotrichoid spread), cigar-shaped yeast |

- All dimorphic fungi exist as **mold in environment (25°C)** and **yeast in tissue (37°C)** — hallmark of the dimorphic fungi group
- Most systemic dimorphic fungi are not endemic to India (important negative fact — exam trap); *Sporothrix* is a global/India-relevant exception

## Dermatophytes (Superficial Mycoses)
- Genera: *Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton* — infect keratinized tissue (skin, hair, nails), collectively cause "tinea/ringworm" infections
- Diagnosis: KOH mount of skin/hair/nail scraping showing branching hyphae; Wood's lamp (fluorescence for some *Microsporum* species); culture on Sabouraud's agar with lactophenol cotton blue mount for species ID
- Treatment: Topical azoles for localized tinea; oral terbinafine/griseofulvin for nail/hair involvement or extensive disease

## Pneumocystis jirovecii
- Historically classified as protozoan, now known to be a fungus (based on molecular/genetic studies)
- Causes **Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP)** — classic AIDS-defining illness at CD4 <200
- Cannot be cultured; diagnosis via **silver stain (GMS)** on bronchoalveolar lavage/induced sputum showing characteristic cup-shaped/crushed ping-pong ball cysts; also PCR, DFA
- Treatment: Cotrimoxazole (TMP-SMX) — also used for prophylaxis at CD4 <200

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Acute-angle (45°) septate branching = *Aspergillus*; wide-angle (90°) aseptate branching = Mucor/Rhizopus — classic histology differentiator
- India ink negative stain showing capsule halo = *Cryptococcus neoformans*; CrAg test more sensitive than India ink
- Rhino-orbital-cerebral mucormycosis classically occurs in uncontrolled diabetics (esp. DKA) — black necrotic eschar is the clue
- Germ tube test positive = *Candida albicans*
- Aspergilloma classically colonizes old TB cavities — "crescent sign" on imaging
- Dimorphic fungi = mold at 25°C (environment), yeast at 37°C (tissue) — universal rule for this group
- Most classic dimorphic systemic mycoses (Histoplasma, Coccidioides, Blastomyces) are US-endemic, not endemic to India
- *Pneumocystis jirovecii* is fungal (reclassified), cannot be cultured, diagnosed by GMS silver stain showing cup-shaped cysts
- PCP is an AIDS-defining illness at CD4 <200; cotrimoxazole is both treatment and prophylaxis
- Broad-based budding yeast = *Blastomyces*; "pilot's wheel" multiple budding = *Paracoccidioides*
- Sporotrichosis follows thorn-prick/gardening injury — "rose gardener's disease," lymphatic spread pattern
