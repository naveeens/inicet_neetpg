# Parasitology Protozoa Malaria

## Overview
Medically important protozoa: blood/tissue protozoa (*Plasmodium*, *Leishmania*, *Toxoplasma*, *Trypanosoma*) and intestinal/luminal protozoa (*Entamoeba*, *Giardia*, *Trichomonas*).

## Malaria (*Plasmodium* species)
| Species | Fever Periodicity | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| *P. falciparum* | 24–48 h (often irregular/continuous) | Most severe — cerebral malaria, blackwater fever, ARDS, severe anemia; no relapse (no hypnozoites); ring forms only, multiple rings/cell, banana-shaped gametocytes |
| *P. vivax* | Tertian (48 h) | Most common in India; **relapse** due to hypnozoites (dormant liver stage) |
| *P. ovale* | Tertian (48 h) | Rare, hypnozoites → relapse; Africa |
| *P. malariae* | Quartan (72 h) | Chronic, low-grade; associated with **nephrotic syndrome** (immune complex glomerulonephritis) |

### Life Cycle (High-Yield)
1. Female *Anopheles* mosquito injects sporozoites → liver (exo-erythrocytic schizogony) → merozoites released
2. Merozoites invade RBCs → erythrocytic schizogony (trophozoite → schizont → rupture) → clinical symptoms (fever coincides with RBC rupture)
3. Some merozoites → gametocytes → taken up by mosquito → sexual cycle (sporogony) in mosquito gut → sporozoites migrate to salivary glands
4. **Hypnozoites** (dormant liver forms) occur only in *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* → cause relapse months/years later

### Diagnosis
- **Peripheral smear (thick + thin)** — gold standard; thick smear for detection (more sensitive), thin smear for species identification, Giemsa/Leishman stain
- Rapid diagnostic tests (RDT): detect HRP-2 (*P. falciparum*-specific) or pan-malarial antigens (pLDH)
- Best time to draw blood: during/just after fever spike (peak parasitemia)

### Treatment
- Uncomplicated *P. falciparum*: **Artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT)** — e.g., artesunate + sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (per national guidelines)
- Uncomplicated *P. vivax*: Chloroquine + Primaquine (Primaquine clears hypnozoites — prevents relapse; check G6PD status first, risk of hemolysis)
- Severe/complicated malaria: IV Artesunate (drug of choice, superior to quinine)
- Pregnancy: Chloroquine safe for vivax; Quinine/Artesunate combinations used for falciparum (Primaquine contraindicated in pregnancy)

## Entamoeba histolytica
- Causes **amoebiasis** — amoebic dysentery, liver abscess
- Life cycle: cyst (infective, quadrinucleate) ingested → trophozoite (invasive form) in colon → invades mucosa → "flask-shaped ulcers"
- **Amoebic liver abscess**: right lobe most common, "anchovy sauce" pus (odorless, unlike pyogenic abscess); serology (antibody detection) very sensitive for extraintestinal disease
- Diagnosis: stool microscopy (trophozoites with ingested RBCs = pathognomonic for *E. histolytica* vs non-pathogenic *E. dispar*, morphologically identical but distinguished by antigen/PCR)
- Treatment: Metronidazole/Tinidazole (tissue) + Paromomycin/Diloxanide furoate (luminal cyst clearance)

## Giardia lamblia
- Flagellate protozoan, causes **giardiasis** — malabsorption, foul-smelling greasy diarrhea (steatorrhea), no blood/mucus (unlike amoebic dysentery)
- Trophozoite: pear-shaped/kite-shaped with 2 nuclei ("old man's face" appearance), 4 pairs of flagella
- Diagnosis: stool O&P (cysts/trophozoites), string test, ELISA stool antigen, duodenal aspirate/biopsy in refractory cases
- Treatment: Metronidazole/Tinidazole

## Trichomonas vaginalis
- Flagellate, no cyst stage (only trophozoite — direct transmission, sexually transmitted)
- Causes vaginitis — frothy, greenish-yellow, malodorous discharge; "strawberry cervix"
- Diagnosis: wet mount (motile trophozoites), NAAT
- Treatment: Metronidazole (partner treatment essential)

## Leishmania
| Species | Disease | Vector | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| *L. donovani* | Visceral leishmaniasis (Kala-azar) | Sandfly (*Phlebotomus*) | Hepatosplenomegaly, fever, pancytopenia, hyperpigmentation ("black fever"); India (Bihar) endemic; **Amastigote (LD bodies)** seen in bone marrow/splenic aspirate macrophages |
| *L. tropica* | Cutaneous leishmaniasis (Oriental sore) | Sandfly | Self-healing skin ulcer |
| Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL) | Cutaneous sequela after VL treatment | — | Occurs mainly in India/Sudan |

- Diagnosis: bone marrow/splenic aspirate showing amastigotes (LD bodies), rK39 rapid antigen test, serology
- Treatment: Liposomal Amphotericin B (drug of choice for VL in India), Miltefosine, Sodium stibogluconate (older)

## Toxoplasma gondii
- Definitive host: cats; humans/other animals = intermediate hosts
- Transmission: undercooked meat (tissue cysts), contaminated cat feces (oocysts), transplacental (congenital toxoplasmosis)
- **Congenital toxoplasmosis triad**: chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, intracranial calcifications
- Immunocompromised (AIDS, CD4 <100): reactivation → **toxoplasma encephalitis** — ring-enhancing brain lesions on CT/MRI (classic differential with CNS lymphoma)
- Diagnosis: serology (IgM/IgG), PCR; treatment: Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Folinic acid

## Trypanosoma (Not endemic to India but exam-relevant)
| Species | Disease | Vector | Notable Feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| *T. brucei* (gambiense/rhodesiense) | African sleeping sickness | Tsetse fly | Winterbottom's sign (posterior cervical lymphadenopathy) |
| *T. cruzi* | Chagas disease | Reduviid (kissing) bug | Romaña's sign (periorbital swelling); cardiomyopathy, megacolon/megaesophagus (chronic) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* relapse due to hypnozoites; *P. falciparum* and *P. malariae* do not have this liver-dormant stage
- Ring forms with multiple parasites/cell + banana-shaped gametocytes = *P. falciparum*
- *P. malariae* is classically linked with nephrotic syndrome (membranoproliferative GN, immune complex-mediated)
- Primaquine clears hypnozoites (anti-relapse) but requires G6PD testing first (hemolysis risk)
- IV Artesunate is the drug of choice for severe/complicated malaria, superior to quinine
- Trophozoite with ingested RBCs on stool exam = pathognomonic for *E. histolytica* (not *E. dispar*)
- "Anchovy sauce" pus + right lobe = amoebic liver abscess
- Giardia trophozoite = pear-shaped, 2 nuclei ("old man's face"); causes fatty, foul, non-bloody diarrhea
- "Strawberry cervix" and frothy discharge = *Trichomonas vaginalis*; always treat partner
- LD bodies (amastigotes) in bone marrow/splenic aspirate = visceral leishmaniasis (Kala-azar)
- Ring-enhancing brain lesion in AIDS patient = think Toxoplasma encephalitis (vs primary CNS lymphoma)
- Congenital toxoplasmosis triad: chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, intracranial calcifications
- Romaña's sign = Chagas disease (*T. cruzi*); Winterbottom's sign = African sleeping sickness (*T. brucei*)
