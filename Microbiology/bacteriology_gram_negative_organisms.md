# Bacteriology Gram Negative Organisms

## Overview
Major gram-negative groups: **Enterobacteriaceae** (gut flora/pathogens), **Vibrio, Campylobacter, Helicobacter** (curved rods), **Pseudomonas**, **Neisseria** (diplococci), **Haemophilus, Bordetella**, and the **zoonotic/fastidious organisms** (Brucella, Yersinia, Francisella).

## Enterobacteriaceae
Oxidase-negative, lactose fermentation differentiates key genera on MacConkey/EMB agar.

| Organism | Lactose | Key Test | Disease | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *E. coli* | Fermenter (pink on MacConkey) | Indole +, IMViC: ++−− | UTI (most common cause), traveler's diarrhea, neonatal meningitis (K1 strain) | EMB agar → green metallic sheen |
| *Klebsiella pneumoniae* | Fermenter | Mucoid, capsule, IMViC: −−++ | Pneumonia (alcoholics, "currant jelly" sputum), UTI, nosocomial infections | Carbapenem-resistant strains (CRE/KPC) major concern |
| *Salmonella* Typhi | Non-fermenter | H2S+, motile, no gas | Typhoid fever | Antigens: O, H, Vi (virulence, capsular) |
| *Salmonella* (non-typhoidal) | Non-fermenter | H2S+ | Gastroenteritis, esp. from poultry/eggs | |
| *Shigella* | Non-fermenter | H2S−, non-motile | Bacillary dysentery | No animal reservoir, low infectious dose; Shiga toxin (*S. dysenteriae*) |
| *Proteus* | Non-fermenter | Swarming motility, urease + | UTI (struvite/staghorn calculi) | H2S+ on TSI |
| *Yersinia pestis* | — | Safety pin morphology, cold enrichment | Plague (bubonic/pneumonic) | Zoonotic, flea vector |

### Typhoid Fever (*Salmonella* Typhi) — Diagnosis Timeline
| Week | Best Test |
|---|---|
| 1st week | Blood culture (highest yield) |
| 2nd–3rd week | Stool/urine culture; blood culture yield falling |
| Any time | Widal test (rising O & H titers, paired sera, low specificity) — largely historical |
| Bone marrow culture | Highest sensitivity throughout illness, especially if antibiotics already started |

- Treatment: **Ceftriaxone** (or azithromycin) — increasing fluoroquinolone resistance in India
- Carrier state: gallbladder (chronic carriers, especially with gallstones)

### *Shigella* vs Enteroinvasive/Enterotoxigenic *E. coli*
- Shiga toxin (from *S. dysenteriae* type 1) inhibits 60S ribosomal subunit (protein synthesis) → also causes **HUS** (along with EHEC O157:H7 via Shiga-like toxin)
- EHEC O157:H7: sorbitol non-fermenter (distinguishes from other *E. coli* on sorbitol-MacConkey), associated with undercooked beef, causes hemorrhagic colitis + HUS

## Vibrio, Campylobacter, Helicobacter (Curved/Comma-Shaped Rods)
| Organism | Feature | Disease | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Vibrio cholerae* | Comma-shaped, motile (single polar flagellum), oxidase + | Cholera — rice-watery stools, massive dehydration | TCBS agar (yellow colonies); hanging drop shows darting motility, immobilized by specific antiserum |
| *Campylobacter jejuni* | Comma/S-shaped, microaerophilic | Bacterial gastroenteritis (common), Guillain-Barré Syndrome trigger | Skirrow's media, growth at 42°C |
| *Helicobacter pylori* | Spiral, urease +++ | Peptic ulcer, gastric adenocarcinoma, MALT lymphoma | Urease breath test, rapid urease (CLO) test, stool antigen, biopsy + Giemsa/Warthin-Starry stain |

- Cholera toxin: activates adenylate cyclase → ↑ cAMP → massive secretory diarrhea (mechanism shared conceptually with LT of ETEC)
- Cholera treatment: aggressive oral/IV rehydration is priority; doxycycline/azithromycin shortens duration
- *H. pylori* eradication: PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin (triple therapy)

## Pseudomonas aeruginosa
- Oxidase positive, non-lactose fermenter, obligate aerobe
- Produces pyocyanin (blue-green pigment) and pyoverdine (fluorescent) — grassy/fruity odor
- Opportunistic pathogen: burns, cystic fibrosis (chronic colonization), ventilator-associated pneumonia, malignant otitis externa (diabetics), ecthyma gangrenosum (neutropenic patients), hot-tub folliculitis
- Intrinsically resistant to many antibiotics; treat with anti-pseudomonal beta-lactams (piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, cefepime, carbapenems), fluoroquinolones, or aminoglycosides — combination therapy often used

## Neisseria (Gram-Negative Diplococci)
| Feature | *N. meningitidis* | *N. gonorrhoeae* |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar fermentation | Glucose + Maltose | Glucose only |
| Disease | Meningococcemia, meningitis (petechial rash, Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome = adrenal hemorrhage) | Gonorrhea, PID, ophthalmia neonatorum, disseminated gonococcal infection (arthritis-dermatitis) |
| Culture | Chocolate agar / blood agar | **Thayer-Martin agar** (selective, contains VCN antibiotics) |
| Habitat | Nasopharynx carriage | Genital tract, strictly human pathogen |
| Prophylaxis/vaccine | Meningococcal vaccine (serogroups A, C, W, Y, B); rifampicin/ciprofloxacin for close contacts | No vaccine; treat with ceftriaxone |

- Both: oxidase positive, require CO2-enriched atmosphere, capsule (meningococcus) is key virulence factor
- Gonococcus: no capsule, but antigenic variation (pilin) evades immunity; culture on chocolate/Thayer-Martin within hours (fastidious, autolyzes quickly)

## Haemophilus influenzae
- Small gram-negative coccobacilli, requires **X factor (hemin) and V factor (NAD)** for growth — grown on chocolate agar; satellitism around *S. aureus* on blood agar (S. aureus provides V factor via hemolysis)
- Capsulated type b (Hib) — meningitis, epiglottitis, pneumonia in unvaccinated children
- Hib conjugate vaccine part of routine immunization
- Non-typeable strains: otitis media, sinusitis, exacerbation of COPD

## Bordetella pertussis
- Gram-negative coccobacillus, strict aerobe
- Culture: **Bordet-Gengou medium** (potato-glycerol-blood agar)
- Whooping cough: catarrhal → paroxysmal (whoop) → convalescent stages
- Pertussis toxin: ADP-ribosylates Gi protein → ↑ cAMP; also tracheal cytotoxin causes ciliary damage
- Prevention: DPT/DTaP vaccine

## Zoonotic Gram-Negative Organisms
| Organism | Disease | Vector/Source | Diagnostic Clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Brucella* | Brucellosis (undulant fever) | Unpasteurized dairy, livestock contact | Blood culture (prolonged incubation), serology |
| *Francisella tularensis* | Tularemia | Rabbits, ticks | Highly infectious — BSL-3 handling |
| *Yersinia pestis* | Plague | Rat fleas (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) | Safety-pin appearance on Wayson/Giemsa stain |
| *Leptospira* | Leptospirosis (Weil disease) | Rodent urine-contaminated water | Dark-field microscopy, MAT (microscopic agglutination test) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- MacConkey agar differentiates lactose fermenters (pink) from non-fermenters (colorless) — first-line stool/urine culture medium
- EMB agar green metallic sheen = classic for *E. coli*
- TCBS agar yellow colonies = *V. cholerae* (sucrose fermenter)
- Widal test is largely obsolete but still exam-favorite for typhoid serology concept
- Blood culture is best in week 1 of typhoid; stool/urine culture better in week 2–3
- Thayer-Martin (VCN) selective media = gonococcus culture
- Chocolate agar required for *H. influenzae* (X+V factors) and *Neisseria*
- Bordet-Gengou medium = *B. pertussis*
- Skirrow's media + 42°C incubation = *Campylobacter jejuni*
- H. pylori urease test (rapid urease/breath test) exploits its potent urease activity
- Pyocyanin (blue-green pigment) + grape/corn-like odor = *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*
- Ecthyma gangrenosum in neutropenic patients strongly suggests *Pseudomonas* bacteremia
- Shiga toxin and Shiga-like toxin (EHEC) both cause HUS via inhibition of protein synthesis
- Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome (bilateral adrenal hemorrhage) is classically associated with meningococcemia
