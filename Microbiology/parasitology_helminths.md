# Parasitology Helminths

## Overview
Helminths are classified into **Nematodes** (roundworms), **Cestodes** (tapeworms), and **Trematodes** (flukes). Diagnosis is mostly by stool/urine microscopy for eggs/larvae, with serology and imaging for tissue-invasive forms.

## Nematodes (Roundworms)
| Organism | Disease | Transmission | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Ascaris lumbricoides* | Ascariasis | Fecal-oral (egg ingestion) | Largest intestinal nematode; larvae undergo pulmonary migration (Loeffler syndrome — eosinophilic pneumonia); can cause intestinal obstruction (heavy loads, esp. children) |
| *Enterobius vermicularis* (pinworm) | Enterobiasis | Fecal-oral, autoinfection | Perianal itching (nocturnal); **Scotch tape/NIH swab test** for eggs at perianal region in morning |
| *Trichuris trichiura* (whipworm) | Trichuriasis | Fecal-oral | Rectal prolapse in heavy infection (children); "barrel-shaped" egg |
| *Ancylostoma duodenale / Necator americanus* (hookworm) | Hookworm disease | Skin penetration (larvae in soil) | Leading cause of iron-deficiency anemia from chronic blood loss; "ground itch" at entry site |
| *Strongyloides stercoralis* | Strongyloidiasis | Skin penetration | Autoinfection → can persist for decades; **hyperinfection syndrome** in immunosuppressed (steroids) — can be fatal |
| *Trichinella spiralis* | Trichinosis | Undercooked pork | Periorbital edema, myalgia, eosinophilia; larvae encyst in skeletal muscle |
| *Wuchereria bancrofti / Brugia malayi* | Lymphatic filariasis | Mosquito bite (*Culex*, *Aedes*) | Elephantiasis, hydrocele; **nocturnal periodicity** of microfilaria in blood (draw blood at night); DEC (diethylcarbamazine) provocation test for daytime sampling |
| *Loa loa* | Loiasis | Deer fly (*Chrysops*) | Calabar swellings, eye worm (subconjunctival migration) — not endemic to India |
| *Onchocerca volvulus* | Onchocerciasis (river blindness) | Blackfly (*Simulium*) | Skin nodules, blindness — not endemic to India |
| *Dracunculus medinensis* | Guinea worm disease | Contaminated water (copepods) | Near-eradicated globally (WHO eradication program success story) |

### Ascaris Life Cycle (High-Yield)
Egg ingested → larvae hatch in intestine → penetrate gut wall → **lung migration** (pulmonary phase, Loeffler syndrome, eosinophilia) → coughed up and swallowed → mature into adults in small intestine → eggs passed in stool

### Filariasis Diagnosis
- Nocturnal periodicity: draw blood between 10 PM–2 AM for microfilaria detection
- Thick blood smear with Giemsa/Leishman stain
- DEC provocation test: gives daytime microfilaremia by mobilizing microfilariae
- Treatment: DEC + Albendazole (mass drug administration for filariasis elimination programs)

## Cestodes (Tapeworms)
| Organism | Disease | Intermediate Host/Source | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Taenia solium* | Taeniasis (intestinal); **Cysticercosis** (tissue, via egg ingestion) | Pig (intermediate), humans (accidental for cysticercosis) | Neurocysticercosis = most common cause of acquired epilepsy in endemic areas; scolex has hooks ("armed") |
| *Taenia saginata* | Taeniasis (intestinal only) | Cattle | No cysticercosis risk in humans (scolex unarmed, no hooks); larger, more proglottids |
| *Echinococcus granulosus* | Hydatid disease (cystic echinococcosis) | Dogs (definitive), sheep/humans (intermediate) | Liver hydatid cysts (most common site); "daughter cysts," eggshell calcification on imaging; **do not aspirate without albendazole cover** — risk of anaphylaxis from cyst fluid leakage |
| *Hymenolepis nana* | Hymenolepiasis | None needed (direct fecal-oral, autoinfection possible) | Most common tapeworm infection in humans overall (esp. children); no intermediate host required |
| *Diphyllobothrium latum* | Diphyllobothriasis | Fish | Vitamin B12 deficiency (worm competes for B12) |

- Neurocysticercosis: caused by ingesting *T. solium* eggs (not undercooked pork — that gives intestinal taeniasis); ring-enhancing lesions on CT/MRI, calcified granulomas
- Hydatid cyst: Casoni's skin test (historical), serology (ELISA), imaging (CT/USG) — do not perform FNAC/percutaneous drainage without antihelminthic cover due to anaphylaxis/dissemination risk
- Treatment: Praziquantel (intestinal tapeworms), Albendazole (cysticercosis, hydatid disease — often with surgery for hydatid)

## Trematodes (Flukes)
| Organism | Disease | Intermediate Host/Source | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Schistosoma haematobium* | Urinary schistosomiasis | Freshwater snails | Hematuria, bladder cancer (squamous cell) risk; terminal-spined egg |
| *Schistosoma mansoni/japonicum* | Intestinal/hepatosplenic schistosomiasis | Freshwater snails | Hepatosplenomegaly, portal hypertension; lateral-spined (mansoni) egg |
| *Fasciola hepatica* | Liver fluke disease | Watercress/aquatic plants, snails | Biliary colic, hepatomegaly |
| *Paragonimus westermani* | Lung fluke disease | Undercooked crab/crayfish | Hemoptysis — mimics TB |
| *Clonorchis sinensis* | Liver fluke disease | Undercooked fish | Cholangiocarcinoma risk with chronic infection |

- Schistosomiasis transmission: cercariae penetrate skin during freshwater contact (swimming, wading — "swimmer's itch")
- *S. haematobium* is strongly linked to squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder (chronic irritation/inflammation)
- Treatment: Praziquantel (drug of choice for all trematodes except *Fasciola*, which requires Triclabendazole)

## Eosinophilia — Helminth Association (High-Yield Pattern)
- Tissue-invasive helminths (migrating larvae) cause marked eosinophilia: *Ascaris* (Loeffler), *Strongyloides*, *Toxocara*, *Trichinella*, filariasis, schistosomiasis
- Purely luminal/intestinal helminths (adult worms confined to gut lumen) cause minimal/no eosinophilia: *Enterobius*, adult *Taenia*

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Loeffler syndrome (eosinophilic pneumonia) = pulmonary larval migration phase of *Ascaris*
- Scotch tape test = diagnostic method for *Enterobius vermicularis* (pinworm), done in early morning
- Hookworm = most common helminthic cause of iron-deficiency anemia globally
- Strongyloides hyperinfection syndrome occurs with corticosteroid use/immunosuppression — can be fatal
- Nocturnal periodicity of microfilariae = filariasis (*Wuchereria bancrofti*); draw blood at night
- Neurocysticercosis (from *T. solium* egg ingestion) = most common cause of acquired epilepsy in endemic regions
- *T. saginata* has no cysticercosis risk (unarmed scolex); *T. solium* does (armed scolex, causes cysticercosis)
- Never aspirate a hydatid cyst without albendazole cover — anaphylaxis/dissemination risk
- *Diphyllobothrium latum* causes vitamin B12 deficiency by competing with host for B12
- *Schistosoma haematobium* → squamous cell carcinoma of bladder; terminal-spined egg
- Praziquantel is the drug of choice for most trematode and cestode infections; Triclabendazole for *Fasciola*
- Tissue-migrating helminths cause eosinophilia; purely luminal helminths generally do not
