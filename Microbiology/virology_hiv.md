# Virology HIV

## Overview
HIV-1 (global, more virulent) and HIV-2 (West Africa, less virulent, slower progression) are retroviruses (Lentivirus genus) that infect CD4+ T-cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells, causing progressive immunodeficiency.

## Structure
- Enveloped, ssRNA (+) diploid genome, icosahedral capsid
- Key genes: *gag* (core proteins p24, p17), *pol* (reverse transcriptase, integrase, protease), *env* (gp160 → cleaved to gp120 + gp41)
- **gp120**: binds CD4 receptor + co-receptor (CCR5 on macrophages/early infection, CXCR4 on T-cells/late infection)
- **gp41**: mediates membrane fusion after gp120 binding
- **p24**: major capsid protein — used as early diagnostic antigen (p24 antigen assay)
- Reverse transcriptase: converts RNA genome to dsDNA (error-prone → high mutation rate, drives drug resistance and immune evasion)
- CCR5-Δ32 mutation: homozygotes are relatively resistant to HIV infection (basis of "Berlin patient" cure concept)

## Pathogenesis / Natural History
| Stage | CD4 Count | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Acute (seroconversion) illness | Normal → falling | Flu-like illness/mononucleosis-like syndrome 2–4 weeks post-exposure; high viral load ("window period" for antibody tests) |
| Clinical latency | Gradual decline | Asymptomatic or mild lymphadenopathy for years; ongoing viral replication in lymphoid tissue |
| Symptomatic/AIDS | <200 cells/µL | Opportunistic infections, malignancies |

### CD4-Associated Opportunistic Infections (classic exam ladder)
| CD4 Count | Infection/Condition |
|---|---|
| <500 | Oral candidiasis, TB (can occur at any CD4), Kaposi sarcoma |
| <200 | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PCP), Cryptococcal meningitis |
| <100 | Toxoplasma encephalitis, Cryptosporidiosis |
| <50 | CMV retinitis, disseminated *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) |

## Diagnosis
| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| **ELISA (4th generation, combined Ag/Ab)** | Screening — detects p24 antigen + antibodies, shortens window period |
| **Western blot / line immunoassay** | Confirmatory test (historically); being phased out for algorithm-based testing |
| **p24 antigen** | Detectable before antibodies — useful in acute infection window period |
| **HIV RNA PCR (viral load)** | Earliest detectable marker; used for monitoring treatment response, diagnosing infants (maternal antibodies confound ELISA in neonates) |
| **CD4 count** | Monitors immune status, guides opportunistic infection prophylaxis, not diagnostic |
| **Rapid tests** | Point-of-care, used in NACO (India) testing strategy with sequential confirmatory algorithm |

- In infants born to HIV+ mothers: use **HIV DNA/RNA PCR**, not antibody tests (maternal IgG antibodies cross placenta and give false positives up to 18 months)
- Window period (4th gen ELISA): ~2–4 weeks; window period with older antibody-only tests: ~4–12 weeks

## Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
### Drug Classes
| Class | Examples | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| NRTI (Nucleoside RT inhibitors) | Tenofovir, Lamivudine, Zidovudine, Abacavir | Chain termination — inhibit reverse transcriptase |
| NNRTI | Efavirenz, Nevirapine | Non-competitive RT inhibition |
| Protease Inhibitors | Ritonavir, Atazanavir, Lopinavir | Block cleavage of gag-pol polyprotein → immature non-infectious virions |
| Integrase Inhibitors | Dolutegravir, Raltegravir | Block integration of viral DNA into host genome |
| Entry/Fusion Inhibitors | Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist), Enfuvirtide (gp41 inhibitor) | Block viral entry/fusion |

- Standard first-line ART (current WHO/NACO guidance): **2 NRTIs + 1 Integrase inhibitor** (e.g., Tenofovir + Lamivudine + Dolutegravir — "TLD")
- ART is lifelong; goal = undetectable viral load, CD4 recovery
- **PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis)**: started within 72 hours (ideally <2 hours) of exposure, continued for 28 days — triple drug regimen
- **PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis)**: Tenofovir + Emtricitabine for high-risk HIV-negative individuals
- **PMTCT (Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission)**: ART throughout pregnancy regardless of CD4, intrapartum management, infant prophylaxis, avoid breastfeeding where safe alternatives exist (or continue ART through breastfeeding per resource-limited settings guidance)

## Opportunistic Infection Prophylaxis
| CD4 Threshold | Prophylaxis | Against |
|---|---|---|
| <200 | Cotrimoxazole (TMP-SMX) | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* |
| <100 | Cotrimoxazole (also covers Toxoplasma) | *Toxoplasma gondii* |
| <50 | Azithromycin | MAC (*M. avium complex*) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- gp120 binds CD4 + co-receptor (CCR5 early, CXCR4 late); gp41 mediates fusion
- p24 antigen and HIV RNA PCR detect infection before antibodies appear — key for window-period diagnosis
- Diagnosis in infants of HIV+ mothers requires PCR (DNA/RNA), never antibody-based ELISA (maternal antibody interference)
- PEP must start within 72 hours (best <2 hours) and continue for 28 days
- First-line ART = 2 NRTIs + Integrase inhibitor (Dolutegravir-based regimen, "TLD")
- CD4 <200 → PCP prophylaxis with cotrimoxazole; CD4 <50 → MAC prophylaxis with azithromycin
- CCR5-Δ32 homozygosity confers natural resistance to HIV infection
- CMV retinitis and disseminated MAC are markers of very advanced immunosuppression (CD4 <50)
- Reverse transcriptase's high error rate drives HIV's rapid mutation and drug resistance
- HIV-2 is less virulent, progresses more slowly, and is geographically concentrated in West Africa
