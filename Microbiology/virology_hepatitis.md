# Virology Hepatitis

## Overview
Five main hepatitis viruses (A–E) differ by genome type, transmission route, chronicity potential, and serologic markers. Fecal-oral (A, E) vs blood-borne/parenteral (B, C, D) is the key organizing split.

## Comparison Table
| Virus | Family/Genome | Transmission | Chronicity | Vaccine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hepatitis A (HAV) | Picornavirus, ssRNA (+), non-enveloped | Fecal-oral | No chronic state | Yes (inactivated) |
| Hepatitis B (HBV) | Hepadnavirus, dsDNA (partially), enveloped | Parenteral, sexual, vertical | Yes (esp. if acquired perinatally) | Yes (recombinant HBsAg) |
| Hepatitis C (HCV) | Flavivirus, ssRNA (+), enveloped | Parenteral (blood/needles) | Yes (~75–80%) | No vaccine |
| Hepatitis D (HDV) | Deltavirus, ssRNA (−), defective, needs HBsAg | Parenteral (co/superinfection with HBV) | Worsens HBV chronicity/cirrhosis risk | Prevented by HBV vaccine |
| Hepatitis E (HEV) | Hepevirus, ssRNA (+), non-enveloped | Fecal-oral (waterborne) | No chronic state (except immunosuppressed) | Available in some countries, not universal |

- **HEV in pregnancy**: high mortality (fulminant hepatitis), especially 3rd trimester — classic exam association
- **HAV/HEV**: self-limited, no carrier state; diagnosed by IgM anti-HAV / IgM anti-HEV during acute illness

## Hepatitis B — Serology (Highest Yield Topic)
| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HBsAg | Surface antigen — current infection (acute or chronic); first marker to appear |
| Anti-HBs | Immunity — either from resolved infection or vaccination |
| HBeAg | Marker of active viral replication and high infectivity |
| Anti-HBe | Seroconversion — reduced replication/infectivity |
| Anti-HBc IgM | Acute infection marker |
| Anti-HBc IgG | Past or chronic infection (persists lifelong) |
| HBV DNA (viral load) | Direct measure of replication — used to guide treatment |

### Classic Serology Patterns
| Pattern | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| HBsAg+, IgM anti-HBc+ | Acute infection |
| HBsAg+, IgG anti-HBc+, HBeAg+ | Chronic infection, high infectivity |
| HBsAg−, anti-HBs+, anti-HBc− | Vaccinated (immune, never infected) |
| HBsAg−, anti-HBs+, anti-HBc+ | Past natural infection, now recovered/immune |
| HBsAg−, anti-HBc+ only ("window period") | Acute resolving infection during window between HBsAg disappearance and anti-HBs appearance — **IgM anti-HBc is the only detectable marker** |

- Genome: partially double-stranded, circular DNA — has DNA polymerase with reverse transcriptase activity
- Vertical transmission risk highest with HBeAg+ mothers; prevented with HBIG + vaccine at birth
- Chronic HBV → cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma (direct oncogenic integration, doesn't need cirrhosis first, unlike HCV)
- Treatment: Tenofovir/Entecavir (nucleos(t)ide analogs) or Peg-interferon

## Hepatitis C
- Six major genotypes (genotype 1 most common globally, genotype 3 common in India)
- No vaccine due to high genomic variability (hypervariable E2 region)
- Diagnosis: anti-HCV antibody (screening) → HCV RNA (confirms active infection, quantifies viral load)
- Chronic HCV → cirrhosis (needed before HCC risk rises significantly, unlike HBV)
- Treatment: Direct-Acting Antivirals (DAAs) — sofosbuvir + velpatasvir/daclatasvir — >95% cure rates, has replaced interferon-based regimens

## Hepatitis D
- Defective/satellite virus — requires HBsAg for assembly/transmission
- **Co-infection** (HBV + HDV simultaneously): usually acute, self-limited, may be severe
- **Superinfection** (HDV on chronic HBV carrier): more severe, higher risk of fulminant hepatitis and progression to cirrhosis
- Diagnosis: anti-HDV antibody, HDV RNA; always test in HBsAg+ patients with unusually severe disease

## Hepatitis E
- Most common cause of sporadic/epidemic hepatitis in developing countries with poor sanitation (waterborne outbreaks)
- Genotypes 1, 2 (human, epidemic, fecal-oral) vs genotypes 3, 4 (zoonotic, pigs/deer, foodborne, sporadic in developed nations)
- High mortality in pregnant women (fulminant hepatic failure) — must always be considered in pregnant patients with jaundice
- Chronic infection possible in immunosuppressed/transplant patients (unusual but tested)

## Lab Diagnosis Summary
- Acute viral hepatitis panel: IgM anti-HAV, HBsAg + IgM anti-HBc, anti-HCV, IgM anti-HEV
- Liver enzymes: ALT usually > AST in viral hepatitis (opposite pattern in alcoholic hepatitis where AST > ALT)
- ELISA is mainstay for serologic screening; PCR (viral load) for confirmation and treatment monitoring in HBV/HCV

## High-Yield One-Liners
- HBsAg = current infection marker; anti-HBs = immunity marker (vaccination or recovery)
- "Window period" of HBV: HBsAg negative, anti-HBs not yet positive — only IgM anti-HBc detectable
- HBeAg positivity = high infectivity/active replication; used to assess transmission risk in pregnancy
- HDV is defective, always requires HBsAg — superinfection (on chronic HBV) worse than co-infection
- HEV in pregnancy = high risk of fulminant hepatic failure and mortality, especially 3rd trimester
- HAV and HEV: fecal-oral, no chronic carrier state, IgM detects acute infection
- HBV integrates into host genome → HCC risk without requiring cirrhosis first; HCV needs cirrhosis first
- No vaccine exists for HCV or HEV (universal); HBV vaccine indirectly prevents HDV
- DAAs (sofosbuvir-based regimens) have revolutionized HCV treatment with >95% cure rates
- ALT > AST typically in viral hepatitis; AST > ALT classically in alcoholic liver disease
