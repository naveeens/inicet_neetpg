# Virology Other Viruses

## Overview
High-yield viral groups beyond hepatitis/HIV: Herpesviruses, Respiratory viruses (Influenza, Paramyxoviruses), Rhabdovirus (Rabies), Arboviruses, and Gastroenteritis viruses.

## Herpesviruses (dsDNA, Enveloped)
| Virus | Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| HSV-1 | Oral herpes, gingivostomatitis, herpes labialis, most sporadic encephalitis | Latency in trigeminal ganglion; Tzanck smear shows multinucleated giant cells |
| HSV-2 | Genital herpes, neonatal herpes | Latency in sacral ganglion |
| VZV | Chickenpox (primary), Herpes zoster/shingles (reactivation) | Latency in dorsal root ganglia; Tzanck smear positive (cannot distinguish from HSV morphologically) |
| EBV | Infectious mononucleosis, Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, PTLD | Infects B cells via CD21 receptor; heterophile antibody (Monospot) positive; atypical lymphocytes on smear |
| CMV | Congenital infection (most common congenital viral infection), retinitis in AIDS, mononucleosis-like illness | "Owl's eye" intranuclear inclusions; heterophile antibody negative (distinguishes from EBV mono) |
| HHV-6 | Roseola infantum (exanthem subitum) | High fever then rash as fever resolves |
| HHV-8 | Kaposi sarcoma | Associated with HIV/AIDS |

- All herpesviruses establish **latency** — hallmark feature of the family
- Tzanck smear: rapid bedside test, shows multinucleated giant cells in HSV/VZV vesicular lesions — cannot differentiate HSV from VZV (needs PCR/culture for that)

## Influenza Virus
- Orthomyxovirus, segmented ssRNA (−), enveloped
- Surface antigens: **Hemagglutinin (H)** — attachment to sialic acid receptors; **Neuraminidase (N)** — release of budding virions
- **Antigenic drift**: minor point mutations → seasonal epidemics (same subtype)
- **Antigenic shift**: major reassortment of segmented genome (e.g., in pigs as "mixing vessel") → new subtype → pandemics (only occurs in Influenza A, due to segmented genome enabling reassortment)
- Types: Influenza A (animals + humans, causes pandemics), B (humans only, no shift), C (mild, no epidemics)
- Treatment: Oseltamivir/Zanamivir (neuraminidase inhibitors); vaccine updated annually based on circulating strains

## Paramyxoviruses
| Virus | Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Measles | Measles (rubeola) | Koplik spots (buccal mucosa, pathognomonic, precede rash); complications: SSPE (subacute sclerosing panencephalitis, years later), otitis media, pneumonia, encephalitis |
| Mumps | Parotitis | Complications: orchitis (post-pubertal males), meningitis, pancreatitis |
| RSV | Bronchiolitis in infants | Leading cause of infant hospitalization for respiratory illness; palivizumab for high-risk prophylaxis |
| Parainfluenza | Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis) | "Steeple sign" on X-ray |

- Measles: MMR vaccine (live attenuated); SSPE is a delayed fatal complication years after acute infection
- Both measles and mumps viruses cause multinucleated giant cell formation (syncytia)

## Rabies (Rhabdovirus)
- Bullet-shaped, enveloped, ssRNA (−) virus
- Transmission: bite of rabid animal (dogs — most common in India); virus travels via retrograde axonal transport to CNS
- **Negri bodies**: eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions in neurons (classically Purkinje cells of cerebellum, hippocampal neurons) — pathognomonic but seen post-mortem/late
- Clinical: prodrome → furious (hydrophobia, aerophobia, agitation) or paralytic (dumb rabies) forms → almost universally fatal once symptomatic
- Post-exposure prophylaxis: wound washing + rabies immunoglobulin (category III exposures) + vaccine series (cell culture vaccine, Essen/Zagreb regimen)
- Diagnosis: direct fluorescent antibody test on corneal impression smear/skin biopsy (antemortem, low sensitivity), brain tissue post-mortem

## Arboviruses (Mosquito/Vector-Borne)
| Virus | Family | Vector | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dengue | Flavivirus | *Aedes aegypti* | 4 serotypes; secondary infection with different serotype → antibody-dependent enhancement → **Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever/Dengue Shock Syndrome**; NS1 antigen (early), IgM (day 5+) |
| Chikungunya | Alphavirus | *Aedes aegypti* | Severe polyarthralgia (can persist for months), rash, fever — rarely fatal |
| Japanese Encephalitis | Flavivirus | *Culex* mosquito | Leading cause of viral encephalitis in Asia; pig/bird reservoir; vaccine available |
| Zika | Flavivirus | *Aedes* mosquito | Congenital microcephaly if maternal infection; also sexually transmitted |
| Yellow Fever | Flavivirus | *Aedes*/*Haemagogus* | Not endemic to India; "black vomit," jaundice; live attenuated vaccine (17D strain) required for travel to endemic zones |

- Dengue warning signs: abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, plasma leakage signs (rising hematocrit + falling platelets = classic combination for DHF)
- Aedes mosquito = day-biting, breeds in clean stagnant water (unlike Anopheles/Culex)

## Gastroenteritis Viruses
| Virus | Family | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Rotavirus | Reovirus, dsRNA | **Most common cause of severe dehydrating diarrhea in infants/young children worldwide**; wheel-like appearance on EM; oral vaccine available (part of universal immunization in India) |
| Norovirus | Calicivirus | Most common cause of viral gastroenteritis in adults; outbreaks (cruise ships, institutions) |
| Adenovirus (40/41) | Adenovirus, dsDNA | Second common cause of pediatric viral diarrhea |

## Other High-Yield Viruses
| Virus | Association |
|---|---|
| HPV | Cervical cancer (16, 18 = high-risk oncogenic types); genital warts (6, 11 = low-risk); vaccine (quadrivalent/nonavalent) |
| Parvovirus B19 | Erythema infectiosum ("slapped cheek," fifth disease); aplastic crisis in sickle cell/hemolytic anemia; hydrops fetalis if maternal infection |
| Poliovirus | Enterovirus (Picornavirus); asymmetric flaccid paralysis (anterior horn cell destruction); OPV (live, induces mucosal/herd immunity) vs IPV (inactivated, safer, no vaccine-associated paralytic polio) |
| Coxsackievirus | Hand-foot-mouth disease, myocarditis, aseptic meningitis |
| Rubella | Congenital rubella syndrome (cataracts, cardiac defects (PDA), deafness — classic triad) if maternal infection in first trimester |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Koplik spots = pathognomonic for measles, appear before the rash
- Negri bodies = pathognomonic cytoplasmic inclusions of rabies, seen in neurons (Purkinje cells)
- "Owl's eye" inclusion = CMV; heterophile-negative mononucleosis-like illness
- Heterophile antibody (Monospot) positive = EBV infectious mononucleosis
- Antigenic shift (reassortment, Influenza A only) causes pandemics; antigenic drift causes seasonal epidemics
- Rising hematocrit + falling platelets = warning sign for dengue hemorrhagic fever/plasma leakage
- Secondary dengue infection with a different serotype → risk of antibody-dependent enhancement → DHF/DSS
- Rotavirus = leading global cause of severe dehydrating diarrhea in children; wheel-shaped on electron microscopy
- Congenital rubella triad: cataracts, cardiac defects (PDA), sensorineural deafness
- Parvovirus B19 causes aplastic crisis in chronic hemolytic anemias (e.g., sickle cell disease) and hydrops fetalis in utero
- OPV gives mucosal immunity (blocks transmission) but carries vaccine-associated paralytic polio risk; IPV is inactivated and safer
- SSPE is a rare, fatal, delayed CNS complication of measles occurring years after acute infection
