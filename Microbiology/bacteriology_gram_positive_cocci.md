# Bacteriology Gram Positive Cocci

## Overview
Major gram-positive cocci of medical importance: **Staphylococcus**, **Streptococcus**, **Enterococcus**. Arrangement helps initial ID: clusters (Staph, catalase +) vs chains/pairs (Strep/Enterococcus, catalase −).

## Staphylococcus
| Feature | *S. aureus* | *S. epidermidis* | *S. saprophyticus* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coagulase | Positive | Negative | Negative |
| Novobiocin | Sensitive | Sensitive | **Resistant** |
| DNase/Mannitol fermentation | Positive | Negative | Negative |
| Habitat/disease | Skin/nose carriage; boils, abscess, food poisoning, TSS, osteomyelitis | Catheter/prosthetic device infections (biofilm) | UTI in young sexually active women |

### Toxins & Diseases (high-yield associations)
| Toxin/Factor | Disease |
|---|---|
| Enterotoxin (heat-stable) | Food poisoning — rapid onset (2–6 h), vomiting > diarrhea |
| TSST-1 | Toxic Shock Syndrome (tampon-associated) |
| Exfoliatin (ET-A/B) | Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (Ritter disease) in neonates |
| Panton-Valentine Leukocidin (PVL) | Necrotizing pneumonia, severe skin infections (CA-MRSA marker) |
| Protein A | Binds Fc of IgG → antiphagocytic (basis of coagglutination tests) |
| Coagulase | Clots plasma → abscess formation, virulence marker |

- **MRSA**: mecA gene → altered PBP2a → resistant to all beta-lactams; detected by cefoxitin disk/oxacillin screen; vancomycin/linezolid for treatment.
- **VISA/VRSA**: reduced/complete vancomycin resistance — emerging concern, use linezolid/daptomycin.
- Golden/yellow pigment on nutrient agar; grows on 7.5–10% NaCl (halophilic, used in mannitol salt agar selection).

## Streptococcus
### Classification (Lancefield grouping + hemolysis)
| Group | Species | Hemolysis | Key Disease |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | *S. pyogenes* | Beta | Pharyngitis, cellulitis, necrotizing fasciitis, scarlet fever, rheumatic fever, PSGN |
| B | *S. agalactiae* | Beta | Neonatal sepsis/meningitis (early & late onset) |
| D | Enterococcus/non-enterococcal | Variable | UTI, endocarditis, biliary infections |
| — | *S. pneumoniae* | Alpha | Pneumonia, meningitis, otitis media, sinusitis |
| Viridans group | *S. mutans, S. mitis, etc.* | Alpha | Dental caries (*mutans*), subacute infective endocarditis |

### *S. pyogenes* (Group A Strep) — Key Virulence Factors
- **M protein**: major virulence factor, antiphagocytic, basis of typing; antigenic mimicry → rheumatic fever
- **Streptolysin O (SLO)**: oxygen-labile, immunogenic → basis of **ASO titer** (rises after pharyngitis, used to diagnose preceding strep infection/rheumatic fever); antigenic
- **Streptolysin S**: oxygen-stable, non-immunogenic, causes beta-hemolysis on blood agar
- **Streptokinase, hyaluronidase, DNase B**: spreading factors
- **Erythrogenic (pyrogenic) toxin**: scarlet fever rash, toxic shock-like syndrome
- Post-streptococcal sequelae: **Rheumatic fever** (after pharyngitis only, preventable by treating pharyngitis) and **PSGN** (after pharyngitis or skin infection, not prevented by treatment)
- Bacitracin sensitive (differentiates from Group B, which is bacitracin resistant)

### *S. agalactiae* (Group B Strep)
- Hippurate hydrolysis positive, CAMP test positive (arrowhead hemolysis enhancement with *S. aureus*)
- Leading cause of neonatal sepsis/meningitis; maternal vaginal-rectal screening at 35–37 weeks gestation; intrapartum penicillin prophylaxis if positive

### *S. pneumoniae* (Pneumococcus)
- Gram-positive diplococci, lancet-shaped, capsulated
- **Alpha hemolytic**, differentiated from viridans strep by:
  - **Bile solubility** (autolysin activated) — pneumococcus soluble
  - **Optochin sensitivity** — pneumococcus sensitive
  - **Quellung reaction** — capsular swelling with type-specific antiserum
- Capsule = major virulence factor (antiphagocytic); >90 serotypes
- Diseases: lobar pneumonia (rusty sputum), meningitis (most common cause in adults), otitis media (children), sinusitis
- Vaccines: PCV (conjugate, 10/13/15/20-valent, children) and PPSV23 (polysaccharide, elderly/high-risk)

## Enterococcus
- *E. faecalis* (most common) and *E. faecium*
- Group D antigen, grow in 6.5% NaCl and bile-esculin agar (bile-esculin positive)
- PYR test positive (differentiates from non-enterococcal Group D strep)
- Cause UTI, endocarditis, intra-abdominal infections; **intrinsically resistant** to cephalosporins
- **VRE** (Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococcus) — vanA/vanB genes — major nosocomial concern; treat with linezolid/daptomycin

## Lab Diagnosis Quick Reference
| Test | Distinguishes |
|---|---|
| Catalase | Staph (+) vs Strep/Enterococcus (−) |
| Coagulase | *S. aureus* (+) vs other Staph (−) |
| Novobiocin | *S. saprophyticus* (R) vs *S. epidermidis* (S) |
| Bacitracin | Group A Strep (S) vs Group B (R) |
| Optochin | Pneumococcus (S) vs viridans strep (R) |
| Bile solubility | Pneumococcus (soluble) vs viridans (insoluble) |
| CAMP test | Group B Strep (+) |
| Bile-esculin + 6.5% NaCl | Enterococcus (+) |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Catalase test = first split between Staph (+) and Strep/Enterococcus (−)
- Coagulase positive = *S. aureus*; coagulase test remains gold standard bedside differentiator
- ASO titer reflects past Group A strep infection — used in rheumatic fever workup, not acute pharyngitis diagnosis
- Rheumatic fever follows only pharyngitis; PSGN follows pharyngitis or skin infection
- Optochin sensitivity + bile solubility = pneumococcus; both exploit autolysin activation
- PVL toxin = hallmark of community-acquired MRSA causing necrotizing skin/lung infections
- Novobiocin resistance = *S. saprophyticus*, classic cause of UTI in young women ("honeymoon cystitis")
- VRE and VRSA/VISA are distinct — vanA/B genes vs altered vancomycin target respectively
- Quellung reaction demonstrates capsular swelling — historically used for pneumococcal typing
