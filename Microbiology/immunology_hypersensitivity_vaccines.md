# Immunology Hypersensitivity Vaccines

## Overview
Core immunology for PG exams centers on **Gell-Coombs hypersensitivity classification**, **immunoglobulin/complement basics**, and the **national immunization schedule** (vaccine types, storage, contraindications).

## Hypersensitivity Reactions (Gell-Coombs Classification)
| Type | Mechanism | Mediator | Classic Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type I** (Immediate/Anaphylactic) | IgE-mediated mast cell/basophil degranulation on re-exposure to antigen | IgE, histamine | Anaphylaxis, allergic asthma, urticaria, allergic rhinitis, food allergy |
| **Type II** (Cytotoxic) | IgG/IgM antibody binds cell-surface antigen → complement activation/ADCC | IgG, IgM, complement | Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, Goodpasture syndrome, hemolytic disease of newborn (Rh), Graves disease, Myasthenia gravis, hyperacute transplant rejection |
| **Type III** (Immune Complex) | Antigen-antibody complexes deposit in tissues → complement activation → inflammation | IgG-antigen complexes, complement | Serum sickness, SLE, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, Arthus reaction |
| **Type IV** (Delayed-Type/Cell-Mediated) | T-cell mediated (no antibody) — sensitized T cells release cytokines | CD4+ Th1 cells, macrophages (or CD8+ cytotoxic) | Tuberculin (Mantoux) test, contact dermatitis (poison ivy, nickel), granuloma formation (TB), graft-versus-host disease, chronic transplant rejection |

- Mnemonic for Type II vs III: Type II = antibody binds **fixed/cell-surface** antigen directly; Type III = antibody binds **soluble** antigen forming circulating immune complexes that deposit later
- Type IV is the only type **not** antibody-mediated — purely cell-mediated (delayed onset, 48–72 h, as in Mantoux test)
- Some conditions show mixed mechanisms (e.g., Rheumatoid arthritis has Type III features; Myasthenia gravis is Type II though pathophysiology involves receptor blocking not just cytotoxicity)

## Immunoglobulins
| Ig | Key Features |
|---|---|
| IgG | Most abundant in serum; only Ig crossing placenta; secondary/memory response; 4 subclasses |
| IgM | First antibody produced in primary response; pentamer (largest); best complement activator; does not cross placenta — IgM in neonate = congenital infection |
| IgA | Mucosal immunity, secretory dimer (with secretory component); found in breast milk, tears, saliva |
| IgE | Mediates Type I hypersensitivity, anti-parasitic (helminth) defense; binds Fc receptors on mast cells/basophils |
| IgD | Function largely unclear; B-cell surface receptor co-expressed with IgM |

- Neonatal IgM elevation suggests congenital infection (TORCH) since maternal IgM cannot cross placenta
- Only IgG crosses the placenta — basis for passive immunity in newborns and for maternal antibody interference in infant HIV/TORCH serology

## Complement System (Key Points)
- Classical pathway: activated by antigen-antibody complexes (IgM most efficient, then IgG)
- Alternative pathway: activated directly by microbial surfaces (no antibody needed)
- Lectin pathway: activated by mannose-binding lectin binding microbial carbohydrates
- All converge at C3 → C5 → **Membrane Attack Complex (C5b-9)** → cell lysis
- C3a, C5a: anaphylatoxins (mast cell degranulation, chemotaxis)
- C5a: potent neutrophil chemotactic factor
- Deficiencies: C1-C4 deficiency → SLE-like immune complex disease; C5-C9 (terminal complement/MAC) deficiency → recurrent **Neisseria** infections (meningococcal/gonococcal)
- C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency → hereditary angioedema

## National Immunization Schedule — India (High-Yield Concepts)
| Vaccine | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| BCG | Live attenuated | At birth; protects against severe TB (meningitis, miliary) in children, not adult pulmonary TB reliably |
| OPV/IPV | Live (OPV) / Inactivated (IPV) | OPV gives mucosal immunity, risk of vaccine-associated paralytic polio (VAPP); IPV safer, no VAPP, given as injectable |
| Pentavalent (DPT + Hep B + Hib) | Mixed (toxoid + inactivated + conjugate) | Primary series at 6, 10, 14 weeks |
| Measles/MR/MMR | Live attenuated | First dose ~9 months, second dose ~16–24 months |
| Rotavirus | Live attenuated (oral) | Prevents severe rotavirus gastroenteritis |
| PCV (Pneumococcal conjugate) | Conjugate | Part of universal immunization program |
| Hepatitis B (birth dose) | Recombinant subunit | Given within 24 hours of birth |
| Vitamin A | Supplement (not a vaccine, but co-administered) | Given alongside measles vaccine per schedule |

### Vaccine Types — Conceptual Classification
| Type | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Live attenuated | BCG, OPV, MMR, Rotavirus, Yellow fever, Varicella | Strong/durable immunity (mimics natural infection); contraindicated in pregnancy and immunocompromised (risk of disseminated infection) |
| Inactivated/killed | IPV, Hepatitis A, Rabies, Influenza (injectable) | Safe in pregnancy/immunocompromised; often need boosters, adjuvants |
| Toxoid | Tetanus, Diphtheria | Targets the toxin, not the organism |
| Subunit/recombinant | Hepatitis B (HBsAg), HPV | Very safe, no live component |
| Polysaccharide | Pneumococcal (PPSV23), older typhoid Vi vaccine | Poor immunogenicity in children <2 years (T-cell independent response) |
| Conjugate | Hib, PCV, MenACWY | Polysaccharide + protein carrier → converts to T-cell dependent response → effective in infants, produces memory/booster response |

- **Why conjugate > plain polysaccharide in infants**: polysaccharide antigens alone are T-cell independent (poor response in children <2 years, no immunologic memory); conjugation to a carrier protein recruits T-cell help → robust response even in infants, plus memory (booster effect)
- Live vaccines generally contraindicated in pregnancy and immunocompromised states (HIV with low CD4, chemotherapy, high-dose steroids)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Type I hypersensitivity = IgE-mediated, immediate (anaphylaxis, asthma); Type IV = only cell-mediated type, no antibody involved (Mantoux, contact dermatitis)
- Type II = antibody against fixed cell-surface antigen; Type III = antibody against soluble antigen forming circulating immune complexes
- Only IgG crosses the placenta; elevated neonatal IgM signals congenital (intrauterine) infection
- IgM is the best complement activator and the first antibody in a primary immune response
- Terminal complement (C5-C9/MAC) deficiency predisposes to recurrent *Neisseria* infections
- C3a/C5a = anaphylatoxins; C5a is also a major neutrophil chemoattractant
- Conjugate vaccines (Hib, PCV) convert T-cell-independent polysaccharide responses into T-cell-dependent ones — essential for efficacy in infants
- Live attenuated vaccines are contraindicated in pregnancy and immunocompromised patients; killed/subunit/toxoid vaccines are safe
- OPV causes vaccine-associated paralytic polio (rare); IPV does not, but IPV lacks the mucosal/herd immunity benefit of OPV
- BCG protects mainly against disseminated/severe childhood TB (meningitis, miliary), not reliably against adult pulmonary TB
- Hereditary angioedema results from C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency, leading to unchecked complement/kinin activation
