# Mycobacteria Anaerobes

## Mycobacterium tuberculosis

### Key Features
- Acid-fast bacilli (AFB) — mycolic acid-rich cell wall resists decolorization by acid-alcohol
- Obligate aerobe, slow growing (generation time 15–20 h)
- Niacin test positive, catalase weakly positive

### Staining
| Stain | Principle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN)** | Carbol fuchsin, decolorized with acid-alcohol, counterstain methylene blue | AFB appear red/pink against blue background; gold-standard rapid smear |
| **Kinyoun** | Cold acid-fast stain | No heating required |
| **Auramine-rhodamine** | Fluorescent stain | Used with fluorescence microscopy — faster screening, more sensitive than ZN |

### Culture
| Medium | Type | Time to Growth |
|---|---|---|
| **Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) medium** | Egg-based, contains malachite green (inhibits contaminants) | 3–6 weeks (gold standard solid medium) |
| **Middlebrook 7H10/7H11** | Agar-based | Faster than LJ, allows colony morphology/drug susceptibility |
| **BACTEC/MGIT (liquid culture)** | Radiometric/fluorescence-based, liquid | 1–2 weeks — much faster |

### Diagnosis (Modern High-Yield)
- **CBNAAT/Xpert MTB-RIF** (Cartridge-Based Nucleic Acid Amplification Test): rapid PCR-based detection + rifampicin resistance in ~2 hours — now first-line under RNTCP/NTEP (India)
- **Tuberculin skin test (Mantoux)**: delayed-type (Type IV) hypersensitivity to PPD; indicates past exposure/latent TB, not active disease
- **IGRA (Interferon Gamma Release Assay)**: measures IFN-γ release by sensitized T-cells; not affected by BCG vaccination (advantage over Mantoux)
- Chest X-ray: primary complex (Ghon focus + hilar lymphadenopathy), post-primary — upper lobe cavitation

### Treatment (DOTS/RNTCP-NTEP regimen concept)
- Intensive phase: **HRZE** (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol) for 2 months
- Continuation phase: **HR** (Isoniazid, Rifampicin) for 4 months
- Key drug toxicities: Isoniazid → peripheral neuropathy (pyridoxine deficiency, prevent with B6), hepatotoxicity; Rifampicin → orange-red body secretions, hepatotoxicity, potent CYP450 inducer; Pyrazinamide → hyperuricemia, hepatotoxicity; Ethambutol → optic neuritis (red-green color vision loss)
- MDR-TB: resistant to Isoniazid + Rifampicin (at minimum); XDR-TB: MDR + resistance to fluoroquinolone + one second-line injectable

## Mycobacterium leprae
- Cannot be cultured in vitro (obligate intracellular, grown only in mouse footpad/armadillo)
- Stained with **Fite-Faraco** stain (modified AFB stain for skin biopsy)
- Two polar clinical forms based on cell-mediated immunity:

| Feature | Tuberculoid (TT) | Lepromatous (LL) |
|---|---|---|
| Immunity | Strong CMI (Th1) | Weak CMI, humoral response dominant (Th2) |
| Bacillary load | Paucibacillary (few/absent AFB) | Multibacillary (numerous AFB, "globi") |
| Lepromin test | Positive | Negative |
| Lesions | Few, well-defined, hypopigmented, anesthetic | Multiple, diffuse, symmetric, leonine facies |

- Treatment: **MDT (Multi-Drug Therapy)** — Rifampicin + Dapsone (+ Clofazimine for multibacillary) per WHO regimen
- Lepra reactions: Type 1 (reversal reaction, shift in CMI) and Type 2 (ENL — erythema nodosum leprosum, immune complex-mediated, associated with high bacillary load)

## Atypical (Non-Tuberculous) Mycobacteria
| Species | Runyon Group | Disease |
|---|---|---|
| *M. avium-intracellulare* (MAC) | III (non-photochromogen) | Disseminated infection in AIDS (CD4 <50) |
| *M. kansasii* | I (photochromogen) | Pulmonary disease resembling TB |
| *M. fortuitum/chelonae* | IV (rapid grower) | Skin/soft tissue infections, post-surgical |

## Anaerobic Bacteria

### Clostridium (Gram-Positive, Spore-Forming Anaerobic Bacilli)
| Species | Disease | Toxin/Mechanism | Key Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| *C. tetani* | Tetanus | Tetanospasmin — blocks release of inhibitory neurotransmitters (GABA, glycine) at spinal cord → spastic paralysis | "Drumstick" spores; wound with devitalized tissue; prevention: tetanus toxoid (active) + TIG (passive, for high-risk wounds) |
| *C. botulinum* | Botulism | Botulinum toxin — blocks ACh release at NMJ (presynaptic) → flaccid descending paralysis | Canned/improperly preserved food; infant botulism from honey (spores germinate in immature gut) |
| *C. perfringens* | Gas gangrene, food poisoning | Alpha toxin (lecithinase/phospholipase C) → myonecrosis, gas production | Crepitus on exam; also causes late-onset food poisoning (heat-labile enterotoxin) |
| *C. difficile* | Pseudomembranous colitis | Toxin A (enterotoxin) and Toxin B (cytotoxin) | Associated with antibiotic use (clindamycin, fluoroquinolones classic); diagnosis via toxin EIA/PCR on stool; treat with oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin (metronidazole now second-line) |

### Other Anaerobes
| Organism | Notes |
|---|---|
| *Bacteroides fragilis* | Most common anaerobic gram-negative bacilli in gut; intra-abdominal abscesses post-appendicitis/bowel surgery; resistant to penicillin (beta-lactamase) — treat with metronidazole/carbapenems |
| *Actinomyces israelii* | Gram-positive branching filamentous anaerobe; "sulfur granules" in draining sinuses/abscess (cervicofacial actinomycosis, associated with dental infection/IUD use); treat with prolonged penicillin |
| *Fusobacterium* | Lemierre syndrome (septic thrombophlebitis of internal jugular vein following pharyngitis) |

### Anaerobic Culture Techniques
- **Robertson's Cooked Meat (RCM) medium**: enrichment for anaerobes
- Anaerobic jar/GasPak system, thioglycollate broth (redox indicator shows oxygen gradient)
- Foul-smelling discharge, gas in tissue, infection near mucosal surfaces (gut, oral cavity) — clinical clues favoring anaerobes

## High-Yield One-Liners
- ZN stain: red AFB on blue background; carbol fuchsin resists acid-alcohol decolorization due to mycolic acid
- LJ medium (egg-based, malachite green) is the classic solid culture for TB; BACTEC/MGIT liquid culture is faster
- CBNAAT (Xpert MTB/RIF) simultaneously detects TB and rifampicin resistance — cornerstone of current national TB program
- IGRA is unaffected by BCG vaccination, unlike Mantoux test
- Ethambutol → optic neuritis (red-green color blindness); Isoniazid → peripheral neuropathy (prevent with pyridoxine)
- Lepromatous leprosy = multibacillary, negative lepromin, Th2-driven; tuberculoid = paucibacillary, positive lepromin, Th1-driven
- Tetanospasmin blocks inhibitory neurotransmitters → spastic paralysis; botulinum toxin blocks ACh release → flaccid paralysis
- *C. difficile* colitis classically follows clindamycin or fluoroquinolone use; treat with oral vancomycin/fidaxomicin
- Sulfur granules in draining sinus tracts = *Actinomyces israelii*
- *Bacteroides fragilis* = most common anaerobe in intra-abdominal abscess, intrinsically penicillin-resistant
- Lemierre syndrome (internal jugular septic thrombophlebitis) is linked to *Fusobacterium* after oropharyngeal infection
