# Thyroid Disorders

## Thyroid Function Tests — Interpretation
| TSH | Free T4 | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Low | High | Primary hyperthyroidism |
| High | Low | Primary hypothyroidism |
| High | High/Normal | TSH-secreting pituitary tumor / thyroid hormone resistance (rare) |
| Low | Low/Normal | Secondary (pituitary) hypothyroidism |
| High | Normal | Subclinical hypothyroidism |
| Low | Normal | Subclinical hyperthyroidism |

## Hyperthyroidism

### Etiology
| Cause | Feature |
|---|---|
| Graves' disease | Most common cause overall; autoimmune, TSH-receptor antibodies (TSI); diffuse goiter, exophthalmos, pretibial myxedema |
| Toxic multinodular goiter | Common in elderly, iodine-deficient regions |
| Toxic adenoma (Plummer's disease) | Single hyperfunctioning nodule |
| Thyroiditis (subacute/de Quervain's, postpartum) | Transient hyperthyroidism → may progress to hypothyroidism |
| Iodine-induced (Jod-Basedow) | Excess iodine in susceptible thyroid |

### Clinical Features
- Weight loss despite ↑appetite, heat intolerance, palpitations, tremors, sweating, anxiety, oligomenorrhea, diarrhea
- Tachycardia/AF, warm moist skin, fine tremor, lid lag, proximal muscle weakness
- Graves'-specific: exophthalmos, diplopia, pretibial myxedema, thyroid acropachy

### Investigations
- TSH (low), free T4/T3 (high)
- TSI/TRAb positive in Graves'
- Radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU): diffuse ↑ uptake in Graves', focal ↑ in toxic adenoma, ↓ uptake in thyroiditis/exogenous thyroid hormone (factitious)

### Management
| Modality | Notes |
|---|---|
| Beta-blockers (propranolol) | Symptomatic control (tachycardia, tremor) — first step, especially before definitive therapy |
| Antithyroid drugs | Methimazole (preferred, except 1st trimester pregnancy — teratogenic); PTU preferred in 1st trimester and thyroid storm (also blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion); agranulocytosis and hepatotoxicity are key side effects to monitor |
| Radioactive iodine (I-131) | Definitive treatment for Graves'/toxic nodules; contraindicated in pregnancy/breastfeeding; may worsen ophthalmopathy |
| Surgery (thyroidectomy) | Large goiter, suspicion of malignancy, pregnancy (if drug intolerant), patient preference |

### Thyroid Storm
- Life-threatening exacerbation: high fever, extreme tachycardia/arrhythmia, altered mentation, precipitated by infection, surgery, iodine load
- Management: PTU (blocks synthesis + peripheral conversion) + iodine solution (Lugol's, given after antithyroid drug to avoid Jod-Basedow effect) + beta-blockers (propranolol, also mildly blocks T4→T3) + steroids (hydrocortisone, blocks conversion + covers relative adrenal insufficiency) + cooling/supportive care

## Hypothyroidism

### Etiology
| Cause | Feature |
|---|---|
| Hashimoto's thyroiditis | Most common cause overall (in iodine-sufficient areas); anti-TPO/anti-thyroglobulin antibodies |
| Iodine deficiency | Most common cause worldwide (endemic areas) |
| Post-ablative/post-surgical | Post RAI/thyroidectomy |
| Secondary | Pituitary/hypothalamic disease |

### Clinical Features
- Weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, bradycardia, menorrhagia, hoarseness, non-pitting edema (myxedema), delayed relaxation of deep tendon reflexes
- Severe: myxedema coma (hypothermia, altered sensorium, bradycardia, hypotension, hypoventilation) — medical emergency

### Investigations
- TSH (elevated — most sensitive test for primary hypothyroidism), low free T4
- Anti-TPO antibodies (Hashimoto's)
- Lipid profile (hyperlipidemia common)

### Management
- Levothyroxine (T4) replacement — titrate to normalize TSH; take on empty stomach, avoid with calcium/iron (impairs absorption)
- Myxedema coma: IV levothyroxine ± T3, IV hydrocortisone (cover possible coexisting adrenal insufficiency), supportive warming, ventilatory support

## Thyroid Nodules & Cancer
| Cancer type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Papillary carcinoma | Most common thyroid cancer; excellent prognosis; spreads via lymphatics; "Orphan Annie eye" nuclei, psammoma bodies |
| Follicular carcinoma | Hematogenous spread; better prognosis than anaplastic |
| Medullary carcinoma | From C-cells (calcitonin-secreting); associated with MEN 2; amyloid stroma |
| Anaplastic carcinoma | Most aggressive, poor prognosis, elderly patients |

- Workup of thyroid nodule: TSH first, then USG, then fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) if indicated by size/features — FNAC is the investigation of choice for nodule characterization

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Graves' disease = most common cause of hyperthyroidism; Hashimoto's thyroiditis = most common cause of hypothyroidism (iodine-sufficient areas)
- TSH is the single most sensitive screening test for primary thyroid dysfunction
- PTU is preferred over methimazole in first trimester of pregnancy and in thyroid storm
- Methimazole is teratogenic (aplasia cutis, choanal atresia) — avoid in first trimester
- Give antithyroid drug before iodine in thyroid storm to prevent Jod-Basedow-type surge in hormone synthesis
- Myxedema coma and thyroid storm are both medical emergencies with significant mortality if untreated
- FNAC is the investigation of choice for a thyroid nodule; papillary carcinoma is the most common thyroid malignancy with the best prognosis
- Delayed relaxation of ankle jerk reflex is a classic bedside sign of hypothyroidism
