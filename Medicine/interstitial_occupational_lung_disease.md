# Interstitial Occupational Lung Disease

## Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) — Overview
Heterogeneous group of disorders causing diffuse parenchymal lung inflammation/fibrosis → restrictive pattern on PFTs, impaired gas exchange.

### Clinical Features (Common to Most ILDs)
- Progressive exertional dyspnea, dry cough, bibasilar fine "Velcro" crackles, digital clubbing (in fibrotic ILDs)
- Restrictive pattern on spirometry: ↓FEV1, ↓FVC, normal/↑FEV1/FVC ratio, ↓ diffusion capacity (DLCO)

### Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)
- Most common idiopathic interstitial pneumonia; unknown etiology, associated with aging, smoking
- HRCT: usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern — subpleural, basal-predominant reticular changes, honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis, minimal ground-glass
- Diagnosis: characteristic HRCT pattern often sufficient (avoids biopsy); lung biopsy if atypical
- Management: antifibrotic agents (pirfenidone, nintedanib) slow progression (no cure); lung transplant definitive for eligible patients; poor prognosis (median survival ~3–5 years from diagnosis)

### Sarcoidosis
- Multisystem granulomatous disease (non-caseating granulomas), unknown etiology; young adults, more common in certain populations (e.g., African descent in Western data)
- Clinical: bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy (classic CXR finding), erythema nodosum, uveitis, hypercalcemia (due to ectopic vitamin D activation by macrophages), Bell's palsy, cardiac involvement (arrhythmia, heart block)
- Diagnosis: elevated serum ACE (non-specific), biopsy showing non-caseating granulomas, exclusion of other granulomatous diseases (esp. TB — important differential, especially in India)
- Management: observation if asymptomatic/stage I; systemic corticosteroids for symptomatic/progressive/extrapulmonary organ-threatening disease

### Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis
- Immune-mediated reaction to inhaled organic antigens (e.g., moldy hay — "Farmer's lung", bird droppings — "Bird fancier's lung")
- Acute: fever, dyspnea, cough hours after exposure; Chronic: progressive fibrosis
- Management: antigen avoidance (most important), corticosteroids for symptomatic disease

### Connective Tissue Disease-Associated ILD
- Seen in systemic sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, dermatomyositis/polymyositis, SLE
- Systemic sclerosis: NSIP (non-specific interstitial pneumonia) pattern common

## Occupational Lung Diseases (Pneumoconioses)

| Disease | Exposure | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Silicosis | Silica dust (mining, sandblasting, stone-cutting) | Upper lobe fibrotic nodules, "eggshell" calcification of hilar nodes; increased TB risk (silicotuberculosis); progressive massive fibrosis in advanced disease |
| Coal Worker's Pneumoconiosis (CWP) | Coal dust | Simple CWP often asymptomatic; complicated form → progressive massive fibrosis |
| Asbestosis | Asbestos fibers (insulation, shipbuilding, construction) | Lower lobe-predominant fibrosis; pleural plaques (characteristic, often asymptomatic marker of exposure); increased risk of mesothelioma and bronchogenic carcinoma (risk is multiplicative with smoking) |
| Berylliosis | Beryllium (aerospace, electronics) | Granulomatous disease resembling sarcoidosis |
| Byssinosis | Cotton dust | "Monday morning fever/chest tightness" — symptoms worse at start of work week, improve with time away |
| Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (occupational) | Various organic dusts | See above |

*High-yield: Asbestos exposure classically causes lower lobe fibrosis (opposite of silicosis's upper lobe predominance) and is strongly linked to malignant mesothelioma; the malignancy risk (bronchogenic carcinoma) is multiplicative, not merely additive, with cigarette smoking.*

### Diagnosis (Pneumoconioses)
- Detailed occupational history is essential
- HRCT: pattern and distribution help distinguish etiologies
- PFTs: typically restrictive; mixed pattern possible with concurrent COPD (e.g., coal, silica exposure also causes airway disease)

### Management (Pneumoconioses)
- No specific cure — primarily preventive (dust control, protective equipment, exposure limits)
- Supportive care, treat complications (TB screening/treatment in silicosis, monitor for malignancy in asbestos exposure)
- Avoid further exposure; smoking cessation critical (especially asbestos-exposed)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- UIP pattern on HRCT (subpleural, basal honeycombing) is characteristic of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
- Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy + erythema nodosum + uveitis suggests sarcoidosis (Löfgren syndrome variant)
- Non-caseating granulomas on biopsy = sarcoidosis; caseating granulomas = TB — key histological differentiator
- Silicosis → upper lobe fibrosis + increased TB risk; asbestosis → lower lobe fibrosis + pleural plaques + mesothelioma risk
- Smoking and asbestos exposure have a multiplicative (not additive) effect on lung cancer risk
- Byssinosis classically causes symptoms that are worst on Monday (first day back at work) and improve through the week
- Antigen avoidance is the single most important intervention in hypersensitivity pneumonitis
- Restrictive pattern (↓FEV1, ↓FVC, normal/increased FEV1/FVC, ↓DLCO) is the classic PFT signature of ILD
