# Dyslipidemia Metabolic Syndrome

## Lipid Profile Basics
- LDL: atherogenic, primary treatment target
- HDL: protective ("good cholesterol"), inversely related to cardiovascular risk
- Triglycerides: elevated levels associated with pancreatitis risk (severe hypertriglyceridemia) and cardiovascular risk
- Total cholesterol = LDL + HDL + (TG/5) (Friedewald equation — invalid if TG >400 mg/dL)

## Classification of Dyslipidemia
| Type | Lipid abnormality | Cause examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary/familial | Genetic (e.g., familial hypercholesterolemia — LDL receptor defect) | Autosomal dominant, very high LDL, premature CAD, tendon xanthomas |
| Secondary | Acquired | Diabetes, hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, alcohol, obesity, certain drugs (steroids, thiazides, protease inhibitors) |

- Familial hypercholesterolemia: heterozygous → LDL often >190 mg/dL, premature atherosclerosis; homozygous → very severe, LDL often >500 mg/dL, xanthomas in childhood, MI before age 20 possible

## Metabolic Syndrome
Cluster of interrelated risk factors driven by insulin resistance/central obesity — increases risk of cardiovascular disease and type 2 DM.

### Diagnostic Criteria (NCEP-ATP III style — need ≥3 of 5)
| Component | Cutoff |
|---|---|
| Waist circumference | >102 cm (men), >88 cm (women) — Asian-specific lower cutoffs often used (~90/80 cm) |
| Triglycerides | ≥150 mg/dL |
| HDL | <40 mg/dL (men), <50 mg/dL (women) |
| Blood pressure | ≥130/85 mmHg (or on treatment) |
| Fasting glucose | ≥100 mg/dL (or on treatment) |

*High-yield: Indian/South Asian patients have lower waist circumference thresholds for abdominal obesity due to greater visceral adiposity at lower BMI — a well-known exam point.*

## Management

### Lifestyle (First-line, All Patients)
- Diet modification (reduce saturated fat/trans fat, increase fiber), weight loss, exercise, smoking cessation, alcohol moderation

### Pharmacologic
| Drug class | Effect | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Statins | ↓↓LDL (primary agent) | First-line for LDL-lowering; mortality benefit in ASCVD; risk: myopathy/rhabdomyolysis, hepatotoxicity — monitor LFTs/CK if symptomatic |
| Ezetimibe | ↓LDL (moderate, blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption) | Added if statin alone insufficient |
| PCSK9 inhibitors | ↓↓LDL (potent) | For very high risk/statin-intolerant/familial hypercholesterolemia; expensive, injectable |
| Fibrates | ↓↓Triglycerides | First-line for severe hypertriglyceridemia (pancreatitis prevention); modest HDL rise |
| Niacin | ↓TG, ↑HDL | Largely fallen out of favor — flushing, no added CV outcome benefit in combination trials |
| Omega-3 fatty acids | ↓Triglycerides | Adjunct for severe hypertriglyceridemia |

- Statin intensity and LDL targets are risk-stratified (primary vs secondary prevention, diabetes, established ASCVD) — current guidelines favor "lower is better" for very high-risk patients; specific numeric LDL goals vary by guideline — verify against latest guideline
- Severe hypertriglyceridemia (>500 mg/dL, especially >1000 mg/dL) → treat urgently to prevent acute pancreatitis; fibrates are first-line here (statins are not primarily for TG reduction)

## Complications of Dyslipidemia
- Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (CAD, stroke, PAD)
- Acute pancreatitis (severe hypertriglyceridemia)
- Xanthomas/xanthelasma, corneal arcus (familial hypercholesterolemia)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Statins are first-line for LDL reduction and have proven mortality benefit in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease
- Fibrates are the treatment of choice for severe hypertriglyceridemia to prevent pancreatitis
- Friedewald equation to estimate LDL is invalid when triglycerides exceed 400 mg/dL
- Metabolic syndrome requires ≥3 of 5 criteria: abdominal obesity, hypertriglyceridemia, low HDL, hypertension, elevated fasting glucose
- South Asians have lower waist circumference cutoffs for central obesity due to greater visceral fat at lower BMI
- Homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia can present with childhood MI and markedly elevated LDL (>500 mg/dL)
- Statin-associated myopathy/rhabdomyolysis risk increases with concomitant fibrate (especially gemfibrozil) use — check CK if symptomatic
