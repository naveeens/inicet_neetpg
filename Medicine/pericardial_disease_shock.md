# Pericardial Disease Shock

## Acute Pericarditis

### Etiology
- Viral (most common in developed settings — Coxsackie), idiopathic, tuberculous (common in India — high-yield), bacterial (purulent), uremic, post-MI (Dressler syndrome / early peri-infarction pericarditis), autoimmune (SLE, RA), malignancy, radiation

### Clinical Features
- Sharp retrosternal chest pain, pleuritic, worse lying flat, relieved by sitting forward/leaning forward
- Pericardial friction rub (classic finding, scratchy, best heard at left sternal border, leaning forward, expiration)

### ECG Findings
- Diffuse ST elevation (concave/saddle-shaped) with PR depression across multiple leads (not localized to one territory, unlike STEMI)
- PR elevation and ST depression in aVR (reciprocal)
- Stages: I (diffuse ST elevation) → II (normalization) → III (T wave inversion) → IV (resolution)

*High-yield exam trap: Differentiate from STEMI — pericarditis has diffuse (not localized) ST elevation, no reciprocal ST depression pattern typical of MI, and PR depression.*

### Management
- NSAIDs/aspirin + colchicine (colchicine reduces recurrence — landmark trial evidence)
- Treat underlying cause (e.g., ATT for TB pericarditis)
- Avoid NSAIDs in post-MI pericarditis if possible (interferes with healing) — aspirin preferred in that setting

## Pericardial Effusion / Cardiac Tamponade

### Pathophysiology
Fluid accumulation in pericardial space → if rapid/large → compresses heart chambers → impairs diastolic filling → ↓ cardiac output.

### Clinical Features — Beck's Triad (Tamponade)
1. Hypotension
2. Raised JVP (distended neck veins)
3. Muffled/distant heart sounds

- Pulsus paradoxus: exaggerated (>10 mmHg) fall in systolic BP during inspiration — classic tamponade sign
- Kussmaul's sign (JVP rises with inspiration) — more classic for constrictive pericarditis, but can occur in tamponade

### Investigations
- Echocardiography: diagnostic test of choice — effusion, diastolic collapse of RV (early sign), RA collapse, respiratory variation in mitral/tricuspid inflow, IVC plethora
- ECG: low-voltage QRS, electrical alternans (beat-to-beat variation in QRS amplitude — swinging heart)
- CXR: "water bottle" shaped cardiac silhouette if large chronic effusion

### Management
- Emergency pericardiocentesis if hemodynamically unstable
- Treat underlying cause
- IV fluids as temporizing measure (maintain preload) while arranging drainage

## Constrictive Pericarditis
- Chronic pericardial thickening/fibrosis/calcification (often post-TB, post-radiation, post-cardiac surgery in India TB is classic cause) → impaired diastolic filling
- Clinical: right heart failure signs (ascites, edema, raised JVP), Kussmaul's sign, pericardial knock
- Differentiate from restrictive cardiomyopathy (mimics clinically) — CT/MRI showing pericardial thickening/calcification favors constriction; endomyocardial biopsy may be needed
- Treatment: pericardiectomy (definitive) for symptomatic cases

## Shock — Overview and Classification

| Type | Mechanism | Examples | Hemodynamics (CO, SVR, PCWP) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypovolemic | Reduced intravascular volume | Hemorrhage, dehydration, burns | ↓CO, ↑SVR, ↓PCWP |
| Cardiogenic | Pump failure | MI, arrhythmia, severe HF | ↓CO, ↑SVR, ↑PCWP |
| Obstructive | Mechanical obstruction to flow | Tamponade, massive PE, tension pneumothorax | ↓CO, ↑SVR, variable PCWP |
| Distributive | Peripheral vasodilation | Septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic | ↑/normal CO (early sepsis), ↓SVR, ↓PCWP |

### Clinical Features (Common to All)
- Hypotension, tachycardia, cold clammy skin (except distributive — warm shock early), altered mentation, oliguria, lactic acidosis (tissue hypoperfusion marker)

### Septic Shock (Distributive)
- Sepsis + hypotension despite adequate fluid resuscitation, requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥65, with elevated lactate
- Management: early broad-spectrum antibiotics (within 1 hour), IV fluids (crystalloids), vasopressors (norepinephrine first-line) if fluid-refractory, source control

### Cardiogenic Shock
- Most commonly post-MI (extensive LV damage, or mechanical complications like VSD/papillary muscle rupture)
- Management: inotropes/vasopressors (dobutamine, norepinephrine), revascularization (PCI) if ischemic cause, mechanical circulatory support (IABP, Impella, ECMO) in refractory cases; avoid excessive fluids

### Hypovolemic/Hemorrhagic Shock
- Classified by % blood loss (Class I–IV, ATLS)
- Management: control bleeding/source, IV fluids/blood products, permissive hypotension in trauma until hemorrhage controlled

## Mnemonic
Beck's Triad for tamponade: **"Beck's Triad = Distant/muffled heart sounds, Distended neck veins, Decreased BP (hypotension)"**

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Pericarditis ECG: diffuse concave ST elevation + PR depression, ST depression/PR elevation in aVR — differs from localized STEMI pattern
- Cardiac tamponade: Beck's triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds) + pulsus paradoxus
- Echocardiography is the investigation of choice for pericardial effusion and tamponade
- Electrical alternans on ECG is a classic (though insensitive) sign of large pericardial effusion
- Constrictive pericarditis in India — think tuberculosis as the leading cause
- Colchicine reduces recurrence of acute and recurrent pericarditis — added to NSAIDs/aspirin
- Septic shock = warm/vasodilated shock; cardiogenic/hypovolemic/obstructive = cold shock with high SVR
- Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor in septic shock
