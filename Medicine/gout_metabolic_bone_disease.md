# Gout Metabolic Bone Disease

## Gout

### Pathophysiology
Deposition of monosodium urate crystals in joints/tissues due to hyperuricemia (overproduction or underexcretion of uric acid) → acute inflammatory arthritis.

### Etiology/Risk Factors
- Overproduction: high purine intake (red meat, seafood, alcohol especially beer), myeloproliferative disorders, tumor lysis syndrome, Lesch-Nyhan syndrome (HGPRT deficiency)
- Underexcretion (more common overall): chronic kidney disease, diuretics (thiazides, loop), low-dose aspirin
- Note: hyperuricemia alone is common and does NOT require treatment unless symptomatic (gout) or in specific high-risk scenarios (e.g., tumor lysis prophylaxis)

### Clinical Features
- Acute gouty arthritis: sudden onset, severe pain, swelling, erythema — classically first MTP joint (podagra), also ankle/knee; often nocturnal onset, precipitated by trauma, alcohol, dietary indiscretion, illness, or starting/stopping urate-lowering therapy abruptly
- Chronic tophaceous gout: tophi (urate deposits) in soft tissue/joints, chronic joint damage with recurrent undertreated disease

### Diagnosis
- Joint aspiration (synovial fluid analysis): needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals under polarized light — gold standard, also rules out septic arthritis (always consider septic arthritis as a differential, can coexist)
- Serum uric acid: often elevated but can be normal during an acute attack (not reliable alone for diagnosis during flare)
- X-ray (chronic disease): "rat bite" erosions, overhanging edges (Martel's sign)

### Management
| Phase | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Acute attack | NSAIDs (first-line if no contraindication), colchicine (effective but narrow therapeutic index — GI side effects), corticosteroids (oral/intra-articular) if NSAIDs/colchicine contraindicated |
| Chronic/urate-lowering therapy (started after acute attack resolves, not during) | Xanthine oxidase inhibitors — allopurinol (first-line) or febuxostat; uricosurics (probenecid) as alternative; indicated for recurrent attacks, tophi, or urate nephropathy/renal stones |

*High-yield: Do not start urate-lowering therapy during an acute gout attack — it can paradoxically worsen/prolong the flare by mobilizing crystals; start only after the acute attack has resolved, with anti-inflammatory prophylaxis (colchicine/NSAID) during initiation to prevent flares.*

- Allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome: severe, potentially fatal reaction — HLA-B*5801 allele is a risk factor (screening considered in certain populations, e.g., some Asian populations)

## Pseudogout (CPPD Disease)
- Calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate crystal deposition — rhomboid-shaped, positively birefringent crystals
- Classically affects the knee; associated with hemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, hypothyroidism, hypomagnesemia
- Chondrocalcinosis on X-ray (cartilage calcification)
- Management: similar to acute gout (NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids) — no specific "urate-lowering" equivalent since underlying metabolism differs

## Osteoporosis

### Definition/Pathophysiology
Reduced bone mineral density and microarchitectural deterioration → increased fracture risk. Primary (postmenopausal — estrogen deficiency; senile — age-related) or secondary (steroids, hyperthyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, immobilization).

### Diagnosis
- DEXA scan: T-score (compares to young healthy adult) — T-score ≤ -2.5 = osteoporosis; -1.0 to -2.5 = osteopenia; used in postmenopausal women/older men
- Z-score used in younger patients/premenopausal women (compares to age-matched norms)
- FRAX score: estimates 10-year fracture risk, incorporates clinical risk factors + BMD

### Management
- Lifestyle: adequate calcium/vitamin D intake, weight-bearing exercise, fall prevention, smoking/alcohol cessation
- Pharmacologic: bisphosphonates (first-line — alendronate, risedronate; risk of osteonecrosis of jaw and atypical femoral fractures with long-term use, though rare), denosumab (RANKL inhibitor, alternative especially in renal impairment where bisphosphonates are relatively contraindicated), teriparatide (PTH analog — anabolic agent, reserved for severe osteoporosis/very high fracture risk), selective estrogen receptor modulators (raloxifene), hormone replacement therapy (limited role given cardiovascular/breast cancer risk considerations — individualized)

## Osteomalacia / Rickets
- Defective mineralization of bone matrix due to vitamin D deficiency (or less commonly phosphate deficiency/renal tubular disorders)
- Rickets = pediatric form (before growth plate closure): bowing of legs, rachitic rosary, widened wrists, delayed fontanelle closure, craniotabes
- Osteomalacia = adult form: diffuse bone pain, proximal muscle weakness, increased fracture risk (especially pseudofractures/Looser zones on X-ray)
- Labs: low vitamin D, low/normal calcium, low phosphate, elevated alkaline phosphatase, secondary hyperparathyroidism
- Management: vitamin D + calcium supplementation

## Paget's Disease of Bone
- Disorganized bone remodeling (excessive osteoclastic resorption followed by disorganized osteoblastic new bone formation) → structurally weak, enlarged bone
- Often asymptomatic, found incidentally (elevated alkaline phosphatase with normal calcium/phosphate — classic lab pattern); bone pain, deformity, fractures, deafness (if skull involvement compresses cranial nerves) in symptomatic cases
- Increased risk of osteosarcoma (rare but important complication)
- X-ray: mixed lytic/sclerotic lesions, cortical thickening
- Management: bisphosphonates for symptomatic/high-risk disease

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Podagra (first MTP joint) is the classic initial presentation of acute gout
- Negatively birefringent, needle-shaped crystals = gout; positively birefringent, rhomboid crystals = pseudogout (CPPD)
- Never initiate urate-lowering therapy during an acute gout flare — start only after resolution, with anti-inflammatory prophylaxis
- Allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome is linked to HLA-B*5801
- DEXA T-score ≤ -2.5 defines osteoporosis
- Bisphosphonates are first-line for osteoporosis; denosumab is preferred in significant renal impairment
- Elevated alkaline phosphatase with normal calcium and phosphate is the classic lab pattern of Paget's disease of bone
- Osteomalacia/rickets shows low vitamin D, low phosphate, elevated alkaline phosphatase, and secondary hyperparathyroidism
- Always rule out septic arthritis when aspirating a joint for suspected gout — the two can coexist
