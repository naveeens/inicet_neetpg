# Inflammatory Bowel Disease

## Overview
Chronic idiopathic inflammatory conditions of the GI tract — Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. Multifactorial etiology (genetic susceptibility, dysregulated mucosal immune response, gut microbiome, environmental triggers).

## Crohn's Disease vs Ulcerative Colitis — Key Differentiators
| Feature | Crohn's Disease | Ulcerative Colitis |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Anywhere mouth to anus, terminal ileum most common; skip lesions | Colon only, starts in rectum, continuous, extends proximally |
| Depth of inflammation | Transmural (full thickness) | Mucosal/submucosal only |
| Distribution | Skip lesions (patchy, "skip areas") | Continuous, no skipping |
| Rectal involvement | Often spared | Almost always involved |
| Gross pathology | Cobblestone mucosa, creeping fat, strictures | Pseudopolyps, continuous ulceration |
| Fistula/abscess | Common (transmural inflammation) | Rare |
| Granulomas | Non-caseating granulomas (characteristic, but not always seen) | Absent |
| Smoking | Worsens disease (risk factor) | Protective (paradoxically) — but not a treatment recommendation |
| Bleeding | Less prominent | Prominent (bloody diarrhea classic) |
| Malignancy risk | Increased but less than UC per unit colon involved | Increased colorectal cancer risk with extensive/longstanding disease |
| Serology | ASCA more often positive | p-ANCA more often positive |

## Clinical Features
- Crohn's: abdominal pain (often RLQ, mimics appendicitis), diarrhea (non-bloody more common), weight loss, perianal disease (fistulas, fissures, abscesses), extraintestinal manifestations
- UC: bloody diarrhea, tenesmus, urgency, lower abdominal cramping, rarely presents with pure constipation

### Extraintestinal Manifestations (Both, Overlapping)
- Musculoskeletal: peripheral arthritis (parallels bowel activity), ankylosing spondylitis/sacroiliitis (independent of bowel activity)
- Skin: erythema nodosum (parallels activity), pyoderma gangrenosum (more UC-associated, doesn't always parallel activity)
- Eye: uveitis, episcleritis
- Hepatobiliary: primary sclerosing cholangitis (strong association with UC — important link, monitor for cholangiocarcinoma risk)

## Diagnosis
- Colonoscopy with biopsy: gold standard — assess distribution, depth, histology
- Imaging: CT/MR enterography for Crohn's (small bowel involvement, strictures, fistulas, abscess)
- Fecal calprotectin: marker of intestinal inflammation, useful to differentiate IBD from functional (IBS-type) disease and to monitor activity
- Serology: p-ANCA (UC), ASCA (Crohn's) — supportive, not diagnostic alone

## Complications
| Crohn's | Ulcerative Colitis |
|---|---|
| Strictures, fistulas (enteroenteric, enterocutaneous, enterovesical), abscesses, malabsorption (B12 deficiency if terminal ileum involved), short bowel syndrome | Toxic megacolon (life-threatening, colonic dilation >6 cm + systemic toxicity), massive hemorrhage, increased colorectal cancer risk (surveillance colonoscopy needed after ~8-10 years of disease) |

*High-yield: Toxic megacolon is a classic, life-threatening complication of severe UC (can also occur in Crohn's colitis or infectious colitis) — avoid antimotility agents and opioids which can precipitate it.*

## Management

### General Approach (Both)
| Severity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild-moderate | 5-ASA compounds (mesalamine — more effective in UC than Crohn's), topical therapy for distal UC |
| Moderate-severe / flares | Systemic corticosteroids (induction only, not maintenance — significant side effect burden with long-term use) |
| Steroid-dependent/refractory / maintenance | Immunomodulators (azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine, methotrexate) |
| Moderate-severe/refractory | Biologics — anti-TNF (infliximab, adalimumab), anti-integrin (vedolizumab), anti-IL12/23 (ustekinumab) — choice individualized |
| Fulminant/refractory UC | Colectomy (curative for UC, unlike Crohn's where surgery is not curative and recurrence is common post-resection) |

*High-yield: Colectomy is curative in ulcerative colitis (disease confined to colon/rectum) but not in Crohn's disease, which can recur anywhere in the GI tract after resection — a key surgical decision-making distinction.*

### Specific Notes
- Toxic megacolon: bowel rest, IV steroids, broad-spectrum antibiotics, surgery if no improvement/perforation
- Crohn's perianal fistulizing disease: antibiotics (metronidazole/ciprofloxacin), anti-TNF agents, surgical drainage of abscess

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Crohn's disease = transmural inflammation, skip lesions, can affect any GI segment, granulomas; UC = mucosal inflammation, continuous, colon/rectum only, no granulomas
- Bloody diarrhea with tenesmus points to UC; RLQ pain mimicking appendicitis with skip lesions points to Crohn's
- p-ANCA associates with UC; ASCA associates with Crohn's disease
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis is strongly associated with ulcerative colitis
- Smoking worsens Crohn's disease but appears protective in ulcerative colitis (not a recommended intervention)
- Colectomy cures ulcerative colitis but is not curative in Crohn's disease due to recurrence risk
- Toxic megacolon is a surgical emergency — avoid opioids and antimotility agents in severe colitis
- Fecal calprotectin helps distinguish organic IBD from functional bowel disease and tracks disease activity
