# Diabetes Mellitus Emergencies

## Diabetes Mellitus — Diagnostic Criteria
| Test | Diabetes | Prediabetes |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting plasma glucose | ≥126 mg/dL | 100–125 mg/dL (IFG) |
| 2-hr OGTT (75 g) | ≥200 mg/dL | 140–199 mg/dL (IGT) |
| HbA1c | ≥6.5% | 5.7–6.4% |
| Random glucose | ≥200 mg/dL + symptoms | — |

- Any one abnormal test confirmed on a separate day (or symptomatic + single random value ≥200) confirms diagnosis

## Classification
- Type 1 DM: autoimmune beta-cell destruction, absolute insulin deficiency, ketosis-prone, usually younger age
- Type 2 DM: insulin resistance + relative deficiency, associated with obesity/metabolic syndrome
- Gestational DM, monogenic (MODY), secondary (pancreatitis, steroids, Cushing's)

## Chronic Complications
| Microvascular | Macrovascular |
|---|---|
| Retinopathy (leading cause of adult blindness) | Coronary artery disease |
| Nephropathy (leading cause of CKD) | Stroke |
| Neuropathy (peripheral, autonomic) | Peripheral artery disease |

- Diabetic nephropathy: earliest marker = microalbuminuria; nodular glomerulosclerosis (Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules) is the classic pathological finding
- Diabetic neuropathy: symmetric distal sensorimotor polyneuropathy most common; monitor with 10 g monofilament testing

## Management of DM (Overview)
- Lifestyle modification for all
- T1DM: insulin (basal-bolus regimen) — mandatory, life-long
- T2DM: Metformin first-line (unless contraindicated — eGFR <30, contrast studies, severe illness/lactic acidosis risk); add SGLT2 inhibitors/GLP-1 agonists especially if cardiovascular/renal disease (cardiorenal benefit shown in landmark trials — current guidelines favor these early in high-risk patients); sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, insulin as needed
- Target HbA1c generally <7% for most adults (individualize — less strict in elderly/comorbid, stricter in newly diagnosed young patients) — verify against latest guideline

## Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)

### Pathophysiology
Absolute/severe insulin deficiency + counter-regulatory hormone excess (glucagon, catecholamines, cortisol) → unrestrained lipolysis → ketogenesis + hyperglycemia (osmotic diuresis → dehydration).

### Precipitating Factors
Infection (most common), insulin omission/non-compliance, new-onset T1DM, MI, stress

### Diagnostic Criteria
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Blood glucose | Usually >250 mg/dL |
| Arterial pH | <7.3 |
| Serum bicarbonate | <18 mEq/L |
| Ketones | Positive (serum/urine) |
| Anion gap | Elevated (>10-12) |

### Clinical Features
- Polyuria, polydipsia, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain (can mimic acute abdomen), Kussmaul breathing (compensatory hyperventilation), fruity (acetone) breath odor, altered sensorium/coma in severe cases

### Management
1. IV fluids: normal saline, aggressive initial resuscitation, then reassess
2. Insulin: IV regular insulin infusion (0.1 U/kg/hr) — do NOT start insulin before addressing hypokalemia if K+ <3.3 mEq/L (insulin drives K into cells → can cause fatal arrhythmia)
3. Potassium replacement: monitor closely — total body K+ is depleted despite normal/high initial serum K+ (due to acidosis-driven extracellular shift)
4. Once glucose reaches ~200–250 mg/dL, add dextrose to fluids and continue insulin (prevents hypoglycemia and cerebral edema, allows ketone clearance)
5. Bicarbonate: only if severe acidosis (pH <6.9) — not routine
6. Identify and treat precipitating cause

*High-yield: Never withhold insulin for a normal/high serum potassium in DKA — total body potassium is always depleted; always check and correct K+ before/with insulin initiation.*

## Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)

| Feature | DKA | HHS |
|---|---|---|
| Typical population | Type 1 DM | Type 2 DM (often elderly) |
| Glucose | Usually >250 mg/dL | Usually >600 mg/dL |
| Ketones | Positive | Absent/minimal |
| pH | <7.3 | >7.3 (usually normal) |
| Osmolality | Variable | Markedly elevated (>320 mOsm/kg) |
| Onset | Rapid (hours) | Gradual (days) |
| Mortality | Lower | Higher (elderly, comorbidities, severe dehydration) |

- Management: aggressive fluid resuscitation is the priority (larger fluid deficit than DKA); insulin at lower rate; correct electrolytes; identify precipitating illness (often infection)

## Hypoglycemia
- Whipple's triad: symptoms consistent with hypoglycemia + low plasma glucose + relief with glucose administration
- Common cause in diabetics: insulin/sulfonylurea excess relative to intake/activity
- Management: oral glucose if conscious; IV dextrose (25–50 g) or IM glucagon if unconscious/unable to swallow

## High-Yield One-Liners
- DKA is defined by triad: hyperglycemia + ketosis + metabolic acidosis (anion gap)
- Never start insulin in DKA if serum K+ <3.3 mEq/L — replace potassium first
- HHS: profound hyperglycemia, minimal/no ketosis, near-normal pH, markedly elevated osmolality — fluid deficit is the priority
- Microalbuminuria is the earliest detectable marker of diabetic nephropathy
- Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules = pathognomonic nodular glomerulosclerosis of diabetic nephropathy
- Metformin is first-line for T2DM but contraindicated in significant renal impairment and lactic acidosis risk states
- SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists now recommended early in T2DM with established cardiovascular/renal disease, independent of glycemic control, per updated evidence
- Fruity breath odor in DKA is due to acetone (a ketone body)
