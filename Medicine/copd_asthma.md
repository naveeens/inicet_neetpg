# COPD Asthma

## COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

### Definition/Etiology
Progressive, largely irreversible airflow limitation due to chronic bronchitis and/or emphysema. Primary cause: cigarette smoking (>90% of cases in developed settings; biomass fuel exposure important cause in India especially in women). Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency → early-onset emphysema (panacinar, lower lobes) in non-smokers/young patients — classic exam association.

### Chronic Bronchitis vs Emphysema
| Feature | Chronic Bronchitis ("Blue Bloater") | Emphysema ("Pink Puffer") |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Productive cough ≥3 months/year for ≥2 consecutive years | Destruction of alveolar walls, airspace enlargement |
| Body habitus | Obese, cyanotic | Thin, barrel chest |
| V/Q mismatch | Significant (hypoxia, cor pulmonale early) | Less pronounced initially |
| Pathology | Mucous gland hyperplasia, Reid index increased | Loss of elastic recoil, alveolar destruction |

### Clinical Features
- Chronic cough, sputum production, progressive exertional dyspnea, wheeze
- Pursed-lip breathing, prolonged expiration, use of accessory muscles, barrel chest (hyperinflation)
- Cor pulmonale in advanced disease (right heart failure from chronic hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction)

### Diagnosis
- Spirometry: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.70 confirms persistent airflow limitation (diagnostic criterion)
- GOLD staging based on FEV1 % predicted (severity) — mild/moderate/severe/very severe
- CXR: hyperinflation, flattened diaphragm; CT: emphysematous changes, bullae

### Management
| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| All stages | Smoking cessation (single most effective intervention to slow decline), vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal) |
| Symptomatic relief | Short-acting bronchodilators (SABA/SAMA) for as-needed use |
| Maintenance (per symptom/exacerbation burden) | Long-acting bronchodilators (LABA/LAMA), combination LABA+LAMA; inhaled corticosteroids added if frequent exacerbations/eosinophilic phenotype (not first-line monotherapy in COPD, unlike asthma) |
| Severe/hypoxemic | Long-term home oxygen therapy — proven mortality benefit if PaO2 ≤55 mmHg or SpO2 ≤88% at rest |
| Very severe/refractory | Lung volume reduction surgery, lung transplant in selected cases |

*High-yield: Only two interventions proven to reduce mortality in COPD — smoking cessation and long-term home oxygen therapy in chronically hypoxemic patients.*

### Acute Exacerbation
- Triggers: infection (viral/bacterial), pollution
- Management: bronchodilators (nebulized SABA/SAMA), systemic corticosteroids (short course), antibiotics if increased sputum purulence/volume or signs of infection, controlled oxygen (avoid over-oxygenation — risk of worsening hypercapnia via loss of hypoxic drive/V-Q effects), non-invasive ventilation if respiratory failure

## Asthma

### Definition/Pathophysiology
Chronic airway inflammation with reversible airflow obstruction, airway hyperresponsiveness, bronchospasm. Th2-mediated (eosinophilic) inflammation in classic allergic asthma.

### Clinical Features
- Episodic wheeze, dyspnea, cough (often nocturnal/early morning), chest tightness
- Triggers: allergens, exercise, cold air, infections, NSAIDs/aspirin (in aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease), emotional stress
- Signs: bilateral wheeze, prolonged expiration; silent chest in life-threatening attack (ominous — no air movement)

### Diagnosis
- Spirometry: obstructive pattern (↓FEV1/FVC), significant reversibility with bronchodilator (FEV1 increase ≥12% and ≥200 mL — supports diagnosis)
- Peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) variability — diurnal variation supports diagnosis, useful for monitoring
- Bronchial provocation testing (methacholine challenge) if diagnosis uncertain and spirometry normal

### Management (Stepwise, per Global Initiative for Asthma-type Approach)
| Step | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild intermittent | As-needed SABA (or as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol per updated approach — reduces exacerbations even in mild disease) |
| Persistent (increasing severity) | Low-dose ICS ± LABA → medium/high-dose ICS+LABA → add-on (LAMA, leukotriene modifier, biologics for severe eosinophilic/allergic asthma) |
| Severe/refractory eosinophilic or allergic asthma | Biologics — anti-IgE (omalizumab), anti-IL5 (mepolizumab), anti-IL4/13 (dupilumab) |

*High-yield: Current guidelines have shifted away from as-needed SABA-only therapy even in mild asthma — as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol is now preferred to reduce exacerbation risk and reduce over-reliance on SABA (major practice change from older "SABA first" teaching) — verify against latest GINA-type guideline.*

### Acute Severe Asthma Exacerbation
- Assess severity: PEFR, ability to speak, accessory muscle use, silent chest, altered sensorium (life-threatening signs)
- Management: high-flow oxygen, nebulized SABA + SAMA (ipratropium), systemic corticosteroids, IV magnesium sulfate in severe cases, consider IV aminophylline/beta-agonist infusion in refractory cases; mechanical ventilation if respiratory failure
- ABG in severe asthma: initially respiratory alkalosis (hyperventilation); a "normalizing" or rising PaCO2 is an ominous sign of impending respiratory failure (fatigue), not improvement

## COPD vs Asthma — Key Differentiators
| Feature | Asthma | COPD |
|---|---|---|
| Reversibility | Largely reversible | Largely irreversible (fixed obstruction) |
| Onset | Often childhood/young adult | Usually >40 years, smoking history |
| Atopy | Common | Uncommon |
| Diurnal variability | Marked | Less marked |

## Mnemonic
Pink puffer = emphysema (thin, pursed-lip breathing, "pink" from compensatory hyperventilation); Blue bloater = chronic bronchitis (cyanotic from V/Q mismatch, "bloated" with edema from cor pulmonale)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Smoking cessation and long-term home oxygen are the only interventions proven to reduce mortality in COPD
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency causes early-onset panacinar emphysema, classically in lower lobes, in young non-smokers
- FEV1/FVC <0.70 post-bronchodilator confirms COPD diagnosis
- Reversibility (FEV1 rise ≥12% and 200 mL post-bronchodilator) favors asthma over COPD
- A silent chest in acute asthma is an ominous sign, not a sign of improvement
- Rising/normalizing PaCO2 during a severe asthma attack signals impending respiratory failure
- Current guidelines favor as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol over SABA-only therapy even in mild asthma
- Avoid uncontrolled high-flow oxygen in COPD exacerbations — target saturation typically 88-92% to avoid worsening hypercapnia
