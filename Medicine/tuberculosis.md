# Tuberculosis

## Etiology/Pathophysiology
Mycobacterium tuberculosis — acid-fast bacilli, aerobic, slow-growing. Transmission via airborne droplet nuclei. Primary infection → contained by cell-mediated immunity (granuloma/Ghon focus) in most; may reactivate later (post-primary/secondary TB) especially with immunosuppression (HIV, malnutrition, diabetes, steroids).

## Primary vs Post-Primary TB
| Feature | Primary TB | Post-primary (reactivation) TB |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Ghon focus — mid/lower lung zones | Apical/posterior segments upper lobes (high O2 tension favors aerobic organism) |
| Lymph node involvement | Prominent (Ghon complex = focus + hilar nodes) | Less prominent |
| Cavitation | Uncommon | Common |

## Clinical Features
- Chronic cough >2 weeks (classic screening criterion), fever (often evening rise), night sweats, weight loss, hemoptysis, anorexia
- Extrapulmonary TB: lymphadenitis (scrofula — most common extrapulmonary site), pleural effusion (exudative, lymphocyte-predominant, low glucose), TB meningitis (basal exudates, cranial nerve palsies), Pott's spine (vertebral TB), abdominal TB, miliary TB (disseminated, "millet seed" nodules on CXR)

## Diagnosis
| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Sputum AFB smear (Ziehl-Neelsen) | Initial screening; rapid, cheap, but low sensitivity |
| CBNAAT / GeneXpert MTB-RIF | Rapid, detects M. tuberculosis DNA + rifampicin resistance simultaneously — now preferred initial diagnostic test under national programs |
| Culture (liquid — MGIT, or solid — Lowenstein-Jensen) | Gold standard for diagnosis; also allows drug susceptibility testing; slow (weeks) |
| Tuberculin skin test (Mantoux) | Detects latent infection/exposure, not active disease; interpretation depends on risk category (induration cutoffs vary: ≥5mm high risk/immunosuppressed, ≥10mm intermediate risk, ≥15mm low risk) |
| IGRA (Interferon Gamma Release Assay) | Latent TB detection, more specific than Mantoux (not affected by BCG vaccination) |
| Chest X-ray | Upper lobe infiltrates/cavitation (post-primary), hilar adenopathy (primary), miliary pattern |

*High-yield: CBNAAT/GeneXpert has largely replaced smear microscopy as the preferred initial test in national TB programs because it gives same-day results and detects rifampicin resistance — a key drug-resistance screen.*

## Treatment — DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course)

### Standard Regimen (Drug-Sensitive TB)
| Phase | Duration | Drugs |
|---|---|---|
| Intensive phase | 2 months | Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E) — "HRZE" |
| Continuation phase | 4 months | Isoniazid + Rifampicin (H R) |

*Note: Exact regimen duration/dosing schedules (daily vs intermittent) have evolved under national programs (e.g., India's RNTCP/NTEP shifted from thrice-weekly to daily dosing) — verify against current national guideline for country-specific specifics.*

### Key Drug Toxicities
| Drug | Toxicity |
|---|---|
| Isoniazid | Peripheral neuropathy (prevent with pyridoxine/vitamin B6), hepatotoxicity, drug-induced lupus |
| Rifampicin | Orange-red discoloration of body fluids (harmless, reassure patient), hepatotoxicity, flu-like syndrome, potent CYP450 inducer (drug interactions — e.g., reduces OCP efficacy) |
| Pyrazinamide | Hepatotoxicity, hyperuricemia (can precipitate gout), arthralgia |
| Ethambutol | Optic neuritis (red-green color blindness, reduced visual acuity) — dose-dependent, monitor vision |
| Streptomycin (if used) | Ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity |

### Drug-Resistant TB
- MDR-TB: resistant to at least isoniazid + rifampicin
- XDR-TB: MDR + resistant to fluoroquinolone + at least one second-line injectable (or per updated definitions, a second-line drug)
- Managed with longer, individualized second-line regimens under specialist supervision (bedaquiline, linezolid, fluoroquinolones, etc. — regimens evolving; verify against current national program guideline)

## Latent TB Infection (LTBI)
- Positive Mantoux/IGRA, no symptoms, normal CXR, not infectious
- Treatment (to prevent reactivation) especially in high-risk groups (HIV, recent contacts, immunosuppressed): isoniazid preventive therapy (6–9 months) or rifampicin-based shorter regimens

## TB in Special Populations
- HIV-TB co-infection: TB is the leading opportunistic infection/cause of death in HIV globally; ART should be started within a few weeks of starting ATT (timing depends on CD4 count) — watch for IRIS (immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome)
- Pregnancy: standard ATT regimen is safe (avoid streptomycin — ototoxic to fetus)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Post-primary (reactivation) TB classically involves the apical/posterior segments of the upper lobes due to higher oxygen tension
- CBNAAT/GeneXpert MTB-RIF gives same-day diagnosis plus rifampicin resistance status
- Isoniazid neuropathy is prevented by co-administering pyridoxine (vitamin B6)
- Ethambutol toxicity = optic neuritis, classically red-green color blindness — monitor visual acuity
- Rifampicin turns body secretions orange-red — a harmless but important patient counseling point
- Rifampicin is a potent CYP450 inducer — reduces efficacy of oral contraceptives and many other drugs
- MDR-TB = resistance to at least isoniazid and rifampicin
- Scrofula (cervical lymphadenitis) is the most common extrapulmonary presentation of TB
- TB meningitis classically shows basal exudates and cranial nerve palsies with lymphocytic CSF, low glucose, high protein
