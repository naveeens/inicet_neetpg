# Hypertension

## Definition / Classification
Multiple guideline thresholds exist (JNC vs ACC/AHA vs ESC) — know the one asked in context, and be aware definitions have shifted over time.

| Category | SBP/DBP (mmHg) — ACC/AHA 2017 |
|---|---|
| Normal | <120/<80 |
| Elevated | 120–129/<80 |
| Stage 1 HTN | 130–139 or 80–89 |
| Stage 2 HTN | ≥140 or ≥90 |
| Hypertensive urgency | ≥180/120 without acute end-organ damage |
| Hypertensive emergency | ≥180/120 WITH acute end-organ damage |

*High-yield: Older/WHO and many Indian exam questions still use 140/90 as the diagnostic cutoff for hypertension — know both frameworks and answer per the convention implied by the question.*

## Etiology
- Primary (essential) hypertension: ~90–95% of cases, multifactorial (genetic + environmental)
- Secondary hypertension (~5–10%) — think of in young patients, resistant HTN, or with clues:
| Clue | Cause |
|---|---|
| Young age, abdominal/flank bruit | Renal artery stenosis |
| Hypokalemia + metabolic alkalosis | Primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome) |
| Episodic HTN, palpitations, sweating, headache | Pheochromocytoma |
| Radiofemoral delay, BP difference in arms | Coarctation of aorta |
| Moon facies, striae, central obesity | Cushing syndrome |
| Snoring, daytime somnolence | Obstructive sleep apnea (common secondary cause) |
| History of NSAIDs, OCPs, decongestants | Drug-induced |

## Clinical Features
- Usually asymptomatic ("silent killer") — detected on routine screening
- Severe/emergency: headache, blurred vision, chest pain, dyspnea, neurological deficits, seizures (hypertensive encephalopathy)

## Investigations (Baseline Workup, All Patients)
- Urinalysis, renal function, electrolytes
- Fasting glucose, lipid profile
- ECG (LVH), fundoscopy (hypertensive retinopathy — Keith-Wagener-Barker grading)
- Consider secondary causes workup if clinical suspicion

## Target Organ Damage
| Organ | Effect |
|---|---|
| Heart | LVH, heart failure, coronary artery disease |
| Brain | Stroke (hemorrhagic/ischemic), hypertensive encephalopathy |
| Kidney | Nephrosclerosis, CKD |
| Eyes | Hypertensive retinopathy (arteriolar narrowing, AV nicking, hemorrhages, papilledema in malignant HTN) |
| Vessels | Aortic dissection, peripheral artery disease |

## Management

### Lifestyle (all patients)
- Weight loss, DASH diet, sodium restriction (<2 g/day), exercise, alcohol moderation, smoking cessation

### Pharmacologic — First-Line Classes
| Class | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ACE-I/ARB | Enalapril, Losartan | Preferred in diabetics/CKD (renoprotective); avoid in pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis |
| CCB | Amlodipine | Good in elderly, isolated systolic HTN; peripheral edema side effect |
| Thiazide diuretics | Chlorthalidone, HCTZ | Effective, cheap; watch for hypokalemia, hyperuricemia, hyperglycemia |
| Beta-blockers | Not first-line for uncomplicated HTN anymore (per updated guidelines) — reserved for compelling indications (post-MI, HF, arrhythmia) |

- Compelling indications drive drug choice: diabetes/CKD → ACEI/ARB; post-MI/HF → beta-blocker + ACEI/ARB; pregnancy → labetalol/methyldopa/nifedipine (avoid ACEI/ARB — teratogenic)
- Most patients require combination therapy to reach target

### Hypertensive Emergency
- Goal: reduce MAP by ≤25% in first hour, then gradually to ~160/100 over next 2–6 h — avoid rapid correction (risk of cerebral/coronary hypoperfusion)
- IV agents: labetalol, nicardipine, sodium nitroprusside, esmolol (context-specific: aortic dissection → esmolol/labetalol + rapid SBP control to <120; stroke has separate, more conservative thresholds)
- Hypertensive urgency: oral agents, gradual reduction over 24–48 h — no need for IV drugs/ICU

## Complications
- Stroke, MI, heart failure, aortic dissection, CKD, hypertensive retinopathy/encephalopathy

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Hypertensive emergency = elevated BP + acute end-organ damage; urgency = elevated BP without end-organ damage
- Do not lower BP too rapidly in hypertensive emergency (except aortic dissection) — risk of ischemic stroke from hypoperfusion
- ACEI/ARB contraindicated in pregnancy and bilateral renal artery stenosis
- Pheochromocytoma: episodic HTN + palpitations + sweating + headache triad
- Conn syndrome: hypertension + hypokalemia + metabolic alkalosis + low renin, high aldosterone
- Beta-blockers no longer first-line for uncomplicated essential hypertension per current guidelines — but remain essential with compelling cardiac indications
- DASH diet and sodium restriction are first-line non-pharmacologic measures for all hypertensive patients
