# HIV AIDS

## Virology/Pathophysiology
HIV-1 (most common globally) / HIV-2 (West Africa, less virulent) — retrovirus, infects CD4+ T-lymphocytes via gp120 binding CD4 receptor + CCR5/CXCR4 co-receptors → progressive CD4 depletion → immunodeficiency.

## Natural History / Staging
| Stage | CD4 count | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Acute retroviral syndrome | Variable (transient drop) | Fever, lymphadenopathy, rash, pharyngitis, mononucleosis-like illness (2–4 weeks post-exposure) |
| Clinical latency | Gradual decline | Asymptomatic, ongoing viral replication |
| Symptomatic/AIDS | <200 cells/μL (or AIDS-defining illness) | Opportunistic infections, malignancies |

## Diagnosis
- Screening: ELISA/rapid antibody tests (4th generation combines antigen p24 + antibody — detects earlier)
- Confirmatory: Western blot or a second different antibody/antigen assay per national testing algorithm (many countries now use algorithm of 2–3 different rapid tests rather than Western blot)
- HIV RNA PCR (viral load): used for early infection (window period), monitoring response to ART, and infant diagnosis (maternal antibodies cross placenta, making antibody tests unreliable in infants <18 months)
- CD4 count: monitors immune status, guides opportunistic infection prophylaxis

## CD4-Guided Opportunistic Infections (Classic Associations)
| CD4 count | Opportunistic infection |
|---|---|
| <200 | Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP), esophageal candidiasis |
| <100 | Toxoplasmosis (CNS), cryptococcal meningitis |
| <50 | CMV retinitis, disseminated Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) |
| Any CD4 (TB can occur at any stage) | Tuberculosis — most common OI in HIV in India/high-burden countries |

*High-yield: TB can occur at any CD4 count in HIV, unlike most other OIs that cluster at very low CD4 — a frequently tested distinction.*

## AIDS-Defining Illnesses (Selected, High-Yield)
- Pneumocystis pneumonia, esophageal candidiasis, CMV retinitis/disease, cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasma encephalitis, Kaposi's sarcoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, invasive cervical cancer, disseminated MAC, extrapulmonary TB, HIV wasting syndrome, PML (progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy — JC virus)

## Opportunistic Infection Prophylaxis
| Infection | Prophylaxis threshold | Drug |
|---|---|---|
| PCP | CD4 <200 | Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) |
| Toxoplasmosis | CD4 <100 (+ IgG positive) | TMP-SMX (same agent covers both) |
| MAC | CD4 <50 | Azithromycin (weekly) |

- Prophylaxis can be discontinued once CD4 rises above threshold on ART for a sustained period (immune reconstitution)

## Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)
- Current standard: ART for all HIV-positive individuals regardless of CD4 count, initiated as early as possible ("test and treat" strategy — major guideline shift from older CD4-threshold-based initiation)
- Standard first-line regimen: 2 NRTIs + an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) — e.g., tenofovir + lamivudine/emtricitabine + dolutegravir (regimens evolve; verify against latest national/WHO guideline)
- Drug classes: NRTIs, NNRTIs, protease inhibitors, integrase inhibitors, entry/fusion inhibitors
- Key drug toxicities: zidovudine (anemia/myelosuppression), tenofovir (nephrotoxicity, ↓bone density), efavirenz (CNS/psychiatric effects, teratogenic), protease inhibitors (metabolic syndrome, lipodystrophy), abacavir (hypersensitivity — screen HLA-B*5701)

## Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS)
- Paradoxical worsening of a pre-existing (often subclinical) infection after starting ART due to recovering immune response — classically seen with TB, cryptococcal disease
- Manage by continuing ART and treating the underlying infection (± steroids in severe cases); does not usually require stopping ART

## Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT)
- ART for all HIV-positive pregnant women regardless of CD4 count, continued lifelong
- Mode of delivery/infant prophylaxis and breastfeeding guidance depend on maternal viral load control — per current knowledge, verify against latest national guideline
- Untreated transmission risk is substantially higher; effective ART dramatically reduces vertical transmission risk

## Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)
- Initiate as soon as possible, ideally within hours, definitely within 72 hours of high-risk exposure
- Duration: 28 days of combination ART
- Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP): tenofovir-based regimen for high-risk HIV-negative individuals

## High-Yield One-Liners
- TB is the leading opportunistic infection and cause of death in HIV-positive individuals in high-burden countries, and can occur at any CD4 count
- PCP prophylaxis (TMP-SMX) starts at CD4 <200; also covers toxoplasmosis prophylaxis
- CMV retinitis and disseminated MAC are classic infections of very advanced AIDS (CD4 <50)
- 4th-generation ELISA (antigen + antibody) narrows the window period compared to antibody-only tests
- HIV RNA PCR, not antibody testing, is used to diagnose HIV in infants under 18 months due to transplacental maternal antibodies
- Current guidelines recommend starting ART in all HIV-positive patients immediately regardless of CD4 count ("test and treat")
- IRIS is managed by continuing ART, not stopping it, while treating the unmasked infection
- Abacavir hypersensitivity is linked to HLA-B*5701 — screen before starting
