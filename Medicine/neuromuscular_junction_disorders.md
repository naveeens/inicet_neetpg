# Neuromuscular Junction Disorders

## Myasthenia Gravis

### Pathophysiology
Autoimmune disease — antibodies against post-synaptic nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (AChR) at the neuromuscular junction → reduced effective receptor density → impaired neuromuscular transmission, worsening with repeated/sustained use (fatigability).

### Clinical Features
- Fatigable weakness — worsens with sustained activity, improves with rest, characteristically worse toward end of day
- Ocular involvement common/early: ptosis, diplopia (often asymmetric, first presenting symptom in many patients)
- Bulbar symptoms: dysarthria, dysphagia, fatigable chewing
- Proximal limb weakness in generalized disease
- Myasthenic crisis: severe generalized weakness with respiratory failure — medical emergency requiring ICU-level monitoring/ventilatory support

### Associations
- Thymic hyperplasia or thymoma — thymus gland plays a key role in pathogenesis; CT chest recommended in all newly diagnosed MG patients to evaluate for thymoma
- Associated with other autoimmune diseases (thyroid disease, etc.)

### Diagnosis
| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Anti-AChR antibody | Positive in majority of generalized MG (most specific serologic test) |
| Anti-MuSK antibody | Positive in a subset of AChR-antibody-negative MG (often more bulbar-predominant, different treatment response profile) |
| Repetitive nerve stimulation | Decremental response (progressive decline in amplitude with repeated stimulation) |
| Single-fiber EMG | Most sensitive electrophysiological test; increased jitter |
| Edrophonium (Tensilon) test | Rapid, transient improvement in weakness after IV edrophonium (short-acting acetylcholinesterase inhibitor) — historically used, less commonly performed now due to availability/safety considerations (bradycardia/cholinergic risk) — largely replaced by antibody testing and electrophysiology in current practice |
| Ice pack test | Improvement of ptosis with cooling — simple bedside test, reasonable sensitivity for ocular MG |

### Management
| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Symptomatic | Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (pyridostigmine) — first-line symptomatic therapy, increases available ACh at the synapse |
| Immunosuppression | Corticosteroids, azathioprine, mycophenolate — for inadequate response to symptomatic therapy alone |
| Thymectomy | Indicated for thymoma (regardless of age); also beneficial in generalized AChR-antibody-positive MG even without thymoma in appropriate age groups (landmark trial evidence supports this) |
| Refractory/severe disease | Biologics (rituximab, complement inhibitors like eculizumab), IVIG/plasmapheresis for crisis or refractory disease |
| Myasthenic crisis | IVIG or plasmapheresis (rapid-acting), ventilatory support as needed, identify/treat precipitant (infection, certain drugs) |

*High-yield: Avoid drugs that can worsen neuromuscular transmission in MG — aminoglycosides, certain fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, magnesium, and some anesthetic agents — a frequently tested clinical pearl.*

### Myasthenic Crisis vs Cholinergic Crisis
| Feature | Myasthenic crisis | Cholinergic crisis (anticholinesterase overdose) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Disease worsening/undertreatment | Excess acetylcholinesterase inhibitor |
| Pupils | Normal/mydriasis | Miosis |
| Other cholinergic signs | Absent | Salivation, lacrimation, diarrhea, bradycardia (SLUDGE-type symptoms) |
| Edrophonium test response | Improves | Worsens |

## Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS)

### Pathophysiology
Autoantibodies against presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels → reduced acetylcholine release from presynaptic terminal.

### Clinical Features
- Proximal muscle weakness that classically IMPROVES with repeated activity (opposite of MG — "warm-up" phenomenon), though may fatigue with sustained/prolonged effort
- Autonomic symptoms common: dry mouth, orthostatic hypotension, erectile dysfunction
- Strongly associated with malignancy — classically small cell lung cancer (paraneoplastic syndrome) — always screen for underlying malignancy at diagnosis

### Diagnosis
- Anti-voltage-gated calcium channel (VGCC) antibodies
- Repetitive nerve stimulation: incremental response (increasing amplitude with repeated/rapid stimulation) — opposite of MG's decremental pattern

### Management
- Treat underlying malignancy if present (paraneoplastic cases may improve with tumor treatment)
- 3,4-diaminopyridine (enhances presynaptic ACh release), pyridostigmine as adjunct, immunosuppression/IVIG for severe cases

## Myasthenia Gravis vs Lambert-Eaton — Key Differentiator Table
| Feature | Myasthenia Gravis | Lambert-Eaton Syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| Antibody target | Postsynaptic AChR (or MuSK) | Presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channel |
| Effect of repeated activity | Worsens (fatigable) | Improves initially ("warm-up"), then may fatigue |
| Repetitive nerve stimulation | Decremental response | Incremental response |
| Ocular/bulbar involvement | Prominent, often early | Less prominent |
| Autonomic symptoms | Absent | Present (dry mouth, orthostasis) |
| Malignancy association | Thymoma | Small cell lung cancer |

## Other NMJ-Related Conditions (Brief)

### Botulism
- Clostridium botulinum toxin blocks presynaptic ACh release (similar mechanism target to LEMS but toxin-mediated, irreversible until new synapse formed)
- Descending paralysis, starting with cranial nerves (diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia) progressing to limb/respiratory muscles; autonomic symptoms also occur
- Sources: improperly canned/preserved food (adult), honey (infant botulism)
- Management: antitoxin (if available/early), supportive care including ventilatory support as needed

### Organophosphate Poisoning
- Acetylcholinesterase inhibition → excess ACh at both muscarinic and nicotinic synapses → cholinergic crisis
- Clinical: SLUDGE symptoms (Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Diarrhea, GI distress, Emesis) + miosis + bradycardia (muscarinic), plus muscle fasciculations/weakness (nicotinic)
- Management: atropine (blocks muscarinic effects), pralidoxime (reactivates acetylcholinesterase — most effective if given early before "aging" of the enzyme-toxin complex)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Myasthenia gravis: fatigable weakness worsening with activity, ocular symptoms often first, decremental response on repetitive nerve stimulation, anti-AChR antibody positive
- Lambert-Eaton syndrome: weakness improves with repeated activity initially, incremental response on repetitive nerve stimulation, strongly linked to small cell lung cancer
- Always image the chest (CT) in newly diagnosed MG to screen for thymoma
- Avoid aminoglycosides, certain fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, and magnesium in myasthenia gravis — they can precipitate crisis
- Myasthenic crisis improves with edrophonium; cholinergic crisis worsens with edrophonium and shows miosis plus SLUDGE-type symptoms
- Pyridostigmine is first-line symptomatic therapy for MG; thymectomy benefits both thymoma-associated and generalized AChR-antibody-positive MG
- Organophosphate poisoning is treated with atropine (muscarinic blockade) plus pralidoxime (enzyme reactivation, most effective early)
- Botulism causes a descending flaccid paralysis starting with cranial nerve involvement, from presynaptic blockade of acetylcholine release
