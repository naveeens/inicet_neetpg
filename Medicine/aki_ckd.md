# AKI CKD

## Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

### Definition (KDIGO Criteria)
Any of:
- Rise in serum creatinine ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, OR
- Rise in serum creatinine ≥1.5x baseline within 7 days, OR
- Urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hr for ≥6 hours

### Classification — Pre-renal, Intrinsic (Renal), Post-renal
| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-renal | Reduced renal perfusion (kidney itself normal) | Hypovolemia, hemorrhage, sepsis/distributive shock, heart failure (cardiorenal), NSAIDs (afferent constriction), ACEI/ARB (efferent dilation — reduces GFR especially with bilateral renal artery stenosis) |
| Intrinsic (renal) | Direct damage to renal parenchyma | Acute tubular necrosis (ischemic or nephrotoxic — most common intrinsic cause), acute interstitial nephritis (drug-induced — classic: NSAIDs, penicillins), glomerulonephritis, vasculitis |
| Post-renal | Obstruction to urine outflow | BPH, stones, malignancy, neurogenic bladder, bilateral ureteral obstruction |

### Differentiating Pre-renal AKI from Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)
| Parameter | Pre-renal | ATN |
|---|---|---|
| FENa (fractional excretion of sodium) | <1% | >2% |
| Urine osmolality | High (>500) — concentrated | Low (isosthenuric, ~300) |
| BUN/Creatinine ratio | >20:1 | <15:1 (~10-15:1) |
| Urine sediment | Bland | Muddy brown casts, tubular epithelial cells |
| Response to fluids | Improves | No improvement |

*High-yield: Muddy brown casts on urinalysis are classic/pathognomonic for acute tubular necrosis.*

### Clinical Features
- Oliguria/anuria (not always present — non-oliguric AKI occurs, especially with nephrotoxic ATN)
- Volume overload signs, uremic symptoms in severe cases (nausea, confusion, pericarditis, asterixis)
- Electrolyte disturbances: hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, hyperphosphatemia

### Management
- Treat underlying cause (fluids for pre-renal, remove nephrotoxins, relieve obstruction for post-renal)
- Avoid/adjust nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast agents, ACEI/ARB in acute setting if hemodynamically significant)
- Monitor and correct electrolytes/acid-base disturbances
- Indications for urgent dialysis (mnemonic "AEIOU"): Acidosis (severe, refractory), Electrolyte abnormality (severe hyperkalemia refractory to medical management), Ingestion/Intoxication (certain dialyzable toxins), Overload (fluid, refractory to diuretics), Uremia (symptomatic — encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding)

## Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

### Definition
Abnormalities of kidney structure or function, present for >3 months, with implications for health — based on GFR and/or markers of kidney damage (e.g., albuminuria).

### Staging (by eGFR)
| Stage | eGFR (mL/min/1.73m²) |
|---|---|
| G1 | ≥90 (normal, but with kidney damage marker) |
| G2 | 60–89 |
| G3a | 45–59 |
| G3b | 30–44 |
| G4 | 15–29 |
| G5 | <15 (kidney failure / ESRD) |

- Albuminuria categories (A1/A2/A3) are combined with GFR staging for overall risk stratification (KDIGO heat map) — verify against latest guideline for exact risk categorization

### Etiology
- Diabetic nephropathy (most common cause of CKD/ESRD globally in most settings)
- Hypertensive nephrosclerosis (second most common)
- Glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, chronic obstruction, reflux nephropathy

### Clinical Features
- Often asymptomatic until advanced stages
- Uremic symptoms (advanced): fatigue, anorexia, nausea, pruritus, uremic encephalopathy, pericarditis, platelet dysfunction (bleeding tendency despite normal counts)
- Anemia (normocytic — reduced erythropoietin production), renal osteodystrophy (secondary hyperparathyroidism from phosphate retention/low vitamin D activation), hypertension

### Investigations
- Serum creatinine/eGFR, urine albumin-creatinine ratio
- Renal USG: small, shrunken, echogenic kidneys support chronicity (vs normal/enlarged size favoring AKI, with exceptions like diabetic nephropathy or amyloidosis which can be large even in CKD)
- Workup for reversible/treatable causes, especially at initial presentation

### Complications
- Anemia (erythropoietin deficiency) — manage with erythropoiesis-stimulating agents + iron
- Renal osteodystrophy: secondary hyperparathyroidism, ↓Vitamin D activation (kidney does 1-alpha hydroxylation), hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia — manage with phosphate binders, active vitamin D analogues, calcimimetics as needed
- Metabolic acidosis (reduced acid excretion) — sodium bicarbonate supplementation may slow progression
- Hyperkalemia, fluid overload/hypertension, cardiovascular disease (leading cause of death in CKD/ESRD patients)

### Management
- Slow progression: strict BP control (ACEI/ARB preferred — reduce intraglomerular pressure/proteinuria, renoprotective independent of BP effect), glycemic control in diabetics, SGLT2 inhibitors now shown to slow CKD progression regardless of diabetes status (guideline update — verify against latest recommendation)
- Dietary modification: protein restriction (modest), sodium/potassium/phosphate restriction as needed
- Avoid nephrotoxins, adjust drug dosing for renal function
- Manage complications (anemia, bone disease, acidosis) as above
- Renal replacement therapy (dialysis or transplantation) when GFR very low with symptoms/complications refractory to medical management — transplantation is the treatment of choice for eligible ESRD patients (better survival/quality of life than long-term dialysis)

## High-Yield One-Liners
- FENa <1% and high urine osmolality support pre-renal AKI; FENa >2% and muddy brown casts support ATN
- Muddy brown casts are pathognomonic for acute tubular necrosis
- Diabetic nephropathy is the leading cause of CKD/ESRD in most populations globally
- ACEI/ARB slow CKD progression by reducing intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria, independent of blood pressure lowering alone
- SGLT2 inhibitors have renoprotective benefits in CKD regardless of diabetes status, per updated evidence
- Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in CKD/ESRD patients, not renal failure itself
- Small, shrunken, echogenic kidneys on ultrasound favor chronicity over acute injury (exceptions: diabetic nephropathy, amyloidosis, HIV nephropathy, polycystic kidney disease may retain normal/increased size)
- Indications for urgent dialysis: refractory acidosis, refractory hyperkalemia, certain toxin ingestions, refractory fluid overload, symptomatic uremia ("AEIOU")
