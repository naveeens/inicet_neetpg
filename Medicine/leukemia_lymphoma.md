# Leukemia Lymphoma

## Leukemias — Overview
| Type | Typical age | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) | Most common childhood leukemia | Blasts (lymphoblasts) on smear, TdT positive; good prognosis in children, especially with certain favorable genetics; CNS involvement common — needs prophylaxis |
| Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) | Most common acute leukemia in adults | Myeloblasts, myeloperoxidase positive; Auer rods (pathognomonic, especially APL/M3 subtype); APL associated with t(15;17), treated with ATRA (all-trans retinoic acid) — dramatically improved prognosis |
| Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) | Most common leukemia in adults overall (Western data), older adults | Smudge cells on smear, often asymptomatic/incidental lymphocytosis; can transform to aggressive lymphoma (Richter transformation) |
| Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML) | Middle-aged adults | Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) → BCR-ABL fusion; markedly elevated WBC count with full spectrum of granulocytic maturation, low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase (LAP score — differentiates from leukemoid reaction where LAP is high); imatinib (tyrosine kinase inhibitor) revolutionized treatment |

*High-yield: Auer rods are pathognomonic for AML (especially acute promyelocytic leukemia); Philadelphia chromosome (BCR-ABL) is the hallmark of CML and is also found in a subset of ALL (worse prognostic marker in ALL context).*

### Clinical Features (General, Acute Leukemias)
- Bone marrow failure triad: anemia (fatigue, pallor), thrombocytopenia (bleeding, petechiae), leukopenia/dysfunctional WBCs (infections) despite high total WBC count (blasts are non-functional)
- Bone pain (marrow infiltration), hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, gum hypertrophy (classic for AML M4/M5 monocytic subtypes)
- APL (AML-M3): high risk of DIC/coagulopathy at presentation — medical emergency, start ATRA promptly

### Diagnosis
- Peripheral smear + bone marrow biopsy/aspirate: blast count ≥20% typically required for diagnosis of acute leukemia
- Immunophenotyping (flow cytometry): differentiates lymphoblastic vs myeloid lineage
- Cytogenetics: prognostic and therapeutic implications (e.g., t(15;17) in APL, t(9;22) in CML/some ALL, t(8;14) in Burkitt lymphoma)

### Management (Overview)
- ALL: multi-agent chemotherapy (induction, consolidation, maintenance) + CNS prophylaxis (intrathecal chemotherapy) — excellent prognosis in pediatric ALL
- AML: induction chemotherapy ("7+3" — cytarabine + anthracycline, classic regimen), consolidation, allogeneic transplant for high-risk/relapsed disease; APL treated with ATRA ± arsenic trioxide (chemotherapy-sparing approach now common) — different pathway from other AML subtypes
- CLL: observation if asymptomatic (many patients don't need treatment for years); targeted therapy (BTK inhibitors like ibrutinib, BCL-2 inhibitors) increasingly preferred over traditional chemoimmunotherapy
- CML: tyrosine kinase inhibitors (imatinib and successors) — first-line, dramatically changed prognosis from historically fatal to a manageable chronic condition

## Lymphomas

### Hodgkin Lymphoma
- Bimodal age distribution (young adults, older adults)
- Reed-Sternberg cells (binucleate, "owl eye" appearance) — pathognomonic
- Painless lymphadenopathy (often cervical/supraclavicular), contiguous spread (nodal-to-nodal, predictable pattern), "B symptoms" (fever, night sweats, weight loss) important for staging
- Ann Arbor staging (I-IV based on nodal groups/extranodal involvement) with A/B suffix for symptom presence
- Alcohol-induced pain at nodal sites — classic but uncommon finding
- Management: combination chemotherapy (e.g., ABVD regimen) ± radiotherapy depending on stage; generally good prognosis, especially early stage

### Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL)
- Heterogeneous group, more common than Hodgkin overall; non-contiguous spread (unlike Hodgkin)
| Subtype | Feature |
|---|---|
| Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma | Most common NHL subtype overall; aggressive but potentially curable |
| Follicular lymphoma | Indolent, t(14;18) — BCL2 overexpression |
| Burkitt lymphoma | Highly aggressive, "starry sky" appearance, t(8;14) — MYC translocation; associated with EBV (especially endemic African form), also seen in immunocompromised |
| MALT lymphoma | Associated with H. pylori (gastric MALT) — can regress with H. pylori eradication alone in early disease |
| Mantle cell lymphoma | t(11;14) — cyclin D1 overexpression |

- Management: chemoimmunotherapy (e.g., R-CHOP for DLBCL — rituximab added to CHOP dramatically improved outcomes), radiotherapy for localized disease, targeted/biologic agents increasingly used

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Auer rods are pathognomonic for AML, especially the M3 (acute promyelocytic leukemia) subtype
- APL (t(15;17)) is treated with ATRA — a classic differentiation therapy, distinct from standard AML chemotherapy, and requires urgent treatment due to DIC risk
- Philadelphia chromosome (BCR-ABL, t(9;22)) is the hallmark of CML; imatinib transformed CML from a fatal disease into a chronic manageable condition
- Smudge cells on peripheral smear are characteristic of CLL
- Reed-Sternberg cells (binucleate, owl-eye) are pathognomonic for Hodgkin lymphoma
- Hodgkin lymphoma spreads contiguously between nodal groups; non-Hodgkin lymphoma spreads non-contiguously
- Burkitt lymphoma shows a "starry sky" histological pattern and is associated with MYC translocation t(8;14) and EBV
- Gastric MALT lymphoma is linked to H. pylori and can regress with eradication therapy alone in early-stage disease
- Rituximab (anti-CD20) added to chemotherapy (R-CHOP) significantly improved outcomes in B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomas
