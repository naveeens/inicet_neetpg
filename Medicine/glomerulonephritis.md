# Glomerulonephritis

## Nephritic vs Nephrotic Syndrome — Key Distinction
| Feature | Nephritic Syndrome | Nephrotic Syndrome |
|---|---|---|
| Proteinuria | Sub-nephrotic (usually <3.5 g/day) | Massive (≥3.5 g/day) |
| Hematuria | Prominent (dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts) | Usually absent/minimal |
| Hypertension | Common | Less common (though can occur) |
| Edema | Present but less than nephrotic | Prominent (periorbital, dependent, anasarca) |
| Mechanism | Inflammatory injury to glomerular capillary wall (immune-mediated) | Increased glomerular basement membrane permeability to protein |

## Nephritic Syndrome — Causes

| Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Post-streptococcal GN | Children, 1-3 weeks after streptococcal throat/skin infection; low C3, resolves spontaneously in most |
| IgA nephropathy (Berger's disease) | Most common cause of primary glomerulonephritis worldwide; episodic hematuria classically 1-2 days after upper respiratory infection ("synpharyngitic" — key differentiator from post-strep GN's latent period) |
| Rapidly progressive GN (crescentic) | Anti-GBM disease (Goodpasture syndrome — also causes pulmonary hemorrhage), ANCA-associated vasculitis (granulomatosis with polyangiitis, microscopic polyangiitis), immune complex-mediated (lupus nephritis, post-infectious) — rapid loss of renal function over days-weeks, crescents on biopsy |
| Lupus nephritis | SLE-associated; various classes (I-VI) with differing prognosis; class IV (diffuse proliferative) most severe |
| Membranoproliferative GN | Can present with nephritic or mixed nephrotic/nephritic features; associated with hepatitis C, cryoglobulinemia |

*High-yield: IgA nephropathy presents with hematuria concurrent with or immediately after an upper respiratory infection (synpharyngitic); post-streptococcal GN has a latent period of 1–3 weeks between infection and nephritis — this timing distinction is a classic exam differentiator.*

### Diagnosis
- Urinalysis: dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts (pathognomonic for glomerular bleeding)
- Complement levels: low C3/C4 in post-strep GN, lupus nephritis, membranoproliferative GN; normal complement in IgA nephropathy, anti-GBM disease, ANCA vasculitis
- Specific serology: ASO titer (post-strep), ANA/anti-dsDNA (lupus), ANCA (vasculitis), anti-GBM antibody (Goodpasture)
- Renal biopsy: definitive diagnosis, especially for RPGN (urgent biopsy needed given rapid progression)

### Management
- Post-strep GN: supportive (self-limiting in most children), treat hypertension/edema
- RPGN: medical emergency — high-dose corticosteroids + cyclophosphamide/rituximab (immunosuppression), plasmapheresis especially for anti-GBM disease/severe ANCA vasculitis with pulmonary hemorrhage
- IgA nephropathy: ACEI/ARB for proteinuria/BP control; immunosuppression reserved for progressive/high-risk disease
- Lupus nephritis: immunosuppression based on class/severity (steroids + mycophenolate/cyclophosphamide typically)

## Nephrotic Syndrome — Causes

| Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Minimal change disease | Most common cause in children; excellent response to steroids; normal glomeruli on light microscopy, podocyte foot process effacement on electron microscopy |
| Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) | Most common cause in adults (especially African-descent populations in Western data); can be primary or secondary (HIV, obesity, heroin use); more steroid-resistant than MCD |
| Membranous nephropathy | Common cause of adult nephrotic syndrome; associated with malignancy (esp. in older adults), hepatitis B, autoimmune disease; anti-PLA2R antibody now a key diagnostic/monitoring marker; "spike and dome" appearance on EM |
| Diabetic nephropathy | Leading cause of nephrotic-range proteinuria overall (via diabetes prevalence); nodular glomerulosclerosis (Kimmelstiel-Wilson) |
| Amyloidosis | Associated with multiple myeloma, chronic inflammatory disease; apple-green birefringence with Congo red stain under polarized light |

### Clinical Features
- Generalized edema (periorbital, scrotal/labial, anasarca), frothy urine (proteinuria)
- Complications: hypercoagulability (loss of antithrombin III — risk of renal vein thrombosis, DVT/PE), infection risk (loss of immunoglobulins), hyperlipidemia (compensatory hepatic lipoprotein synthesis), protein malnutrition

### Diagnosis
- 24-hour urine protein or spot urine protein-creatinine ratio (≥3.5 g/day-equivalent)
- Serum albumin (low), lipid profile (elevated)
- Renal biopsy for adults (to determine specific etiology); often not needed in children (empirical steroid trial given high likelihood of minimal change disease)

### Management
- Minimal change disease: corticosteroids (excellent response, first-line); relapse common but usually remains steroid-sensitive
- FSGS/membranous: steroids ± other immunosuppressants (calcineurin inhibitors, cyclophosphamide) depending on response; secondary causes treated by addressing underlying disease
- General supportive: ACEI/ARB (reduce proteinuria), diuretics for edema, statins for hyperlipidemia, anticoagulation if severe hypoalbuminemia/high thrombosis risk (especially membranous nephropathy)
- Salt restriction

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Dysmorphic RBCs and RBC casts on urinalysis are hallmarks of glomerular (nephritic) bleeding
- IgA nephropathy presents with hematuria synpharyngitic with a URI; post-streptococcal GN has a 1-3 week latent period
- Minimal change disease is the most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in children; FSGS is most common in adults
- Membranous nephropathy in an older adult should prompt a malignancy workup; anti-PLA2R antibody is a key diagnostic marker
- Nephrotic syndrome causes a hypercoagulable state due to urinary loss of antithrombin III — renal vein thrombosis is a classic complication (especially membranous nephropathy)
- Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis (crescentic GN) is a medical emergency requiring urgent biopsy and immunosuppression
- Low C3/C4 complement occurs in post-streptococcal GN, lupus nephritis, and membranoproliferative GN; normal complement occurs in IgA nephropathy and ANCA-associated disease
- Goodpasture syndrome (anti-GBM disease) classically presents with combined pulmonary hemorrhage and rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis
