# Acute Coronary Syndrome

## Spectrum
| Type | Troponin | ECG |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable Angina | Negative | Normal / ST depression / T inversion |
| NSTEMI | Positive | ST depression / T inversion (no persistent ST elevation) |
| STEMI | Positive | Persistent ST elevation / new LBBB |

## Pathophysiology
Rupture of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque → platelet aggregation + thrombus formation. UA/NSTEMI = subtotal occlusion; STEMI = complete occlusion with transmural infarction.

## Clinical Features
- Retrosternal chest pain >20 min, radiating to left arm/jaw, not relieved by rest/nitrates
- Associated: sweating, nausea, dyspnea
- Silent MI more common in **diabetics, elderly, women** (atypical presentation — high-yield exam trap)

## ECG Localization (Coronary Artery Correlation)
| Leads | Wall | Artery |
|---|---|---|
| II, III, aVF | Inferior | RCA (usually) |
| V1–V2 | Septal | LAD (septal branch) |
| V3–V4 | Anterior | LAD |
| V5–V6, I, aVL | Lateral | LCX |
| V1–V2 (ST depression, tall R) + inferior changes | Posterior | RCA/LCX (posterior descending) |
| V4R / right-sided leads | RV infarct | RCA (proximal) — check in every inferior MI before nitrates! |

*High-yield: Never give nitrates/morphine in RV infarction — preload dependent, causes profound hypotension.*

## Biomarkers
| Marker | Rise | Peak | Normalize |
|---|---|---|---|
| CK-MB | 4–6 h | 24 h | 48–72 h |
| Troponin I/T | 3–4 h | 24–48 h | 7–10 days (T) / 5–7 days (I) |
| Myoglobin | 1–2 h (earliest) | 4–12 h | 24 h |

- Troponin = most sensitive & specific; preferred marker
- CK-MB useful for detecting **reinfarction** (troponin stays elevated too long)

## Management

### Immediate (all ACS) — "MONA-BASH" (mnemonic, but modernized)
- **A**spirin (loading 325 mg, chewed) — mortality benefit
- **B**eta-blocker (unless cardiogenic shock, bradycardia, decompensated HF)
- Anticoagulation: Heparin (UFH/LMWH) / Fondaparinux
- **S**tatin (high-intensity, e.g., atorvastatin 80 mg) — plaque stabilization
- **H**eparin
- Oxygen — only if SpO2 <90%
- Nitrates — symptomatic relief (avoid in RV infarct/hypotension)
- Morphine — for pain refractory to nitrates (caution: may mask ongoing ischemia, some data ↑ mortality)

### STEMI-Specific — Reperfusion is time-critical
- **Primary PCI** = gold standard if available within **90 min of first medical contact (door-to-balloon)**
- **Thrombolysis** (streptokinase/tenecteplase/alteplase) if PCI not available within 120 min — door-to-needle **<30 min**
- "Time is muscle" — golden period for salvage <12 h (best <6 h)
- Absolute contraindications to thrombolysis: prior hemorrhagic stroke, active internal bleeding, recent major trauma/surgery, suspected aortic dissection

### NSTEMI/UA — Risk stratify (TIMI / GRACE score)
- High-risk (dynamic ECG changes, positive troponin, hemodynamic instability): early invasive strategy (angiography <24 h)
- Dual antiplatelet therapy: Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor (Clopidogrel/Ticagrelor/Prasugrel)

## Complications (time-based — classic exam favorite)
| Timing | Complication |
|---|---|
| First hours | Arrhythmia (VF = most common cause of pre-hospital death) |
| 24–72 h | Papillary muscle dysfunction, pericarditis (early) |
| 3–7 days | **Free wall rupture** (peak risk), papillary muscle rupture → acute MR, VSD |
| Weeks | Dressler syndrome (autoimmune pericarditis), ventricular aneurysm, mural thrombus |

## High-Yield One-Liners
- Most common cause of death in first hour post-MI: **ventricular fibrillation**
- Most common overall cause of death post-MI: **cardiogenic shock / pump failure**
- Troponin is preferred over CK-MB for diagnosis; CK-MB preferred for detecting reinfarction
- Inferior MI → always check right-sided leads (V4R) for RV involvement before nitrates
- Post-MI, best next step for recurrent angina after successful thrombolysis with no reperfusion signs → rescue PCI
