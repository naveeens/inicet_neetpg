# Rheumatoid Arthritis SLE Connective Tissue Disease

## Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

### Pathophysiology
Chronic autoimmune inflammatory polyarthritis — synovial inflammation (pannus formation) → cartilage/bone erosion. Genetic association: HLA-DR4.

### Clinical Features
- Symmetric, small joint polyarthritis (MCP, PIP, wrist — classically spares DIP joints, unlike osteoarthritis)
- Morning stiffness >1 hour, improves with activity (differentiates inflammatory from mechanical/degenerative joint pain)
- Deformities (late disease): ulnar deviation, swan neck deformity, boutonniere deformity, Z-thumb deformity
- Extra-articular: rheumatoid nodules (subcutaneous, pressure points), interstitial lung disease, pericarditis, episcleritis/scleritis, Felty syndrome (RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia), Caplan syndrome (RA + pneumoconiosis with pulmonary nodules)

### Diagnosis
- Rheumatoid factor (RF): moderate sensitivity/specificity
- Anti-CCP (anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide) antibody: more specific than RF, can predate clinical disease, useful for early diagnosis and prognosis (associated with more erosive disease)
- Elevated ESR/CRP (inflammatory markers, non-specific)
- X-ray: periarticular osteopenia, joint space narrowing, marginal erosions (early changes); classification criteria (ACR/EULAR) combine joint involvement, serology, acute phase reactants, symptom duration

### Management
| Category | Drugs |
|---|---|
| Symptom control | NSAIDs (symptomatic only, no disease-modifying effect), short-course corticosteroids (bridge therapy) |
| DMARDs (disease-modifying, cornerstone of treatment) | Methotrexate (first-line DMARD), sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide |
| Biologics (for inadequate response to conventional DMARDs) | Anti-TNF (etanercept, infliximab, adalimumab), anti-IL6 (tocilizumab), anti-CD20 (rituximab), abatacept, JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib) |

*High-yield: Early initiation of DMARDs (especially methotrexate) is critical — "window of opportunity" concept — to prevent irreversible joint damage; current strategy is aggressive early treat-to-target approach rather than the older stepwise/pyramid approach.*

- Methotrexate side effects: hepatotoxicity, bone marrow suppression, pneumonitis; folic acid supplementation reduces some side effects
- Screen for latent TB and hepatitis B before starting biologics (especially anti-TNF agents) — reactivation risk

## Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

### Clinical Features (Multisystem)
- Malar ("butterfly") rash (spares nasolabial folds), discoid rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers (painless), non-erosive arthritis, serositis (pleuritis/pericarditis), renal involvement (lupus nephritis), neurological (seizures, psychosis), hematological (cytopenias), Raynaud's phenomenon

### Diagnosis
- ANA (antinuclear antibody): highly sensitive screening test (positive in nearly all SLE), but not specific
- Anti-dsDNA: highly specific for SLE, correlates with disease activity/lupus nephritis flares
- Anti-Smith (anti-Sm): highly specific, doesn't correlate with activity
- Anti-Ro/La (SSA/SSB): associated with neonatal lupus/congenital heart block, also seen in Sjögren's
- Antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-beta2 glycoprotein I): associated with thrombosis, recurrent miscarriage (antiphospholipid syndrome can be primary or secondary to SLE)
- Low complement (C3/C4) during active disease/flares (consumption)

### Management
- Hydroxychloroquine: foundational therapy for essentially all SLE patients (reduces flares, organ damage — mortality benefit shown); requires periodic ophthalmologic monitoring (retinal toxicity risk with long-term use)
- Corticosteroids: for flares/organ-threatening disease, tapered to lowest effective dose
- Immunosuppressants (mycophenolate, azathioprine, cyclophosphamide): for major organ involvement (nephritis, CNS lupus)
- Biologics (belimumab, anifrolumab): add-on for refractory disease (evolving evidence base)
- NSAIDs for mild musculoskeletal symptoms

### Complications
- Lupus nephritis (major cause of morbidity/mortality — see glomerulonephritis notes for classes)
- Increased cardiovascular risk (accelerated atherosclerosis), infection risk (disease + immunosuppression), antiphospholipid syndrome-related thrombosis

## Other Connective Tissue Diseases (Brief High-Yield Points)

### Sjögren Syndrome
- Dry eyes (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) + dry mouth (xerostomia) — autoimmune destruction of exocrine glands
- Anti-Ro/La positive; increased risk of MALT lymphoma
- Schirmer's test for dry eyes; management is symptomatic (artificial tears/saliva substitutes) + immunosuppression for systemic involvement

### Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)
- Limited (CREST: Calcinosis, Raynaud's, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia) — anti-centromere antibody
- Diffuse — more widespread skin/organ involvement, anti-Scl-70 (anti-topoisomerase) antibody, higher risk of early severe interstitial lung disease and scleroderma renal crisis
- Scleroderma renal crisis: malignant hypertension + AKI — treated urgently with ACE inhibitors (specific exception where ACEI is used emergently in AKI setting)

### Polymyositis/Dermatomyositis
- Proximal muscle weakness (symmetric), elevated CK/muscle enzymes
- Dermatomyositis-specific skin findings: heliotrope rash (periorbital), Gottron's papules (over knuckles)
- Associated with increased malignancy risk (especially dermatomyositis) — warrants age-appropriate cancer screening
- Diagnosis: elevated CK, EMG, muscle biopsy, myositis-specific antibodies
- Management: corticosteroids first-line, steroid-sparing immunosuppressants for maintenance

### Mixed Connective Tissue Disease
- Overlap features of SLE, systemic sclerosis, polymyositis; anti-U1-RNP antibody characteristic

## Mnemonic (SLE — "SOAP BRAIN MD" — ACR-style criteria, illustrative)
Serositis, Oral ulcers, Arthritis, Photosensitivity, Blood disorders, Renal disorder, ANA positive, Immunologic disorder, Neurologic disorder, Malar rash, Discoid rash

## High-Yield One-Liners
- RA classically spares the DIP joints and involves MCP/PIP/wrist symmetrically; anti-CCP is more specific than RF and predicts erosive disease
- Early DMARD (methotrexate) initiation within the "window of opportunity" prevents irreversible joint damage in RA
- ANA is the most sensitive screening test for SLE; anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith are the most specific
- Anti-dsDNA titers correlate with SLE disease activity, especially lupus nephritis flares; anti-Smith does not
- Hydroxychloroquine is foundational therapy in nearly all SLE patients and requires periodic retinal screening
- CREST syndrome (limited scleroderma) is associated with anti-centromere antibody; diffuse scleroderma with anti-Scl-70
- Scleroderma renal crisis is treated urgently with ACE inhibitors despite the AKI — an important exception to usual practice
- Dermatomyositis carries an increased risk of underlying malignancy and warrants cancer screening
